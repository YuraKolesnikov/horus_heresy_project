# Luna Wolves / Sons of Horus
Brutal, ruthless and unwavering but also honourable and loyal beyond question...

## Legion Structure and Customs
It was said that the XVI were unleashed to begin and end wars their enemies did not know they were fighting. They would come out of the night or in the dawn, carried in the holds of Stormbirds and Storm Eagles, flanked by squadrons of escorting Lightning Crows. Those who witnessed such attacks say that the XVI were fighting eye-to-eye with the enemy before the thunder of their missiles and shell-strikes faded.

Proud, little given to humour or empathy, nor mysticism nor even the ritual of military elite, they were a breed apart from the true human forces that fought at their side. Their aggression was clear in their every action, but so was the strength of the control that held it in check, and the needs of that age easily accommodated such a belligerent and cantankerous attitude.

First as the Luna Wolves and later as the Sons of Horus, the Legion kept much of their outward structure as it had existed since the wars for Terran and Solar unity.  The smallest formation was the squad. This consisted of a group of Luna Wolves under the command of a sergeant. Squads varied widely in both size and specialisation, with the majority of units ranging between 10 and 20 Space Marines within the XVI Legion. Conversely very specialised squads, such as reconnaissance units, or those that might have suffered heavy casualties, might only consist of a handful of Space Marines in active service.

The specialities and equipment of squads in the Luna Wolves included all of those generally found in other Legions, including those designated Destroyer and Seeker units, shunned b y some in lesser quantities. They also maintained considerable resources in terms of armour and vehicles, with tithed industrial worlds they had been brought into compliance and good relations with the Mechanicum ensuring a steady supply of munitions and materiels for the Legion, which spent such coin readily.

It is noticeable that until the Lactrical Onslaught the number of shield-equipped Breacher squads was relatively low in the XVI Legion. The contribution of such units amongst Imperial Fists forces cannot have escaped his notice. After the annihilation of the Lactrical, the number of such squads in the ranks of the Luna Wolves rose noticeably. Other anecdotal evidence of this adaptability and willingness to embrace new weapons of warfare can be found in Horus' vocal backing of the Tactical Dreadnought Armour project, with the result that his Legion was one of the first and most widely equipped with Terminator armour and at the forefront of the development of tactics for its use in assaults.

If Horus and his Legion showed a preference for a particular squad formation it was towards the Tactical squad. Squads configured in this form within the Sons of Horus outnumbered all other squad types combined throughout the Great Crusade. The presence of Tactical-type squads designed to be held in reserve and unleashed once a weakness in an enemy line had been identified, trained in fire saturation and carrying additional close combat weaponry for use in overrunning enemy positions — the so called Despoiler squads — shows again the dominance of the Tactical squad in Horus' tactical thinking. Such was the effectiveness of this subdivided tactic that its use was copied in several different Legions, such as the White Scars and Iron Warriors, who fielded Despoiler squads of their own.

There was no fixed strength for a Company within the Sons of Horus. While other Legions codified and enforced strict limits on the size of similar formations, this was not the case amongst the Sons of Horus, which had begun in a more regimented form, but has become increasingly ad hoc in structure and disposition over time.

By way of example, the 17th Company, known as the Hesperus Guard, had a standard strength of 205 at the time of the bombing of Isstvan III. Tactical squads made up half this strength numerically (with the Legion placing in general great importance on the use of Tactical squads in every deployment). The rest consisted of two veteran units, three reconnaissance units, a heavy support squad and multiple batteries of support weaponry. The 1st Company showed even greater variation. Small in number it contained two distinct sub formations: the Justaerin Terminator squads formed one part and the Catulan Reaver assault squads the other. Both sported the black armour worn only by this elite company and each was led by a captain under the overall command of the First Captain.

### Warrior Lodges
Warrior lodges seem to have existed within the Sons of Horus since their first contact with the Davin and the warrior lodges of the Davinite tribes on which many believe they were based, and it may be that this benighted and thrice cursed world was the source of the cancer that grew at the heart of the Imperium's finest warriors.

### Mournival
The Mournival were Horus' most trusted advisors and confidants. Consisting of four chosen captains, the Mournival existed outside of the rest of the XVI Legion's command structure. Together the Mournival functioned as the soul of the Luna Wolves, and later the Sons of Horus, supporting their Primarch and steering the Legion's temperament and decisions. Horus relied on the Mournival to balance his decisions with questions and different perspectives. In this way the four captains were a combination of counsellors, confidants and naysmiths. It is known that there was an element of ritual to the bond between the Mournival brothers: meetings of the Mournival took place in moonlight, a different phase of the moon was graven on to the helm of each brother, and its oaths of brotherhood were made over the reflected image of a moon. The origins of the Mournival are unclear although there are parallels with the rites of brotherhood amongst the Jutigran clans and the practices of the gene-cults of Luna. Whatever the Mournival's origin its existence within the Legion certainly lies in the early decades of the Legion's creation, and it is known to have persisted even after the Isstvan III atrocity.

## Legion Armory
- Within the Sons of Horus squads also commonly had their own honorific or epithetic titles rather than simple numeration: Illuminators Prime, Death Makers, Jerrok's Reavers, the First Sons, and similar, while some were named for the sergeant or chieftain that led them where their leader's own reputation was strong enough alone. One avatar of this increasing influence was the late re-emergence of Chthonian gang-sigils graven into a particular Space Marine’s armour recording notable kills and deeds, as well as to which Company they belonged, a practice which accelerated rapidly after the Legion had transitioned into its identity as the Sons of Horus.
- The white of the Luna Wolves turned sea-green, then darkened further to a murky verdigris green-black as the Sons of Horus further cast off the disciplined Terran traditions of warfare and erred in pride.
- Beneath Horus were his captains, beneath them were the unit sergeants, and where a formation of squads came together for a purpose, the informal rank of 'Chieftain' was given to the sergeant granted field command authority—a matter not always of seniority, but rather selection of the best or most suited for the task at hand; an approach which fitted well with the Legion's pragmatic and sometimes impulsive approach to warfare. Beneath them were the battle-brothers of the Sons of Horus. This simple hierarchy belied the truth of matters when applied in practice within the Sons of Horus.
- There were distinctions between those who had fought with the Legion for longer, between those who had fought in different campaigns, between those who had received certain honours, and between ordinary squads and those who formed a captain's honour guard.

## Legion Warriors

### Legionary Rem — 16th Independent Battalion, Sons of Horus

This pict-record, taken during the mustering of the Censure Host before the invasion of Prospero, depicts Legionary Rem of the 16th Independent Battalion, one of several thousand Sons of Horus assigned to support Leman Russ. At the time, the Legion was undergoing extensive reorganisation, including the adoption of its new sea-green heraldry, leaving many warriors equipped with a mixture of old and new insignia. Horus was also introducing significant doctrinal reforms and encouraging recruitment into the secretive Warrior Lodges, which would later play an important role in the coming Heresy.

Evidence from subsequent personnel transfers and the events at Isstvan suggests that Horus may have used the Prospero campaign to identify and eliminate Legionaries unwilling to accept his reforms or future plans. Although the 16th Independent Battalion participated in relatively few engagements, it suffered an unusually high casualty rate. Rem and several members of his unit were officially recorded as killed in action near Old Tizca, during the final hours of the battle, despite the district having already been cleared of hostile forces.

These suspicious circumstances have led historians to propose two explanations: accidental friendly fire during the reality-distorting Impossible Battle, or a deliberate internal purge conducted by the Sons of Horus. While neither theory has been conclusively established, the unusually high losses among troops who saw little actual combat, combined with Horus' ongoing political reorganisation, suggest that the destruction of Prospero may have served a second purpose: removing potentially disloyal elements from his own Legion before the outbreak of the Horus Heresy.

### Chieftain Adraan — The Butcher of the Icosian Districts

Adraan commanded the 4th Company of the Sons of Horus' 16th Independent Battalion, which was tasked with clearing civilians from the Icosian Districts of Old Tizca during the invasion of Prospero. Officially, these operations were classified as "Enemy Populace Reduction", an Imperial designation traditionally used for the systematic extermination of non-compliant or genetically deviant civilian populations.

Although formally holding the rank of veteran sergeant, Adraan adopted the title "Chieftain" and styled himself the "Butcher of the Icosian Districts" in recognition of his actions. Rumours claimed that he crucified captured Prosperine civilians along major streets, following the brutal traditions of famine purges conducted on his home world of Cthonia.

However, significant discrepancies emerged between the estimated population of the Icosian Districts and the 4th Company's recorded kill counts. While such inconsistencies might ordinarily be attributed to the chaos of battle, additional irregularities appeared in the Sons of Horus' transport manifests. Their dropships showed unexplained increases in cargo weight and supply consumption during the return journey from Prospero, suggesting that some civilians may have been secretly transported off-world rather than executed.

Although this theory remains unproven, the later deployment of substantial numbers of rogue psykers by the Sons of Horus during the Horus Heresy lends it credibility. The possibility that Horus deliberately extracted psychically gifted Prosperines suggests that the massacres in Old Tizca may have concealed a broader operation to acquire valuable human assets for his approaching rebellion.

Ummaos Ludarch was a Sons of Horus squad leader who served for over a decade in Luc Sedirae's 13th Company, participating in the conquest of dozens of worlds. After Sedirae was assassinated during the Dark Compliance of Dagonet, Ludarch distinguished himself in the brutal reprisals that killed or punished thousands of civilians. His ruthlessness earned him promotion to Muster-Driver, commanding several thousand enslaved mortal troops in Horus' service.

He wore modified Mk IV power armour decorated with Cthonian gang traditions and warrior lodge insignia. His gold-plated skull trophies reflected his violent origins, while the whip at his belt symbolised both his authority and his methods of controlling the troops under his command.

## Legion Chronicles

### Unification Wars
Though it would grow under the demands of conquest, the XVI Legion most likely had a strength of no more than a thousand at its birth, and yet despite their numeration are known from a wealth of evidence to have been one of the first active service units of the Legiones Astartes, with many battle honours in the later Wars of Unification.

Many of the XVI Legion's early intake came from the hunter-clans of the Jutigran Bowl and the Samsatian sub-plate slums.

XVI became synonymous with sudden and overwhelming shock assaults. To the XVI fell the swift prosecution of battle and the bloody termination of campaigns; their attacks were pre-emptive as often as they were part of an existing conflict, their forces either the first deadly thrust or preserved to enact a final killing blow. The First Pacification of Luna was perhaps their most famous of these early victories, but the breaking of the Coriolis Enclaves and the Fire Winter left scars in the collective consciousness of Terra that persist even now where in the Capridian Sinks traders and gamblers still refer to the Legion's number of sixteen as 'the counting of the Wolf’.

### Rediscovery of Horus Lupercal [801.M30]
The Cthonians remade the XVI" Legion. They did it without thinking, perhaps even without being conscious of what they did. As the Terrans fell in battle their voices and the more ordered military traditions they had been trained in became fewer and fainter. Examples of this change came slowly. Topknots and mohawks as sported by the Chonian gang head-hunters became common throughout the ranks as armour and personal decoration. Blooding in battle to honour a worthy foe was marked often by a scratch across a helm's eye socket. Perhaps most tellingly, the Chonian word for cutting the throat of an enemy gang-killer in single combat "Aebathan" became a common term in the ranks to describe the completion of a campaign. In battle though the character of the Luna Wolves did not change, if anything it seems to have become stronger over time. The Legion strove to maintain the flexibility to allow them to fight any war or enemy they might encounter on their own terms, but where possible the application of sudden and overwhelming force was their favoured form of attack, and that of their Primarch.

The Luna Wolves' approach to the Crusade was direct and brutal, and their results were often inelegant if unmistakably effective. They would think nothing of unleashing the full power of an orbiting fleet on a fortress city while deploying thousands of Luna Wolves to the surface with orders to kill every living thing within the city's walls. In battle each warrior of the Legion acted seamlessly both as an individual and as part of an integrated squad, a beast with a score of eyes and hands which moved as one.

Other Legions, sometimes hostile to the interference of their brother Legions, would request campaign placement amongst the Luna Wolves and welcome it in return.

### Istvaan III [005–006.M31]
For the attack on the sprawling Choral City itself four primary target zones had been identified from orbit. The final, and perhaps most difficult, target was located in the east of the city, a vast and ancient complex of tomb-spires, shrines and temples known by the indigenous peoples as the Siren Hold. The Sons of Horus assigned to the attack with orders to destroy everything and everyone they found within.

Across the city, with the sundering force of the claw-strike of a great predatory beast, the Sons of Horus drop pod assault descended upon the Siren Hold. scattered and under heavy fire from the outset, the Sons of Horus nevertheless rallied to the attack with their customary discipline and battle-lust, and the Siren Hold quickly became a tortuous, multi-storey killing ground, filled with shrapnel and smoke.

Horus didn’t have a choice but to back Angron's headlong assault and ordered to prepare for ground attack.

One Sons of Horus Storm Eagle, returning to the bay of its mothership, conducted a suicide attack "to avenge the blood of Terra", ramming itself into a line of re-arming gunships, killing full company of Sons of Horus Legionaries in process.

Loyalist part of 16th legion defaced their armour and reclaimed the name of Luna Wolves once more. After two solar months Loken and Luna Wolves still commanded the Precentor’s Palace despite the best efforts of Emperor’s Children and World Eaters to take it by storm.

Horus launched simultaneous attack with Mortarion, sending Abaddon and Justaerin Terminators with heavy transport support on south-west part of the city. They immediately crashed and encircled any pockets of resistance. Orbital conveyors began to land fresh maniples of the Legio Mortis and Legio Audax.

The Luna Wolves lacked nothing for courage in the face of their hateful foe, but they were hopelessly outgunned and outnumbered, and their ammunition low. Some sought to fight free and scatter if they could, for on this day survival itself was a great victory against fearful odds. Others, some injured, cut off or simply too tired and bitter in spite to retreat, stood their ground before the onslaught and brought time for their brothers with their lives… Siren Hold fell, but hundreds managed to escape.

Heavily engaged on the surface of Istvaan III, it is estimated that some 30 000 of the Sons of Horus Legionaries were dead in the aftermath of that battle. The Warmaster was deliberately cautious with the use of his Legion during the Dropsite Massacre. In this it is thought that he hoped perhaps to preserve as much of the Sons of Horus as possible for the intended push on Terra that was planned to follow, or perhaps he simply meant to have the others blood themselves in his cause.  

During the course of the first wave of the attack, the Sons of Horus contingent fought on the defensive, limiting themselves to the core and the most heavily defended bastions, and driving the Imperial Army before them into Loyalists’ guns. There was, however, one notable exception to this; their Reaver and assault companies who deployed to counter the strike units of the Raven Guard in the maze of ravines and rock falls that flanked the walls of the Urgall Depression.

### Raid on Rangda [006.M31]

#### The Pride of Conquerors — the Sons of Horus raid on Rangda, 006.M31

Although conventional histories place the First Legion's first intervention in the Heresy at Thramas, records identify an earlier engagement in 006.M31, shortly after Isstvan III. Rangda was guarded by around 1,500 warriors of the 51st Chapter, the Sable Griffons, and the chantry station of the Order of the Broken Claw. Much of the garrison was away on patrol. The Sons of Horus heavy cruiser Conqueror's Pride arrived using legitimate identification and security ciphers under the pretext of resupply, then attacked after ignoring Knight-praetor Kordray Mordred's demands for contact. The raiding force numbered nearly a full Chapter, including a Company of Justaerin Terminators. The target was the Order's chapter house and the dangerous Rangdan artefacts held in stasis in its vaults, rather than ordinary military installations.

Only twelve members of the Order defended the chapter house, whose concealed defences isolated nearly fifty Justaerin within its corridors. The Cenobites inflicted severe losses on the attackers, and three surviving defenders withdrew into the vaults. Successive Sons of Horus champion groups attempted to overcome them in close combat, with the last remaining Cenobite finally killed by Destroyers using phosphex. The raiders seized selected Rangdan weapons and devices, but the surviving Dark Angels retook the astropathic nexus and summoned their patrol forces. Mordred and his Deathwing lifeguard captured a grounded Stormbird and recovered its cargo of relics; Ravenwing Xiphon Interceptors destroyed another transport. The ancient Promethean cruiser Invidious returned, forcing Conqueror's Pride to flee rather than risk open battle. Despite achieving part of their objective, the Sons of Horus lost several hundred warriors, irreplaceable transports and some of the relics, while inflicting relatively few First Legion casualties. Their attack transformed the Dark Angels' opposition from a matter of loyalty and duty into a demand for retribution. The episode provides a direct example of Order specialists, Deathwing guardians, Ravenwing aviation and the Legion's relic fleet working within the same theatre.

### Scouring of the Cyclops Cluster [006–007.M31]

- The Sons of Horus battlecruiser *Ikon* destroyed Imperial defence monitors at Gethsamaine, struck an orbital station at Taracanis and helped conquer Moab through intimidation and suppression of its orbital defences.
- Horus' wider strategy was to sever communications, weaken isolated territories and seize industry before the planned assault on Terra.

### Manachean War [008.M31]

- First Captain Ezekyle Abaddon led the assault on Hive Ilium from the battle barge *King Eater*. Sons of Horus Reaver, Despoiler, Breacher and Terminator formations participated; after eleven days Abaddon personally led the final assault on the Palace of Light and killed Imperial Commander Pryamus Beket.
- At Port Maw, Horus' *Vengeful Spirit* struck the loyalist flagship *Triumph of Reason*. Horus boarded the vessel, wielded Worldbreaker, and killed Grand Admiral Ospheus LaBray. A former Sons of Horus captain, styled Tyrant of Port Maw, subsequently occupied the captured *Triumph of Reason*, renamed *The Lash*.
- Chieftain K'lathall was the first to break the inner Palace of Light defences. He wore specialised Mk IV Reaver armour with Cthonian details and a Phaeton-pattern heavy-lift jump pack.
- An unidentified Sons of Horus Terminator present at Port Maw wore Tartaros armour decorated with Cthonian gang-script and mirror-coins (his membership in a new elite reserve aboard the *Vengeful Spirit* is considered probable).

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Sons of Horus spearheaded Horus' advance towards Terra, while detached formations hunted survivors of Isstvan V alongside the Emperor's Children. Captain Tybalt Marr eventually recognised that repeated Shattered Legion attacks formed a coordinated strategy intended to gradually exhaust the Warmaster's forces.

Tybalt Marr hunted Shattered Legions forces and led Sons of Horus in engagements including the Ultinian Rings. Captain Raxhal Koraddon, formerly a Reaver chieftain, served in the Isstvan III and V campaigns and subsequently commanded operations against loyalist forces.

Raxhal Koraddon

Raxhal Koraddon was a captain of the Sons of Horus, formerly a Reaver chieftain. He participated in the purge of Loyalists at Isstvan III, the Dropsite Massacre at Isstvan V, and around 20 Dark Compliance campaigns. Favoured by Horus, he was sent as the Warmaster’s emissary to Forge World Xana, where he negotiated its allegiance and the transfer of powerful Ordinatus war machines to the Traitors.

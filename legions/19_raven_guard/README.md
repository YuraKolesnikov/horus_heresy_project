# Raven Guard

## Legion Structure

The XIXth Legion followed a conventional hierarchy of chapters, battalions, companies, and squads, but favoured small, independent company-level operations. Commanders delegated considerable authority to subordinate officers, allowing them to make tactical decisions without awaiting orders from higher command. This decentralised approach originated from the Xeric tribes’ traditions of unconventional warfare against numerically superior enemies. Raven Guard units were tightly coordinated, self-sufficient, and capable of operating independently for extended periods, surviving on local resources and supplies captured from defeated foes.

As the XIXth Legion expanded, it incorporated the full range of Astartes war machines, including heavy tanks, artillery, assault vehicles, and fleet assets. Despite this versatility, the Raven Guard continued to favour rapid strikes, flanking manoeuvres, mobility, and stealth over conventional frontal assaults. Reconnaissance remained central to the Legion’s doctrine. The XIXth fielded unusually large numbers of reconnaissance squads, and every Legionary received training in scouting and infiltration. Consequently, the distinction between Tactical and Reconnaissance squads became increasingly blurred, with both frequently employing similar tactics and specialised equipment.

The Raven Guard employed unusually large numbers of Moritats, solitary warriors whose extreme brutality distinguished them even among the ruthless Xeric veterans. These Legionaries willingly undertook suicidal missions, often serving as assassins and saboteurs with little expectation of survival. Much of the Legion’s covert warfare remained deliberately concealed from Imperial historians. Remembrancers were generally permitted to document victories only after the fighting had ended, preventing them from witnessing the Raven Guard’s actual methods. Consequently, the true extent of the Legion’s assassination and sabotage operations remains unknown.

Following the Council of Nikaea, the Raven Guard formally disbanded its Librarius and prohibited the use of psychic powers, although Corvus Corax’s attendance at the Council remains unrecorded. Most former Librarians were reassigned to conventional combat roles, while some may have joined Seeker squads or served as Vigilators and Moritats. Whether these warriors secretly continued using their psychic abilities during unrecorded operations remains unknown. Most are believed to have subsequently perished during the Dropsite Massacre at the Urgall Depression on Isstvan V.

Corvus Corax formalised the Raven Guard’s combat doctrines around the guerrilla warfare tactics he had developed during the Lycaean Uprising. These principles of stealth, mobility, and precision strikes closely matched the XIXth Legion’s existing methods, allowing for a relatively smooth transition under his leadership. Corax also systematically replaced senior Terran officers with warriors from Deliverance, including former comrades who had fought alongside him against the slaver-lords of Lycaeus and subsequently become Space Marines. Following the devastating Battle of Gate Forty-Two, Deliverance-born Legionaries dominated the Raven Guard’s leadership, with only a handful of Terran veterans retaining senior command positions.

Corvus Corax maintained a relatively decentralised command structure, encouraging honest advice from his officers and rejecting the excessive reverence demanded by many other Primarchs. During the Lycaean Uprising, his followers insisted he accept a personal bodyguard despite his objections. These warriors later joined the Raven Guard as an elite honour guard known as the Shadow Wardens, who protected Corax while remaining largely unseen. The Shadow Wardens were renowned for their constant vigilance, yet Corax himself was the only individual capable of consistently evading their watch, inspiring the saying that he could “slip his own shadow.”

Corvus Corax frequently operated alone or alongside small groups of elite Raven Guard known as the Mor Deythan, or Shadowmasters, whose stealth abilities closely resembled his own. This approach reflected his experience during the Lycaean Uprising, where small, independent cells had proven more effective than conventional forces. Unlike most Primarchs, Corax personally conducted reconnaissance and deep infiltration missions, leaving his officers to command the main army. Often, the larger force served as a diversion, drawing enemy attention while Corax penetrated their defences and delivered a decisive strike against their leadership or most vulnerable positions.

Following the Isstvan III atrocity, Rogal Dorn informed Corvus Corax of Horus’ betrayal, prompting the Raven Guard to abandon their campaign in the Thetos-Groton system and immediately prepare for war. Corax divided his Legion to protect Deliverance from a possible counterattack. Approximately 1,000 Raven Guard, accompanied by the Therion Cohort, returned to defend their homeworld. Their commanders were selected by lottery, as none wished to miss the confrontation with the Traitors. Just under 80,000 Raven Guard departed for Isstvan V, unaware of the devastating betrayal awaiting them.

Several small Raven Guard detachments remained beyond the Imperium’s frontiers, operating independently or alongside Rogue Traders. Among them was a fleet commanded by Shade Lord Arkhas Fal, the Legion’s former leader, whom Corax had sent into exile. Their ultimate fate remains unknown, and Corax apparently made no attempt to recall them. The Raven Guard committed nearly their entire strength to Isstvan V, believing they would fight alongside six loyal brother Legions. Instead, the Dropsite Massacre devastated their ranks, and the subsequent 98-day struggle reduced them to barely 4,000 Legionaries, including those stationed on Deliverance. For the first time, the Raven Guard faced near-extinction, with their future uncertain.

## Legion Stratagems

Named for a Terran avian seen by many cultures as the herald of fate and messenger of death, the Raven Guard have served the Emperor faithfully throughout the glories of the Unification and the Great Crusade. From its earliest days, the warriors of the XIXth Legion were known as cunning and patient hunters, adept at biding their time until the moment to strike was at hand. When the Legion was united with its Primarch – Corvus Corax, the Raven Lord, the Deliverer, theirs was a form of warfare exemplifying speed, stealth and precision which was codified into their doctrine, and under the ivory-skinned, shadow-eyed and sable-haired Primarch, the XIXth carved its name in the annals of the Great Crusade. As the tragic histories of our age recount, however, the Legion's glories were to be scattered upon the black sands of Isstvan V, and the Imperium almost shorn of one of its greatest champions.

Domains: Deliverance, Kiavahr and associated system realm

## Legion Chronicles

### Unification Wars and Early Great Crusade

The XIXth Legion, later known as the Raven Guard, may have been created to serve as the Emperor’s hidden hand, specialising in hunting rebels, suppressing resistance, and eliminating enemies who refused Imperial rule. Unlike the Imperial Fists or Ultramarines, its intended role was covert and punitive warfare. Drawing upon the traditions of its techno-savage Terran recruits, the early Legion excelled at reconnaissance, infiltration, and precision strikes. Its warriors carefully studied enemy positions before launching sudden, devastating assaults from unexpected directions, destroying their targets swiftly rather than engaging in prolonged battles.

During the final years of the Unification Wars, the XIXth Legion specialised in forcing enemies into submission through intimidation and precision assassination. Imperial emissaries offered opposing leaders a simple choice: surrender to the Emperor or face death. Many capitulated once they realised the Raven Guard could eliminate them without warning. Those who resisted faced sudden, devastating attacks from the shadows. The XIXth targeted enemy commanders, shattered chains of command, and exploited confusion to defeat armies far larger than themselves. When resistance continued, they withdrew only to strike again from another direction, denying their opponents any opportunity to recover. Much of the Legion’s early military history remained unrecorded, as its operations were deliberately concealed. Even after reuniting with Corvus Corax, the Raven Guard continued to avoid public recognition, preferring effectiveness over glory.

The earliest Raven Guard recruits came primarily from the Xeric tribes of Terra’s Asiatic Dustfields, fierce but technologically sophisticated warriors who had fought countless wars against rival tribes and the larger Yndonesic Bloc over scarce resources. Following their conquest by the Emperor and his Thunder Warriors, the Xeric tribes were forced to surrender their firstborn sons for transformation into Space Marines. These new Legionaries were then deployed to enforce Imperial authority over their own people. The XIXth Legion inherited the Xeric warriors’ ruthless approach to controlling conquered populations, maintaining constant surveillance and crushing any signs of rebellion before resistance could develop. Although exceptionally effective, these traditions also fostered cruelty towards enemies and contempt for perceived weakness, characteristics that would remain influential within the Legion even after its reunification with Corvus Corax.

The XIXth Legion participated in the conquest of central Asia during the Unification Wars, fighting against the tyrant Kalagann of Ursh. Its tactics established a pattern that would endure until the Legion’s reunification with Corvus Corax: identifying enemy weaknesses, infiltrating small strike forces into strategic positions, and launching sudden, devastating attacks. The Raven Guard eliminated Kalagann’s warlords and herded his defeated armies into an enormous prison camp. However, the campaign exposed them to horrific psyker-wrought weapons, leaving many Legionaries psychologically scarred. Some veterans never recovered from these experiences, eventually seeking death in suicidal assaults, while others became the earliest predecessors of the Legion’s feared Moritat warriors.

Following its victories during the Unification Wars, the XIXth Legion was deployed to the Jovian region to eliminate xenos forces occupying Imperial settlements. During the Scouring of Lysithea, the Legion successfully exterminated the aliens but suffered severe casualties, including the deaths of many human settlers. The campaign left a disturbing legacy. Numerous surviving Raven Guard veterans were recalled by the Emperor’s gene-forges and never returned, suggesting that something extraordinary had occurred. The battle’s true horrors remain unknown. Survivors commemorated Lysithea with a distinctive Jovian rune marked with the numeral X, later preserved on Raven Guard Dreadnoughts. Among them was Shade-Captain Vholtari, whose injuries led to his interment in a Furibundus Dreadnought. He continued serving for decades before finally dying in battle at Gate Forty-Two.

### Great Crusade and Rediscovery of Corvus Corax

The XIXth Legion spent nearly two centuries without its Primarch, during which it developed a close military relationship with Horus and his Luna Wolves. Horus recognised the Legion’s exceptional capabilities and frequently requested its support during the opening campaigns of the Great Crusade. The two Legions developed highly complementary tactics. While the Luna Wolves launched overwhelming frontal assaults, the XIXth infiltrated enemy territory, blocked escape routes, and eliminated retreating forces. Survivors were relentlessly hunted, while captives remained under Raven Guard supervision until they accepted Imperial Compliance. The XIXth became a feared instrument of Imperial conquest, capable of transitioning instantly from patient observation to ruthless violence. Worlds that resisted faced swift, often unseen attacks, while those that peacefully accepted Imperial rule were spared the Legion’s devastating intervention.

Horus came to value the XIXth Legion so highly that he increasingly treated it as an extension of his own Luna Wolves, rather than an independent Legion. While the Luna Wolves expanded through recruitment from Cthonia, the XIXth remained comparatively small and closely attached to its original Terran traditions. The Legion retained its distinctive Xeric tribal heritage, wearing grey armour and sometimes applying Asiatic dust as war paint before battle. These customs earned them nicknames such as the Pale Nomads and the Dust Clad. The XIXth Legion’s gene-seed also produced unusual physical characteristics, including exceptionally pale skin, jet-black hair, and, in some cases, completely black eyes. These traits would become increasingly pronounced following the Legion’s reunification with Corvus Corax, eventually defining the distinctive appearance of the Raven Guard.

When Corvus Corax assumed command of the XIXth Legion, he radically reformed its culture and combat doctrines, combining the stealth and rapid manoeuvres perfected during his rebellion on Lycaeus with the Legion’s existing strengths. He deliberately purged many of its older, ruthless Terran traditions, regarding their methods of oppression as disturbingly similar to those of his former enslavers. Several senior officers were removed from command, including Lord Arkhas Fal, who had led the Legion for three decades before Corax’s arrival. Under their Primarch, the Raven Guard refined their unconventional warfare tactics to extraordinary levels. Corax even defeated Roboute Guilliman three times in strategic simulations, employing unexpected formations and specialised troops, including early Moritat warriors. However, Guilliman adapted to these tactics, and Corax was never able to defeat him again.

During its reorganisation under Corvus Corax, the Raven Guard acquired specialised technologies designed to enhance stealth and rapid deployment. These included the Shadowhawk, a modified Thunderhawk gunship equipped with advanced systems that rendered it nearly invisible to enemy sensors. The Legion also obtained the Whispercutter, a silent, gravitic aircraft capable of deploying ten Space Marines into hostile territory with minimal risk of detection. Unlike the Shadowhawk, the Whispercutter was developed by the master artificers of Kiavahr, whose technological expertise operated outside the mainstream Martian Mechanicum. Their innovations were eventually shared with Raven Guard Techmarines, although this relationship may have risked provoking the jealous authorities of Mars.

The Raven Guard suffered one of their greatest setbacks when Horus, newly appointed Warmaster, ordered them to abandon their specialised operations near the Ghoul Stars and join a conventional military campaign. This decision would foreshadow the catastrophe awaiting the Legion at Isstvan V. The Akum-Sothos Cluster, previously conquered by the Luna Wolves, had fallen into rebellion due to a parasitic xenos infestation. The creatures developed inside their victims’ eye sockets, gradually taking control of their hosts and forming a collective consciousness directed by powerful individuals known as the Unsighted Kings. Determined to reclaim the worlds he had originally conquered, Horus swore to crush the rebellion regardless of the cost, setting the stage for a campaign that would prove disastrous for the Raven Guard.

Horus planned a rapid, overwhelming assault against the Unsighted Kings, bringing together the Luna Wolves, Space Wolves, Iron Warriors, and Raven Guard. His objective was to eliminate the xenos leadership while preserving the Akum-Sothos Cluster’s infrastructure, proving himself worthy of his new position as Warmaster. After securing the outer worlds, Horus ordered the Raven Guard to launch a frontal assault against the heavily fortified Gate Forty-Two. Corvus Corax protested, arguing that such an attack would needlessly sacrifice his warriors. Instead, he proposed using feints to draw defenders away, allowing the other three Legions to breach the fortress more easily. Perturabo accused Corax of cowardice, and the confrontation nearly turned violent before Leman Russ intervened. Russ urged Corax to respect Horus’ authority while channeling his anger into the coming battle. Reluctantly accepting the order, Corax assigned many of his Terran-dominated companies to lead the assault, particularly those whose commanders were most willing to follow the Warmaster’s plan.

The Battle of Gate Forty-Two became the Raven Guard’s darkest hour before the Dropsite Massacre. Their frontal assault suffered devastating casualties under overwhelming defensive fire, forcing Corvus Corax himself to lead the final breakthrough. Gate Forty-Two fell, allowing Horus to execute the Unsighted Kings and free the Akum-Sothos Cluster from xenos control. The victory came at a catastrophic cost. Thousands of Raven Guard, predominantly Terran veterans, were killed, while millions of civilians suffered under the parasites’ influence. The Legion was reduced to approximately 80,000 Space Marines, making it the smallest of the Legiones Astartes. The disaster permanently damaged Corax’s relationship with Horus. Enraged by the needless sacrifice of his warriors, Corax withdrew the Raven Guard from the Warmaster’s command and swore never to fight alongside him again.

The Battle of Gate Forty-Two had an important political consequence for the Raven Guard. Most of the Terran officers who had served under Horus before Corax’s arrival were killed, effectively eliminating the Warmaster’s remaining influence within the Legion. Many of these veterans had also belonged to the secret warrior lodges, whose influence later helped spread rebellion throughout other Legions. Their deaths largely eradicated these organisations from the Raven Guard. Some critics accused Corax of deliberately sacrificing his Terran veterans to consolidate his authority and reshape the Legion according to his own ideals. Whether intentional or not, the outcome helped protect the Raven Guard from the internal corruption that would eventually consume many of their brother Legions.

### Istvaan V
Raven Guard recon ship, Ad Temperesta, was the first to break the real-space at the edge of the system. Scanning and remaining unseen, it journeyed on a wide orbit of Istvaan’s star.  

The Raven Guard infiltrator’s reconnaissance had been thorough, identifying the vast majority of the Traitor’s activity planet-side as being confined to an area called Urgall Depression, with fresh bastions and defence lines raised by the Traitors, using the eroded alien battlements as their foundations.  

To the right flank was a maze of broken rock piles, jutting mesas and sudden deadfalls more than four kilometres wide which several companies of Sons of Horus picked for their own killing ground. Knowing full well their skill in manoeuvre and fluid warfare, Ferrus Manus’ plan had committed the bulk of the Raven Guard, and in this quarter the battle took on a life of its own, becoming a fractured anarchy of hit and run combats. Over the tangle of canyons and killing grounds, Corax and his assault companies swept in on jets of fire, decapitating and dismembering swathes of the enemy.. But the sons of the archtraitor fought on with bitter arrogance..  

After 3 hours of battle, Salamanders and Raven Guard began to fall back to regroup and rearm, having expended their supplies. It is held in some sources that, upon the arrival of four fresh Legions, Corax was the first to call for a withdrawal for those fighting in favour of the newcomers to take the lead. His Legion had suffered severely in the battle and he had no desire to waste further lives in pointless attrition.

### Aftermath of Istvaan V

In the aftermath of the Dropsite Massacre on Isstvan V, the surviving Loyalist Legions reacted differently to their devastating betrayal. The Salamanders continued fighting against overwhelming odds, sacrificing most of their remaining warriors. The Iron Hands, enraged by the death of their Primarch Ferrus Manus, were consumed by hatred and vengeance. The Raven Guard, however, chose a different path. Guided by their surviving Primarch Corvus Corax, they withdrew from open battle into the shadows, prioritising survival over futile resistance. This decision ultimately saved the Raven Guard from destruction.

Recognising the trap closing around the Loyalists at Isstvan V, Corvus Corax ordered the Raven Guard to withdraw after escaping direct combat with the traitor Primarchs Lorgar and Konrad Curze. Drawing upon their expertise in rapid manoeuvres and hit-and-run warfare, the Raven Guard executed a coordinated fighting retreat despite the surrounding chaos. Corax transmitted coded rendezvous points and emergency withdrawal orders, using battle-cant derived from the ancient Lycaean Uprising. Suspecting that the Legion's communications network had been compromised, he relied on these secret codes to prevent the Traitors from intercepting his plans. This precaution saved many Raven Guard Legionaries, allowing them to escape the massacre rather than perish in a hopeless battle. As the Raven Guard fought their way out of the Isstvan V killing zones, Corvus Corax personally intervened to rescue Legionaries surrounded by Word Bearers. Despite losing one of his lightning claws during his confrontation with Konrad Curze, Corax slaughtered numerous Traitors with relentless fury. Using his black-winged jump pack, he repeatedly descended upon enemy formations, devastating their ranks and creating escape routes for isolated Loyalist survivors. Rather than fighting purely for vengeance, Corax used his overwhelming combat prowess to save as many of his sons as possible.

Corvus Corax deliberately channelled his fury into buying time for the Raven Guard to escape the Urgall Depression. Reconnaissance squads identified withdrawal routes and rallying points, while the Legion’s Moritats launched deadly attacks against enemy forces blocking their retreat, often sacrificing themselves to save their brothers. Within an hour of the Traitors’ betrayal, the surviving Raven Guard who could escape were already breaking through enemy rearguards and withdrawing into the surrounding hills. Those unable to reach these routes were effectively doomed. Corax himself remained trapped at the centre of the battlefield, surrounded by thousands of Traitors. Although he had already slaughtered hundreds of enemies and could have killed many more, even a Primarch could not withstand such overwhelming numbers indefinitely. His desperate rearguard action had saved many of his sons, but now threatened to cost him his own life.

With Corvus Corax isolated and surrounded by thousands of Traitors at the centre of the Urgall Depression, a lone Raven Guard Thunderhawk braved intense enemy fire to rescue him. The gunship blasted a landing zone near the Primarch, allowing him to escape just before the enemy overwhelmed his position. As the Thunderhawk ascended, Corax stood at its open assault ramp, furiously cursing the Traitors below. The gunship's engines engulfed the battlefield in flames, leaving behind the bodies of the Loyalists who had fought and died alongside their Primarch.

The Thunderhawk that rescued Corvus Corax was commanded by Strike Captain Alvarex Maun, the Raven Guard’s Master of Descent, who refused to abandon his Primarch. Despite intense enemy fire that had already destroyed dozens of aircraft, Alvarex successfully reached Corax and evacuated him from the battlefield. However, during their escape, the Thunderhawk was struck by overwhelming enemy fire, losing a wing and suffering catastrophic cockpit damage that instantly killed both pilots. With the aircraft plunging towards the ground, Alvarex took control of the crippled gunship and managed to turn a fatal crash into a controlled landing. Although most of the crew were killed and Alvarex himself was severely wounded, his courage and sacrifice saved Corax’s life. The two survivors eventually rejoined the Raven Guard as the sun set on the day of the Dropsite Massacre.

The first night following the Dropsite Massacre on Isstvan V descended into chaos, with surviving accounts describing relentless Traitor attacks, indiscriminate gunship bombardments, and savage close-quarters fighting. Scattered Raven Guard forces were pursued through the hills and ravines, constantly threatened by enemies attacking from behind and above. With ammunition running dangerously low, many Raven Guard Legionaries were forced to fight with combat knives and their bare hands. Despite overwhelming odds, they repeatedly risked their lives to rescue wounded brothers, carrying those unable to walk as they struggled to escape. By dawn, the majority of the surviving Raven Guard had finally broken free of their pursuers, finding temporary refuge in the labyrinthine ravines beyond the Urgall Hills. Although they had survived the night, their safety remained uncertain, and the threat of renewed Traitor attacks was far from over.

As the Raven Guard fought to escape Isstvan V, Loyalist Legionaries left behind were systematically slaughtered by the Traitors. The victors combed the battlefield, executing the dying and torturing wounded survivors, while some Traitor Legions burned their own fallen warriors on funeral pyres. The World Eaters committed particularly horrific atrocities, mutilating the bodies of dead and dying Loyalists, severing their heads, stripping flesh from their skulls, and piling them into enormous monuments of bone. Meanwhile, the Word Bearers performed sinister victory rituals inspired by the teachings of their Primarch Lorgar, foreshadowing their growing devotion to Chaos. The aftermath of the Dropsite Massacre had become not merely a military victory for Horus, but a display of extreme cruelty and ritualistic brutality by the Traitor Legions. The Traitor Legions mutilated tens of thousands of fallen Loyalist Space Marines, forcibly extracting their Progenoid glands, which contained the gene-seed essential for creating new Astartes. The ultimate purpose of this mass harvesting remained unknown, but the theft deprived the Loyalist Legions of vital genetic material while potentially providing the Traitors with resources to replenish or expand their own forces.

As the Raven Guard escaped their pursuers on Isstvan V, the wreckage of Loyalist warships destroyed in the Traitors’ orbital ambush began falling towards the planet. Desperate distress signals filled the communications networks, but the surviving Raven Guard Techmarines and Masters of Signal could do little to help. Soon, enormous explosions illuminated the upper atmosphere as shattered warships broke apart during re-entry. Massive fragments struck the surface with the destructive force of orbital bombardment, creating enormous craters, mushroom clouds, and devastating shockwaves. This was only the beginning. Hundreds of wreckage impacts would continue over the following days, weeks, and months, spreading destruction across Isstvan V and indiscriminately threatening both surviving Loyalists and Traitor forces. With communications disrupted by falling orbital wreckage and visibility severely reduced, the Raven Guard retreated deeper into the Illium Rifts, a vast network of ravines beyond the Urgall Hills. By the second evening, the fate of the Iron Hands and Salamanders remained largely unknown. Corvus Corax, torn between anger and despair, repeatedly ventured into the surrounding hills with selected warriors to search for missing Raven Guard and other Loyalist survivors. Although he successfully rescued several groups, many others had already been hunted down and killed by the Traitors. Throughout the night, more scattered Raven Guard survivors reached the refuge, many severely wounded and psychologically devastated. The betrayal at Isstvan V had inflicted something unprecedented upon the Legion: a crisis of faith and morale. For the first time, warriors who had endured the horrors of the Great Crusade found their resolve pushed to the breaking point—not merely by defeat, but by the betrayal of their own brother Legions.

As the Raven Guard hid within the Illium Rifts, the Traitor Legions began withdrawing from Isstvan V. Massive transport vessels descended to evacuate hundreds of thousands of Legionaries, their support personnel, and even Titans, preparing Horus’ forces for the next stage of the Heresy. Raven Guard reconnaissance squads observed the evacuation from distant volcanic highlands. At Corax’s request, they confirmed that most Traitor Legions were departing but leaving behind smaller contingents, whose purpose remained unknown. The World Eaters were the sole exception. Rather than withdrawing, they continued hunting surviving Loyalists across the hills and wastelands, slaughtering fugitives and collecting skulls. Their Primarch, Angron, apparently refused to leave Isstvan V until every remaining Loyalist had been hunted down and killed.

As World Eaters and other Traitor death squads intensified their hunt for survivors on Isstvan V, Corvus Corax organised a series of diversionary attacks to draw enemy forces away from the Raven Guard’s refuge in the Illium Rifts. Using their expertise in guerrilla warfare, Raven Guard squads lured their pursuers into distant mountains and deserts, occasionally rescuing survivors while witnessing the Traitors’ increasingly horrific atrocities. During one operation, a Raven Guard detachment ambushed and destroyed a Sons of Horus death squad, rescuing three Legionaries from the Raven Guard’s 12th Battalion. However, the survivors had been driven into a state of uncontrollable rage by their ordeal and immediately attacked their rescuers, killing one of their own brothers. The three were eventually subdued, but both sides suffered further injuries. Even while restrained, the rescued Legionaries continued struggling violently and cursing their comrades. The incident revealed the devastating psychological consequences of the massacre, demonstrating that some Raven Guard survivors had been traumatised to the point of turning against their own brothers.

An increasing number of Raven Guard survivors began displaying uncontrollable rage, prompting former Librarians, who had abandoned their psychic duties following the Edict of Nikaea, to investigate. They sensed a steadily intensifying psychic disturbance across Isstvan V, initially attributing it to the immense violence and suffering of the Dropsite Massacre. However, they soon realised that a far more sinister force was at work. Corvus Corax had already recognised the phenomenon, though he kept the full extent of his knowledge secret. The disturbance originated from the World Eaters, whose actions had unleashed a dark psychic influence, revealing yet another disturbing sign of the Traitor Legions' growing corruption.

Across the Urgall Depression and surrounding hills, the World Eaters constructed enormous pyramids from the flensed skulls of fallen warriors, performing savage victory rituals around them. These monuments were eventually identified as the source of a sinister psychic disturbance, radiating unnatural anger and bloodlust that drove nearby survivors, including several rescued Raven Guard, into uncontrollable madness. Raven Guard reconnaissance squads witnessed the rituals firsthand. While some dismissed them as primitive acts of brutality, others recognised signs of something far darker, reminiscent of the forbidden horrors of Old Night. The true nature of the phenomenon would remain unknown for several years, although Corvus Corax appeared to understand more than he revealed. Determined to weaken the psychic influence, Corax ordered selected Raven Guard strike teams to destroy several skull pyramids using melta bombs. The attacks successfully demolished their targets and reduced the surrounding psychic corruption, but failed to eliminate it entirely.

Corvus Corax gradually reorganised the shattered Raven Guard into an effective guerrilla fighting force, determined to survive and resist the Traitors. Small strike teams ambushed isolated enemy patrols, often fighting with knives and improvised weapons to conserve ammunition and scavenge supplies. Their growing success eventually allowed the Legion to replenish its ammunition and launch more ambitious operations. During their reconnaissance missions, Raven Guard scouts discovered a horrifying display of Traitor brutality along the Urgall Hills. Thousands of iron spikes had been driven into the volcanic ground, bearing the mutilated bodies of captured Loyalist Space Marines. Many had been executed, while others remained alive despite their terrible injuries. The discovery revealed the full extent of the Traitors' cruelty and reinforced the Raven Guard's understanding of the fate awaiting anyone who resisted Horus. Further east, Raven Guard scouts discovered an enormous hidden underground complex resembling ancient alien ruins, offering the Legion a far more secure refuge. Corax immediately ordered his forces to relocate there. The timing proved fortunate: just as the last Raven Guard departed their previous sanctuary, a massive fleet belonging to the Traitor Mechanicum began landing among the extinct volcanoes to the west, introducing another dangerous enemy presence on Isstvan V.

The Traitor Mechanicum deployed enormous transport vessels to Isstvan V, unloading vast numbers of tech-helots, servitors, and tech-thralls to begin a massive salvage operation across the Urgall Depression. Their objective was to recover valuable military equipment from the aftermath of the Dropsite Massacre and replenish Horus’ war supplies. The battlefield contained hundreds of wrecked super-heavy tanks, fallen Titans, and the remains of hundreds of thousands of Space Marines, whose advanced power armour, weapons, and equipment represented an immense technological treasure. Even machinery damaged beyond repair could provide valuable raw materials and components. Within hours, Mechanicum salvage teams spread across the battlefield, using plasma cutters to dismantle wreckage and systematically harvest the remains of the Loyalist armies, turning the devastation of Isstvan V into a major logistical advantage for the Warmaster.

The Traitor Mechanicum’s massive salvage operation continued for weeks, systematically stripping the Urgall Depression of valuable military equipment until little remained. Even weeks after the Dropsite Massacre, some Space Marines were discovered still alive among the dead, their enhanced physiology having placed them in suspended animation to survive otherwise fatal injuries. Elements of the Word Bearers and Emperor’s Children, increasingly consumed by dark corruption, took sadistic pleasure in discovering these survivors. Some deliberately revived the unconscious warriors using chemical preparations, only to torture and execute them. The Raven Guard launched guerrilla attacks to disrupt the salvage operations and avenge the fallen. Legion Seeker squads assassinated dozens of Tech-Priest overseers with precision sniper fire, while other warriors planted explosive traps among the wreckage awaiting recovery. Despite these efforts, the Traitor Mechanicum’s operation was simply too vast to stop, and the Raven Guard’s attacks inflicted only minor setbacks.

Around twenty days after the Dropsite Massacre, the Raven Guard’s struggle for survival entered a new phase. The Traitors had become aware that significant Loyalist forces remained on Isstvan V and repeatedly attempted to draw them into open battle. Corvus Corax refused to engage in a confrontation his weakened Legion could not win, instead launching carefully planned strikes to disrupt enemy operations and challenge their control of the Urgall Depression. The World Eaters, led by Angron, remained the largest Traitor force, roaming the wastelands in massive, destructive formations rather than dividing into smaller hunting parties. Other Traitor contingents operated independently, often displaying poor discipline and little cooperation. The Sons of Horus hunted survivors in small squads accompanied by cyber-mastiffs capable of tracking even concealed Raven Guard warriors, while the Word Bearers pursued mysterious rituals and deliberately avoided their supposed allies. The Iron Warriors maintained smaller but heavily mechanised forces, patrolling the wastelands in compact armoured columns. Their three largest formations presented Corax with the opportunity he had been seeking, setting the stage for a carefully planned counterattack against the Traitors.

Corvus Corax launched a campaign against the Iron Warriors’ armoured columns that would continue for several weeks, producing some of the fiercest fighting on Isstvan V. Raven Guard reconnaissance squads secretly tracked enemy movements, gathering intelligence on their strength, composition, and vulnerabilities. The Iron Warriors appeared poorly coordinated, with officers seemingly competing rather than cooperating, allowing Corax to identify opportunities to isolate and ambush individual formations. Each of the three largest Iron Warriors columns contained several dozen armoured vehicles, including Rhinos and Land Raiders carrying infantry, Predator battle tanks, and Thunderstrike heavy artillery tanks. Their greatest advantage, however, was their squadrons of Scimitar jetbikes, which scouted ahead and hunted surviving Loyalists. Despite the Raven Guard’s superior reconnaissance and stealth tactics, these mobile patrols occasionally detected their scouts. Several Raven Guard warriors were killed while gathering vital intelligence, but their sacrifices helped Corax prepare his planned strikes against the Iron Warriors.

Having gathered sufficient intelligence, Corvus Corax personally led a surprise assault against the smallest of three Iron Warriors armoured columns north of the Urgall Depression. Attacking shortly before sunrise, the Raven Guard caught the Traitors completely unprepared. Corax and his assault squads rapidly overran their positions, killing hundreds of Iron Warriors and destroying dozens of vehicles. The engagement lasted less than an hour before Corax ordered a withdrawal, having achieved his objective and anticipating enemy reinforcements. Although the victory was relatively minor compared with the devastation of the Dropsite Massacre, it represented a crucial morale boost for the surviving Raven Guard. They had demonstrated that the Traitors could still be defeated, restoring a measure of pride and determination to the shattered Legion. During their withdrawal, Corax and several Mor Deythan witnessed mysterious mechanical walkers approaching the battlefield, accompanied by unnatural sounds and beams of red light. These were the first recorded sightings of the “blind-hunters,” sinister Traitor Mechanicum automata equipped with formidable weaponry and a dark intelligence. Designed to systematically hunt down the remaining Loyalists on Isstvan V, their appearance revealed yet another terrifying element of Horus’ preparations to exterminate the survivors.

Only days after their previous victory, Corvus Corax ordered another major assault against an Iron Warriors armoured column advancing eastward into the Illium Rifts. Leading his warriors personally, Corax attacked the Traitors within the narrow, dark ravines, where the fighting quickly descended into brutal hand-to-hand combat. Raven Guard jump-pack units executed wide flanking manoeuvres, surrounding the Iron Warriors and cutting off their escape routes. Despite being completely encircled, the Iron Warriors fought with extraordinary determination, refusing to surrender or retreat even as their numbers dwindled. Eventually, fewer than a hundred Traitors remained, concentrated inside a deep crater and protected by twelve Dreadnoughts. The Raven Guard halted their advance, recognising that a direct assault against this heavily armoured defensive formation would result in devastating casualties. With both sides briefly disengaging, Corax stepped forward to confront an ancient Iron Warriors Contemptor Dreadnought, its power claws stained with the blood of fallen Loyalists. The Primarch recognised the veteran entombed within the machine and addressed him by name, suggesting they had once fought alongside each other during the Great Crusade. Following this bitter exchange, Corax and the Dreadnought prepared to fight a personal duel, while the surviving warriors watched the confrontation unfold.

The confrontation between Corvus Corax and the Iron Warriors Contemptor Dreadnought ended almost instantly. Moving faster than the recording systems could track, Corax leapt onto the war machine’s back, tore through its armour with his bare hand, and ripped out the entombed veteran’s braincase and spinal column. The Dreadnought immediately collapsed, and its destruction signalled the Raven Guard’s final assault. Corax personally engaged and destroyed several more Dreadnoughts, while his assault squads attacked the remaining Iron Warriors. The Traitors fought to the death, refusing to surrender or flee, but were eventually annihilated. The battle left over a hundred Iron Warriors dead and all twelve Dreadnoughts destroyed, though dozens of Raven Guard also perished in the fighting. With the enemy force completely eliminated, Corax ordered his Legion to withdraw, leaving the devastated battlefield as a warning to the Traitors. The victory demonstrated that the Raven Guard remained a formidable fighting force despite the catastrophe of Isstvan V—and that Corax intended to continue exacting vengeance upon his former brothers.

The Raven Guard’s hidden sanctuary in the Illium Rifts came under increasing threat as Word Bearers patrols and cyber-mastiff hunting packs pushed deeper into the region, searching for surviving Loyalists. With enemy forces approaching dangerously close, the Legion relocated its wounded, supplies, and command facilities further into the vast underground cavern system. Deeper within the tunnels, the Raven Guard discovered an enormous hall of unmistakably alien construction, built from an unidentified, extraordinarily dense material. Its walls and ceilings appeared to suppress electromagnetic radiation and interfere with psychic activity, potentially shielding the occupants from both technological sensors and psychic detection. The mysterious structure may have been crucial to the Raven Guard’s survival. Despite repeatedly passing within metres of its entrances, Traitor search parties failed to discover the refuge, suggesting that the ancient alien complex effectively concealed the Legion from its pursuers.

The Raven Guard committed every available warrior to a desperate assault against the approaching Iron Warriors, determined to prevent the discovery of their hidden sanctuary. The battle quickly deteriorated into a brutal struggle for survival, with the Iron Warriors relying on their characteristic discipline and relentless defensive tactics. However, the Raven Guard began abandoning their usual precision and tactical restraint, allowing hatred and desperation to dictate their actions. Some accounts suggest that the Legion reverted to the savage methods of its distant past, before Corvus Corax had reshaped its doctrines. For a brief moment, the ruthless, bloodthirsty nature of the original XIX Legion resurfaced, threatening to consume its warriors at the very moment their survival hung in the balance.

At the height of the battle against the final Iron Warriors armoured column, Corvus Corax personally plunged into the enemy ranks, unleashing the full might of a Primarch. Armed with his remaining lightning claw, he tore through the Traitors with extraordinary speed and ferocity, his movements almost impossible to follow. His devastating intervention turned the tide, preventing the Raven Guard from succumbing to their own uncontrolled bloodlust. The third Iron Warriors column was completely annihilated, effectively ending the Legion's ability to conduct major offensive operations against the surviving Loyalists on Isstvan V. Although Iron Warriors garrisons still occupied fortifications around the Urgall Hills, their mobile hunting forces had been destroyed. The victorious Raven Guard returned to their underground sanctuary in grim silence, deeply disturbed by the savage instincts that had resurfaced within their own ranks. Meanwhile, the ominous calls of the Traitor Mechanicum's blind-hunters continued echoing across the wastelands, and blackened rain began falling upon the devastated landscape of Isstvan V.

The black rains sweeping across Isstvan V forced a temporary halt to the fighting, allowing the Raven Guard to regroup, reorganise their depleted forces, and tend to their wounded within their underground sanctuary. During the worst of the storms, Corvus Corax departed alone into the wastelands, relying on his extraordinary stealth abilities to move undetected through enemy territory. Corax’s movements remain largely unknown, although later accounts suggest he penetrated deep into Traitor-controlled territory, possibly even reaching the devastated Urgall Depression. Upon returning, he assembled his senior commanders and declared that the Raven Guard must survive at all costs. His purpose extended beyond preserving the Legion for the Imperium. Corax intended to ensure that the Traitors would never escape justice, believing that as long as even a single Raven Guard warrior remained alive and fighting, Horus’ followers would never be safe from vengeance.

Following the contaminated black rains, a dense, blood-tainted fog spread across hundreds of square kilometres of Isstvan V, possibly carrying Warp corruption unleashed by the Word Bearers’ dark rituals. The toxic atmosphere, combined with weeks of relentless fighting, exhaustion, and psychological trauma, pushed many Raven Guard towards the limits of their endurance. Once-disciplined warriors became increasingly consumed by rage and despair, their battered armour patched together from equipment scavenged from fallen Legionaries of both sides. Around forty days after the Dropsite Massacre, a large Word Bearers force led by the war-leader Elexis entered the ravines north of the Raven Guard’s sanctuary. Elexis had correctly deduced that the Loyalists his warriors had previously encountered belonged to a much larger surviving force and intended to destroy them. Crucially, Elexis concealed his discovery from the other Traitor Legions, particularly the World Eaters, whose Primarch Angron was still searching the wastelands for his surviving brothers. Rather than coordinating an overwhelming assault, Elexis chose to pursue the Raven Guard independently, preparing a covert infiltration that threatened to expose their hidden refuge.

The Raven Guard deliberately allowed Elexis’ Word Bearers to advance deep into the Illium Rifts, using concealed positions and feigned retreats to lure them into a carefully prepared trap. Once the enemy was sufficiently dispersed, the Loyalists launched a coordinated ambush, simultaneously attacking dozens of isolated units and preventing them from supporting one another. Hundreds of Word Bearers were killed, while several were captured. However, all but one of the prisoners committed suicide before they could be restrained. The sole surviving Word Bearer was brought before Corvus Corax in an ancient crater beneath unnatural lightning and renewed black rainfall. The Primarch revealed his full, terrifying presence, abandoning the supernatural concealment he normally maintained. His overwhelming aura of authority and power left even his own warriors awestruck, while the captive Traitor was forced to prostrate himself before him. Despite being physically and psychologically overwhelmed by Corax’s sheer Primarch presence, the Word Bearer remained consumed by hatred, demonstrating the fanatical devotion and corruption that would increasingly define the Traitor Legions.

Following the interrogation of the captured Word Bearer, Corvus Corax learned that Angron was the only Traitor Primarch still present on Isstvan V, with the World Eaters remaining at full Legion strength. More importantly, Angron knew Corax had survived and was preparing a massive offensive to scour the entire Illium Rifts and force his brother into battle. The Word Bearers commander Elexis had attempted to find and eliminate the Raven Guard first, seeking glory for himself. Corax immediately ordered another evacuation of the Raven Guard’s underground sanctuary, despite objections from warriors who preferred to make a final stand. His response was absolute: the Legion would fight and die only on his command, and he alone would determine when and where its last battle would take place. The withdrawal came just in time. Traitor Mechanicum blind-hunters and a massive force of World Eaters discovered the abandoned refuge shortly after the Raven Guard departed. Corax personally observed the enemy’s arrival alongside his rearguard, watching as the frustrated World Eaters devastated the surrounding area with explosions, enraged that their prey had escaped once again.

Following the evacuation of their sanctuary, the Raven Guard entered an increasingly desperate struggle for survival. More than eighty days of continuous warfare had claimed hundreds of additional lives, leaving the Legion weaker than immediately after the Dropsite Massacre. Many severely wounded warriors took their own lives rather than burden their brothers during the retreat. Morale deteriorated dramatically, with surviving Legionaries becoming withdrawn and increasingly resigned to death. Many continued fighting not because they expected victory, but because surrender meant certain destruction. The situation worsened as the World Eaters and Traitor Mechanicum’s blind-hunters relentlessly pursued the Raven Guard through the Illium Rifts. Without a secure refuge, the Legion could no longer properly regroup, conduct reconnaissance, or prepare coordinated counterattacks. Despite their expertise in guerrilla warfare, they had lost the initiative entirely. The Traitors gradually forced the Raven Guard eastward towards the Gular Salt Plains, a vast, uncharted wasteland so hostile that even Space Marines could not survive there indefinitely. With their numbers dwindling, supplies exhausted, and enemies closing in, Corax’s Legion faced the growing possibility of complete annihilation.

The Traitor Mechanicum began systematically incinerating vast regions of Isstvan V after stripping them of valuable salvage, unleashing enormous firestorms that spread eastward from the Urgall Depression into the Illium Rifts. Meanwhile, the Raven Guard continued their desperate retreat, relentlessly pursued by Angron and his World Eaters, who outnumbered them by at least ten to one. The World Eaters deployed Whirlwind and Scorpius artillery tanks to bombard the Raven Guard’s flanks, gradually narrowing their escape routes and forcing them towards the deadly Gular Salt Plains. With no hope of reinforcements, resupply, or finding additional survivors, the Legion faced almost certain annihilation. Despite the worsening situation, Corvus Corax refused to order a final stand, keeping his intentions secret even from his officers. On the ninety-eighth day after the Dropsite Massacre, only 3,000 Raven Guard Legionaries remained. They reached the edge of the toxic Gular Salt Plains, trapped between an inhospitable wasteland ahead and tens of thousands of charging World Eaters behind them. The Legion stood on the brink of destruction, with seemingly no avenue of escape.

On the ninety-eighth day after the Dropsite Massacre, Corvus Corax finally ordered the remaining 3,000 Raven Guard to make their last stand at the edge of the Gular Salt Plains. Surrounded by overwhelming World Eaters forces and with no possibility of further retreat, the Legion prepared to fight to the death rather than surrender or betray its oaths. Their determination reflected the legacy of the Lycaean Uprising, where resistance against impossible odds had forged the Raven Guard’s identity. Angron personally led the World Eaters’ final charge, and the first enemy shots struck the Raven Guard as Corax issued what appeared to be his last orders. However, just as the Traitors closed in, a fleet of black-winged aircraft suddenly descended from the skies, unleashing devastating firepower against the exposed World Eaters. The unexpected aerial assault shattered Angron’s attack and saved the Raven Guard from certain annihilation. After ninety-eight days of relentless warfare, the surviving Legionaries were finally rescued and evacuated from Isstvan V, escaping the world Horus had intended to become their tomb.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Raven Guard began rebuilding under Corax, who introduced accelerated recruitment into his experimental Raptor companies, although many recruits proved unstable. While the main Legion attacked worlds that had joined Horus, several dozen smaller groups apparently continued independent raiding campaigns.

Corvus Corax pursued experimental accelerated recruitment to rebuild the devastated XIX Legion, forming Raptor companies. Many of the recruits were unstable, and other Raven Guard detachments operated independently throughout the ensuing civil war.

### Ashen Claws in the Nostramo Sector [017.M31]

The documents investigate a mystery from the Scouring: the disappearance of roughly 30,000 Night Lords from the Nostramo Sector. In 017.M31, Loyalist forces arrived expecting a major confrontation but discovered abandoned fortresses, destroyed warships, and massacred VIII Legion garrisons. At the fortress-moon Tenebor, Blood Angels investigators found hundreds of dead Night Lords, apparently killed by unidentified Astartes wearing black-and-red armour and using ancient Legion weaponry. Similar mysterious heraldry was later discovered among Astartes remains on the ruined hive world of Cairn.

The strongest explanation points towards the Ashen Claws, formerly the 18th Chapter of the Raven Guard. Led by Nerat Kirine, these predominantly Terran-born veterans had been exiled by Corvus Corax following the Battle of Gate Forty-Two because of their association with the old Legion's brutal traditions and loyalty to Horus. Sent into the distant Ghoul Stars, they disappeared from Imperial records and eventually developed into an independent force, owing allegiance to neither Horus nor the Emperor.

Their involvement is particularly evident during the Battle of Desperation, where mysterious Astartes warships attacked the Night Lords while their black-and-red armoured warriors simultaneously ambushed Ultramarines and looted military installations. The attackers then withdrew without identifying themselves. The documents strongly suggest that the Ashen Claws were responsible for devastating the Night Lords' Nostramo holdings, although the full extent of their campaign remains unconfirmed.

Essentially, the mystery is that a forgotten Raven Guard offshoot may have independently destroyed much of the Night Lords' remaining empire, while fighting Loyalists whenever it suited its own interests.

The collapse of the Nostramo Sector was partly caused by the Ashen Claws, a renegade Raven Guard fleet that had abandoned Imperial service in favour of raiding and acquiring resources. Originally dispatched in 002.M31 under Praetor Calvus, a veteran loyal to Corax, the expedition comprised approximately 4,000 Astartes, 13 capital warships, and 20 smaller vessels. By its return in 011.M31, Calvus had likely died, and nine years of isolation had apparently transformed the force into an independent, mercenary warband.

However, the Ashen Claws were not solely responsible for Nostramo's destruction. Increasing xenos incursions from the Ghoul Stars and bitter infighting among the leaderless Night Lords had already weakened the sector. The Ashen Claws exploited this instability, devastating Night Lords strongholds and plundering supplies. Following the sector's collapse, the High Lords of Terra declared Nostramo and neighbouring regions forbidden to most Imperial vessels. The ultimate fate of the Ashen Claws remains unknown, and they may still survive somewhere beyond Imperial control.

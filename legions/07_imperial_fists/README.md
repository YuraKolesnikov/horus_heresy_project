# Imperial Fists

## Legion Structure

At the time of Horus’ betrayal, much of the early Legion structure remained in the Imperial Fists. Ever a practical leader, Rogal Dorn most likely saw little reason to change what had been proven to work. As the Great Crusade progressed, the Legion’s particular role and developing preferences for waging war began to change this.

At the squad level, the Imperial Fists showed a noted schism in their dominant unit types as time passed. Firstly of note was the number of assault-configured units, which was very high, as were auxiliary units armed with specialised weaponry suited to the destruction of armour and fortifications. The second notable dominance was that of heavy weaponry, which was selected not only for raw power but also for its accuracy and adaptability in the field, with, for example, the multi-purpose missile launcher preferred over arguably more indiscriminate autocannon and the precision and range of the lascannon over the shorter ranged multi-melta. The Imperial Fists were also amongst the strongest proponents of the development of Tactical Dreadnought Armour, and fielded a large number of squads equipped in all patterns of the so-called Terminator armour. Notably, they also were amongst the first to field significant numbers of the Indomitus pattern produced by forges of Deimos. At the time of the Great Betrayal, they alone fielded Terminators armed with prototypes of the Absinia and Iliastus pattern assault cannon, retrofitted and rescaled from the Kheres pattern which was proved such a powerful addition to the Contemptor pattern Dreadnought chassis.

Above the squad was the company, in this case a formation which could vary in size from a few squads to several hundred Imperial Fists. The strength of companies often waxed and waned as they took casualties, or were bolstered by fresh recruits. No matter their size, each company was grouped into a battalion, and two battalions when paired were often referred to as a regiment, but latterly also called a ‘Crusade’ or ‘Household’. Larger formations comprised of multiple companies were formed as needed for a particular task or deployment, rather than as standing units.

Raised by Rogal Dorn during the first decade of his command, the Templars were the warriors charged with guarding the Temple of Oaths aboard the Phalanx. There, beneath the tattered and burned banners of defeated enemies and the statues of dead heroes, the Imperial Fists returned to renew their perpetual oaths of loyalty to their Emperor and Primarch. Nominally the First Company of the Imperial Fists, the Templars’ strength rarely fell below 1000 warriors, and their members were drawn from veterans across the Legion. Skill at arms was not enough to earn such a responsibility; each Templar was a warrior in whom the crusader light had come to burn with undimming brightness, and zealous, remorseless and tempered by decades of war, they were exemplars of what it was to be an Imperial Fist of the Great Crusade.

Legion Command Structure

Complete and total obedience was demanded by Dorn and given by each Imperial Fist from the lowest warrior to the most exalted captain. Beneath Dorn were the senior captains of the Legion’s regiments, Crusades and Households. Unlike other Primarchs, Dorn maintained no fixed circle of advisors.

Beneath the senior captains were line captains and centurions, who led the Legion’s battalions and companies. A second layer of authority existed above them - fleet masters, siege masters, appointed theatre commanders and marshals and ultimately the First Captain. The First Captain of the Imperial Fists was also the commander of the Templars, and by tradition the finest proven warrior within the Legion. As the guardian of the oaths of every Imperial Fists warrior, the First Captain held not only great authority, but commanded respect from all of his brothers.

A master of the fleet held complete authority over it for a fixed time or until a campaign was complete. Selected for their skill in void warfare, the fleet masters represented many of the Legion’s finest strategists. Likewise, siege masters were charged with arraying and overseeing the forces engaged in a siege. It is a mark of importance Rogal Dorn placed on these two methods of warfare that those who commanded in such theatres were raised to positions that could only be overruled by the Primarch himself.

Additional titles existed for those who commanded permanent fortresses built by the Legion. The titles of castellan and Legion seneschal were subtly different in both the honour and duties they bestowed, but both were of sufficient weight that their bearers retained the title even after they were returned to the rest of the Great Crusade.

At the time of the Istvaan III atrocity, the bulk of the Imperial Fists were returning to Terra with Rogal Dorn. Though the Emperor had called all of the Imperial Fists to Terra, in practice such a total withdrawal from the Great Crusade would have been impossible. Some forces stayed on the edge of the Great Crusade to complete campaigns or individual duties. Other forces remained scattered across the Legion’s fortresses and bastions.

The strength of the Imperial Fists was never in its numbers. Their tally of warriors during the Great Crusade rarely rose above 100 000. Aside from the number of its Space Marines, no review of the strength of the Legion can be complete without noting the strength of their fleet. The Imperial Fists had over 1500 warships under their direct command, and many more bonded by oath and fealty. This naval might was the greatest of any of the Legiones Astartes, and was further enhanced by the fact that many of the ships were the largest in the Imperium. Even the Sons of Horus and the Ultramarines could not rival such strength alone.

## Legion Stratagems

Loyalty in a time of betrayal is not easy to measure. One cannot look at the face of an ally and know if he is a friend or an enemy. Even after Horus’ treachery screamed from the death plans of Istvaan III and Istvaan V, the truth of who was a traitor and who remained loyal was far from clear. Faces of old friends might be the masks of treachery and truths of the past night be cloaks for assassins. If Horus could fall then so could any other. In this age of betrayal, one Legion stood as a pillar of loyalty amongst the seas of doubt. The Imperial Fists and their Primarch Rogal Dorn, ever true, never swerving from the toll demanded by a war of brothers, bore the weight of loyalty without breaking, but not without cost. Sacrifice without limit changes those who must survive. Only now, with so much lost and so much more remade, can we see that the Imperial Fists who played their part in the Imperium’s foundations, died with the Great Crusade.

Observed Strategic Tendencies: Ship-borne Assaults and Boarding Actions, Defensive and Fortification Operation In Extremis, Stronghold Assaults, the Conquest and Forced Compliance of Void-faring Civilisations.

Domains: Inwit, Terra and the Solar Domains

### Guardians of the Imperial Truth

#### The Imperial Fists — Guardians of the Imperial Truth

Beyond their renowned role as defenders of the Imperium, the Imperial Fists may have been deliberately employed by the Emperor to combat Daemonology and the influence of the Warp. The VII Legion's genetically ingrained loyalty, discipline, and unwavering devotion to duty made its warriors particularly resistant to corruption. Unlike Magnus, whose pursuit of forbidden knowledge frequently exceeded the limits imposed upon him, Rogal Dorn possessed the restraint and caution necessary to study dangerous subjects without becoming consumed by them. The Emperor kept Dorn close throughout much of the Great Crusade, and some accounts suggest that He gradually entrusted the Primarch with secrets concerning the true nature of the Warp, the purpose of the Imperial Truth, and humanity's hidden enemies.

The Imperial Fists consequently became zealous defenders of the Imperial Truth, developing specialised methods for confronting sorcerers and Warp-corrupted adversaries. They pioneered the use of psyarkana devices, technologies designed to counter psychic phenomena and supernatural influences. Dorn also assembled an enormous collection of forbidden artefacts and esoteric knowledge within the vaults of the Phalanx, concealing these resources even from his brother Primarchs. Although he remained reluctant to employ such dangerous instruments, this secret arsenal proved invaluable during the Siege of Terra.

## Legion Chronicles

### Unification Wars

The first battalions of the VIIᵗʰ Legion were raised from across Terra. While many other Legions drew their recruits from a particular source, the flesh of the VIIᵗʰ Legion came from Terra as a whole. Even in domains where other Legions had ‘Rights of Tithe’, the VIIᵗʰ took some of the youth as initiates. Often these would be those who exhibited the greatest capacity for endurance, both in mind and body. Many were of a taciturn nature, slow to talk but quick to act. Why so many of such a wide pool of recruits should be similar is unclear. Certainly, the processes used to activate the VIIᵗʰ Legion’s gene-seed seem to have inflicted intense pain, and so perhaps it was a purposeful selection of stock suited to surviving such a process. It is also possible that a pattern of recruitment once formed, perpetuated until it was tradition. No matter the reason, the grim nature of those recruited into the VIIᵗʰ Legion was well suited to their use.

In war the VIIth Legion was concerned with conquest. While all of the Emperor’s forces fought and died to expand the Imperium, many saw only part of the greater vision. Defeat the enemy, tear down his strongholds, break his beliefs and still you would have a land that could turn against the Imperium in the future, or provide other enemies with a weakness to strike at. Victory was not enough, to conquer one had not only to defeat one’s enemies, but to hold the fruits of that victory. This philosophy underpinned every action of the VIIth. In attack they would pay any price in their own blood to secure victory, and once they had victory they would begin to consolidate what they had taken.

This pattern can be seen time and again in the later conquest of Ancient Terra. It was the VIIth Legion which broke the Cities of the Crystal Sea, and then raised the Fortress of the Fifth Circle from their ruins. In the ice-wrapped pinnacles of Himalazia they lost three battalions to secure the defeat of the witchery of the Wind Caller clans, but the first Imperial bastions began to rise against the cold sky within days of that victory. Across Terra the fruits of their fortress-craft gazed down on those who dwelt in the land around, a constant sign that the strength which had conquered them remained, rooted into the earth. In the first decade of their existence, the Imperial Fists raised six hundred citadels upon the lands of the conquered. It is said that the dead of the Legion lie still in the foundations of each, their skulls and blood mingling with the stone and mortar of their walls. With these bastions pinning the conquered land to the Imperium, order would spread amongst the people of these new domains. The old ways would change, fall or be replaced by the new, and if they did not then the looming fortresses would answer the question of what the response to rebellion would be.

The VIIth Legion were more than builders and castellans. At their root they were the most direct expression of the Emperor’s design of uniting humanity; they were crusaders. Fortresses solidified conquest, and the VIIth sought conquest with a focused hunger. While fortresses and ordered domains sprang up in their wake, the VIIth Legion would never linger, but were always moving on, invading uncompliant domains and pushing the frontiers even as they reinforced what they had just taken. Massed shock assaults, using the full array of weaponry within the Legion, typified the VIIth’s approach to war. Multiple battalions often took to the field enmasse, breaking enemies with hammer blow force. On the plains of Kenneatar, the 5th Battalion of the VIIth broke the lines of the Tyrancy with an arrowhead of fifty war machines. It is said that the dust cloud thrown up in their wake blotted out the sun. In the tunnels of Galabaz, they cracked the crust above the buried city and dropped into the exposed tunnels beneath while the explosions were still echoing across the mountains. But always, in the wake of the destruction they wrought, they replaced what they had broken with something stronger.

It was from these early conquests that the VIIth Legion acquired its name. When many looked on the lands taken by the VIIth, they said that it was as if “the hand of the Emperor had descended and gripped with an unbreakable fist”. The description of the service done by the VIIth Legion must have pleased the Emperor for he personally decreed that they would be known from then on as the Imperial Fists and bestowed on them the right to bear the Laurels of Victory as part of their heraldry. Dutiful and taciturn as ever, it is said that the renamed Imperial Fists accepted their honours in humble silence.

### The Unheard War

The battle honours of the Imperial Fists were among the most distinguished of the Great Crusade, yet many of their victories were purchased through extraordinary sacrifice. One of the earliest examples occurred at the Azurite Stations of Uranus, before the Crusade extended beyond the Sol System. These interconnected void citadels, named for the blue corrosion on their interior hulls, housed a civilisation of artisans and scavengers that had overthrown its tyrants centuries earlier. The Azurites accepted Imperial authority peacefully, supplying the expanding fleet with experienced crews, void suits, augury scanners and shard weaponry. Their new allegiance offered little immediate protection from the Solar Pirates, a loose confederation of raiders and petty despots among whom the mutations, witchcraft and other horrors of Old Night persisted. While the Emperor’s armies were occupied around Neptune’s moons, the pirates attacked the stations for plunder.

Answering the Azurites’ appeal, a squadron of heavily armed Imperial system craft delivered fifty Imperial Fists to the besieged station. Within twenty minutes the Legionaries had repelled the initial boarders; within an hour they had reorganised the surviving garrison and defences sufficiently to defeat three further assaults. The retreating pirates left behind a final horror: a damaged boarding torpedo carried a mutilated old man rather than a conventional boarding party. Before dying, he uttered the word “Silence”, despite having no tongue. An hour later, the Azurite militia began to succumb to the Screaming, an ancient psychic plague believed eradicated during the Emperor’s rise. It had previously devastated civilisations and turned the lower Hzentian Basin to glass as it consumed the minds of lost Edioth. Its victims’ screams propagated the contagion through the Warp, and its effects reached even the adepts of the Silent Mountain on Terra.

The Imperial Fists alone remained resolute against the outbreak. Armed with boarding shields and volkite weapons, they confronted the infected inhabitants, but recognised that the tens of millions living throughout the stations made containment impossible through conventional battle. Even a single escaped victim or transmitted cry might spread the plague beyond Uranus. Ordering the supporting fleet to withdraw, the fifty warriors advanced in silence towards the station’s generators. Only a handful survived long enough to overload the reactors and transmit a final explanation of their actions before the complex was destroyed. Imperial forces subsequently hunted the Solar Pirates to extinction within a month. The Screaming never returned to afflict humanity, and tradition holds that the Emperor ordered the newly raised Bell of Lost Souls to toll for the first time in recognition of the fifty Imperial Fists.

### Rediscovery of Rogal Dorn

While the character of a Legion can be seen in the flesh and blood of its recruits, it can be seen with even greater clarity in its Primarch. Though spun from the seed of humanity the Primarchs are not human, they are transcendent, holding a portion of the sublime and unknowable in their nature. All the qualities which seem strong in a warrior of a Legion exist more strongly, more deeply and with greater subtlety in a Primarch. This nature often seems to enhance and focus the qualities gifted to a Legion by their gene-seed. So it is that at the moment at which Primarch and Legion unite, there is often a point at which a Legion’s character may seem to shift. In the case of the Imperial Fists, the discovery of their Primarch, and the planet which had raised him, only strengthened the character the Imperial Fists had shown since their creation.

Inwit was, and is, a world of death and cold. Its star is old and withered, bleeding the last of its heat as cold, red light. Tidally locked against its dying star, perpetual darkness soaks one side of the planet, faded sunlight the other. Crevasse mazes, frozen mountain ranges and plains of frost dunes cover the planet’s dark side—this is the Splintered Land, the beast-stalked wilderness which shapes the bodies and beliefs of the human population that clings to life here. Under the ice crust, thick seas flow in sluggish tides and pale and sightless creatures swim the waters, hunting by vibration and a preternatural taste for blood. Far above this desolation, great and ancient space stations and shipyards look down on the cold-shrouded world through perpetual auroras – created in a lost past, these citadels of the void have looked down on Inwit since before any records or tales can recall. Whilst on the planet, the light side of Inwit offers little more comfort than the dark, being a land of drift-crusted saline seas and sparse bare rock under the unblinking gaze of the red sun. There is little of value on Inwit: its seas are buried or lifeless, its mountains bare of riches and its native species vicious. There is, however, one thing that this harsh world produces that led it to conquer a star cluster and endure as an island empire of order in the Age of Strife: its people. Though they are barbaric, they are far from unsophisticated.

The warriors of Inwit are raised to endure and survive. The world that bears them teaches them to never relent and that the price of weakness is death, for them and the rest of their kin. Death comes in many forms on Inwit: in the ice storms that can freeze and cover a man in seconds, at the claws of the predators that roam the Splintered Lands, and in the lapse in concentration that allows the cold to penetrate the warmth-seals of a hold. These factors make a certain kind of people: strong, grim and dedicated to the survival of the whole rather than the individual. Much of the population is nomadic, moving between the subterranean ice hives to trade in weapons, fuel and technology. Conflict between the roaming clans is common and young warriors learn how to defend against their clan’s enemies as early as they learn how to endure the death touch of Inwit’s merciless chill. They know how to learn, have an innate sense of an object’s functional value and, most importantly, they have the strength to conquer those who possess knowledge they do not.

Long ago, before the coming of the Emperor was even a dream on night-shrouded Terra, the people of Inwit began to create their own realm in the stars. On every world they took, they assimilated, realigned and reinforced. With each conquest their culture and learning grew, but Inwit itself remained unchanged even as it became the centre of an empire. The ice hives and clan disputes remained and while their world birthed star ships and ringed its orbits with weapon stations, its rulers kept to the old ways, the ways that had created their strength, the warlords and matriarchs who commanded armies amongst the stars still living lives little easier than their vassals. So it was, and so it is now. It was as part of this burgeoning empire that Rogal Dorn grew to manhood, and then to rule its domains as emperor. Much of his early years remains unknown, or at least little talked about. What is known is that from the cold and darkness of Inwit the boy, named Rogal by his adopted kin, rose to lead the House of Dorn of the Ice Caste and then to the rule of the Inwit Cluster. His qualities married perfectly with those of Inwit, and he pushed their empire further than any other, ordered and trained its armies, and fashioned space craft the like of which had not been seen before. When the true Emperor was reunited with Rogal Dorn, he regained not only a lost son, but the strength of a star spanning society already forged into a tool of war.

Dorn was possessed of a single-minded energy tempered by a reserved and stoic nature. Many have remarked on the dour and emotionless disposition of both Primarch and Legion, but such an assessment misses much. Reserved, but terrifying in anger, Dorn was both cautious and calculating, and capable of pursuing an end with relentless energy. While he would rarely show emotion, when he did it was capable of shaking the ground or darkening the sun. During the near disastrous resurgence of the Xahelican breed in the Adonis Cluster, Dorn’s cold rage is said to have held the battlements as much as the arms of those standing upon them. His admonishment of the reinforcements at Castoris is said to have echoed from the fire-touched sky to the still burning sea. “As swift and unforgiving as the falling edge of an axe,” is how Leman Russ is said to have described his brother.

Dorn was also capable of brooding and letting matters eat at him beneath his stone-cast demeanour. For as much as he was a warrior of absolute loyalty, he was also an idealist – the reasons why he fought were as important to him as the outcome of his efforts. During the time of the Great Crusade few ever saw this quality in Dorn; there was little cause, though we who knew him well can perhaps see hints of it in his near fatal confrontation with Konrad Curze in the Cheraut System and his brief schism with Ferrus Manus after Hannizare. It is only now, with so much lost never to be rebuilt, and blood still staining the birth of the Imperium which survives, that we can see that perhaps even in perfect loyalty there can be a flaw. At the moment that the Imperial Fists were united with Rogal Dorn, however, the shadows of Horus’ treachery still waited far in the future.

Few integrations of Primarch and Legion were as swift or as complete as that between Rogal Dorn and the Imperial Fists. The ideals of the Imperium, and the purpose of the Great Crusade fitted with Dorn’s outlook and drive, and the warriors of the Imperial Fists were exemplars not only of everything that he had built in the Inwit Cluster, but everything he had dreamed of for its future. From the first moment Dorn met his gene-sons, he demanded of them everything that he would ask of himself. It is said that when he met Legion Master Mathias and veteran contingents of the Imperial Fists he said nothing, maintaining his silence even after they had knelt and pledged him fealty. Only when he had observed them in battle did he break his silence and speak to them directly. He said that they had much to do, and more to learn. To Mathias he gave a single word of thanks for his service, and named him High Castellan of the Inwit Cluster. Such an honour was also a deep duty, for the next command he gave was to raise thirty regiments of new Imperial Fists from the Inwit systems. Without waiting or looking back, Rogal Dorn and his sons plunged back into the stars.

### Great Crusade

Over the next sixteen decades, the Imperial Fists fought on the burning edge of the Great Crusade. Relentlessly they pushed from war zone to war zone, were honoured by each of their brother Legions, and rose high in the estimation of many. In their methods of war, the ways of Inwit and the echoes of the VIIth Legion’s victories combined. They drove ever on, without pause or respite. Just as on Terra they fortified and built to secure what they conquered, but just as before they did not linger to rule their conquests. While a castellan with a household of warriors might remain to maintain its defences, they did not administer, or draw up and enforce laws; they were warriors of the Imperium, not its masters, and they existed to serve in war and die for its survival.

What they did take from all the lands they conquered were recruits. The Great Crusade lived on the blood and flesh of its warriors. The further it reached and the wider its spread, the more fell in battle and the more battles there were to fight. All Legions recruit from across the galaxy, by necessity as much as for any other reason, but the Imperial Fists did so with a hungry zeal rivalled only perhaps by Lord Guilliman’s Ultramarines or Lorgar’s Word Bearers. While other Legions might draw heavily from a single world, the Imperial Fists drew from many.

Even as the embers were cooling, the Imperial Fists would be sifting a conquered population for suitable material to fill its ranks. Those who had to live by the force of their will, those who had been tempered by hardship and suffering; these above all were prized by the Imperial Fists. From the hunter tribe on the fire blighted world of Tiberina, they took every male child who could carry a spear. On Sevan they cleared the warrens beneath the factory-plates of tech-brat gangs and took half their number into the void. From the Nedoran ship clans they took every youth who had seen more than 10,000 ship cycles. Many of these potential recruits would die before they ever became Space Marines, winnowed away for weakness or some other unacceptable flaw. Those who survived often formed complete companies, battalions or even regiments depending on the size of the intake. Often these formations retained the stamp of their birthworld on their character and culture. Mannerisms of speech, physical marks of allegiance and even idiosyncrasies of warfare, all might be touched by a formation’s common ancestry. Over time such sub-cultures often broke down under the attrition and necessities of war, but some endured for decades.

As the Great Crusade progressed, the Imperial Fists rose high in honour and in the favour of the Emperor. Ever dependable, they were often used to reinforce flagging campaigns, to hold crumbling fronts and break deadlocked sieges. The Emperor also frequently called on both Dorn and his sons to fight beside him, bestowing this honour on the Imperial Fists more than on any other Legion. When the Imperial Host descended on Ophelia VII, the Emperor led the assault at the head of 100 Custodians and 10,000 Imperial Fists. Again at Askanisa, the Emperor called on not only Horus and the Luna Wolves, but Dorn and the Imperial Fists to form his vanguard in breaking the Shrouded Dynasties. The Emperor also used Dorn to ensure war and Compliance was achieved according to his wishes and vision. Time and again, in ways large and small, the Imperial Fists acted at the direct order of the Emperor. Other Primarchs and Legions blazed a more brilliant trail or spread the Imperial Truth to more worlds, but to many the Imperial Fists were the rock on which the foundations of the Imperium were built.

High in favour and honour though they were, the Imperial Fists’ status did not sit well with some of their brother-Legions. The animosity between the Imperial Fists and Iron Warriors was well known, but Perturabo and his Legion were not alone in their resentment. Though few reached the Lord of Iron’s pinnacle of spite, others did chafe at the trust placed on the Imperial Fists. Dorn’s nature did not ameliorate matters. Truth speaking, blunt and uncompromising in both his ideals and their expression, his manner often aggravated his peers as much as it drew their admiration. It was, perhaps, this quality which caused the Emperor to pass over Dorn as Warmaster when he withdrew from the Great Crusade. Horus, unlike Dorn, was a master of diplomacy and maintaining a coordinated balance between fractious forces. Where Dorn would cause conflict, Horus would unify, where Dorn would not compromise, Horus would find a way of satisfying all. But even as Horus took up the reins of the Great Crusade, Dorn was invested Praetorian of Terra. Even as Horus would push the Great Crusade on, the Imperial Fists would withdraw with the Emperor to Terra. And with that decision, the fate of the Emperor and all his sons was set.

### The Consus Drift

The Consus Drift was a vast debris belt near the Sol System, inhabited by billions of descendants of travellers stranded by a recurring Warp phenomenon known as the Mirror Race. Over generations they had constructed interconnected asteroid cities from wrecked vessels, surviving through salvage and fierce competition for water, food and territory. The Driftborn were divided into clans identified by patterns painted on their armoured void suits and tattooed upon their bodies. Although accustomed to violence from childhood, they regarded warfare as a necessity governed by strict customs rather than a pleasure. Their ships, boarding craft and weapons had been accumulated across thousands of years, making them exceptionally experienced void fighters.

The Imperial Fists’ 3rd Expeditionary Fleet encountered the Drift accidentally when the Mirror Race displaced its ships from the Warp, leaving them damaged and disoriented. The clans attacked before the fleet could recover. Their initial advantage was considerable, but the VII Legion’s disciplined counterattack drove the boarders from the Imperial vessels and carried the fighting into the Driftborn’s own void cities. Coordinated naval fire and boarding operations allowed the Imperial Fists to gain control of the cities’ life-support systems. The fleet then ceased firing and issued a single ultimatum: accept Compliance or face extermination. Confronted by a force whose organisation and mastery of void warfare exceeded anything in their experience, the Drift Clans surrendered.

Before departing, the Imperial Fists recruited the youngest and strongest male warriors from each clan. Those who survived initiation were concentrated in the 356th Company, which subsequently established an unrivalled reputation for fighting upon the outer hulls of spacecraft and conducting ship assaults. Its clustered-star heraldry and elaborate full-body tattoos preserved the customs of its Driftborn ancestors, demonstrating how the VII Legion could absorb a conquered culture’s martial traditions and turn them into a lasting specialist capability.

### The Two Wars of Araneus

The Araneus Continuity was an industrial pocket empire near Sol, united by ancient Warp gates of unknown origin and governed by a technonobility based on Araneus Prime. Its algae-producing ocean worlds sustained planets almost entirely given over to industry, while mining operations consumed planets and moons alike. Contact began when a lost Imperial squadron returning from the Gehenial Prosecution was expelled from the Warp and overwhelmed by cybernetically augmented warriors. An Imperial embassy subsequently demanded allegiance, but the technonobility, unconvinced by accounts of the Imperium’s size, instead invited the Emperor to become its tributary. The task of bringing the Continuity to Compliance was entrusted to Hashin Yonnad, Commander of the 39th Household of Inwit, a celebrated strategist, fleet commander and siege master.

After months of preparation, Yonnad departed Tallarn with thousands of ships carrying more than 20,000 Imperial Fists and millions of Imperial Army and Mechanicum troops. His forces conquered the first peripheral system in a single day. Drawing upon intelligence obtained by the Imperial envoys, he exploited the Continuity’s own Warp-gate network to sustain the offensive, dividing his fleet into predetermined formations that secured and fortified conquered systems before advancing further. The enemy’s counterattacks repeatedly encountered the Imperial Fists defending the very routes that had once united their empire. Within two months, Araneus Prime stood alone and its technonobility surrendered.

Compliance was immediately followed by a far more terrible war. As Imperial Iterators arrived to address the newly conquered populations, unidentified alien fleets emerged from the ancient gates. Their ships included smaller dark vessels accompanying immense craft, but the surviving accounts offer only obscure descriptions of their appearance. Neither their identity nor their motive is known beyond their apparent determination to destroy rather than conquer. Six systems were consumed, their stars burning violently before collapsing into cold remnants. Yonnad resolved to defend what remained, even at catastrophic cost. Imperial forces resisted the invaders as the surviving gates were destroyed, denying them further passage. The mysterious enemy vanished when deprived of the gates, although whether its ships escaped or were annihilated remains unknown.

Only Araneus Prime survived, and even that world was devastated by the death of its gate and the alien vessels that had penetrated its defences. Thousands of Imperial Fists and billions of humans had perished; the once-great world-city was reduced to ash and broken towers. In recognition of a planet that had endured near-total destruction yet remained alive, the Imperium renamed it Necromunda. The two wars exemplified both the VII Legion’s methodical approach to conquest and its willingness to suffer immense losses in defence of territory and populations already brought into the Emperor’s domain.

### Battle of Phall

The Battle of Phall was one of many devastating engagements orchestrated by Horus during the opening stages of the Heresy. His strategy relied on coordinated betrayals, ambushes, and surprise attacks designed to cripple Loyalist forces before the Imperium could fully mobilise. Although overshadowed by the massacres at Calth and Isstvan, Phall formed part of this broader campaign to secure a swift Traitor victory. The battle's true significance lay in the enormous losses suffered by both opposing fleets. Two powerful Legiones Astartes naval forces were severely weakened at a critical moment when either might otherwise have decisively influenced the wider war. Their mutual destruction arguably helped transform what Horus intended to be a short campaign into a prolonged conflict lasting years. Beyond its strategic consequences, Phall became a symbol of extraordinary heroism, sacrifice, and tragic opportunity lost. The battle demonstrated the courage of Loyalist warriors confronting betrayal, but also the devastating role of chance in determining the fate of entire fleets. Its legacy endured as a painful reminder of the Imperium's suffering during the Horus Heresy.

Rogal Dorn became the first Primarch to learn of Horus’ betrayal when his returning fleet encountered the Eisenstein, carrying Loyalist survivors fleeing the Isstvan III atrocities. Both fleets had been caught in the same violent Warp storms, leading to their unexpected meeting. Dorn responded by dividing the Imperial Fists between the defence of Terra and a punitive expedition against Horus. Nearly one-third of his Legion’s strength was assigned to Fleet Master Yonnad, reinforced by auxiliary vessels and hunter-killer squadrons from the Sol System’s military reserves. Over 500 warships were assembled into a Retribution Fleet and dispatched towards the Isstvan system through the raging Warp storms. Its mission was to punish Horus for his treachery and deliver the Emperor’s justice, driven by Dorn’s determination to avenge his brother’s betrayal.

The Imperial Fists’ Retribution Fleet never reached Isstvan, instead becoming trapped in devastating Warp storms that scattered and crippled its forces. Forced to seek refuge in the Phall system, barely two-thirds of the original fleet survived the journey, with many remaining warships severely damaged and their crews suffering heavy casualties. The survivors found themselves completely isolated at Phall, surrounded by impassable Warp storms that prevented both escape and astropathic communication with the wider Imperium. Their messages were distorted and reflected back by the turbulent Warp, leaving them unable to request reinforcements or report their situation. The disaster was compounded by the death of Fleet Master Yonnad, one of Rogal Dorn’s most trusted and accomplished commanders, who perished during the passage. The surviving Imperial Fists were now stranded, weakened, and leaderless in a system from which they had no apparent means of escape.

Following Fleet Master Yonnad’s death, Captain Alexis Polux assumed command of the stranded Imperial Fists Retribution Fleet, having been personally designated as his successor. Although relatively young and less renowned than veteran captains such as Tyr, Pertinax, and Scallus, Polux possessed exceptional strategic potential that Yonnad had recognised long before the disaster. Polux immediately transformed the surviving fleet into a formidable defensive force. He committed supplies originally intended for the campaign against Horus to repairing damaged warships and restoring their combat readiness. He then arranged the vessels into a constantly shifting spherical formation at the edge of the Phall system, creating a heavily armed mobile fortress capable of responding rapidly to enemy attacks or escaping through the Warp if the storms subsided. Over the following months, the Imperial Fists maintained extraordinary discipline and preparedness, meticulously studying the system’s gravitational conditions, conducting extensive combat drills, and practising coordinated fleet manoeuvres. Despite repeated attempts to escape, the surrounding Warp storms remained impassable. Polux’s leadership proved decisive, turning a scattered and badly damaged fleet into a highly organised fighting force. His unexpected rise to command would ultimately have profound consequences for the future of the Imperial Fists Legion.

Alexis Polux’s decision to maintain constant defensive readiness reflected the Imperial Fists’ doctrine of disciplined vigilance, but also his growing suspicion that something was wrong with the Phall system. Its two inhabited agricultural worlds, Phall I and II, had been mysteriously depopulated, with no bodies or explanation for the disappearance of their populations. This disturbing discovery suggested that Phall might have been deliberately prepared as a trap, with unnatural Warp storms potentially engineered to strand the Retribution Fleet in a predetermined killing ground. Meanwhile, Polux’s relentless drills and defensive manoeuvres served another purpose: channeling the Imperial Fists’ anger over Horus’ betrayal into discipline and preparation. Their desire for vengeance would soon be tested by an unexpected enemy attack.

Polux’s suspicions were confirmed when the Retribution Fleet suffered a devastating psychic disturbance, spreading agonising visions and screams throughout its warships. Investigations revealed hundreds of hidden devices scattered across the Phall system, each containing a mutilated Astropath connected to powerful psychic amplifiers. When activated, the tortured psykers scanned the system for information before dying, their final screams piercing the surrounding Warp storms to transmit messages and signal the fleet’s presence to an unknown enemy. The discovery strongly suggested that Phall had been prepared as an elaborate trap long before the Imperial Fists arrived. Its vanished populations, manipulated Warp storms, and concealed psychic devices all pointed towards a coordinated plan to isolate the Retribution Fleet and prepare it for destruction.

The Iron Warriors launched a massive surprise attack on the Imperial Fists’ Retribution Fleet at Phall, with an initial vanguard of 100 warships emerging from the Warp and immediately opening fire. The veteran warship Hammer of Terra was among the first destroyed, followed by dozens of smaller vessels. More Iron Warriors ships rapidly arrived, forming an enormous assault fleet. The Imperial Fists were shocked by the betrayal, having been isolated from communications and unaware of the Dropsite Massacre or the defection of several other Legions. Their fleet maintained a constantly shifting spherical formation, designed to respond flexibly to attacks from any direction. Perturabo personally commanded the Iron Warriors from his 15-kilometre flagship, Iron Blood, directing a concentrated wedge-shaped assault intended to overwhelm a single point in the Imperial Fists’ formation. However, the attack initially penetrated rather than shattered their defences, setting the stage for a prolonged naval confrontation.

Despite the overwhelming Iron Warriors assault, Alexis Polux prevented the Imperial Fists’ fleet from collapsing through an unconventional and carefully coordinated defence. Rather than simply holding their formation, he ordered some ships to withstand enemy fire while others appeared to retreat in disorder, deliberately drawing the attackers deeper into the battle. The apparent retreat was a calculated trap. Once beyond the Iron Warriors’ firing range, the withdrawing vessels executed high-speed turns and struck the enemy’s exposed flanks with lance batteries and torpedoes, targeting slower and damaged warships. Although dozens of Imperial Fists vessels were sacrificed to execute the manoeuvre, Polux successfully disrupted Perturabo’s initial assault and transformed a potentially catastrophic defeat into a coordinated counterattack. The Iron Warriors nevertheless continued pressing forward with relentless force.

The Battle of Phall escalated into a devastating war of attrition, with Imperial Fists and Iron Warriors warships exchanging relentless macro-cannon volleys, lance fire, and torpedo barrages. Ammunition supplies intended to last hours were exhausted within minutes, while hundreds of boarding actions erupted as opposing vessels grappled together, sometimes resulting in their mutual destruction. Both fleets unleashed their full arsenals, including swarms of fighters and bombers alongside rarely deployed, proscribed weapons such as volkite implosion-spheres, vortex warheads, and phosphex munitions. Warships were destroyed by the second, and millions of crew members perished. The battle demonstrated the horrifying destructive potential of the Imperium’s own military might, turning the weapons that had conquered the galaxy during the Great Crusade against their former brothers.

Following news of the Dropsite Massacre on Isstvan V, Rogal Dorn desperately attempted to recall the Retribution Fleet to Terra. With multiple Legions destroyed, missing, or revealed as Traitors, and Mars engulfed in rebellion, Dorn feared that the Imperial capital itself might soon face attack. However, violent Warp storms prevented his urgent astropathic orders from reaching the stranded Imperial Fists. At the height of the Battle of Phall, Dorn’s messages finally broke through the storms, their psychic intensity overwhelming the receiving Astropaths. The orders were unequivocal: the Retribution Fleet was to abandon its mission and return to Terra immediately. Unaware of the wider catastrophe unfolding across the Imperium, the Imperial Fists obeyed without question, despite having gained the upper hand against the Iron Warriors and being on the verge of striking at Perturabo himself. Their absolute loyalty to Dorn would ultimately cost them a potentially decisive victory.

Although both fleets at Phall fielded comparable classes of warships, the Iron Warriors possessed a numerical advantage of potentially two to one. Their vessels were generally more heavily armoured and designed for sustained planetary assaults, making them exceptionally durable but less capable of rapid acceleration and manoeuvring. This disadvantage proved significant against Alexis Polux’s unconventional tactics. The Imperial Fists possessed one of the strongest, most diverse, and best-equipped fleets of the Great Crusade. Their Retribution Fleet contained a higher proportion of fast cruisers and battlecruisers, many equipped with powerful long-range lance batteries. They also had access to rare weapons, including vortex torpedoes supplied from Terra. These differences helped the Imperial Fists counter the Iron Warriors’ superior numbers, allowing Polux to exploit his fleet’s greater mobility. However, neither side’s technological advantages alone could determine the battle’s outcome.

Following Rogal Dorn’s orders, the Imperial Fists began withdrawing from the Battle of Phall through a temporary opening in the surrounding Warp storms. Their ships disengaged in stages, while damaged vessels and veteran warships remained behind to delay the pursuing Iron Warriors, sacrificing themselves to protect their brothers. Barely one-third of the Retribution Fleet’s original strength escaped intact, with some returning to Terra and others scattered across the galaxy. Captain Armandus Tyr and his boarding force were effectively abandoned aboard Perturabo’s flagship, the Iron Blood, when the withdrawal deprived them of reinforcements. Although their ultimate fate remains unconfirmed, accounts suggest they reached Perturabo’s own chambers before being slain by the Primarch himself. Meanwhile, Alexis Polux survived by capturing the Iron Warriors warship Contrador after his own flagship, the Tribune, was crippled. The Battle of Phall ended in a costly Imperial Fists defeat, although the Iron Warriors also suffered devastating losses. Dorn’s recall order had reversed what might have become a Loyalist victory, raising the disturbing possibility that its arrival at precisely that moment had been deliberately orchestrated by Horus, his allies, or the dark powers manipulating the Heresy.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Imperial Fists remained concentrated around Terra under Rogal Dorn, preparing its defences. However, survivors scattered during the Phall campaign and isolated garrison detachments continued fighting independently throughout the Imperium.

Imperial Fists garrison survivors remained active after Phall. Some joined Autek Mor's forces in the 008.M31 attack on Bodt and fought alongside Iron Hands against the World Eaters. At Nagathar Imperial Fists and Dark Angels defended civilians against a Word Bearers occult sacrifice.

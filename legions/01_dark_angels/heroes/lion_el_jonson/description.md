## Notes

Lion El'Jonson's Legion learned of the rebellion only after Isstvan V. The Night Lords drew the Dark Angels into the Thramas Crusade, delaying reinforcement of Terra (Horus' role in arranging their earlier isolation is uncertain).

### Caliban, reunification and the Thramas Crusade

The Son of the Forest: Lion El'Jonson and Caliban

The Lion disappeared with the other Primarchs from the Emperor's laboratories before the Great Crusade. More than 150 years later, Knights of the Order found him in Caliban's forests, where monstrous chimaeric beasts, possibly weapons left from the Age of Strife, threatened the remaining human settlements. How long he lived alone is unknown; local tales of a mysterious forest spirit predate his discovery by almost a century, but these are not verified biographical records. His childhood taught him caution, self-reliance, ruthless determination and an absolute conception of victory. Luther persuaded the knights to spare him, named him Lion El'Jonson, the Son of the Forest, and taught him the laws and traditions of the Order. The Lion rapidly became an outstanding warrior, strategist and leader, abolishing restrictions that reserved knighthood for the nobility. Recruitment expanded despite opposition from traditionalists.

Within a decade the Order was able to go on the offensive against Caliban's beasts. Lion and Luther led a long campaign whose heavy cost did not deter the Lion from his objective of total eradication. He refused compromises with either the beasts or the rebels who resisted his changes, destroying their strongholds and armies. After victory, he was acclaimed Grand Master of the Order. The Lion regarded titles and acclaim with indifference and failed to appreciate the jealousy his rise awakened in Luther, formerly his equal and mentor. The Emperor arrived shortly thereafter, claimed his lost son and granted him command of the First Legion. The Lion renamed it the Dark Angels, drawing on Calibanite legend, and departed to continue the same kind of war on a galactic scale.

A Blade Reforged: the return of the Primarch

Only 500 warriors, mostly Host of Death veterans, accompanied the Emperor to Caliban because the rest of the First was scattered. Their black armour and funerary insignia resembled the figures of Calibanite legend. The Lion personally tested their captain in a duel, matching Calibanite steel against a power-field-wreathed blade and leaving his Cataphractii-armoured opponent wounded. Caliban was incorporated into the Imperium and began providing recruits; Gramarye's Council of Masters responded with mixed shame, pride and a desire to prove its worth. The Emperor instructed the Lion on Terra about the war and the particular role intended for him. El'Jonson accepted his function as a destroyer of the enemies of Mankind, largely uninterested in the rivalries or ambitions of his brothers. He was a formidable swordsman and strategist, able to challenge Fulgrim and Guilliman in their own fields, although his distinction lay in his singular determination rather than supremacy in every individual skill.

The Lion combined Terran Hexagrammaton traditions with the knightly practices of Caliban. While the first young Calibanite recruits underwent gene-seed implantation, hypnagogic indoctrination and combat training, he began gathering the Legion's scattered Companies with an initial force of 20,000, perhaps a third of its strength, including the original 500, other Chapters, Calibanite Imperial Army auxilia and a small retinue of Xana Tech-Magi. Xana's adepts assisted him in locating dispersed detachments through Divisio Militaris records. He tested each Company in combat and earned loyalty through personal action rather than speeches. Within a few years he had assembled nearly 100,000 warriors at Gramarye. There he defeated the Council's champion, Pyrrhus Calagat, Master of the Host of Fire, in an hour-long duel and accepted the offices of Grandmaster of the First Legion, Grandmaster of the six Wings of the Hexagrammaton and High Preceptor of the Orders Militant. He had united the Legion's overlapping authorities in one person. His oath bound the Legion to victory and the protection of the Imperium regardless of sacrifice.

A Duty of Sacrifice and Shadows: the reborn Dark Angels

The Lion appointed Wing Masters and formalised the existing Orders in the fashion of Caliban's knightly fraternities. The first influx of older Calibanite companions included warriors who had undergone dangerous augmentation rather than conventional Astartes implantation. Positions were awarded by merit, not seniority or origin; some Terran veterans resented having to yield authority to relative newcomers. The old grand chantry at Gramarye was replaced with a more modest fortress protecting its industry, while Caliban remained a major stronghold. The true centre of authority became the ancient Gloriana flagship Invincible Reason. To establish the new Legion's purpose through deeds, the Lion responded to a plea from Karkasarn, where Praetor Artareon of the XIIIth and his garrison had endured eight months of siege after rebels released a biogenic phage that turned inhabitants into ravenous flesh-ghola. Leading 1,000 Dreadwing veterans and backed by the full 10,000 warriors of his fleet, the Lion destroyed the enemy. He demanded neither the world's surrender to the First nor repayment from the Ultramarines, leaving an empty banner as the only mark of the old debt discharged.

The renewed Rangdan war subjected the rebuilt Legion to severe losses. Recorded battles include Vorksag, the void battles over Morcar and the seven-week battle of Morro, where three Companies held against more than a million neuro-shackled servitors. The Space Wolves lost approximately 5,000 in breaking Xana's siege, and the First was reduced to a tenth of its former number by the time victory was proclaimed. Some attributed the Terran veterans' sacrifices to their desire to prove themselves worthy of the Lion or correct their earlier failure against the Rangda; others alleged the Lion deliberately expended them to replace them with Calibanites, an unconfirmed rumour. The subsequent expansion of true Calibanite Astartes across the Wings and Orders created a Legion shaped by knightly ritual and less burdened by the old Terran rivalry. Its fleets dispersed once again, guarding the dark frontiers rather than building extensive territorial holdings.

Sarosh, Luther and the coming of the Heresy

At the request of Jaghatai Khan, with whom the Lion shared a mutual respect for plain dealing, the Dark Angels took charge of Sarosh. The world had accepted Imperial rule when confronted by the White Scars but resisted its terms, and attempted to kill the Lion with a hidden nucleonic charge. Although the plot failed and rebellion was crushed, the ease with which the Saroshi penetrated the Legion's defences raised doubts over the commitment of certain veterans. Luther and others from both Terra and Caliban were sent back to serve as the home world's garrison and overseers. Their removal was not officially exile, but it separated them from the Great Crusade. The Lion prioritised duty and excellence above personal attachment.

Luther's ultimate fate and the later catastrophe on Caliban remain deliberately obscured. His Order of the Black Key, ostensibly founded to foster cooperation with other Legions, particularly the Sons of Horus, and the unusual disposition of First Legion forces leaving Caliban isolated towards the end of the Heresy are presented as possible signs of a larger conspiracy. The later withdrawal of the Dark Angels, disappearance of many warriors and quarantine of their home system suggest an internal betrayal; Luther's exact role and the extent of Horus' influence remain unconfirmed. No definitive account of the Fall of Caliban is supplied here.

As the Great Crusade continued, the Lion and his Legion withdrew from its ceremonial fellowship and became increasingly absorbed in their assigned campaigns. Horus had attempted to cultivate them, but their Orders and Wings rejected his warrior lodges and their pride resisted outside influence. The Lion possessed the Emperor's confidence and access to Terra's hidden arsenals. Horus ensured that the First was diverted from Ullanor and that the Lion was absent from the Triumph. The Lion made no public response to Horus' appointment as Warmaster. Horus eventually sought to neutralise three Legions: the White Scars, whom he hoped to recruit; the Blood Angels, whom he hoped to destroy; and the Dark Angels, whom he sought to send so far away that his rebellion would be complete before their return. The First's combination of numbers, expertise, forbidden technology and loyalty made it an especially dangerous opponent.

The Diamat Incursion and the Lion's Return

Horus had dispatched the Dark Angels to conquer the distant Shield Worlds, an enclave of humanity in the darkness between galaxies associated with an unknown xenos species. This assignment kept Lion El'Jonson away from the Imperium during the opening stages of the rebellion. When reports of unrest reached him, the Lion detached fifteen of his fastest capital ships and a force of veteran warriors, making for Tanagra. Its position at the junction of stable Warp corridors offered routes into Imperial space, while the Forge World of Diamat promised supplies and intelligence. Instead, the Dark Angels found that Diamat had already sworn allegiance to Horus. After pacifying the forge, the Lion discovered records confirming that the Warmaster was directing a rebellion long in preparation: within Diamat's vaults lay enormous Ordinatus engines of an unfamiliar design, incorporating technology forbidden by Mars and secretly commissioned by Horus some fifty years earlier.

Perturabo met the Lion in the ruins of Diamat, pretending that the Iron Warriors remained loyal. He informed his brother of the Isstvan massacres and Curze's offensive in the Eastern Fringe, but withheld the full extent of the betrayal. Acting on Horus' instructions, Perturabo steered the First Legion towards the Night Lords, ensuring that it remained occupied far from Terra. The Lion also entrusted the recovered weapons of Diamat to the Iron Warriors, through whom they would ultimately reach the Warmaster. Departing for Triplex, El'Jonson summoned the remaining elements of his Legion from beyond the Imperium's borders, intending to reach the Forge Worlds before Curze.

Savage Weapons: The Confrontation on Tsagualsa

The battle at Thramas marked the beginning of the second year of the crusade according to the account. The Dark Angels held the initiative, but the Traitors still controlled numerous worlds in Thramas and almost all of Aegis. Gulgorahd remained comparatively free of Traitor influence, its surviving Bastion worlds maintaining their resistance despite extensive devastation. Pressure from the Dark Angels and the forces of Tigrus drew Traitor armies away from Gulgorahd, allowing the latter's legions to capture Parthac and strip its resources to replenish their depleted forges. The Night Lords, meanwhile, retook Yaelis. Both sides were approaching exhaustion, and the civilian population increasingly regarded the Legiones Astartes as uncontrolled instruments of destruction rather than the liberators of the Great Crusade. Trade and food distribution broke down, refugees fled their homes, and worlds cut off from Imperial protection became vulnerable to famine and xenos attacks.

On Tsagualsa, Curze summoned Sevatar, Sheng, Barbatos and other favoured commanders and sent the Lion an invitation to meet him at the new Night Lords stronghold. The strategic objectives of Horus had become secondary to the Night Haunter's desire for a confrontation with his brother that might change the fate revealed by his visions. The possibility of ending a war that had already consumed thousands of his Legionaries and innumerable mortals compelled the Lion to accept, despite his expectation of a trap. Unwilling to weaken the wider Loyalist front, he took only a single Chapter and a squadron of fast ships, leaving the Invincible Reason behind. Tsagualsa lay near the southern edge of the Nostramo sector, within Traitor-controlled space. The increasingly turbulent Warp, disturbed by the Word Bearers' rituals at Calth, dispersed the squadron, though no ships were lost. The Lion arrived first aboard the strike cruiser Vehemence and waited for his forces to regroup. The Night Lords made no attempt to intercept them and instead invited the Primarch to descend to the surface.

The Lion teleported to Tsagualsa accompanied only by Alajos, Praetor of the Ninth, and Corswain, with his remaining warriors standing by aboard their ships. This was the first meeting of the two Primarchs since the beginning of the Heresy. Curze used what he knew of Horus' plans and his own prophetic visions to provoke his brother, attacking the pride that underpinned the Lion's sense of duty. His central accusation was that all Primarchs were savage instruments of war, including the supposedly honourable Lord of the First Legion. It was the Lion who struck first. The honour guards joined the confrontation and the battle spread as both Legions committed their forces. The encounter degenerated into a violent, disordered struggle in which neither side achieved a meaningful strategic objective. Eventually the Night Lords dragged their severely wounded Primarch away, while the Dark Angels were forced to withdraw as more Traitor ships arrived. The Chapter that had accompanied the Lion was greatly diminished. More damaging than the physical wounds was the accusation Curze had planted in his brother's mind: the conduct of both Legions on Tsagualsa had made the distinction between them seem far less clear.

The Forbidden Battle: Perditus and Tuchulcha

The Lion's expedition had taken him to the mysterious system known as Perditus, meaning Forbidden. Of the 30,000 Dark Angels who departed with him, considerably fewer returned. The identity of his opponents and the precise purpose of the expedition remained secret; rumours suggested that another Legion had been present, but the account does not identify or confirm it. What is certain is that the Lion recovered an object that the First Legion called the Gatekeeper or the Key. It enabled the Dark Angels to circumvent the Warp storms that had isolated much of the Eastern Fringe and restricted their ability to pursue the Night Lords. The Lion regarded it as a necessary evil, a temporary instrument to be used as circumstances required and subsequently set aside. One record preserved in the guarded archives of Terra gives it the name Tuchulcha, associates it with the Deadly Seas and categorises it as a daemon. The precise nature of the object and the events of its recovery are not explained in the supplied account.

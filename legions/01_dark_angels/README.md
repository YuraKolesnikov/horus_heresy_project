# Dark Angels

## Legion Structure

### Legion organisation, Hosts, Wings, Orders and command hierarchy

#### PART II — THE LEGION'S INTERNAL STRUCTURE

#### Three overlapping systems, one Legion

The Dark Angels' organisation preceded the Principia Bellicosa and provided much of its original basis. The First developed numerous battlefield specialists in its formative years, experimenting with formations until successful ones could be preserved as an integrated system. Lion El'Jonson retained the core of this system, adapting it to Calibanite practices and bringing its authorities under a unified command. A warrior's position in the conventional Chapter, Company and squad structure was only one element of his identity. The second and third were memberships in the broad specialist Hosts (later Wings) and, for those admitted, the much more narrowly focused Orders of Battle. Together these formed the three chambers of authority: the Principia Bellicosa (Corpus Nobilis), the Hexagrammaton (Corpus Dextera), and the Hekatonystika (Corpus Sinister). They were overlapping frameworks, not three separate armies, and a formation of ordinary infantry could include initiates of several Wings and Orders.

The Hosts and Orders served a common purpose: collecting martial knowledge and ensuring its availability wherever needed. Hosts were large bodies dedicated to a broad aspect of warfare, often numbering hundreds or more and distributed throughout the Legion. Orders were numerous but small, frequently no more than a few dozen initiates, dedicated to defeating a particular enemy, operating on a particular battlefield or mastering an unusual weapon or skill. Hosts generally recruited openly within the Legion, while Orders kept their membership, rites and specialised knowledge secret. When a situation demanded it, members of a Host could be assembled en masse into a specialist formation, while members of an Order formed a temporary cenobium to lead and advise the force. This made even relatively small Dark Angels contingents unusually versatile.

#### The ancient Hosts — the foundations of the Hexagrammaton

The following nine Hosts are reconstructed from records of 753.M30; they are the principal formations then known, not an exhaustive catalogue of all early Hosts. Their specialisms were developed over centuries and many of their methods passed into the later six Wings.

#### Host of Crowns

Among the oldest Hosts, comprising the original core of champions from the pre-Unity armies of Terra. Its specialists excelled as vanguard fighters and line-breakers, with expertise in formal honour duels and champion-led assaults. They served both as powerful individual fighters and as visible symbols of triumph. The later Deathwing arose from its shattered remnants, inheriting its elite assault and personal-combat traditions.

#### Host of Blades

The infantry core of the early Legion, mastering close-order tactics, disciplined ranks, sustained gunfire and assault columns. The Host was organised around the Legion Tactical squad and played a major role in the development of the first volumes of the Principia Bellicosa. Its methods underpin the conventional line infantry doctrine that the later Stormwing embodied.

#### Host of Pentacles

The First's earliest attempt to integrate Terran war-witches and psychic practitioners into the line of battle. Its members, known as Quaesitors, studied knowledge recovered from defeated occult tyrants and the psychic capacities granted by the Emperor's gene-craft. The Host's early successes were offset by severe dangers posed by uncontrolled psychic power. At the Sealing of the Black Gate in 810.M30, its adepts proved indispensable but some succumbed to entities or lost control of their abilities. The Host was disbanded afterwards, its surviving warriors distributed among other Hosts. Its Master, Idrik Kybalos, vanished from the Legion's records before reappearing almost a century later in the founding charter of the Librarius alongside Magnus and Jaghatai Khan. Some of its teachings were preserved and incorporated with those of Prospero and Chogoris into the Librarius' training regime.

#### Host of Iron

Originally drawn from the Thule nomads of Terra, the Host pioneered the employment of armoured vehicles, adapting traditions of ancient ground-crawlers to the Astartes' new war engines. Its members developed many of the armoured warfare doctrines later shared across the Legiones Astartes. It merged with the Host of Stone to form the Ironwing.

#### Host of Fire

A covert fraternity of spies, assassins, infiltrators and interrogator consuls, serving as scouts and executioners. Its methods relied on reconnaissance, secret warfare and selective violence rather than massed assault. The Firewing retained much of its ancient culture and operational character. The Host's Master Pyrrhus Calagat was the Council's champion when the Lion reunited the First.

#### Host of Bone

Also known as the Skandic Host, reflecting both its recruiting traditions and its brutal methods. It sought to break an enemy's resolve and exploit its hidden weaknesses rather than merely defeat its army. Its adherents disregarded conventional martial restraint and employed the Legion's most terrible weapons and tactics. The Dreadwing evolved from this tradition.

#### Host of Stone

Siege specialists, masters of static defence, fortification and the destruction of strongpoints. The Host's stoic ethos was expressed in its ability to endure bombardment or assault without wavering. Together with the Host of Iron it became the later Ironwing, whose siege initiates continued to use the old title Stoneborn.

#### Host of Wind

Skirmishers, cavalry and fast assault troops, adept with light armour and jetbikes. Their daring earned many honours but also a high casualty rate. Its surviving elements were combined with the Host of the Void and Caliban's Order of the Raven to form the Ravenwing.

#### Host of the Void

One of the last great Hosts to emerge, specialising in orbital deployment, teleport assaults, shipboard boarding operations and aerial warfare. Its forces included teleport-equipped shock infantry, boarding cadres and aircraft pilots. Its expertise became an important component of the later Ravenwing. The Host of Death was associated with Vendraig's lifeguard and the 500 veterans first sent to Caliban; its distinct doctrine remains uncertain.

#### The Hexagrammaton — the Six Divine Ways

The title Hexagrammaton, loosely translated from ancient Terran as the Six Divine Ways, came into use around 830.M30, when the First reached a nominal strength of 100,000 and many unsuccessful or redundant Hosts had disappeared. The Lion preserved the six surviving doctrinal pillars, reorganising their hierarchies to resemble Calibanite knightly cohorts and renaming them Wings. The six remained the foundation of the Legion's specialist structure throughout the Heresy, though several suffered near-destruction. The descriptions below reflect the organisation at the time of the Dark Angels' return to the Imperium during the Thramas Crusade.

#### Stormwing — disciplined infantry and the main battle line

The largest Wing encompassed most line infantry, training battalions and mobile ordnance batteries. Its domain was close-order fighting, pitched battles, the holding of defensive lines and coordinated infantry offensives. Stormwing veterans formed the core of infantry Companies, executing complicated manoeuvres under fire and maintaining cohesion when armies met in massed battle. They stood in the leading ranks to stiffen the resolve of other warriors and acted as centres of order amid the confusion of combat. Their particular skill was reading the shifting rhythm of a mass engagement and applying the Legion's considerable skill-at-arms at the decisive moment. The outer circles changed continually as warriors advanced or were lost; the inner circles were unusually conservative, stable and resistant to easy admission. A prospective Marshal had to pass exacting trials, and only veterans of the greatest engagements earned such titles. Battle scars themselves were valued distinctions. The Wing also incorporated a significant part of the Apothecarion; its members operated at the centre of mass infantry assaults, and originally the Master of the Stormwing also headed the Legion's Apothecarion. Its characteristic assets were conventional line infantry, their supporting mobile artillery and disciplined formations rather than a particular exotic weapon.

#### Deathwing — champions, line-breakers and sworn guardians

Formed from the remnants of the Host of Crowns, whose old expertise included line-breaking squadrons and marksmanship, the Deathwing bound its inner circles by an oath never to leave the field in enemy hands, preferring death to dishonour or retreat. It specialised in high-value battlefield tasks, often supporting the larger Stormwing. Among its many sub-disciplines were elite assault formations designed to shatter enemy lines and create openings for other troops, and personal lifeguards assigned to officers in combat. Few high-ranking officers lacked a small Deathwing cadre sworn to defend them with their lives. Detachments also guarded the inner citadels of Caliban and Gramarye, with responsibility for the Primarch's safety. Membership was comparatively small, stringent and prestigious; veterans could assume battlefield command where the authority of other formations was unclear. As the Heresy depleted the officer corps, the Deathwing took increasing responsibility across the Legion and within the Council of Masters. At Thramas, its role was illustrated by the Deathwing Companions in distinctive bone-white armour, fighting at the Lion's side, and the use of its veterans as elite boarding and assault forces. Deathwing formations employed varied armour and weapon patterns.

#### Ravenwing — mobility, reconnaissance and rapid intervention

The Wing most substantially changed by the Lion, the Ravenwing was named for the Calibanite Order of the Raven, whose mounted warriors were renowned on their home world. Its foundation combined that Order with the surviving Hosts of Wind and Void. Its doctrine emphasised mobile attacks, hit-and-fade operations, skirmishing and rapid concentration against opponents who sought to evade the Legion or possessed overwhelming strength. It used light skimmers, jetbikes, aircraft and reconnaissance infantry; some of its infantry specialised in drop-pod and orbital assault, reflecting the Host of the Void's legacy. The white corvid was a mark of its initiates. Ravenwing warriors were often regarded by more traditional Wings as mavericks or glory-seekers, retaining customs from remote feudal recruiting worlds. Calibanites who entered its ranks were frequently those seeking their own reputations or out of favour with more prestigious Wings. The Ravenwing's role extended beyond mounted warfare: intelligence gathering, deep penetration, interception, orbital descent and swift reinforcement were all expressions of the same mobile doctrine. The siege of Mykana provides a concrete example of its jetbike and speeder forces penetrating a shielded fortress while the main Legion force waited outside.

#### Dreadwing — extinction warfare and forbidden technology

The Dreadwing evolved from the Host of Bone, retaining many Skandic ceremonies and titles while Calibanite recruits moderated aspects of its old temperament. Its purpose was the complete annihilation of enemies, populations or worlds through purge operations, terror, devastation and Exterminatus-class weaponry. It incorporated most of the Legion's Destroyer and Moritat cohorts, numerous Techmarines and forge adepts, and a substantial number of Apothecaries. Its members were not merely berserkers: their task also included the custody of technologies too dangerous for normal Imperial use. In their sealed vaults were the Legion's last stable old-pattern Dreadnoughts, whose occupants' minds were trapped in memories of ancient wars, and artificial warriors of a still more sinister kind. The earlier Thramas chapter identifies the Excindio as one such class of imprisoned machine intelligence, released under the Ikaros Contingency in the war against the Dark Mechanicum. In the later Heresy, warriors granted special dispensation by the Lion to resume the use of psychic powers also found a place in the Dreadwing. Its exceptional title Eskaton denoted a warrior who had overseen the final destruction of a race or world. The Wing's doctrine demanded that victory take precedence over prohibitions or the moral cost of its means; its historical reputation was more formidable than its numerical influence within the Legion.

#### Ironwing — armoured warfare, siegecraft and overwhelming firepower

The Ironwing merged the ancient Hosts of Iron and Stone. Its domain was the destruction of enemy resistance through superior firepower, massed armoured assaults, heavy armour and the breaking of fortified positions. It included most armoured vehicles, heavy Dreadnoughts and field artillery batteries, as well as heavy infantry, heavy weapon support squads, breacher cadres and Terminators. Ironwing Marshals specialised in engaging enemy war engines and heavy guns, including the destruction of monstrous engines encountered during the First's hidden campaigns. In the final years of the Great Crusade, the Ironwing fielded the largest concentration of Terminators among the Legions, including experimental patterns unavailable to other forces. These warriors were often concentrated for assaults on fortresses or to resist powerful offensives; many retained the Host of Stone emblem and the title Stoneborn. The inner circles were dominated by the Legion's forge lords, whose access to ancient or proscribed technology made the Wing particularly distrusted by the Mechanicum. Its expertise included the technical understanding required to overcome unfamiliar defences, as demonstrated by Lieutenant Iksal Mordwen at Mykana. The Heresy severely depleted the Wing, which repeatedly bore the cost of leading assaults or holding defensive positions.

#### Firewing — intelligence, assassination and the destruction of command

The smallest of the six Wings specialised in the subtleties of warfare, concentrating on the enemy's leadership, morale and weak points. Its ranks comprised stalkers, champions, Moritats, duellists and assassins united by their skill with blade and knife. Some eliminated commanders in formal challenges, others through covert attacks far from the battlefield. The Wing preferred to destroy an enemy's will and command structure rather than spend lives defeating its entire army. Knowledge was one of its principal weapons: the Firewing possessed the most extensive library of any Wing, containing accounts of thousands of xenos and sub-human civilisations, guarded by a specialised subset of its initiates. Of the six, it remained closest to its Unification-era character. Many senior Marshals were ancient Terran veterans, and its elaborate rites were little affected by Calibanite culture; recruitment traditionally favoured Terra and nearby populated colonies. These origins had once given it close ties to the Sons of Horus, which became bitter enmity after the rebellion. The Firewing reportedly suffered severe losses against Rangdan warmasters and reavers during the second Rangdan Xenocide, reducing it to a fraction of its earlier size. Its strength lay in specialised intelligence, targeted violence and precision rather than numbers or a single weapon system.

#### The Hekatonystika — the Hundred Esoteric Arts

The Hekatonystika was the concealed counterpart of the Hexagrammaton, its name roughly translated from the ancient Terran language of Mykene as the Hundred Esoteric Arts. Each Order Militant was an autonomous fraternity that preserved a specific body of secret knowledge. This might concern the biology and weaknesses of a particular xenos species, combat on an extreme world, a particular weapon, or a specialised method of warfare. Members were scholars as well as combatants. They were embedded in Chapters and Wings and rarely gathered as an entire Order. A small body of members summoned for a particular battle was called a cenobium; its warriors advised line officers and led operations requiring their expertise. Hundreds of Orders are recorded over the Legion's history, many extinct by the Heresy.

Orders maintained distinct hierarchies, insignia, ritual ciphers and concealed meeting places. Advancement conferred access to progressively deeper knowledge and responsibility for maintaining it. Most had sanctum chambers aboard ships carrying their members, while larger Orders controlled dedicated stations or strongholds. Candidates were identified by senior members on merit and debated privately before undergoing physical and mental trials, often dangerous and conducted under vows of silence. Even unsuccessful candidates were forbidden by honour to reveal what they had witnessed. Full induction granted access to the first tier of an Order's lore and the right to display its acolyte's mark. Fewer than half the Legion's warriors could claim membership of even an outer circle of an Order Militant. Some warriors belonged to multiple Orders or transferred between them while retaining their former secrets. Knight-captain Atreus Deucalion, a Firewing Marshal and bearer of Chrysaor, belonged to three separate Orders and combined their sword techniques. The Lion, as High Preceptor, held authority over every Order.

#### Order of Shattered Crowns

Dating to the Unification Wars, the Order specialised in identifying and openly destroying enemy leaders. Its warriors were champions rather than clandestine assassins, using obscure Terran duelling techniques to kill the figure around whom resistance was organised. Most also belonged to the Deathwing or Firewing, whose doctrines complemented its role; the Order's Preceptor was rumoured to be a member of the Dreadwing's Inner Circle. It was an example of an Order defined by a very narrow method of attacking the enemy's morale and command.

#### Order of Broken Wings

Its membership lay almost entirely within the Ironwing. It specialised in anti-aircraft warfare, deflection targeting, heavy autocannon craftsmanship and the positioning and operation of anti-aircraft defences against aerial incursions. Commanders summoned its adepts to oversee such deployments or crew key defensive emplacements. Its focus illustrates how a single technical speciality could be preserved within the much broader armoured and firepower doctrine of a Wing.

#### Order of Santales

Dedicated to identifying and eliminating xenos dependent on psychic or physical parasitism. It maintained a large arsenal of psyarkana collected from defeated civilisations. Its honours included Rangda, Nemodiae and Muspel, campaigns presented as comparable in severity to Ullanor. Following the Council of Nikaea, the Order lost members and influence when warriors of the former Librarius were stripped of their positions. The full extent of its psyarkana remains unknown.

#### Order of the Argent Spire

Specialists in warfare on frozen death worlds and ice-bound terrain. They practised endurance rites for extremes of cold and distinctive blade-kata designed to maintain stability on shifting ice and snow. Their adherents were most often Stormwing initiates and could be assembled into small cells to lead operations in winter environments. The sword techniques of their Inner Circle knights were also admired by Firewing veterans.

#### Order of Broken Claws (also rendered Broken Claw)

The Order preserved the only complete account of the Rangdan conflict and detailed knowledge of Rangdan bio-forms, derived from Firewing intelligence and Dreadwing cheirophages. Its adepts were trained to fight these xenos and counter the methods of their warmasters, gaining renown in the Third Rangdan Xenocide. With the enemy's apparent extinction the fraternity dwindled. By the Heresy only a single chantry station remained over the ruined Advex-mors world, according to the Order profile; the Rangdan campaign account and the later raid narrative place its chantry at Rangda. These locations are presented as given rather than equated. The Order also recovered the sealed records of Canis-Balor, and its warriors later guarded dangerous Rangdan relics against the Sons of Horus. The text describes its establishment in the wake of the Second Rangdan Xenocide, wording that is not fully consistent with the campaign overview's terminology.

#### Order of Broken Spears

Experts in ambushes and stealthy warfare in densely obstructed environments. Their orbital stronghold above the broken mesas and canyons of Argyel III could house all 138 known members. The death world's rock was rich in heavy metals that interfered with active sensors, making it particularly suitable for the Order's training, study and retreat.

#### Order of the Empty Sky

Cenobite Astilaeus and his cenobium accompanied the Dark Angels' landing force sent to eliminate the Night Lords in the ruins of Thramas. The Order mastered close combat conducted in concert with lethalis-category artillery support, taking advantage of the disruption caused by heavy shelling even as its warriors advanced through the impact area. The tradition originated with heavily armoured Calibanite knights who fought while their serfs and armsmen showered the battlefield with primitive projectiles; many initiates were Calibanite. Cataphractii Terminator armour enabled these specialists to endure the fragmentation and fire of Great Crusade artillery and strike enemy formations while they were suppressed.

#### Other named Orders and fraternities

The Order of the Bloody Shrouds served as a disciplinary corps, intervening at the Black Gate when uncontrolled psychic practitioners endangered their brothers. The Orders of Extinction and Annihilation maintained knowledge of enemies against which the Legion might be deployed; Canis-Balor exposed the danger of encountering a species absent from their records. The Order of the Black Key was founded by Luther, ostensibly to improve inter-Legion cooperation, and is mentioned in the context of possible later betrayal. The Calibanite Order of the Raven was a precursor contributing to the Ravenwing, not necessarily a separate Hekatonystika Order under that name. The Advex-mors narrative also names the Order of Crowns, distinct in wording from the ancient Host of Crowns; their relationship remains unclear.

#### The Five Hundred Companions and the Lion Codex

The original 500 warriors who stood beside the Lion on Caliban formed an honorary Company within the Orders Militant. Their task was to study the Primarch himself: his methods of combat, tactics, preferences and the best means of reshaping the Legion to fight alongside him. They served as his lifeguard, accumulating knowledge in the heavily enciphered Lion Codex, which became a closely guarded resource for the Council of Masters. The Five Hundred were its only keepers and accepted no new initiates, meaning their knowledge would perish with them. The Lion neither explicitly praised nor prohibited their study; his silent acceptance of their presence before battle was regarded as a high distinction. This fraternity should not be confused with all Deathwing Companions or the wider Deathwing.

#### Orders Civilis — the hidden support structure

Alongside the Orders Militant were Orders Civilis, devoted to specialised non-combat work. They seldom included full Astartes and instead honoured Legion serfs and failed aspirants who maintained the war effort as armourers, quartermasters, cargo pilots and maintenance crews. Their work was vital, but they held no representation in the Conclave of Preceptors, and little is recorded of their specific structures or histories.

#### Legion command hierarchy and titles

Each of the Legion's three chains of command followed a broadly conventional internal hierarchy, but their relative authority was determined by the requirements of the engagement rather than a permanent ranking between branches. A Chapter commander established the overall strategy; when an officer of a Wing or Order possessed the knowledge best suited to the immediate situation, that specialist could assume direction of the fighting. This depended on mutual trust, long indoctrination and shared knowledge of the traditions, rather than an easily deciphered chart. Outsiders often found the complex heraldry and shifting authority confusing; even experienced allies could struggle to identify which warrior commanded a contingent. Guilliman repeatedly sought to persuade the Lion to adopt his own more rigid structure. The Raven Guard applied comparable principles to parts of its organisation, but other Legions lacked the centuries-old veteran core necessary to reproduce the First's complete system.

Line officers originally used the standard titles praetor, centurion and consul. After the Lion's return, knightly Calibanite titles became widespread, though some Terran and Gramarye veterans retained the old forms. Within the Orders, Preceptor indicated the leader, Seneschal a senior member supervising hidden chambers or halls, and Cenobite an honorary title used in open battle to mark the temporary primacy of Order authority over ordinary rank. In the Wings, rank indicated both authority and skill, with occasional special honours such as the Dreadwing's Eskaton.

At the strategic level, the Lion was the absolute authority as Lord of the First Legion, Grandmaster of the Hexagrammaton and High Preceptor of the Hekatonystika. The Council of Masters, made up of Wing heads or their agents, allocated forces and commanders to fleets and theatres, maintained the broader strategic direction and undertook administration while the Lion led individual battlegroups. The Conclave of Preceptors rarely assembled in full and normally assumed strategic command only when a conflict required its specialised knowledge, although its members provided a reserve of experienced leaders. The resulting system allowed the Legion to operate across many simultaneous theatres, independently of direct supervision by its Primarch. The Lion preserved the structure in recognition of its effectiveness and the competence of his veteran warriors.

### Firewing Enigmatii Cabal

#### Firewing Enigmatii Cabal

The Firewing exists to prosecute a swift and silent style of war, to smother the enemy’s ability to resist, steal their will to fight with precision strikes, and gain the acquisition of key intelligence for use by other forces. The Enigmatii are the foremost practitioners of this style of war, operating in small elite units to eliminate the enemy’s leaders, disrupt their lines of advance and throw their plans into disarray. Armed and equipped with the finest weapons and equipment available, they are a deadly threat to those deemed dangerous enough by the Lion to warrant their attention.

#### Legion identity and purpose

The First Legion was the Emperor's original model for the Legiones Astartes: an army intended to master every form of warfare, not a formation built around one preferred doctrine. Its Primogenitor was Lion El'Jonson; its earlier cognomina were the Angels of Death and the archaic Uncrowned Princes. Its principal domains were Caliban, Gramarye and Terran enclaves. Its recorded allegiance was Fidelitas Constantus. The Legion's defining purpose was extermination of threats to Mankind, including enemies too dangerous for ordinary Imperial forces or even other Legions. It did not seek to govern conquered realms, erect monuments or preserve its reputation through public honours. Many of its most important campaigns were deliberately expunged from records, and its warriors accepted isolation and notoriety as the price of this role. Hector Thrane's attributed declaration of 828.M30 encapsulates that identity: the First existed to destroy the Emperor's enemies, not to build an empire or leave a celebrated legacy.

### Arsenals, equipment, doctrine and war disposition

#### The forbidden arsenals and characteristic equipment

The First Legion had access to technology comparable in sophistication to that available to the Legio Custodes, including specialised weapons produced at dedicated manufactoria on Terra and elsewhere according to ancient designs maintained by its forge masters. Exotic plasma weapons, archaic and unstable munitions, and distinctive armoured vehicle and voidcraft variants formed part of its everyday arsenal. More terrible reserves were kept in sealed vaults on Caliban and Gramarye and in secret caches across the Imperium, guarded by dedicated Orders. These included gene-phages capable of destroying all life on a planet within hours, core-breaching magna munitions, and stocks of ancient silica-vitae, proscribed by Mars. These devices were entrusted to the First as instruments of extermination, not ordinary conquest.

The equipment described in the chapter reflects the Legion's diversity rather than a universal preferred loadout. Infantry and knightly traditions valued blades and disciplined close-order fighting; Cataphractii plate appears both in the Lion's first encounter with the Legion and in the Order of the Empty Sky's artillery-supported assault doctrine. Heavy infantry and Terminators were particularly concentrated in the Ironwing; the Deathwing's defining assets were its veteran assault specialists and lifeguards, not a monopoly on any particular armour type. The Ravenwing fielded light skimmers, jetbikes, speeders and aircraft; the Dreadwing fielded Destroyer and Moritat cadres, arcane weapons and ancient engines. Heavy autocannon are explicitly associated with the Order of Broken Wings, and psyarkana with the Order of Santales. The battle accounts additionally identify Fellblade and Falchion super-heavy tanks, Sokar- and Anhur-pattern Stormbirds, Storm Eagles, Fire Raptors, Xiphon Interceptors, and an ancient graviton cannon aboard Invincible Reason. These are attested examples of equipment in service rather than a comprehensive Armoury list. Earlier Thramas excerpts provide further examples, including the Interemptors' plasma weapons, Terminator-armoured Naufragia and the Excindio, all operating within the First's specialised extermination doctrine.

#### Operational doctrine, deployment and logistics

At the end of the Great Crusade the First claimed around 180,000 warriors, placing it among the largest Legions alongside the Ultramarines and Sons of Horus. Some external records give strengths as low as 50,000, possibly reflecting difficulties interpreting Dark Angels organisation and heraldry. Another passage places total strength at the outbreak of the Heresy somewhat below 200,000. The Legion's dense cipher systems, secret formations and unconventional command could cause setbacks when coordinating with other Imperial forces. Some veterans deliberately arrived without identifying their commander, expecting allied officers to recognise rank from heraldry; correct judgement or prudent silence earned respect. Such tests reflected their preference for demonstrated competence over reputation.

The First maintained its exceptionally experienced veteran core through the transmission of accumulated knowledge in Wings and Orders. It preferred numerous independently operating Chapters and Companies to mass deployments, often concentrating only a few thousand warriors in a theatre. Flexible formations and powerful weapons enabled small forces to face threats that would otherwise have required much greater manpower. Large concentrations of black-armoured warriors were regarded as signs of exceptional danger, and Imperial Army troops dreaded supporting the First because of the nature of its battlefields rather than any routine mistreatment. Those who survived such service treasured the honours awarded to them by the Lion.

Its vast fleet was similarly dispersed. Support flotillas were built around Gloriana-class battleships or other first-rate vessels with cruiser squadrons, transporting assigned Chapters, providing orbital fire support, servicing aircraft and enabling major assaults without extensive auxilia. Deep-range patrol squadrons generally carried no more than a Company, using long-range cruisers and scouts equipped with distinctive augurs. They located hidden xenos enclaves or hostile human settlements, assessed their threat through limited raids, and either eradicated them from orbit or left manageable targets to subsequent Expeditionary Fleets. The First's primary home was therefore its fleet rather than a network of fixed garrisons. Gramarye, its Legion home world before 846.M30 according to the planetary dossier, housed the Council of Masters and concealed industrial facilities. Caliban held the Order's fortress and became the major recruiting and cultural centre. Terran recruiting enclaves and remote chantry houses were less heavily defended, functioning primarily as supply, training and archival sites. The Legion's fortress garrisons generally consisted of recruits, Masters, Seneschals and Deathwing lifeguards. Caliban is described as a heavily forested temperate feudal death world in Segmentum Obscurus/Proxima; Gramarye as an industrial/primitive fortress world in Segmentum Solar/Sinistra. Their sensitive system data were classified.

#### War disposition at the outbreak of the Horus Heresy

Horus had deliberately divided the Dark Angels and assigned them to distant campaigns. No more than a third of their strength accompanied the Lion beyond the eastern frontier; perhaps 120,000 remained within reach of the Imperium, mainly scattered in the galactic south and west in forces generally no larger than 5,000 warriors. Many had recently completed costly campaigns or were still fighting. Supply and communications with Caliban and Gramarye had deteriorated, and the Primarch was unreachable. Horus assumed these isolated forces could not oppose him effectively. He underestimated both the resistance of the First's Orders and Wings to his warrior lodges and their ability to operate independently of the central Imperial command structure.

The scattered Knight-praetors launched independent campaigns against worlds openly aligned with Horus, raiding supply routes and industrial centres on the southern and western rims and threatening Barbarus, thereby reducing the flow of reinforcements and resources to the Traitor front. By the time the Loyalist force was assembling for Isstvan V, the Dark Angels were already active at a dozen distant worlds. They used devastation and denial against territories they could not hold, while avoiding premature concentration in a single decisive battle. Their small, mobile forces resisted the pattern of isolation and annihilation that devastated other Loyalist Legions. Though unable to halt the rebellion alone, these actions bought Dorn time to organise Terra's defences and left much of the First intact for the Lion's return. The home worlds remained isolated by the growing Warp storms, Word Bearers' esoterica and unusual silence within the Legion's leadership. The full extent of these internal difficulties remains unclear in the supplied chapter.

## Legion Warriors

Dark Angels Lance-Decurion

Pelagor Marner was a Calibanite Dark Angels Lance-Decurion serving aboard the ancient warship Dark Sovereign. His last confirmed deployment before the Horus Heresy was the Sibelius Campaign in 002.M31. The ship's subsequent activities remain unknown until its surprise involvement in the Xana Incursion, although it apparently did not participate in the Thramas Crusade.

Marner wore a distinctive Paravane-pattern variant of Mk II power armour, developed by the Dark Angels for improved performance in void operations. His heraldry combined ancient Terran symbols, standard Legion insignia, and Calibanite traditions, forming a complex system of markings whose full meanings were understood only by certain members of the Legion.

Terminator Knight Cenobite

The unidentified Dark Angels Terminator belonged to the 13th Chapter of the Third Host and participated in the Xana Incursion, fighting Mechanicum Thallax cohorts aboard the freighter Thorn-Macro-878. His presence remains mysterious, as his formation was not normally assigned to the Dark Sovereign. He wore modified Cataphractii Terminator armour decorated with Deathwing, Hexagrammaton, and ancient Terran insignia, alongside ceremonial keys awarded for distinguished service.

### Dark Angels pict-records

#### Cenobite Astilaeus — Order of the Empty Sky

Deployed as part of the landing force that would eradicate the Night Lords' presence in the ruins ofThramas, Cenobite Astilaeus and the other members of his cenobium made the specialised skills of the Order of the Empty Sky available to the Dark Angels' spearhead and were experts in the prosecution of close-quarters battle in conjunction with lethalis category artillery support, exploiting the chaos of lethalis category fire support as shells burst in the very midst of their assault. It was a tradition that had begun on Caliban, with the heavily armoured knights fighting even while their serfs and armsmen showered the field with shafts and shot from primitive projectile weaponry, and many of its adherents were of Calibanite stock. The heavy Cataphracti Terminator armour granted them by the Lion as part of his vaunted First Legion, allowed them to weather the storm of shrapnel and flame produced by the terrible weapons of the Great Crusade with limited risk and bring the fury of their assault into the heart of enemy formations that were cowering in the face of the artillery's fury.

#### Legionary Zediel Ardaral

Though a simple Legionary of the line, an ordinary warrior within the host, Legionary Ardaral also bears the rank of Proctor within the Storm.wing, and that of cenobite within the Order of the Crimson Field. Within his unit he held a higher rank within the Stormwing than any other and was often called upon to advise the sergeant of his squad on appropriate tactics and organisation, as well as setving outside his squad in a cenobium of the Crimson Field during two separate engagements. Overall, he serves as an excellent example of the distributed command structure utilised by the Dark Angels, one designed to put skills and experience before simple rank and too complex to be made easy use of by those not well-versed in its intricacies. Of particular note in this pkt-record is the Edict of Incorporation attached to his left vambrace, the left hand being that associated with the Orders Militant, and signifying a warrior honoured by selection to stand as part of a cenobium in battle. Legionary Ardaral would survive the Thramas Crusade to great honour, fighting in the vanguard of the assault on Sheol IX. There he and his unit formed a vital part of the force that cut the Night Lords off from their ships, allowing the Lion and the secondary waves of the attack to catch their elusive foe off-guard and vulnerable. By the end of the battle, his company had been rendered almost combat ineffective, with casualties near halving their numbers, though the tally of their own kills more than proved their valour and skill in the face of such a fierce foe.

#### Deathwing Veteran Knight-Sergeant Idravain Mors

Knight-sergeant Mors was inducted into the inner circles of the Deathwing after the battle on Mykana, where he took a Mykanan phase-lock musket shot meant for his commander, leaving him on the brink of death once victory was secured. His armour bears a number of elements of the unique heraldry used by the Dark Angels, and often considered indecipherable by their brethren in the other Legions. Of particular note are the white plating segments of his modified MkIV battle plate, which signify a warrior of the Death wing that has taken a mortal wound meant for another and survived, the votive ceramite plug on the left section of his chest plate marks the breach in his armour as a badge of honour with few equals among the pragmatic Dark Angels. Also of note are the crossed keys on his left greave and the Death wing initiation icons on his right pauldron, both of which are intended to mark him as a warrior who is master in the arts of the Deathwing, as a champion and line breaker, on the field of battle should he be needed. Knight-sergeant Mors would fall again on the fields of Sheol IX, once again shielding the Knight-praetor of the Shattered Mantle, though this time from the vengeful talons of the Night Haunter himself His shattered body was recovered from the field of battle and interred in a Dreadnought sarcophagus in honour of his sacrifice.

One of the few remaining Gramaryan recruits still with the main force of the Legion as of the late Great Crusade, with most having been reassigned to chantry houses within the Imperium proper or the oft-overlooked fortifications of Gramarye itself, Knight-sergeant Decur was assigned to the Seeker cadre of the 14."' ## Legion Chronicles

### Origins: the Primordial Strain and the Uncrowned Princes

The Legion's origins were hidden even within the Imperium. The Emperor selected the gene-code of the first Primarch, described as the most stable of his sons, to establish the template for subsequent Space Marines without deliberately emphasising particular inherited characteristics. Research may have begun a century or more before the end of the Unification Wars. Vast numbers of subjects died in the development process; the earliest proto-Astartes, known in surviving archival fragments as the Primordial Strain, scarcely survived surgical testing and early combat. The experiments nonetheless established the procedures and earliest cultural foundations used by the First and its successors. Initial subjects included youths from defeated enemies and purchases from nomadic slave clans, followed by flesh-tithes from incorporated territories. The First therefore drew on many gene-stocks and cultures rather than one dominant recruiting population. Francish and Alban infantry traditions, Anatolic cavalry methods, Skandian berserker practices and numerous other martial cultures were combined in the Legion's early doctrine.

The Unbegotten Father — among the unnamed experimental subjects, a handful served as functional prototypes rather than merely biological test material. Most of these prototypes were derived from the stable strain of the first Primarch. One is named in a surviving record: Abrasax of the fourteenth Ghent intake. An obscure later account of the Siege of the Imperial Palace names an Abraxus Ghent, an unadorned armoured guardian who fought in the rearguard while the Emperor and his Primarchs assaulted Horus' flagship. Neither his death nor inclusion in the Siege rolls is recorded. The resemblance is suggestive, but their identity remains unconfirmed.

The first few hundred warriors were encouraged to abandon old tribal identities, adopting names drawn from surviving legends such as Gilgamesh, Heracles, Tarchon and Hengist. They fought in small groups among the Emperor's armies, earning the title Uncrowned Princes or Crowns. From their overlapping traditions arose informal specialist Hosts, distributed across Companies rather than tied to a single commander. At least eighteen distinct Host heraldic devices appear in early records. At Antioch in 603.M30, nine Hosts were represented across four Companies of fewer than thirty warriors apiece. As the Legions expanded, the First tested formations and tactics that would later underpin the Principia Bellicosa. Some Hosts were superseded or destroyed; the successful ones transmitted their accumulated expertise, strengthening both the Legion and the wider Astartes institution.

### The First Legion in the Unification Wars

The Astartes were designed to replace the unstable Thunder Warriors, who could possess even greater individual physical strength but lacked the restraint and cohesion of a true army. By 668.M30 the First numbered nearly 10,000, while its brother Legions still consisted of only a few hundred each. At Samerkend, with the Emperor at their head and contingents of four other Legions alongside them, 10,000 First Legion warriors faced 200,000 gene-forged Udug Hul, the poisonous-blooded slave-soldiers of the King of Akkad whose strength exceeded that of ten unaugmented men. Within ten hours the enemy was broken, Samerkend was in ruins and its king was dead. Hector Thrane, the newly appointed Grandmaster, was granted the title Sinestra of the Emperor, the Warlord of Terra's left hand. The Emperor ordered the city and almost all records of its horrors destroyed. The victory validated the Astartes as an army and accelerated recruitment of the other Legions.

From Fortress Thirty-one in the Thulean wastes to Karnakon amid the cryo-volcanic mountains of Sedna, the First confronted exceptional horrors, including xenos and phenomena capable of disrupting reality itself. The Emperor granted it access to forbidden weapons in the Imperial Palace armouries. Under Thrane, it obliterated the warped cities of Khadun and Molay and used gene-phage weapons against the Khrave infestation of Enceladus. Details of many such engagements were suppressed. Imperial soldiers who served with the First sometimes never returned, whether killed by the enemy or, according to rumours, silenced by the Legion. The First became associated with the personification of Death, adopting skeletal and funerary imagery and regarding its separation from other troops as an obligation rather than a disgrace.

Branded All As One — a tradition among nomads near Mount Ararat claims that grey-armoured warriors bearing a common mark slaughtered the surviving Thunder Warriors after their final victory. The First Legion's early grey armour, numeral and secret campaigns make it a possible candidate, but no documentary proof survives. At least three later instances of First Legion fleets diverting to hunt renegade Thunder Warriors are cited as circumstantial evidence of a particular interest in their eradication. Neither the identity of the killers at Ararat nor the reasons for that interest are confirmed.

### The Emperor's own Angels of Death

During the buildup to the Great Crusade, the First guarded the outer Solar System for almost a decade, clearing the Oort Cloud, frozen moons and scattered human outposts and watching the heliopause. In those years, the informal networks of specialists developed into the first Orders, preserving tightly focused knowledge in ciphers, rituals and archives. The Legion returned in deep black armour rather than the early grey. At Saturn, the Emperor granted it a fleet of ancient vessels preserved from before Old Night: Gloriana battleships, heavily shielded Promethean cruisers and weapons-laden Tiamat destroyers. Other Legions received only a handful of such relics. The First was entrusted with a whole fleet because its mission demanded exceptional capabilities, not merely as a reward.

While other Expeditionary Fleets brought worlds into Compliance, the First was dispatched against horrors whose continued existence was considered intolerable. At Behtelgeim IV, the 3rd Chapter, principally drawn from the Hosts of Stone and Iron, assaulted a planet hollowed out by a slime-like organism whose infestation had spread across twelve worlds. In the Osiryne Cluster, the 19th Expeditionary Fleet fought a sentient planet-killing machine left behind by an unknown civilisation; Thrane sealed the battle records. The First commonly employed proscribed gene-phage and rad weapons. Its apparent record of Compliance was modest because worlds it eradicated were often excluded from public accounts. Its many successes and failures nevertheless shaped the combat precepts used by all Legions.

### The Sealing of the Black Gate — 810.M30

The Black Gate was a legendary route into the region later called the Eye of Terror, sought by traders and explorers hoping to reach the ruins of the Eldar empire. After numerous expeditions vanished, Zenith IX received a garbled astropathic message announcing the Gate's opening. The Imperial cruiser Lionheart was dispatched with a division of 25,000 Solar Auxilia and returned in less than a month with a badly damaged hull, its captain driven insane and only a few hundred broken survivors. Terra responded with a First Legion force of three Chapters aboard a Gloriana battleship and two heavy Promethean cruisers under Hector Thrane. They continued beyond Zenith rather than evacuate or reinforce its garrison.

At the end of the route, the Legion discovered a rupture in reality from which an enormous mass of living tendrils emerged, anchoring itself to a basalt planetoid studded with ancient structures. Fleet fire proved largely ineffective. The ground assault encountered both aerial creatures and a psychic assault that disrupted the warriors' minds. A quarter of the first hundred dropships failed to reach the surface intact; the 4,000 survivors formed columns under Thrane and the Hosts of Iron and Bone. Even the Fellblades and Falchions could not permanently damage the main tendrils, which regenerated after each attack. The Host of Pentacles became essential to shielding the Legion from the psychic onslaught. Thrane gathered the surviving forces into a single wedge, with Bone warriors at the vanguard, Fire initiates attacking enemy concentrations on the flanks, and Pentacles distributed throughout to hold back the psychic assault. Untrained or overwhelmed psykers became threats to their own brothers, forcing the Order of Bloody Shrouds to intervene. The Legion reached the breach after four hours of fighting, having regrouped with only 2,000 warriors before the final advance.

Idrik Kybalos, Master of the Host of Pentacles, used his remaining adepts to attack the entity's mind directly. Their psychic assault drew its core into realspace, where the trapped cruiser Revenant destroyed itself and the creature through the detonation of its reactor. Only a little over 800 First Legion warriors survived. Kybalos and the remaining Quaesitors sealed the rupture, at further cost to their ranks. Thrane declared the system and its surroundings forbidden, officially warning of contamination by a powerful gene-phage. Zenith station's wreckage bears signs of such a plague, although no record establishes its actual cause. The engagement demonstrated both the extraordinary value and the risks of battlefield psykers before the Librarius, prompting the subsequent dissolution of the Host of Pentacles. It also illustrates the First's ability to transfer authority between specialists and combine multiple doctrines under extreme conditions.

### A Fortress of Pride: Canis-Balor and the death of Hector Thrane

The First was initially the largest and most heavily armed Legion, with the Grandmaster occupying an influential position in the Imperial Court. Its pride gradually became a strategic liability. It preferred opponents capable of testing its prowess and left lesser conquests to following armies. Hosts and Orders grew increasingly jealous of their own doctrines, traditional recruitment enclaves received preference, and the once-fluid Hexagrammaton became rigid. The success of the younger Legions intensified resentment: the Ultramarines surpassed its manpower, while the Imperial Fists possessed powerful relics such as the Phalanx. At Canis-Balor, an unknown xenos species, unrecorded by the Orders of Extinction and Annihilation, defeated repeated assaults by an overconfident small First Legion force. Thrane led a final attack but could not overcome the enemy's numerical superiority. Recognising his error, he remained with his guard so that valuable forces could be recovered. The planet was then reduced to ashes by orbital nucleonic fire. Records of its inhabitants were sealed, later recovered by the Order of Broken Claws and sealed again. Thrane's death left the Legion's Council divided between rival Hosts and Orders, each convinced its own doctrine could have secured victory.

### The Ill-made Knight: Urian Vendraig and the quest for renown

The conflict over succession paralysed the Great Hall of Council at Gramarye. Malcador the Sigillite broke the deadlock by sponsoring a warrior outside the senior Host and Order leadership: Urian Vendraig, a Terran veteran and former captain of the 14th Company, 8th Chapter. Uninitiated into the inner mysteries of any one faction, Vendraig commanded unanimous support. He sought to restore unity and public prominence, admitting a small group of remembrancers to his entourage. His first great opportunity was the Rangda, encountered by the Vth Legion's 105th Pioneer Company on the northern galactic rim. Vendraig destroyed their outpost system of Advex-mors in 839.M30, proving the First's formidable power but failing to cure its desire for recognition. One account places the four-month campaign's First Legion losses at around 5,000; another gives around 10,000 and the loss of fifty ships of the line. The loss estimates differ.

Vendraig pursued further victories, increasingly reckless in execution, until the First arrived at Karkasarn, a fortress-world that Guilliman had been besieging for almost a month. Rejecting the Ultramarines' methodical strategy, 10,000 warriors of the First stormed its enormous defences, suffering heavy losses but reaching the central citadel ahead of the XIIIth. A concealed atomic mine killed Vendraig and his Host of Death lifeguard. Guilliman refused to celebrate a victory purchased at such unnecessary expense, praising neither the Legion's judgement nor its methods. The First abandoned the world to the Ultramarines and removed its name from its own rolls of honour, retaining only an empty laurel. The Council of Masters again took control. The 9th and 14th Chapters seized Melnocch's coral citadels from the Fra'al at the cost of a tenth of their strength, seeking to outpace the Luna Wolves; on Vorsingun, 1,000 Host of Iron initiates from eleven Companies, crewing more than 400 war engines, engaged an Ork force three times their size ahead of the main assault. Such contests exhausted the First without restoring its old supremacy. Its dispersed formations continued to refine their skills but were in danger of destroying themselves in the pursuit of pride. Caliban's discovery by a White Scars fleet offered a new beginning.

### The Rangdan Campaigns — three wars at the edge of the Imperium

The Rangdan wars were among the most dangerous conflicts of the Great Crusade. Surviving records describe a highly intelligent, physically powerful xenos civilisation with advanced technology, vast slave armies and ambitions of domination. Its precise nature is obscured by censorship. More than 80,000 Astartes and uncounted millions of Imperial Army personnel reportedly died across the wars. The First Legion was prominent in suppressing records of the Rangda and the extent to which the Imperium had been threatened.

The first campaign began with Advex-mors in 839.M30, an isolated outpost rather than the heart of Rangdan civilisation. The second began in 862.M30, when thousands of vessels and more than a dozen war-moons struck the northern Imperium, defeating garrisons and enslaving colonists. The Vth and XIXth Legions held the enemy at the Forge World of Xana for eight months, losing 3,000 Astartes and hundreds of thousands of Mechanicum thralls before the Dark Angels and Death Guard broke the siege. Nineteen inhabited systems were devastated; exclusion orders were placed beyond Endyris and Morox. Nine Legions participated, and more than 300,000 Space Marines were deployed at the climax on Taxal. Three Primarchs are known to have led forces against the Rangda, with the Lion generally considered the principal commander. The last recorded battle of the second campaign is dated 882.M30, a chance encounter with a battered enemy fleet. Irrecoverable worlds were abandoned and veterans silenced or sworn to secrecy.

The final Rangdan war, called the Rangdan Xenocide, followed the discovery of surviving nests by White Scars after the exclusion edict was lifted in 887.M30. Under the Divisio Militaris, the Dark Angels and Space Wolves conducted a systematic extermination of the Rangda on their home worlds, completing the campaign in roughly a year. The world of Rangda was reduced to plains of fused glass; a First Legion chantry house of the Order of Broken Claws remained to guard the last codices describing the enemy. No surviving Rangdan are known to have returned, though the guardians continued their vigil. The wars ended, while the possibility of Rangdan survival beyond the galaxy remains unresolved.

#### The Battle of Advex-mors — 839.M30

Much of the detailed information about the First's initial encounter with the Rangda survives in records recovered from Xana during the Scouring. Urian Vendraig assembled around 50,000 First Legion warriors and 100,000 support troops from Imperial Army and Mechanicum Taghmata, with nearly a hundred capital ships and many times as many escorts. The leading squadron included three Gloriana battleships: Invincible Reason, Paradigm of Hate and Truth's Razor. The six-world system appeared heavily fortified, but was later found to be only a Rangdan outpost. Its slave populations were controlled through neural collars, while the xenos themselves fielded advanced energy weapons, including electromagnetic shadow blasters that caused severe radiation sickness among ship crews. The most dangerous asset was an artificial war-moon over Advex-mors Primus, with firepower comparable to a fleet and armour resistant to capital-ship weapons.

The invasion began on Advex-mors Extremis, a minor liquid-hydrogen refining world. Vendraig led the 8th Chapter; 1,000 Astartes defeated 10,000 enslaved soldiers in three hours at a cost of fewer than 200 casualties, and the Grandmaster killed their Rangdan overseer. Over the next months four outer worlds were conquered, their isolated slave garrisons destroyed in turn, while the Legion examined Rangdan weapons and tactics. The main battle at Primus was different. The Rangda had gathered their strength behind shielded fortifications and around the war-moon, using their ships in a defensive constellation rather than advancing blindly. A plan to attack both planet and moon was disrupted by the severity of the void battle. The battleship Paradigm of Hate, already badly damaged and infested by enemy drones, sacrificed itself by striking the war-moon; its crew and embarked warriors held off the Rangda until the reactor detonated, crippling the enormous construct. Invincible Reason and Truth's Razor then opened the way for the landings on Primus, abandoning the effort to capture the moon intact.

The first mobile raiding forces, led by the Order of Crowns and Marshals of Wind and Fire, gathered vital intelligence at considerable cost. Vendraig then committed nearly 30,000 combat-capable Astartes against the enemy's main force. The Host of Blades and Iron initially screened the advance, with veteran Crowns and Bone formations forming a mobile reserve; Wind detachments on jetbikes and speeders provided reconnaissance and harassment. The true Rangdan warriors proved formidable opponents, combining physical strength with energy shielding and advanced weapons, including a crystalline blade bearing a potent neurotoxin. Vendraig fought and killed the Rangdan warmaster in an hour-long duel despite serious wounds and poisoning. With the enemy's leadership defeated and orbital control secured, the First reduced the surviving fortresses and sent Breacher and Destroyer cadres to clear the war-moon. The detailed battle account records around 10,000 First Legion dead and fifty ships of the line destroyed, including Paradigm of Hate; the shorter historical account instead states approximately 5,000 Legion losses. The victory was a declaration of the First's determination to pursue annihilation regardless of cost, but the wars that followed proved how little of the Rangdan empire had yet been defeated.

### The Son of the Forest: Lion El'Jonson and Caliban

The Lion disappeared with the other Primarchs from the Emperor's laboratories before the Great Crusade. More than 150 years later, Knights of the Order found him in Caliban's forests, where monstrous chimaeric beasts, possibly weapons left from the Age of Strife, threatened the remaining human settlements. How long he lived alone is unknown; local tales of a mysterious forest spirit predate his discovery by almost a century, but these are not verified biographical records. His childhood taught him caution, self-reliance, ruthless determination and an absolute conception of victory. Luther persuaded the knights to spare him, named him Lion El'Jonson, the Son of the Forest, and taught him the laws and traditions of the Order. The Lion rapidly became an outstanding warrior, strategist and leader, abolishing restrictions that reserved knighthood for the nobility. Recruitment expanded despite opposition from traditionalists.

Within a decade the Order was able to go on the offensive against Caliban's beasts. Lion and Luther led a long campaign whose heavy cost did not deter the Lion from his objective of total eradication. He refused compromises with either the beasts or the rebels who resisted his changes, destroying their strongholds and armies. After victory, he was acclaimed Grand Master of the Order. The Lion regarded titles and acclaim with indifference and failed to appreciate the jealousy his rise awakened in Luther, formerly his equal and mentor. The Emperor arrived shortly thereafter, claimed his lost son and granted him command of the First Legion. The Lion renamed it the Dark Angels, drawing on Calibanite legend, and departed to continue the same kind of war on a galactic scale.

### A Blade Reforged: the return of the Primarch

Only 500 warriors, mostly Host of Death veterans, accompanied the Emperor to Caliban because the rest of the First was scattered. Their black armour and funerary insignia resembled the figures of Calibanite legend. The Lion personally tested their captain in a duel, matching Calibanite steel against a power-field-wreathed blade and leaving his Cataphractii-armoured opponent wounded. Caliban was incorporated into the Imperium and began providing recruits; Gramarye's Council of Masters responded with mixed shame, pride and a desire to prove its worth. The Emperor instructed the Lion on Terra about the war and the particular role intended for him. El'Jonson accepted his function as a destroyer of the enemies of Mankind, largely uninterested in the rivalries or ambitions of his brothers. He was a formidable swordsman and strategist, able to challenge Fulgrim and Guilliman in their own fields, although his distinction lay in his singular determination rather than supremacy in every individual skill.

The Lion combined Terran Hexagrammaton traditions with the knightly practices of Caliban. While the first young Calibanite recruits underwent gene-seed implantation, hypnagogic indoctrination and combat training, he began gathering the Legion's scattered Companies with an initial force of 20,000, perhaps a third of its strength, including the original 500, other Chapters, Calibanite Imperial Army auxilia and a small retinue of Xana Tech-Magi. Xana's adepts assisted him in locating dispersed detachments through Divisio Militaris records. He tested each Company in combat and earned loyalty through personal action rather than speeches. Within a few years he had assembled nearly 100,000 warriors at Gramarye. There he defeated the Council's champion, Pyrrhus Calagat, Master of the Host of Fire, in an hour-long duel and accepted the offices of Grandmaster of the First Legion, Grandmaster of the six Wings of the Hexagrammaton and High Preceptor of the Orders Militant. He had united the Legion's overlapping authorities in one person. His oath bound the Legion to victory and the protection of the Imperium regardless of sacrifice.

### A Duty of Sacrifice and Shadows: the reborn Dark Angels

The Lion appointed Wing Masters and formalised the existing Orders in the fashion of Caliban's knightly fraternities. The first influx of older Calibanite companions included warriors who had undergone dangerous augmentation rather than conventional Astartes implantation. Positions were awarded by merit, not seniority or origin; some Terran veterans resented having to yield authority to relative newcomers. The old grand chantry at Gramarye was replaced with a more modest fortress protecting its industry, while Caliban remained a major stronghold. The true centre of authority became the ancient Gloriana flagship Invincible Reason. To establish the new Legion's purpose through deeds, the Lion responded to a plea from Karkasarn, where Praetor Artareon of the XIIIth and his garrison had endured eight months of siege after rebels released a biogenic phage that turned inhabitants into ravenous flesh-ghola. Leading 1,000 Dreadwing veterans and backed by the full 10,000 warriors of his fleet, the Lion destroyed the enemy. He demanded neither the world's surrender to the First nor repayment from the Ultramarines, leaving an empty banner as the only mark of the old debt discharged.

The renewed Rangdan war subjected the rebuilt Legion to severe losses. Recorded battles include Vorksag, the void battles over Morcar and the seven-week battle of Morro, where three Companies held against more than a million neuro-shackled servitors. The Space Wolves lost approximately 5,000 in breaking Xana's siege, and the First was reduced to a tenth of its former number by the time victory was proclaimed. Some attributed the Terran veterans' sacrifices to their desire to prove themselves worthy of the Lion or correct their earlier failure against the Rangda; others alleged the Lion deliberately expended them to replace them with Calibanites, an unconfirmed rumour. The subsequent expansion of true Calibanite Astartes across the Wings and Orders created a Legion shaped by knightly ritual and less burdened by the old Terran rivalry. Its fleets dispersed once again, guarding the dark frontiers rather than building extensive territorial holdings.

### The Barbarian and the Knight — Dulan and the Space Wolves

The battle at Dulan is usually remembered for the Lion and Leman Russ' legendary duel after the Dark Angels seized the final victory while the Space Wolves were still besieging the world. The two Legions fought together in numerous campaigns before and after Dulan, often with shared camaraderie. Both Primarchs valued directness, loyalty and deeds over ceremony, and both treated oaths as binding. Their cooperation during the Rangdan wars and their reunion towards the end of the Heresy are stronger evidence of their relationship than later tales of an unending feud. The duel is accepted as historical; the enduring rancour often attributed to it is treated as doubtful. Dulan is therefore interpreted as a test of two proud warriors rather than the origin of irreconcilable hostility.

### Sarosh, Luther and the coming of the Heresy

At the request of Jaghatai Khan, with whom the Lion shared a mutual respect for plain dealing, the Dark Angels took charge of Sarosh. The world had accepted Imperial rule when confronted by the White Scars but resisted its terms, and attempted to kill the Lion with a hidden nucleonic charge. Although the plot failed and rebellion was crushed, the ease with which the Saroshi penetrated the Legion's defences raised doubts over the commitment of certain veterans. Luther and others from both Terra and Caliban were sent back to serve as the home world's garrison and overseers. Their removal was not officially exile, but it separated them from the Great Crusade. The Lion prioritised duty and excellence above personal attachment.

Luther's ultimate fate and the later catastrophe on Caliban remain deliberately obscured. His Order of the Black Key, ostensibly founded to foster cooperation with other Legions, particularly the Sons of Horus, and the unusual disposition of First Legion forces leaving Caliban isolated towards the end of the Heresy are presented as possible signs of a larger conspiracy. The later withdrawal of the Dark Angels, disappearance of many warriors and quarantine of their home system suggest an internal betrayal; Luther's exact role and the extent of Horus' influence remain unconfirmed. No definitive account of the Fall of Caliban is supplied here.

As the Great Crusade continued, the Lion and his Legion withdrew from its ceremonial fellowship and became increasingly absorbed in their assigned campaigns. Horus had attempted to cultivate them, but their Orders and Wings rejected his warrior lodges and their pride resisted outside influence. The Lion possessed the Emperor's confidence and access to Terra's hidden arsenals. Horus ensured that the First was diverted from Ullanor and that the Lion was absent from the Triumph. The Lion made no public response to Horus' appointment as Warmaster. Horus eventually sought to neutralise three Legions: the White Scars, whom he hoped to recruit; the Blood Angels, whom he hoped to destroy; and the Dark Angels, whom he sought to send so far away that his rebellion would be complete before their return. The First's combination of numbers, expertise, forbidden technology and loyalty made it an especially dangerous opponent.

### The Siege of Mykana — the Burning Veil of Laconis

Late in the Great Crusade, Commander Paitux of the Emperor's Children's 28th Millennial assaulted Mykana's last city, Laconis, expecting a straightforward victory against its techno-feudal defenders. The local warriors used combat drugs that increased strength and allowed them to fight through mortal wounds. Their techno-shaman activated an ancient reactor whose energy field, the Burning Veil, incinerated anything crossing its perimeter. Paitux's force was trapped outside the city, reduced to less than half its strength and nearly out of ammunition. His repeated counter-attacks kept a landing zone open while waiting for reinforcements. Instead of the IIIrd Legion, a full Dark Angels Chapter arrived under the Lion, using Dreadwing Fire Raptors and assault troops to break the enemy forces outside the walls. The Burning Veil nonetheless resisted even orbital bombardment.

The solution came from Ironwing Lieutenant Iksal Mordwen, who established that a sufficiently dense energy beam could temporarily disrupt a small section of the shield. An ancient graviton cannon from Invincible Reason opened the way for Ravenwing volunteers mounted on jetbikes and speeders, led by Mordwen. The Dark Angels of the Shattered Mantle gathered outside under the Lion, while the infiltrators fought their way to the central spire against overwhelming numbers. Its destruction after a little over an hour allowed the main assault to begin. Mordwen and a few dozen survivors were stranded amid the enemy, whose final combat-drug overdose had driven them to suicidal resistance. Paitux commandeered Storm Eagles and led his own survivors to their aid. The two Legions held the ruined spire for another three hours, eventually fighting with blades and bayonets after exhausting ammunition. The Dark Angels relieved them, completed the conquest and departed without claiming honours, leaving the Emperor's Children to record the world's Compliance. Mordwen explained to Paitux that being entrusted with the battlefield by the Lion was itself exceptional praise. The shield generator was never recovered; the theory that the Dark Angels secretly secured it remains speculation. This campaign illustrates how Ironwing technical expertise, Ravenwing mobility, Dreadwing air support and the main assault force could combine to defeat a specialised obstacle.

### The Pride of Conquerors — the Sons of Horus raid on Rangda, 006.M31

Although conventional histories place the First Legion's first intervention in the Heresy at Thramas, records identify an earlier engagement in 006.M31, shortly after Isstvan III. Rangda was guarded by around 1,500 warriors of the 51st Chapter, the Sable Griffons, and the chantry station of the Order of the Broken Claw. Much of the garrison was away on patrol. The Sons of Horus heavy cruiser Conqueror's Pride arrived using legitimate identification and security ciphers under the pretext of resupply, then attacked after ignoring Knight-praetor Kordray Mordred's demands for contact. The raiding force numbered nearly a full Chapter, including a Company of Justaerin Terminators. The target was the Order's chapter house and the dangerous Rangdan artefacts held in stasis in its vaults, rather than ordinary military installations.

Only twelve members of the Order defended the chapter house, whose concealed defences isolated nearly fifty Justaerin within its corridors. The Cenobites inflicted severe losses on the attackers, and three surviving defenders withdrew into the vaults. Successive Sons of Horus champion groups attempted to overcome them in close combat, with the last remaining Cenobite finally killed by Destroyers using phosphex. The raiders seized selected Rangdan weapons and devices, but the surviving Dark Angels retook the astropathic nexus and summoned their patrol forces. Mordred and his Deathwing lifeguard captured a grounded Stormbird and recovered its cargo of relics; Ravenwing Xiphon Interceptors destroyed another transport. The ancient Promethean cruiser Invidious returned, forcing Conqueror's Pride to flee rather than risk open battle. Despite achieving part of their objective, the Sons of Horus lost several hundred warriors, irreplaceable transports and some of the relics, while inflicting relatively few First Legion casualties. Their attack transformed the Dark Angels' opposition from a matter of loyalty and duty into a demand for retribution. The episode provides a direct example of Order specialists, Deathwing guardians, Ravenwing aviation and the Legion's relic fleet working within the same theatre.

### Thramas Crusade

- Horus instructed Konrad Curze and the Night Lords to devastate the Thramas Sector and draw the Dark Angels into a prolonged conflict, reducing the possibility of the First Legion reinforcing Terra. Horus regarded Lion El'Jonson's Legion as a dangerous opponent.

#### Campaign account

[Thramas Crusade — full campaign digest](../common_info/thramas_crusade.md).

##### The Diamat Incursion and the Lion's Return

Horus had dispatched the Dark Angels to conquer the distant Shield Worlds, an enclave of humanity in the darkness between galaxies associated with an unknown xenos species. This assignment kept Lion El'Jonson away from the Imperium during the opening stages of the rebellion. When reports of unrest reached him, the Lion detached fifteen of his fastest capital ships and a force of veteran warriors, making for Tanagra. Its position at the junction of stable Warp corridors offered routes into Imperial space, while the Forge World of Diamat promised supplies and intelligence. Instead, the Dark Angels found that Diamat had already sworn allegiance to Horus. After pacifying the forge, the Lion discovered records confirming that the Warmaster was directing a rebellion long in preparation: within Diamat's vaults lay enormous Ordinatus engines of an unfamiliar design, incorporating technology forbidden by Mars and secretly commissioned by Horus some fifty years earlier.

Perturabo met the Lion in the ruins of Diamat, pretending that the Iron Warriors remained loyal. He informed his brother of the Isstvan massacres and Curze's offensive in the Eastern Fringe, but withheld the full extent of the betrayal. Acting on Horus' instructions, Perturabo steered the First Legion towards the Night Lords, ensuring that it remained occupied far from Terra. The Lion also entrusted the recovered weapons of Diamat to the Iron Warriors, through whom they would ultimately reach the Warmaster. Departing for Triplex, El'Jonson summoned the remaining elements of his Legion from beyond the Imperium's borders, intending to reach the Forge Worlds before Curze.

The black warships entered Triplex and destroyed the Mechanicum defence vessels and transports bearing the Eye of Horus with weapons of extraordinary range. A Night Lords squadron of heavy cruisers and frigates recognised the heraldry of the newcomers and engaged with unusual hesitation. The leading cruiser, Shadow of Justice, was crippled, and the rest of the squadron withdrew. Dreadwing warriors boarded the vessel and exterminated its defenders with phosphex and bio-phages, preserving the bridge for the Lion himself. El'Jonson overcame the cruiser’s praetor, who had sacrificed his own warriors in an unsuccessful attempt to strike the Primarch from behind. Rather than kill him, the Lion took him aboard the Invincible Reason for interrogation, seeking intelligence on the actions of Horus and Curze. His return marked the end of the First Legion's enforced absence from the civil war.

##### The Fall of Triplex

The initial Dark Angels formation consisted of eighteen capital ships and no more than one hundred frigates and lesser vessels. Around Galatia, the Mechanicum assembled over one hundred gunships and defence barges, the remnants of the Night Lords squadron and vessels recently completed in its orbital shipyards. The defenders hesitated to attack while the Lion's interrogators extracted intelligence from their captive and additional First Legion formations arrived from the Warp. Nearly one hundred Dark Angels capital ships ultimately converged on the system. Despite being outnumbered approximately two to one, they shattered the assembled Traitor fleet with their superior relic weaponry. The Night Lords abandoned Galatia's defence and concentrated their efforts on crippling the battleship Undying Will, losing two ships in the process. Three surviving cruisers and their escorts broke away into the Warp, leaving the Mechanicum vessels to be destroyed. Within hours, the First Legion controlled Galatia's orbit.

The Magi of Galatia relied upon their surface fortifications, believing the valuable industrial infrastructure would deter indiscriminate orbital bombardment and that they could withstand a siege until reinforcements arrived from Phall. The Lion instead committed the Deathwing to a direct assault upon the principal forge-fane. Many landing craft and hundreds of veterans were lost to the volkite and photon defences, but the surviving Deathwing established a beachhead against the Galatian Thallax and tech-thrall custodian regiments. The second wave, led by the Dreadwing and Eskaton Marduk Sedras of the Order of the Shattered Sceptre, employed Interemptors and Terminator-armoured Naufragia to devastate the sacred forge halls and force the Mechanicum to commit its reserves. Galatia and Phall had long conducted secret research into forbidden technology and dark theorems. Faced with destruction, the Galatian Magi unleashed towering automata armed with reality-distorting weapons and raw Warp energy, alongside swarms of Harpax artificia whose collective intelligence increased with their numbers. The Mechanicum's cybertheurgic arts neutralised much of the Dark Angels' heavy armour and forced the Dreadwing onto the defensive.

##### The Ikaros Contingency and the Excindio

The Mechanicum was indispensable to the Great Crusade, but its independent religious doctrine, technological resources and military power also made it a potentially existential threat to the Emperor's Imperium. To guard against rebellion, the Emperor established secret contingencies, among them the Ikaros Contingency, entrusted solely to Lion El'Jonson and the First Legion. Its first clause authorised the Dark Angels to retain prohibited relics from Old Night, including technologies capable of resisting the Mechanicum's arts. Its second permitted the Legion to undertake pre-emptive military action against Mechanicum elements judged to be acting against the interests of the Imperium. This second authority is recorded as having been invoked eight times in Imperial history; the encounter at Triplex was, at that point, the only openly acknowledged instance. Other details remained sealed in the Dark Angels' vaults, and the Lion would exercise his authority again before the Heresy ended.

On Galatia, El'Jonson invoked the contingency and ordered the release of twelve Excindio from the deepest stasis vaults of the Invincible Reason. Delivered to the surface within a detached section of the ship's hull, these immense humanoid constructs were surviving examples of the silica animus that had once ravaged humanity during the Golden Age. Mutilated and bound to the Lion's service, they carried weapons outlawed by the Emperor himself. Immune to the Mechanicum's cybertheurgy, the Excindio tore through the creations of the fallen Magi, while the Lion sought Galatia's commander. He confronted an immense multi-legged construct guarding the archmagi's sealed salvation-vaults. The precise details of the ensuing confrontation are not recorded, but when the Dark Angels returned to the battlefield at nightfall, El'Jonson stood alone amidst the wreckage and the dormant forms of seven surviving Excindio, their power reserves exhausted. The Dreadwing uncovered the vaults, and Sedras oversaw the destruction of the surviving Galatian leadership and forbidden research. Over the following weeks, the First Legion systematically eliminated the remaining hostile Tech-Priests and automata and demolished the forge's industrial infrastructure, preserving only the orbital shipyards for Imperial use.

Phall, the largest and last intact Forge World of Triplex, had gathered the surviving military assets of the system, but the fate of Galatia divided its Magi. Some fled, while the more orthodox elements overthrew their renegade brethren and offered the Lion unconditional surrender before his fleet arrived. Recognising the strategic value of Phall's industry, El'Jonson accepted their submission rather than destroy another forge. He seized the surviving forbidden research and installed Sedras with a Dreadwing garrison to administer Phall and supply the First Legion's coming campaign. Horus had thus lost the principal industrial prize of his eastern offensive, while the Dark Angels acquired a vital base of their own.

##### The Forbidden Battle: Perditus and Tuchulcha

The Lion's expedition had taken him to the mysterious system known as Perditus, meaning Forbidden. Of the 30,000 Dark Angels who departed with him, considerably fewer returned. The identity of his opponents and the precise purpose of the expedition remained secret; rumours suggested that another Legion had been present, but the account does not identify or confirm it. What is certain is that the Lion recovered an object that the First Legion called the Gatekeeper or the Key. It enabled the Dark Angels to circumvent the Warp storms that had isolated much of the Eastern Fringe and restricted their ability to pursue the Night Lords. The Lion regarded it as a necessary evil, a temporary instrument to be used as circumstances required and subsequently set aside. One record preserved in the guarded archives of Terra gives it the name Tuchulcha, associates it with the Deadly Seas and categorises it as a daemon. The precise nature of the object and the events of its recovery are not explained in the supplied account.

##### Desperation Cuts Keenly: The Battle of Sheol IX

With the Key in his possession, the Lion was able to strike at the Night Lords with unprecedented speed. With Horus pressing towards Terra and alarming reports emerging from Ultramar, the First Legion could not afford several more years of fighting in the east. The Night Lords operated in dispersed raiding groups, making a single decisive battle difficult to arrange. Recovered data from ships destroyed over Thramas revealed their hidden anchorage near Sheol IX, in a system long abandoned by the Loyalists. The Lion first concentrated his fleet against isolated Traitor formations, scattering eight flotillas over the following months and forcing the Night Lords to withdraw from open combat. He then supplied the lords of Gulgorahd with the location of surviving elements of Legio Victorum, anticipating that their ancient hatred would draw them into an immediate offensive. Their deployment opened an apparent weakness in the Loyalist line, giving the Night Lords an incentive to gather for another attack. The Lion accepted the likely sacrifice of Gulgorahd's forces as the price of securing the confrontation he needed.

Using his new means of Warp travel, the Lion brought approximately 300 capital ships and twice as many smaller craft into the Sheol system, appearing less than one astronomical unit from its outer planets in formation around the Invincible Reason. The Night Lords possessed perhaps 200 capital ships and three times that number of lighter craft, but many remained at anchor over Sheol IX with the bulk of their Legionaries still on the surface. The relic warships of the First Legion enjoyed superior accuracy and effective range; their sudden arrival overwhelmed the VIII Legion's initial defence. Some Traitor ships fled while others attempted to organise resistance or evacuate their stranded brethren. Of the more than 100 capital ships that advanced against the Dark Angels, barely 50 broke through their lines, with many crippled vessels subsequently captured as prizes. The Lion declined to pursue the fugitives and instead concentrated against those that had remained to rescue the warriors on Sheol IX, including the Gloriana-class Nightfall and the heavy cruisers Shroud of Eventide and Feral Heart.

Curze himself was still on the surface, where the Night Lords attempted to protect their remaining embarkation points and destroy armour and supplies that could not be recovered. The battle became a desperate struggle both in orbit and on the planet. In two hours the VIII Legion suffered losses approaching a quarter of its remaining strength. The Lion confronted the Night Haunter once again, defeating him in a brutal clash. The surviving Night Lords committed their reserves to save their Primarch, and a full company sacrificed itself to cover the evacuation of the unconscious Curze to the Excoriator. The Nightfall, meanwhile, remained under attack from the surrounding First Legion ships and their Firewing boarding forces. Escape became possible only when the badly damaged Feral Heart destroyed itself by detonating its Warp core, tearing apart nearby ships and opening a breach in the Loyalist encirclement. The Nightfall forced its way through, followed by surviving vessels. The Absolute and Crown of Ashes remained behind to cover the retreat and were destroyed; three more ships were lost to catastrophic Warp-drive failures as the fleet escaped. The Dark Angels remained to destroy the Traitor forces left on Sheol IX. Amid the ruins, the Lion continued to brood on Curze's accusation that the Primarchs were all dangerous weapons whose deeds would be judged by history.

##### A Legion Rent Asunder

After more than three years of war, Sheol IX marked the effective end of the Thramas Crusade and the collapse of the Night Lords as the Legion they had once been. Their fleet had been reduced to a shadow of its former size, and Curze's condition was so grave that some of his warriors feared he would never recover, while others hoped that he would not. With his authority absent, numerous warlords saw little reason to continue the Warmaster's rebellion instead of pursuing their own ambitions. To prevent the complete dissolution of the VIII Legion into independent bands of pirates and reavers, Sevatar and the Atramentar fought a brief internal war, established a new ruling council and gathered the remaining loyal elements at the star of Qetesh. Those unwilling to accept the new order were allowed to disappear into the Thramas sector.

The Night Lords gathered their surviving ships from Yrrkesh, Taur and other rally points, only to be surprised once again by a vastly superior Dark Angels fleet. As the Traitors attempted to escape, Curze awoke aboard the Excoriator and ordered a final, futile boarding assault against the Invincible Reason. The account places him alongside Sevatar and the Atramentar in battle aboard the Lion's flagship, after which he was counted among the missing. Of the more than 100,000 warriors of the VIII Legion who had entered the crusade, fewer than 60,000 are recorded as escaping the ambush at Sheol IX. These events marked the end of the old VIII Legion, an army that had helped win the Great Crusade before losing the constraints that once gave its methods purpose.

##### A Resolve Tarnished

The Dark Angels had suffered far fewer casualties, with the account placing their combat dead at no more than 10,000, but the price of victory was measured in the destruction of the empire they had sought to preserve. Hundreds of worlds had been abandoned or sacrificed, and entire populations had been destroyed to prevent their exploitation by the Traitors. The Lion had long accepted the sacrifice of individual worlds for the survival of the Imperium, yet the scale of the Thramas Crusade made that principle increasingly difficult to distinguish from the methods of his fallen brother. Curze's accusations troubled him more deeply than physical injury or the judgement of others. The Lion believed that history would vindicate his loyalty and sacrifices, but he could not easily dismiss the possibility that the final stages of the war had been driven as much by hatred of Curze as by strategic necessity. The chronicler contrasts this willingness to sacrifice worlds with the compassion of Sanguinius and Guilliman's concern for productive civilisation.

With Thramas ruined and the Night Lords scattered, the Lion regarded further regret as futile. The First Legion had emerged battered but unbroken, and he resolved to continue the war in the Emperor's name. His ships did not turn towards distant Terra, still concealed by the Warp storms, but towards Ultramar, following disturbing reports received from Tigrus concerning the devastation wrought by the World Eaters and Word Bearers and the raising of a new banner amid the ruins of the Imperium. The Dark Angels departed in full battle order, prepared to confront another possible rebellion. Their destination was Imperium Secundus.

### Xana Incursion and Pale Stars [Horus Heresy]

The operation was supported by the Dark Angels aboard the Dark Sovereign, who attacked Xana's fleet and deployed Terminators. The attackers also unleashed preserved remains of fallen Iron Hands, Raven Guard, Salamanders, Imperial Fists and White Scars, which inexplicably rose to fight again. The nature of this phenomenon remains unknown.

Haar's forces escaped, but the raid failed to capture its intended prizes and inflicted enormous destruction on Xana.

During the Pale Stars campaign of 008–010.M31, Alpha Legion Strike Master Ijax attempted to conquer the region for Horus but encountered fierce resistance from the Dark Brotherhood, an independent Blackshield warband. Its mysterious leader, the Nemean, was rumoured to be a Terran veteran of the Dark Angels who had fought in the Third Rangdan Xenocide. The Brotherhood rejected both Horus and the Emperor, conducting raids against Alpha Legion fleets, stealing supplies, and establishing its own dominion. Their conflict devastated several systems, while Ijax deliberately sacrificed local forces to lure the Blackshields into ambushes.

The campaign also attracted the attention of Malcador's Knights-Errant. Nathaniel Garro, formerly of the Death Guard, travelled to the Pale Stars alongside Zaven of the Emperor's Children and Rubio of the Ultramarines. Their mission was to contact the Nemean and potentially recruit his Blackshields into Malcador's service. At the Conclave of Optera, they encountered representatives of other isolated Legions, including Blood Angels survivors stranded far from their Primarch.

At the Conclave of Optera, Nathaniel Garro and his fellow Knights-Errant, Rubio and Zaven, attempted to recruit the Nemean, mysterious leader of the Dark Brotherhood Blackshields, into Malcador's service. The gathering also included Captain Serron of the Blood Angels' 121st Company, whose warriors had been scattered by Warp storms while travelling to Nartaba, and Nirun, a White Scars commander leading approximately 500 Legionaries. Garro revealed the truth of Horus' betrayal at Isstvan III and invited all three leaders to join the Knights-Errant. Serron refused, determined to reunite with his Legion, while Nirun remained committed to Jaghatai Khan's secret orders. The Nemean appeared willing to consider Garro's offer.

Before negotiations could conclude, Alpha Legion forces under Strike Master Ijax launched a surprise assault on Optera. The resulting battle scattered the assembled Astartes, forcing Garro, Rubio, and Zaven to escape by teleportation. Serron's Blood Angels eventually escaped into the Warp towards Segmentum Solar, with rumours placing survivors at the Siege of Terra. Nirun's White Scars fought the Alpha Legion at Phargos Rex before disappearing into a violent Warp storm, their fate unknown. The Nemean survived and apparently joined Malcador's Knights-Errant, with later accounts suggesting that he fought during Terra's final defence.

One particularly unusual incident involved a joint force of Dark Angels, White Scars, and Iron Warriors that infiltrated the fortified palace of Magister-Militaris Karpath in the Viridis Sector and executed him. The Iron Warriors' participation remains unexplained.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Dark Angels fought the Night Lords throughout the Thramas Crusade. Following Lion El'Jonson's victory over Konrad Curze at Tsagualsa, numerous Dark Angels strike forces pursued scattered Traitors across the eastern galaxy, often becoming isolated themselves.

At Nagathar, Dark Angels and Imperial Fists defended approximately one million civilians against a Word Bearers attempt at ritual sacrifice. Dark Angels vessels also participated in the Xana Incursion, including the *Dark Sovereign* and its Terminator forces.

#### Historical uncertainties

The accounts of Advex-mors give differing loss estimates. The terminology and locations associated with the Order of Broken Claw(s) and the Rangdan Xenocides also vary. The second Rangdan war is described as lasting roughly two decades; the Lion's biographical account describes a near-decade of fighting. Exact dates and the relationships between the ancient Hosts and later Wings remain uncertain where records do not establish them.

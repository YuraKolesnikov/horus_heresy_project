# Death Guard
Of all the Space Marine Legions raised by the Emperor as part of his nascent Legiones Astartes, few had beginnings so glorious and yet ends as tragic and nightmarish as would befall the Death Guard. Once a byword for resilience and unshakable courage, whose warriors served the overthrow of alien tyrants and warlords on more than a hundred worlds, and at whose hands scores of xenos races met their destruction, horror was to overtake them. The Death Guard were to become the very thing they once fought so tirelessly against: inhuman, unrecognisable and soulless monsters of the dark-the very stuff of nightmares.

## Legion Structure
Before they were united with their Primarch, the Dusk Raiders conformed very closely  to the standard raiment of arms and organisational patterns, with some bias to heavy assault formations and attritional engagements, as evidenced by the particular use of close-ranged weaponry among its specialised units. Mortarion was at his heart an infantryman, and in his Death Guard he saw the chance to perfect the principle of the utilitarian warrior on foot who could go anywhere, fight on any ground and destroy any enemy through resilience, resolve and implacable aggression. As part of this doctrine squad formations were not largely static as with most Legions but were formed, reformed and ordered as needed. To this end a Death Guard Legionary was trained to be equally adept at any infantry role he might be called on to perform on the battlefield rather than fighting in a particular role, acting as a Tactical Marine in one battle, a support weapons operator in another and so on, with only Techmarines and other highly specialised occupations remaining separate.

At its most fundamental level the Death Guard Legion was organized around the principle of equipping individual Space Marines as well as possible, so that they could endure and prevail against any foe encountered, and operate for extended periods without resupply or support if needed. This was a dogma espoused by Mortarion which was readily embraced by the wider Legion, as it built on the independence and surety with which the Dusk Raiders had always fought.

Bolter, melta and flamer were now the trinity of weapons around which their wargear was based, keeping supply needs to a minimum. Other types of ranged weapons were deployed sparingly only as the tactical situation or nature of the enemy demanded. Each Death Guard also carried a close combat blade as well—broad trench daggers, warscythes and the heavy slashing Kukra of Barbarus, forged of dense black ceramsteel, these brutal and efficient weapons soon became a hallmark of the Legion. This reliance on simple but effective tools of war was an outward manifestation of the cardinal belief held by the Legion that the Space Marine himself was the greatest of all weapons at the Imperium’s disposal—a tireless engine of war before which any enemy would eventually succumb. The Death Guard relied almost exclusively on its infantry to provide its strategic strength, with the bulk of tactical support firepower coming from heavily armed support squads, and latterly with considerable numbers of Terminators and Dreadnoughts—a tradition begun by the Dusk Raiders, providing reinforcement and assault spearheads where needed. This focus was also one of the reasons the Death Guard Legion developed a particularly admirable record in fighting to clear space hulks of alien infestation and for their ability to destroy fortifications and citadels from within.

While the Death Guard did maintain and utilise field armour, support vehicles and transports (as Mortarion would not allow his forces to be circumvented, cut-off or left wanting for their lack), these were not given any primacy in the Legion’s tactical doctrine. The only exceptions to this were dedicated siege units such as the Vindicator which was fielded in disproportionately high numbers by the Legion, alongside squadrons of Fellblade super-heavies and Land Raider Spartans in mass warfare. It was notable in hindsight—and would prove to be a telling factor on Isstvan III—that a proportionately high number of Death Guard Legion war machine crews were of Terran blood by the time of the Heresy, while the heaviest infantry strike units, particularly the Legion’s sizable core of Terminator armour-equipped shock troops, were of Barbarus stock.

Obedience and order in the ranks of the Death Guard was absolute and expected, and a clear and unbroken chain of command ran like blood in the Legion’s veins. Legionaries were extensions of their sergeants’ will, sergeants their captain’s, and captains their commander’s, and through them all were instruments of Mortarion, body and soul. Few other ranks or divisions of formal organisation existed, except in very specialized, often technical, roles and each member of the Legion knew their place and the seniority and tasks of those around them without recourse to heraldry and symbol. All conformed to Mortarion’s decree without rancour or dissent, and rivalries and internal strife were observed by outsiders to be few, particularly in comparison to more notably fractious Legions such as the Night Lords or Space Wolves. When an officer died in battle, his successor stepped into his place swiftly and decisively without need for orders or discussion, and so the Legion’s chain of command was seamless even under the heaviest losses, contributing to the Death Guard’s reputation for endurance in war.

The overall Legion Mortarion had re-ordered into seven ‘Great Companies’ of considerably larger size than standard Legion organisational structures would mandate—the number seven and its derivations seeming to hold occult and ritual significance both to Mortarion and the culture of his blighted world of adoption. Notionally each company was to hold some 70,000 Space Marines, the desired strength of the Death Guard Legion’s number then some 490,000 Legionaries Astartes—although in reality nothing approaching this number was ever achieved. Attrition and bloodshed, as well as the constraints of time and resources, were to see the Legion never reach this number but the figure remains, if nothing else, a measure of Mortarion's ambition. Elite units, favoured or chosen warriors singled out from the rest, and sub-formations within the Death Guard Legion were also noticeably lacking in comparison to other Legions, with only a few exceptions - the most notable of which were known as the Deathshroud. Singled out by Mortarion they were veteran warriors, chosen for their skill-at-arms, fearlessness and their proven endurance, and were often selected from Death Guard who had survived where their comrades had fallen, often quite literally having been left the 'last man' from an assault force or particularly bloody defence. The Deathshroud formed Mortarion's silent bodyguard, and in peace or war it was said that at least two of their number remained within forty-nine paces of their Primarch at any time.

The XIV Legion came to the Isstvan system with its almost complete strength, the most accurate of which placed it at approximately 95,000 Space Marines… Deployed to the surface of Isstvan III it is believed were a little under a third of the Legion's forces, comprising the principal elements of its 2nd, 5th and 7th Great Companies, as well as a detachment of Legion Fellblades and siege gear. Ironically it was also Mortarion's doctrine of endurance and self-sufficiency, and the Legion's experiences fighting on the deadliest of battlefields that allowed the betrayed Death Guard to respond swiftly and effectively to their plight and danger — for in proportion it appears more of them survived the initial viral bombardment of Isstvan III than any other Legion force stranded there, and it was their dogged resistance and will to fight despite the odds which allowed them to prove such a thorn in the traitors' side.

### Reapers of Corruption

#### The Death Guard — The Reapers of Corruption

Mortarion and the Death Guard also played a significant role in suppressing Warp-related threats, though their methods were considerably more ruthless. Driven by his hatred of sorcery and the supernatural, Mortarion actively sought worlds contaminated by psychic practices or forbidden rituals. Rather than approaching these populations as a liberator, he descended upon them as an executioner, accompanied by the XIV Legion's feared Destroyer formations.

The Death Guard were among the earliest Legions to employ bale flamers and psyk-out or psi-reactive munitions on a large scale. Mortarion regarded Warp corruption as an incurable disease that threatened to spread unless completely eradicated. His campaigns therefore resembled systematic purges, reflecting his conviction that sorcery was a contagion requiring extermination rather than understanding or containment.

## Legion Chronicles

### Unification Wars
The story of the Dusk Raiders begins in the earliest days of the Unification Wars. The base stock for the majority of the initially raised Legions came from Terra, and in the case of the XIV Legion the main bulk of the gene-recruits used were drawn from the ancient and warlike clans of Old Albia. Indeed such was the suitability of this stock for induction to the ranks of the Space Marines, recruits drawn from Old Albia's towering, soot-blackened Castram-cities were also to be found in the ranks of the founding VIII and X Legions, although to a lesser degree than the XIV who were by blood and culture shaped by the traditions of the warlords of Albia as well as the hand of the Emperor. By taking its brightest and best into the Legions and the other growing Imperial organisations marked for war amid the stars, the Emperor ensured they could never threaten unification from within. 

The warlord clans of Old Albia did not readily bend the knee to the Emperor however, having by then finally thrown off the tyranny of the last descendants of the Unspeakable King they refused to have another master in their place. Instead the Albians met the Emperor's Thunder Regiments with their own battalions of steam-belching proto-dreadnoughts and armoured Ironside soldiers. In battle after battle the forces of Unification were held in check although at shattering cost for the Albians, who would not give in despite the onslaught. Admiring the martial temper and indomitable courage of the foe, the Emperor called a ceasefire and sought victory over the warlord clans by negotiation, knowing that to crush them by force alone would entail a grinding bloodbath that would only serve to decimate his own forces and gain him nothing but a handful of ashes as a prize of war. Thus it was that despite the contrary advice of his human counsellors and generals, the Emperor pursued peace. Going before them, unarmed and clothed in white and crimson, the Emperor spoke to the warlords' parliament and laid before them his vision of a future Mankind reunited and ascendant, of tyrants toppled and nightmares slain. He offered them glory among the stars and most importantly redemption for many hundreds of years of kin-slaying and bloodshed. The warlords of Old Albia - to the shock of many-accepted. Albian clans became among the Unification's most zealous supporters, although still untrusted by many, lending their warriors and war machines to the Emperor's cause.

Operating in the role of heavy infantry, they were experts at survival and endurance, and quickly gained a reputation among the other newly-forged Legions as relentless and disciplined fighters. In defence they were stubborn and indefatigable — able to stand unwaveringly against the heaviest fire and hold their position against all comers to the last living body and bolt shell if needed. In attack they systematically destroyed a given target, crashing upon an enemy in wave after wave of armoured bodies, excelling in close range fire-fights and bloody attrition. Their grey and unadorned power armour began to carry the symbols of rank and decoration, now modified that once formed the armorial imagery of the Ironsides of Old Albia, and most tellingly their right vambraces, gauntlets and shoulder plates were painted the deep crimson of drying blood, now symbolising the red right hand of the Emperor's justice where once it had proclaimed the murderous reach of the Unspeakable King.

### Early Great Crusade [798.M30 - 854.M30]
As the XIV Legion fought in the last battles of Terran unification and the first battles of the Great Crusade off-world, they were given the epithet of ‘Dusk Raiders' by those they fought and took it for their own. This name was a consequence of their use of the ancient Albian tactic of conducting major ground attacks at fall of night when the shift of light confused an enemy's watch, and gathering darkness would shadow an advance across open ground. As relentless as they were in attack, the Dusk Raiders were known to be honourable opponents as well, who would hold to bargains struck for early surrender and honour symbols of truce. So matters continued, and for more than eight decades the Dusk Raiders fought on unwavering in the service of the Great Crusade. Still fatherless during this time as other Legions gained their Primarchs until only a handful remained to be discovered, this 'orphan' status further bred a self-reliance and a quiet, stubborn pride in the Dusk Raiders' self-forged character, and the martial triumphs of the XIV Legion were many and enviable.

### Rediscovery of Mortarion [854.M30]
[After reunion with Mortarion] In the decades that followed the renamed XIV fought tirelessly in the service of the Great Crusade, never relenting in battle beneath their Primarch's gaze they pursued the liberation of Mankind with a fervour the Great Crusade had never known. Their restless fleet ploughed the cold void from one campaign to the next, resupplying on the move, never pausing but t o make war. They did not garrison, they did not build-they only tore down and slew, coldly, determinately and with the inexorable progress of a contagion or a tsunami wave, and worlds fell before them. The newly christened Death Guard were remorseless, relentless and resilient. They were a tide of steel and ceramite that crashed upon a foe heedless of casualties or loss, utterly unstoppable in assault and near-indestructible in defence. Mortarion's hand and mind was at work everywhere remaking his Legion, from changing tactical doctrines to equipment selection of candidates and changing procurement and, some say, behind the practices in the Legion's Apothecarion — where he gained the latter knowledge to interfere is best left unconsidered.

Decades of endless battle changed the Death Guard, and over time the Terran influences on the Legion became less and less apparent, with the panoply and traditions of the Dusk Raiders and the Officio Militaris erased in favour of Barbarus' bleak creed of war. The bare ivory-grey unpainted ceramite that Mortarion had favoured for the Legion's powered armour became increasingly less adorned, save for new murky-jade markings set to echo the corrosive-resistant swamp mud applied to coat the iron plates of the human warriors that had once served the Primarch of Barbarus, and shorn of older heraldry and Terran influence. The martial stratifications of Old Earth's warmasters were done away with too, and eventually the Legion's Librarius was disbanded thanks to Mortarion's hatred of witchery such as that the hated charnel masters of Barbarus had once wielded. The Death Guard cared little for emblems of rank-except where needed for battlefield recognition. Nor for badges of honour or trinkets to commemorate deeds of valour — instead the closest the Space Marines of the Death Guard came to such decoration was respect for the marks of battle itself on their armour, be they from an enemy's blade or bullet, or the corrosive stains of an environment none but the Death Guard could have endured. So long as the armour plate itself was not compromised such damage was left to adorn the Death Guard as a statement that there was nothing they could not endure, adding to the Legion's increasingly sinister appearance and deepening reputation among their fellow warriors.

As fresh intakes of new Space Marines came in from Barbarus, the surviving core of Terran blood became a minority as the Great Crusade burned on across the stars, the Death Guard at the forefront of the fighting in the most hellish war-zones imaginable. Although second perhaps in their father's eyes, the Terran contingent remained stubborn in the ranks, hardened veterans and a force to be reckoned with in the Legion — until the treachery of Isstvan III where Mortarion moved quickly to rid himself of these warriors who had served their macabre master faithfully despite any misgivings they may have had.

##### The Stygian Scrolls of Lackland Thorn

Much of Mortarion’s early history remains obscure, apparently because the Emperor Himself had suppressed or altered accounts of Barbarus at the time of the Primarch’s discovery. The principal surviving account is the Stygian Scrolls of Lackland Thorn, a celebrated but disreputable historian attached to the Crusade fleet that penetrated the dark nebula surrounding the planet. A xeno-translator, antiquarian and poet with a fascination for the macabre, Thorn recorded Mortarion’s origins before mysteriously disappearing shortly after completing his work. The Scrolls became an integral part of the Death Guard’s culture, with a copy presented to every Space Marine upon formal admission into the Legion. Elsewhere, the text was widely suppressed and its horrors dismissed as allegory, although surviving accounts maintain that they were real.

##### Barbarus: A World of Charnel Lords

Barbarus embodied the nightmare of humanity’s isolation during the Age of Strife. Its mountains were permanently enveloped in toxic fog, corrosive rains and lethal vapours, while the valleys below received little light from the system’s ancient, swollen sun. Human settlements survived amid swamps and forests, behind crude walls that offered little protection from the predators descending after nightfall. The world’s ecology was so singularly hostile that it may have been engineered by its alien overlords to sustain them and keep their human subjects under control.

The identity of these xenos has been deliberately erased from the record. Towering figures in rusted armour, roughly three times the height of a man, they had used science and necromantic knowledge to overcome natural death. Their rivalries produced endless wars fought by reanimated corpses, shape-changing predators, winged constructs and monstrous amalgamations of living flesh. Humanity was regarded less as a subject population than as a renewable source of food and raw material for their creations. The overlords’ strongholds stood high above the valleys, beyond the reach of ordinary humans, protected by an atmosphere that grew increasingly poisonous with altitude.

##### The Foundling and His First Father

According to the Scrolls, the greatest of the charnel lords discovered the infant Primarch among the dead following the destruction of a major human settlement. Intrigued by a seemingly human child capable of surviving the poisonous mists, he spared him and took him as the son and heir that his own kind could never naturally produce. He named him Mortarion, meaning one born of death. His adopted father tested his resistance to the atmosphere and built a fortified keep at the highest altitude the boy could tolerate, while maintaining his own black stronghold higher still, where even a Primarch could not survive for long.

Raised in isolation among the overlord’s unnatural servants, Mortarion was educated to become a commander and living weapon. He mastered battle doctrine, arcane learning, machinery and vivisection, and fought alongside his adoptive father in wars against rival charnel lords and raids upon human settlements. As he matured, his intellect and curiosity led him to question his upbringing, particularly the nature of the human population in the valleys. His father forbade him to descend, but Mortarion eventually escaped his keep, leaving behind a furious master who threatened to kill him should he return. The experience of breathing comparatively clean air and encountering ordinary human life confirmed his true kinship. Recognising that he had been raised among the creatures who preyed upon his own people, he resolved to destroy them.

##### The Liberator and the First Death Guard

Mortarion’s appearance initially terrified the inhabitants of Barbarus: his immense stature, gaunt pallor and sunken black eyes made him resemble the monsters they feared. He earned their trust gradually, helping them build defences, cultivate crops and endure the hardships of valley life. When a lesser overlord and its revenants attacked one of these settlements, Mortarion intervened with a great two-handed harvesting scythe of his own making. He pursued the fleeing master into the toxic fog and killed it, demonstrating that the apparently invulnerable rulers of Barbarus could be defeated. His victory transformed him into a symbol of liberation, justice and revenge among the human settlements.

The rebellion spread as Mortarion taught the inhabitants the fundamentals of warfare and industry. The once one-sided raids became battles that the humans increasingly won. The overlords might have overcome the uprising had they united, but their ancient rivalries prevented effective cooperation. Their poisonous highland refuges nevertheless allowed them to escape whenever threatened, making lasting victory impossible through defensive warfare alone. Mortarion therefore recruited the most resilient and determined humans into a disciplined army, equipped them with crude iron armour, heavy blades and blackpowder firearms of his own design, and developed respirators with the assistance of the finest local artificers. This human fighting force was the original Death Guard, predating the name’s adoption by the XIV Legion.

In their first major test, the Death Guard defeated an invading charnel lord and destroyed its unnatural army. Over the following decades, improvements to their breathing apparatus and understanding of human endurance allowed them to operate at progressively greater heights, isolating and eliminating one enemy stronghold after another. Eventually, only Mortarion’s adoptive father remained, secure within his fortress on the highest peak of Barbarus. The atmosphere surrounding it destroyed respirators and killed within moments; even Mortarion’s extraordinary physiology could not withstand it for long. Repeated attempts to overcome this final barrier ended in failure.

##### The Emperor and the Unfinished Vengeance

Returning from one of his unsuccessful assaults, Mortarion discovered that a stranger from the stars had arrived and assumed the role of liberator among his people. The Emperor’s intervention and offer to kill the last overlord were bitterly resented by the Primarch, whose life had been devoted to destroying the creature himself. The Scrolls preserve an apparently edited and internally inconsistent account of their first meetings, but describe an agreement: if Mortarion could defeat the charnel lord unaided, the Emperor would leave him and Barbarus alone; if he failed, the Emperor would intervene, Barbarus would join the Imperium and Mortarion would swear obedience.

Against the protests of his Death Guard, Mortarion climbed alone through the ruins of his childhood home and towards the final fortress. His respirator succumbed to the atmosphere, its hoses disintegrating as the vapours burned his flesh and ravaged his lungs and throat. Within sight of his goal, he collapsed and saw his former master approaching to kill him. The Emperor intervened and slew the overlord with His burning blade. Barbarus was freed and Mortarion survived, but the Primarch was denied the vengeance for which he had fought for decades. He honoured his oath and submitted to the Emperor after recovering, carrying physical and psychological wounds from the ordeal that, according to the account, never entirely healed.

##### A Mournful Unity: From Dusk Raiders to Death Guard

Mortarion was immediately placed in command of the XIV Legion, the Terran-born Dusk Raiders. Appearing before his new warriors as a spectral figure carrying the great black scythe formerly belonging to his foster father, he proclaimed them his Death Guard. The old name passed into history, and the Legion adopted an increasingly uncompromising doctrine shaped by the experiences of Barbarus. Mortarion believed humanity could be liberated from oppression and terror only by destroying those who threatened it, enduring whatever hardship was necessary and accepting even the most savage means in pursuit of victory. This creed both reinforced and radicalised the existing character of the XIV Legion.

Throughout the Great Crusade, the Death Guard fought campaign after campaign, replenishing their supplies in transit and rarely stopping for anything other than war. They did not seek to build or administer conquered territories; their purpose was destruction of the enemy. Terran customs, older heraldry and the traditions of the Officio Militaris gradually gave way to the austere culture of Barbarus. Mortarion favoured largely unpainted ivory-grey ceramite with murky jade markings recalling the protective swamp coating applied to the iron armour of his original human army. Rank insignia were retained when necessary for battlefield recognition, but ornamental awards were disdained. Battle scars, corrosion and other signs of exposure to hostile environments were deliberately left on armour so long as its protective integrity remained intact. Such marks demonstrated what the Death Guard had survived. Mortarion also eventually disbanded the Legion’s Librarius, reflecting his hatred of the witchcraft practised by the overlords of Barbarus.

As recruitment from Barbarus increased, the surviving Terran veterans became a minority within the Legion. Despite being somewhat disparaged by their Primarch, they remained formidable, stubborn warriors until the betrayal at Isstvan III, when Mortarion sought to purge those whose loyalty to the Emperor might outweigh their devotion to him. Their inherited endurance allowed the loyalist Death Guard to resist their former brothers and inflict a heavy cost upon the traitors. Among them was Garro, who escaped the Warmaster’s trap with his companions and carried news of Horus’ betrayal to Terra. The same qualities of endurance and determination that Mortarion had cultivated could thus be turned against his own rebellion.

Accounts of Mortarion’s reunion with the Emperor were altered and remain inconsistent; the identity of the charnel overlords and the precise chronology of his early life remain uncertain.

### The Death Lord's Hypocrisy

#### The Death Lord's Hypocrisy

Mortarion's alliance with Horus represented one of the greatest contradictions of the Heresy. Having spent much of his life opposing sorcery and psychic corruption, the Death Guard Primarch nevertheless joined a rebellion increasingly dependent upon Daemonic powers. His estranged sons condemned this apparent betrayal of his principles, although the extent to which Mortarion genuinely accepted the Warp remains uncertain.

Nathaniel Garro later suggested that Mortarion may have experienced considerable internal conflict and perhaps joined Horus while secretly intending to undermine the Ruinstorm from within. Under this interpretation, Mortarion's loyalty to the Warmaster might have concealed an effort to weaken the supernatural forces Horus intended to unleash upon the Imperium.

Captain Morturg offered another possibility: Mortarion may eventually have regretted his decision and longed for the earlier Great Crusade, when he had fought alongside Ferrus Manus against humanity's enemies. Nevertheless, the chronicler questions whether such regrets could excuse the atrocities already committed by the Death Guard.

The central contradiction remains unresolved: Mortarion despised the Warp yet allied himself with those who sought to unleash it. Whether this reflected hypocrisy, a concealed strategy, or a catastrophic misjudgement, his Legion had already become a devastating threat to the Imperium, even without the additional horrors of Daemonic corruption.

### Istvaan III [005–006.M31]
Significant portions of the Death Guard and their Primarch Mortarion gathered in Istvaan System.

For the attack on the sprawling Choral City itself four primary target zones had been identified from orbit. The western fortification wall with its Imperial-built bastions and the bunker network they protected were to be a target of the Death Guard’s assault, with the goal of either eradicating or boxing-in the garrison forces they contained.

Death Guard moved in relentlessly, Terminators and Heavy Support Squads toppling guntowers and punching their way into bunkers, while Tactical squads stormed trench after trench.

Deep within the Tomb Spires, the Sons of Horus spearhead, under the command of senior Captain Garviel Loken fought their way into a strange, corpse-filled shrine of foul idolatry and slew the Warsingers there, while at roughly the same time, although having suffered heavy causalities.

To the north-west, the Death Guard loyalists faced the direst situation of battle. Although heavily dug into the Isstvanian III bunker networks, trench lines and the screening city walls, and well-supplied with arms and munitions taken from the underground vaults of the planetary defence armouries, they were caught between the wrath of two implacable foes. From the inner city came Angron and his World Eaters, hammering again and again against their defences, hacking their way into tunnel networks, heedless of casualties taken in the breach and leaving everyone within a red ruin of flesh and shattered ceramite. Beyond the city wall Mortarion, as implacable and relentless as death itself had braved the storm and landed the great relic-assault ship Omen amid the howling gales. Showing far more respect for the foe he faced than the traitor Emperor’s Children had, Mortarion had Omen's turret guns sweep the ground clear, smashing the nearby trench works and bunkers flat so they could provide no hiding place for a loyalist ambush before unleashing its cargo. In defiance of the hurricane and the darkness, the Death Guard marched forth in ordered ranks, and with them the Legion's behemoths of war—Spartan heavy assault transports and Fellblade and Typhon super-heavy tanks — and began their systematic and relentless attack with the Reaper-Primarch marching slowly at their head, counting the slain.

With the support of Legio Mortis Titans, Death Guard started storming trenches and bunkers, where their loyalist brothers dig in.

Some of the alleged traitors after two months of warfare turned their guns, cannons and tanks against traitor Death Guard and World Eaters. Chaotic battle unraveled in which Durak Rask and his command squad was slain by Crysos Morturg Destroyer Squad. Mortarion was injured by plasma shot from loyalist tank.

When, after two months, storms finally settled down, Mortarion poured his Legion’s fresh companies into the area loyalist Death Guard legionaries were situated in. Salvaged Malcador tanks were no match against Mortarion’s Fellblade squadrons and Legio Mortis titans. With full control of battlefield Death Guard came in such density, they marched on battlefield shoulder to shoulder.  
With the conclusion of the bitter fighting on Istvaan III, it is estimated Mortarion’s Legion had been reduced by well over 25 000 casualties, including both the stubborn Terran Loyalists and Mortarion’s sons of Barbarus. It is believed that protracted fighting during the Istvaan III betrayal has exacted a heavy toll on the Death Guard’s armoured divisions, many of which had been amongst the suspected Loyalists.  

### Istvaan V
On the left flank great clouds of poisonous fog swept across the trenches, dissolving flesh through the slightest breach in armor, and by the choked-screams elicited by such ugly deaths was the Reaper Mortarion heralded.  

With arcing sweeps of his scythe, the Primarch of the Death Guard cut through the Salamanders in his path and not even the stalwart Firedrakes could stop his bleak progress..  

During the action, the Death Guard made extensive use of surprise attacks from concealed positions, mines, alchemical weaponry and heavy sustained close-range fire, principally against the Salamanders Legion, and were met in resolve and fury by Vulkan’s Legion, incurring massive casualties on both sides.

### Manachean War [008.M31]

- At Cryxa, the Death Guard warship *Fourth Horseman* landed a company of Grave Warden Terminators in mining complexes, beginning a month-long massacre.

### Campaigns after the Conquest of Manachea

- Mortarion withdrew the Legion's main forces after Manachea's fall. Death Guard Poison Ships, at times working with Dark Mechanicum, continued using chemical and biological weapons to weaken loyalist worlds.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Death Guard accompanied the main Traitor advance alongside the Sons of Horus. Their forces repeatedly clashed with Shadrak Meduson's raiders, who attacked Traitor positions and supply lines. Some Death Guard detachments deliberately committed atrocities against civilian worlds to lure isolated Loyalists into battle.

The Death Guard fought Iron Warriors alongside Alpha Legion forces in the siege campaigns surrounding the Coronid Deeps, including the Siege of Vharna described in the Shattered Legions account.

The Siege of Epsilon-Stranivar IX exposed the growing divisions between Horus' Traitor Legions during the early Horus Heresy. Fortress Stranivar, situated near the Coronid Deeps, was one of the last Loyalist strongholds on the Warmaster's northern flank. Its defenders included a small Imperial Fists garrison reinforced by Raven Guard, Salamanders, Iron Hands, and stranded Blood Angels. Despite overwhelming odds, these forces remained united in their determination to hold the fortress for the Emperor. The attacking Iron Warriors, Death Guard, and Alpha Legion refused to cooperate effectively. Each Legion pursued its own objectives, withheld intelligence and resources, and blamed its allies for setbacks. The Iron Warriors' 114th Grand Battalion suffered particularly badly. Alpha Legion forces repeatedly allowed Perturabo's warriors to assault fortified Loyalist positions without support, exploiting the resulting confusion to strike high-priority targets and claim the glory themselves.

During the decisive Siege of Vharna, the Alpha Legion and Death Guard effectively abandoned the 114th Grand Battalion as a sacrificial rearguard. Of the 6,000 Iron Warriors committed, fewer than 2,000 survived. Their surviving senior officer, Nârik Dreygur of the Apolakron, swore vengeance for his fallen brothers. The Loyalists temporarily held Fortress Stranivar, but the planet fell to a renewed Sons of Horus assault less than a year later. Nevertheless, the campaign revealed a critical weakness in Horus' armies: their inability to overcome rivalry and distrust. Loyalist commanders subsequently exploited these divisions to resist superior Traitor forces, while resentment between the Warmaster's Legions continued to deepen.

### Battle of Perditus [009.M31]

One notable confrontation was the Battle of Perditus in 009.M31, where the 98th Clan Company of the Iron Hands intercepted a Death Guard force led by its First Company near a forbidden Mechanicum research facility. The Death Guard sought an artefact believed capable of calming the Ruinstorm, whose power had engulfed the Ultima Segmentum less than two years earlier. The two Legions fought for more than a month before Lion El'Jonson intervened, ordering both forces to withdraw. According to Iron Hands accounts, the Dark Angels subsequently seized the artefact, considering it essential for navigating the Warp during the Ruinstorm.

## Notes

Mortarion led the Death Guard's campaign in the Cyclops Cluster. At Dominica Minor he and his Deathshroud fought the surviving Salamanders at the Guildmasters' fortress. Consul-Centurion Shar'rac Grenn struck and staggered Mortarion in a Contemptor Dreadnought before Mortarion destroyed the machine and killed him. After conquering Manachea, Mortarion withdrew the main Death Guard forces.

### The Warp and the Death Lord

The Death Guard — The Reapers of Corruption

Mortarion and the Death Guard also played a significant role in suppressing Warp-related threats, though their methods were considerably more ruthless. Driven by his hatred of sorcery and the supernatural, Mortarion actively sought worlds contaminated by psychic practices or forbidden rituals. Rather than approaching these populations as a liberator, he descended upon them as an executioner, accompanied by the XIV Legion's feared Destroyer formations.

The Death Guard were among the earliest Legions to employ bale flamers and psyk-out or psi-reactive munitions on a large scale. Mortarion regarded Warp corruption as an incurable disease that threatened to spread unless completely eradicated. His campaigns therefore resembled systematic purges, reflecting his conviction that sorcery was a contagion requiring extermination rather than understanding or containment.

The Death Lord's Hypocrisy

Mortarion's alliance with Horus represented one of the greatest contradictions of the Heresy. Having spent much of his life opposing sorcery and psychic corruption, the Death Guard Primarch nevertheless joined a rebellion increasingly dependent upon Daemonic powers. His estranged sons condemned this apparent betrayal of his principles, although the extent to which Mortarion genuinely accepted the Warp remains uncertain.

Nathaniel Garro later suggested that Mortarion may have experienced considerable internal conflict and perhaps joined Horus while secretly intending to undermine the Ruinstorm from within. Under this interpretation, Mortarion's loyalty to the Warmaster might have concealed an effort to weaken the supernatural forces Horus intended to unleash upon the Imperium.

Captain Morturg offered another possibility: Mortarion may eventually have regretted his decision and longed for the earlier Great Crusade, when he had fought alongside Ferrus Manus against humanity's enemies. Nevertheless, the chronicler questions whether such regrets could excuse the atrocities already committed by the Death Guard.

The central contradiction remains unresolved: Mortarion despised the Warp yet allied himself with those who sought to unleash it. Whether this reflected hypocrisy, a concealed strategy, or a catastrophic misjudgement, his Legion had already become a devastating threat to the Imperium, even without the additional horrors of Daemonic corruption.

### Mortarion — Born of Death

#### The Stygian Scrolls of Lackland Thorn

Much of Mortarion’s early history remains obscure, apparently because the Emperor Himself had suppressed or altered accounts of Barbarus at the time of the Primarch’s discovery. The principal surviving account is the Stygian Scrolls of Lackland Thorn, a celebrated but disreputable historian attached to the Crusade fleet that penetrated the dark nebula surrounding the planet. A xeno-translator, antiquarian and poet with a fascination for the macabre, Thorn recorded Mortarion’s origins before mysteriously disappearing shortly after completing his work. The Scrolls became an integral part of the Death Guard’s culture, with a copy presented to every Space Marine upon formal admission into the Legion. Elsewhere, the text was widely suppressed and its horrors dismissed as allegory, although surviving accounts maintain that they were real.

#### Barbarus: A World of Charnel Lords

Barbarus embodied the nightmare of humanity’s isolation during the Age of Strife. Its mountains were permanently enveloped in toxic fog, corrosive rains and lethal vapours, while the valleys below received little light from the system’s ancient, swollen sun. Human settlements survived amid swamps and forests, behind crude walls that offered little protection from the predators descending after nightfall. The world’s ecology was so singularly hostile that it may have been engineered by its alien overlords to sustain them and keep their human subjects under control.

The identity of these xenos has been deliberately erased from the record. Towering figures in rusted armour, roughly three times the height of a man, they had used science and necromantic knowledge to overcome natural death. Their rivalries produced endless wars fought by reanimated corpses, shape-changing predators, winged constructs and monstrous amalgamations of living flesh. Humanity was regarded less as a subject population than as a renewable source of food and raw material for their creations. The overlords’ strongholds stood high above the valleys, beyond the reach of ordinary humans, protected by an atmosphere that grew increasingly poisonous with altitude.

#### The Foundling and His First Father

According to the Scrolls, the greatest of the charnel lords discovered the infant Primarch among the dead following the destruction of a major human settlement. Intrigued by a seemingly human child capable of surviving the poisonous mists, he spared him and took him as the son and heir that his own kind could never naturally produce. He named him Mortarion, meaning one born of death. His adopted father tested his resistance to the atmosphere and built a fortified keep at the highest altitude the boy could tolerate, while maintaining his own black stronghold higher still, where even a Primarch could not survive for long.

Raised in isolation among the overlord’s unnatural servants, Mortarion was educated to become a commander and living weapon. He mastered battle doctrine, arcane learning, machinery and vivisection, and fought alongside his adoptive father in wars against rival charnel lords and raids upon human settlements. As he matured, his intellect and curiosity led him to question his upbringing, particularly the nature of the human population in the valleys. His father forbade him to descend, but Mortarion eventually escaped his keep, leaving behind a furious master who threatened to kill him should he return. The experience of breathing comparatively clean air and encountering ordinary human life confirmed his true kinship. Recognising that he had been raised among the creatures who preyed upon his own people, he resolved to destroy them.

#### The Liberator and the First Death Guard

Mortarion’s appearance initially terrified the inhabitants of Barbarus: his immense stature, gaunt pallor and sunken black eyes made him resemble the monsters they feared. He earned their trust gradually, helping them build defences, cultivate crops and endure the hardships of valley life. When a lesser overlord and its revenants attacked one of these settlements, Mortarion intervened with a great two-handed harvesting scythe of his own making. He pursued the fleeing master into the toxic fog and killed it, demonstrating that the apparently invulnerable rulers of Barbarus could be defeated. His victory transformed him into a symbol of liberation, justice and revenge among the human settlements.

The rebellion spread as Mortarion taught the inhabitants the fundamentals of warfare and industry. The once one-sided raids became battles that the humans increasingly won. The overlords might have overcome the uprising had they united, but their ancient rivalries prevented effective cooperation. Their poisonous highland refuges nevertheless allowed them to escape whenever threatened, making lasting victory impossible through defensive warfare alone. Mortarion therefore recruited the most resilient and determined humans into a disciplined army, equipped them with crude iron armour, heavy blades and blackpowder firearms of his own design, and developed respirators with the assistance of the finest local artificers. This human fighting force was the original Death Guard, predating the name’s adoption by the XIV Legion.

In their first major test, the Death Guard defeated an invading charnel lord and destroyed its unnatural army. Over the following decades, improvements to their breathing apparatus and understanding of human endurance allowed them to operate at progressively greater heights, isolating and eliminating one enemy stronghold after another. Eventually, only Mortarion’s adoptive father remained, secure within his fortress on the highest peak of Barbarus. The atmosphere surrounding it destroyed respirators and killed within moments; even Mortarion’s extraordinary physiology could not withstand it for long. Repeated attempts to overcome this final barrier ended in failure.

#### The Emperor and the Unfinished Vengeance

Returning from one of his unsuccessful assaults, Mortarion discovered that a stranger from the stars had arrived and assumed the role of liberator among his people. The Emperor’s intervention and offer to kill the last overlord were bitterly resented by the Primarch, whose life had been devoted to destroying the creature himself. The Scrolls preserve an apparently edited and internally inconsistent account of their first meetings, but describe an agreement: if Mortarion could defeat the charnel lord unaided, the Emperor would leave him and Barbarus alone; if he failed, the Emperor would intervene, Barbarus would join the Imperium and Mortarion would swear obedience.

Against the protests of his Death Guard, Mortarion climbed alone through the ruins of his childhood home and towards the final fortress. His respirator succumbed to the atmosphere, its hoses disintegrating as the vapours burned his flesh and ravaged his lungs and throat. Within sight of his goal, he collapsed and saw his former master approaching to kill him. The Emperor intervened and slew the overlord with His burning blade. Barbarus was freed and Mortarion survived, but the Primarch was denied the vengeance for which he had fought for decades. He honoured his oath and submitted to the Emperor after recovering, carrying physical and psychological wounds from the ordeal that, according to the account, never entirely healed.

#### A Mournful Unity: From Dusk Raiders to Death Guard

Mortarion was immediately placed in command of the XIV Legion, the Terran-born Dusk Raiders. Appearing before his new warriors as a spectral figure carrying the great black scythe formerly belonging to his foster father, he proclaimed them his Death Guard. The old name passed into history, and the Legion adopted an increasingly uncompromising doctrine shaped by the experiences of Barbarus. Mortarion believed humanity could be liberated from oppression and terror only by destroying those who threatened it, enduring whatever hardship was necessary and accepting even the most savage means in pursuit of victory. This creed both reinforced and radicalised the existing character of the XIV Legion.

Throughout the Great Crusade, the Death Guard fought campaign after campaign, replenishing their supplies in transit and rarely stopping for anything other than war. They did not seek to build or administer conquered territories; their purpose was destruction of the enemy. Terran customs, older heraldry and the traditions of the Officio Militaris gradually gave way to the austere culture of Barbarus. Mortarion favoured largely unpainted ivory-grey ceramite with murky jade markings recalling the protective swamp coating applied to the iron armour of his original human army. Rank insignia were retained when necessary for battlefield recognition, but ornamental awards were disdained. Battle scars, corrosion and other signs of exposure to hostile environments were deliberately left on armour so long as its protective integrity remained intact. Such marks demonstrated what the Death Guard had survived. Mortarion also eventually disbanded the Legion’s Librarius, reflecting his hatred of the witchcraft practised by the overlords of Barbarus.

As recruitment from Barbarus increased, the surviving Terran veterans became a minority within the Legion. Despite being somewhat disparaged by their Primarch, they remained formidable, stubborn warriors until the betrayal at Isstvan III, when Mortarion sought to purge those whose loyalty to the Emperor might outweigh their devotion to him. Their inherited endurance allowed the loyalist Death Guard to resist their former brothers and inflict a heavy cost upon the traitors. Among them was Garro, who escaped the Warmaster’s trap with his companions and carried news of Horus’ betrayal to Terra. The same qualities of endurance and determination that Mortarion had cultivated could thus be turned against his own rebellion.

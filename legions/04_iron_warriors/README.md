# Iron Warriors

## Legion Structure

Perturabo exercised absolute authority over the Iron Warriors, treating Terran and Olympian Legionaries equally as expendable instruments of war. Although the IVth Legion had long adhered rigidly to its original military doctrines, Perturabo introduced sweeping reforms that modernised its organisation and tactics without fundamentally changing its established character. His reforms combined Olympia’s sophisticated military traditions with the extensive knowledge he acquired while serving alongside the Emperor and studying on Terra. Drawing upon centuries of human military history, strategic theory, and his own extraordinary intellect, Perturabo rapidly developed a comprehensive new doctrine for the Legion. The result was a thoroughly reorganised Iron Warriors Legion, shaped by Perturabo’s precise planning and ruthless philosophy, retaining its traditional discipline and relentless determination while becoming an even more sophisticated instrument of warfare.

The Grand Battalion became the principal organisational unit of the Iron Warriors under Perturabo. Roughly equivalent to a standard Legion Chapter, it nominally comprised 1,000 Space Marines but possessed significantly greater organic support, including extensive armoured forces, artillery, and logistical resources. The Iron Warriors’ exceptionally high casualty rates required continuous recruitment and reinforcement, particularly to replace inexperienced Legionaries killed during their early years of service. Consequently, Grand Battalion strength fluctuated considerably, with severely depleted formations sometimes being absorbed into other units. Although 1,000 warriors remained the nominal standard, individual Grand Battalions could range from as few as 500 to as many as 4,000 Legionaries, reflecting the Legion’s relentless attritional warfare and flexible approach to maintaining operational strength.

The Iron Warriors maintained a structured but flexible military hierarchy, adapting their formations to operational requirements. Below the Grand Battalions were Cohorts or Grand Companies, consisting of three to five line companies supported by human auxiliaries, Mechanicum forces, and heavy equipment.

Smaller formations included Line Companies of approximately 100 Legionaries and Armour Centuries of 20–50 vehicles. The Legion also maintained elite veterans known as Tyranthikos (“Dominators”), hardened warriors who had survived numerous brutal sieges and specialised in breaking enemy lines, frequently using Terminator armour.

Perturabo envisioned the Iron Warriors as a disciplined, relentlessly aggressive force defined by calculated savagery. Their warfare combined precisely coordinated artillery bombardments, devastating armoured assaults, and unwavering infantry advances. In close combat, their ferocity could rival even the World Eaters, yet they remained capable of instantly restraining themselves under Perturabo’s absolute authority. The Legion prioritised collective victory over individual achievement, seeking to destroy enemy resistance through the most efficient and decisive methods available. Personal glory and heroism were considered irrelevant compared to the success of the entire force. Perturabo regarded his Legionaries as valuable but expendable military resources, showing remarkably little concern for individual lives. Every warrior existed to serve the Legion’s objectives, and casualties were simply another calculated cost of achieving victory.

The Iron Warriors organised their Legion to absorb heavy casualties and maintain operational efficiency, prioritising the identity of the Legion as a whole over individual units. They pioneered accelerated recruitment and frequently reassigned warriors between formations, roles, and commanders according to tactical necessity. Advancement depended primarily on survival and specialised expertise, rather than combat prowess alone. Every Iron Warrior was expected to master technical disciplines alongside warfare, becoming proficient in engineering, machinery, and construction. Perturabo’s gene-seed also appeared to influence his sons’ abilities and temperament. Many possessed exceptional analytical intelligence, rapid comprehension, and abstract reasoning skills, but also exhibited pronounced suspicion, distrust, and violent tempers, even towards fellow Legionaries.

The rank of Warsmith became the Iron Warriors’ principal senior command title, largely replacing traditional ranks such as Praetor and Lord Commander. Warsmiths were selected for their technological expertise, strategic intelligence, and mastery of Perturabo’s preferred methods of warfare.

A Warsmith was expected to control every aspect of a battlefield, from logistics, siege engineering, and artillery coordination to large-scale strategic planning. They bore personal responsibility for victory or defeat, with failure often punished severely by Perturabo. This system produced a ruthless, highly independent, and frequently paranoid class of commanders, whose undefined hierarchy encouraged bitter rivalries. Most Warsmiths commanded Grand Battalions, while others led specialised formations such as the Stor-Bezashk, Expeditionary fleets, fortress garrisons, or strategically important industrial worlds. Three Warsmiths personally favoured by Perturabo were elevated to the rank of Triarch, forming the Trident, the Iron Warriors’ highest advisory council. Although officially serving as the Primarch’s closest advisers, the Triarchs primarily communicated and enforced Perturabo’s orders throughout the Legion. Their privileged position carried considerable danger, particularly as Perturabo became increasingly paranoid and ruthless in later years.

The Iron Warriors (IVth Legion) served as the Great Crusade’s foremost siege specialists, renowned for breaking seemingly impregnable fortresses and conducting brutal wars of attrition. Their Primarch, Perturabo, was an exceptionally gifted but ruthless strategist who could identify and exploit enemy weaknesses, regarding victory as worth any sacrifice. However, decades of relentless warfare, perceived mistreatment, and growing resentment gradually poisoned the Legion. Its warriors became increasingly bitter towards the Imperium, while Perturabo himself grew paranoid, withdrawn, and distrustful. By the time Horus’ betrayal was revealed at Isstvan III, the Iron Warriors were already suffering a severe internal crisis, compounded by their brutal suppression of a rebellion on their homeworld of Olympia. Despite initially answering the call to punish Horus, Perturabo instead led the bulk of his Legion to Isstvan V, where the Iron Warriors turned their weapons against the Loyalists during the Dropsite Massacre. Their long-simmering resentment had finally erupted into outright treachery, with devastating consequences for the Imperium.

## Legion Armory

The Iron Warriors possessed enormous reserves of military equipment, including thousands of suits of Terminator armour and ammunition stockpiles exceeding those of several other Legions combined. Their warriors were also highly skilled in maintaining and modifying their own equipment. Practicality took priority over appearance. The Legion favoured reliable, easily repaired weapons and specialised equipment, such as portable lascannons for anti-armour warfare and Mk III power armour for siege assaults. During prolonged campaigns, damaged armour was repaired only enough to remain functional, with cosmetic restoration postponed until victory. This reflected the Iron Warriors’ relentless emphasis on efficiency, endurance, and battlefield effectiveness.

The Iron Warriors possessed the largest armoured reserves and artillery capacity of any Legion during the Great Crusade. Their arsenal included extensive numbers of heavy and super-heavy vehicles such as Land Raiders, Typhons, Stormblades, and Mastodons, alongside vast fleets of Rhinos, Predators, and Basilisks. The Legion maintained dedicated manufacturing facilities capable of rapidly replacing vehicle losses, allowing it to treat lighter armoured units as effectively expendable. It also fielded numerous specialised war machines, including Cerberus, Scorpius, and Thunderstrike tanks. This combination of industrial production, extensive vehicle reserves, and integrated armoured support gave the Iron Warriors unmatched capabilities in mechanised warfare and sustained artillery bombardment.

The Stor-Bezashk were the Iron Warriors’ elite siege warfare specialists, responsible for constructing, maintaining, and deploying the Legion’s most devastating weapons, including rare war engines normally found only within the Mechanicum’s Ordo Reductor.

Their Siege Masters controlled vast stockpiles of atomic, phosphex, and gravitic munitions, as well as specialised Destroyer units, to which Legionaries were frequently assigned as punishment. The Stor-Bezashk could operate independently as a formidable siege-breaking force or provide specialised artillery and destructive firepower to Grand Battalions during major campaigns.

## Legion Chronicles

### Unification Wars and Early Great Crusade

The Iron Warriors (IVth Legion) were founded on Terra during the final stages of the Unification Wars, with their earliest mustering grounds established atop the ruins of a defeated fortress on the Auro Plateau of Sek-Amrak. Their initial recruits came from the region’s warlike gun-tribes, blood grieves, and Tek-enclaves, whose territories had recently been brought under Imperial control. The IVth Legion’s gene-seed proved exceptionally adaptable, with unusually low implantation rejection rates compared to many other early Legions. This genetic stability allowed the Iron Warriors to expand rapidly, fielding several fully operational battalions while some other proto-Legions could barely muster a single century. Their rapid growth made the IVth one of the earliest Legions deployed into active military service, fighting alongside the Ist (Dark Angels) and Vth (White Scars) Legions during the Imperium’s initial expansion.

The Iron Warriors began their combat history during the final battles of Terran Unification, before participating in the pacification of the Sol System. Their earliest recorded victories included engagements at the Cydo-Tyre Orbital, Zidec Archipelago, and Ice Station Echo. Their most significant early campaign was the Battle of Mehr Yasht during the conquest of Venus. Fighting under the direct command of the Emperor himself, the IVth Legion helped defeat the formidable Litho-Gholem armies of the War Witches. This victory established the Iron Warriors’ reputation as a disciplined and dependable military force during the Imperium’s earliest expansion.

The Iron Warriors’ early victories earned them priority access to advanced weapons and war machines supplied through the Emperor’s alliance with Mars. They also received additional Terran recruits originally intended for the IIIrd Legion (Emperor’s Children), whose expansion had been crippled by a catastrophic gene-seed crisis. Combined with their highly adaptable gene-seed and proven battlefield effectiveness, these advantages made the IVth one of the largest Legions during the early Great Crusade. Their rapidly growing numbers allowed the Iron Warriors to divide their forces among several major Expeditionary fleets while continuously replacing casualties.

The Iron Warriors became the leading force of the 8th Expeditionary Fleet, which conquered or reclaimed 29 major star-system clusters and destroyed several xenos civilizations during an eleven-year campaign. These victories were instrumental in securing Imperial control over Segmentum Solar and earned the IVth Legion the Emperor’s personal recognition. The Legion subsequently adopted the Winged Bolt emblem from the 8th Expeditionary Fleet as its first heraldic symbol. Despite these achievements, the Iron Warriors gradually lost their cultural ties to Terra as the Great Crusade expanded beyond Segmentum Solar. Unlike other Legions, they remained without their Primarch until the late 840s.M30, preventing any significant transformation of their original identity. Consequently, the IVth Legion retained the rigid organisation, doctrines, and military traditions established during the earliest years of the Great Crusade, remaining largely unchanged until its eventual reunification with Perturabo.

The Iron Warriors retained their original Terran military doctrines, relying on relentless, methodical application of overwhelming force rather than elaborate strategies or individual heroics. While other Legions developed increasingly distinctive cultures and combat styles, the IVth remained rigidly pragmatic and largely unchanged. This approach earned them a reputation among other Legions, reportedly including Horus, as unimaginative, mechanical, and even dishonourable warriors. However, Imperial High Command valued precisely these qualities, considering the Iron Warriors exceptionally reliable, disciplined, and willing to obey orders without complaint. Consequently, the IVth became the Great Crusade’s dependable “workhorse” Legion, repeatedly assigned to prolonged sieges, brutal wars of attrition, and other essential but thankless campaigns. Their effectiveness and unquestioning obedience made them indispensable, but also condemned them to some of the Imperium’s most exhausting and costly military operations.

Unlike the Space Wolves, Iron Hands, and Sons of Horus, the Iron Warriors willingly divided their forces and accepted orders from ordinary human commanders. They undertook thankless sieges, prolonged suppression campaigns, and dangerous garrison duties that other Legions often refused, fighting wherever the Great Crusade required without complaint or recognition. This relentless service gradually depleted the IVth Legion’s once-formidable numbers, while other Legions surpassed them in prestige and glory. Despite taking pride in their endurance and obedience, the Iron Warriors increasingly found themselves overworked, underappreciated, and overshadowed by their brothers. Even before their reunification with Perturabo, resentment had begun to take root, creating a growing divide between the Iron Warriors and the other Legions that would eventually contribute to their downfall.

The Iron Warriors were among the most technologically proficient and disciplined Legions, favouring overwhelming firepower and meticulously planned offensives. Their preferred tactics involved detailed battlefield preparations, concentrated artillery bombardments, and decisive assaults by heavy armour and mechanised infantry. To support this approach, the IVth Legion assembled one of the largest dedicated artillery forces of the Great Crusade. However, their calculated approach to planning contrasted sharply with their extreme stubbornness once battle began. The Iron Warriors rarely withdrew without explicit orders from their highest commanders, continuing their offensives even when faced with unexpected setbacks or catastrophic casualties. This refusal to accept defeat became a defining source of pride within the Legion. Through discipline, endurance, and sheer firepower, they secured numerous victories, but their inflexibility often resulted in enormous losses, gradually establishing a tradition of victory at almost any cost.

The liberation of the Forge World Incaladion in 842–843.M30 exposed the Iron Warriors’ greatest weakness: their refusal to abandon a failing battle plan. Despite an unexpected enemy counterattack that shattered their initial assault, the IVth Legion stubbornly continued fighting, eventually securing victory through sheer attrition. The cost was catastrophic: nearly 29,000 Iron Warriors and over two million Imperial Army soldiers were killed, including many of the Legion’s most experienced warriors and senior commanders. Critics within the Imperial Court accused the IVth of deliberately courting disaster simply to prove that it could overcome any obstacle, regardless of the casualties. Incaladion marked a turning point in the Legion’s decline. Once among the largest and most celebrated Legions, the Iron Warriors had become depleted, politically unpopular, and increasingly overshadowed by the Luna Wolves, Dark Angels, and others. Their reliable gene-seed allowed them to replenish their ranks, but continuous warfare had eroded their former superiority. It was to this battered, embittered, and increasingly directionless Legion that Perturabo would finally return, inheriting warriors already deeply scarred by decades of relentless and often thankless service.

### Rediscovery of Perturabo and the Great Crusade

Perturabo’s discovery and integration into the Great Crusade proceeded remarkably smoothly, unlike the reunions of several other Primarchs. The tyrant Dammekos willingly surrendered his adopted son’s service to the Emperor in exchange for continued authority over Olympia as an Imperial ruler. Perturabo himself had already deduced that he was an artificially created superhuman and had long anticipated meeting his creator. Perturabo quickly distinguished himself as perhaps the most scientifically and technologically gifted of all the Primarchs. His extraordinary intellect allowed him to absorb knowledge at remarkable speed, but he remained cold, calculating, secretive, and socially detached, showing little interest in explaining himself even to his fellow Primarchs. The Emperor recognised Perturabo’s immense potential as a military commander, valuing his intellectual brilliance despite his difficult personality. Perturabo approached warfare as a series of problems to be analysed, dismantled, and solved, combining ruthless strategic calculation with overwhelming military force. His arrival promised to transform the Iron Warriors into an even more formidable instrument of conquest.

After briefly serving alongside the Emperor and studying the Great Crusade’s history, technology, and military operations, Perturabo assumed full command of the Iron Warriors (IVth Legion). Approximately 35,000 Legionaries were assembled under his direct authority, with perhaps another 17,500 scattered across Imperial territories in smaller garrisons and detachments. Perturabo immediately conducted a comprehensive review of his Legion’s military record, doctrines, and performance, comparing them with those of the other Legions. Finding his sons inadequate despite their decades of service and sacrifice, he imposed a brutal punishment: decimation, the execution of one in every ten warriors. Perturabo ordered the execution of one in every ten Iron Warriors, selecting victims by lottery and forcing their own comrades to kill them with their bare hands. He held the entire Legion collectively responsible for its perceived shortcomings, declaring that war was merciless and punishment must be equally indiscriminate. The decimation provoked outrage within the Imperial Court, particularly from Roboute Guilliman, who condemned the needless deaths of veteran warriors who had served the Imperium faithfully. The incident created lasting hostility between Guilliman and Perturabo, although the Emperor ultimately silenced all criticism. For the surviving Iron Warriors, the message was unmistakable: failure meant death, and Perturabo demanded absolute superiority. Their punishment was not for actual military incompetence, but for failing to reach what he considered their full potential. Merely being better than other Legions was insufficient—Perturabo expected his sons to become supreme among them.

Perturabo immediately began transforming the IVth Legion into his ideal instrument of war, testing its newly imposed discipline during the conquest of the Meratara Cluster. The Iron Warriors defeated the formidable Black Judges, exterminated the Ecto-Saurids of Verikhonia, and subjugated the rebellious Knight-fiefdom of Lyxos. The conquest of Lyxos proved particularly significant, ending a millennia-old conflict between its rulers and the Mechanicum. This victory earned the Iron Warriors considerable favour among the Lords of Mars. The Meratara campaign marked the complete transformation of the IVth Legion. Through relentless trials, ruthless experimentation, and brutal discipline, Perturabo reshaped his warriors according to his own uncompromising vision. By its conclusion, the old Terran Legion had effectively ceased to exist, replaced by the Iron Warriors forged in the image of their Primarch’s cold and merciless philosophy.

Perturabo transformed Olympia into a major industrial and military centre for the Iron Warriors, working closely with the Mechanicum to establish shipyards, foundries, and weapons production facilities. Industrial installations salvaged from conquered systems were relocated and expanded, while the worlds of the Meratara Cluster supplied fresh recruits, weapons, and ammunition. His reforms intensified the IVth Legion’s existing ruthlessness rather than fundamentally changing its nature. Where the Iron Warriors had previously accepted heavy casualties as the price of victory, they now regarded such losses as almost irrelevant.

Under Perturabo’s direction, the Iron Warriors became a relentless, highly mechanised engine of conquest, approaching warfare as a mathematical problem to be solved through overwhelming force, discipline, and absolute determination, regardless of the human cost.

Perturabo concentrated the bulk of the Iron Warriors within the newly formed 125th Expeditionary Fleet, transforming it into one of the Great Crusade’s most formidable military forces. The IVth Legion earned an unrivalled reputation for siege warfare, heavy artillery, and armoured assaults, becoming renowned for its ability to destroy virtually any fortress or enemy army. However, the Legion’s growing military prowess came at a terrible cost. Perturabo’s ruthless leadership deepened the Iron Warriors’ resentment, paranoia, and distrust towards their brother Legions, while his disregard for casualties encouraged an increasingly callous attitude towards life. The Iron Warriors reportedly suffered the highest cumulative casualty rates of any Legion during the Great Crusade, yet their remarkable ability to replenish losses allowed them to maintain their fighting strength and rarely suffer outright defeat. Nevertheless, decades of relentless warfare and sacrifice gradually corroded the Legion’s collective psyche, reinforcing the bitterness and alienation that would eventually contribute to its betrayal of the Imperium.

Perturabo and the Iron Warriors remained deeply isolated from their fellow Legions, viewing them as undisciplined, vain, and weakened by superstition. Their closest relationships were with elements of the Mechanicum, although even these allies distrusted the Legion’s technological independence and disregard for the Omnissiah’s faith. The Iron Warriors developed an especially sinister reputation among Imperial Army forces, treating human soldiers as expendable military resources. They routinely sacrificed thousands of auxiliaries in frontal assaults to exhaust enemy ammunition, expose defensive positions, or simply measure an opponent’s firepower. This calculated disregard for human life reinforced the Legion’s growing alienation from the wider Imperium, making the Iron Warriors feared and resented even by those fighting alongside them.

The Iron Warriors’ ruthless treatment of Imperial Army troops earned them the hated nickname “Corpse Grinders.” Their willingness to sacrifice human auxiliaries in enormous numbers led to increasingly frequent mutinies, which the Legion suppressed with brutal massacres. Eventually, Horus issued a standing order assigning them primarily convicted criminals and enslaved populations to prevent further damage to Imperial Army morale. Relations with other Space Marine Legions deteriorated throughout the Great Crusade. The Iron Warriors developed a bitter rivalry with the Raven Guard following the Icesunder War, while their hostility towards the Imperial Fists grew increasingly severe. Even successful joint campaigns failed to improve their reputation. Despite their contributions to major victories, including the war against Waaagh! Mashogg, Perturabo and his Legion were often met with indifference or contempt, deepening their isolation and resentment towards their fellow Legions.

Perturabo’s achievements continued to be overlooked by his brother Legions. During the campaign against Waaagh! Mashogg, the Space Wolves and White Scars repeatedly failed to breach the Orks’ massive orbital fortifications. Perturabo eventually devised the successful assault, yet their official chronicles acknowledged him only as an unnamed “comrade-in-arms.” The Iron Warriors’ growing resentment intensified after Horus became Warmaster. New deployment orders from Terra and Horus scattered the Legion across numerous Expeditionary fleets, assigning its warriors to dangerous garrisons, thankless sieges, and isolated campaigns that steadily depleted their strength. Meanwhile, Perturabo’s 125th Expeditionary Fleet was repeatedly sent against exceptionally dangerous enemies, receiving no additional reinforcements or resources beyond what it could obtain independently. Despite his bitterness, Perturabo obeyed every order. These relentless assignments further alienated the Iron Warriors from the Imperium, deepening their psychological deterioration and resentment. Some later accounts even suggest that this systematic exhaustion and isolation may have been a deliberate part of Horus’ plan to turn Perturabo against the Emperor.

### The Hrud Campaign

The Iron Warriors suffered catastrophic losses during their campaign against the Hrud, whose reality-altering abilities caused temporal distortions, solar storms, and tectonic instability. Fighting under apparent orders from Terra, the Legion lost more than one-fifth of its active strength in what many regarded as a pointless expedition. Enraged by news of Olympia’s rebellion, Perturabo abandoned the Hrud campaign, leaving behind a sacrificial force to finish the war. Returning home, he unleashed a merciless campaign of destruction, personally demolishing the defences of Lochos, his former home. Entire cities were massacred, mountain fortresses destroyed, and millions of Olympians killed, while the planet and its system were brutally enslaved. The destruction of Olympia marked a profound moral collapse for Perturabo and his Legion. Some accounts suggest they were overcome by guilt and despair, while others claim they finally embraced their own cruelty. Whatever the truth, the massacre became a devastating act of self-destruction, carried out under the authority of the Warmaster.

### Destruction of Olympia and the Eve of Heresy

The Horus Heresy struck the Iron Warriors after years of mounting losses, bitterness, and psychological strain. The greatest crisis was the rebellion on their homeworld of Olympia, where the death of the tyrant Dammekos reignited bitter political rivalries. Resentment towards Imperial rule and the repeated recruitment of Olympia’s young men into the Legion further fuelled the uprising. The rebellion came at an especially devastating moment for Perturabo and his warriors. For over a year, the Iron Warriors had been fighting an exhausting campaign against the horrifying xenos known as the Hrud (or Temporaferrrox) across the Sak’trada Deeps. The prolonged Hrud campaign had inflicted severe casualties and psychological trauma, leaving the Legion dangerously weakened when news of Olympia’s rebellion arrived, setting the stage for another catastrophe.

Following the destruction of Olympia, the Iron Warriors descended further into paranoia, isolation, and brutality. Their commanders became increasingly ruthless, violently suppressing populations under their control and responding to even minor rebellions with genocidal force. By the outbreak of the Horus Heresy, the Legion was estimated to number 150,000–180,000 Space Marines, supported by a formidable fleet containing over 100 capital warships. However, its true strength remained uncertain because numerous detachments and garrisons were scattered throughout the Imperium. The Legion may have been considerably larger than these estimates suggest, having accelerated its recruitment, indoctrination, and shipbuilding programmes on Olympia shortly before the rebellion.

The Iron Warriors strengthened their ties with certain Mechanicum factions, particularly the Legio Cybernetica, and reportedly sheltered renegade Tech-Priests and outcasts under Perturabo’s protection. They also established the Thorakata, a highly trained and fanatically loyal human auxiliary army used to enforce their authority. These developments laid the foundations of the Empire of Iron, a militarised domain centred on devastated Olympia that would expand during the Horus Heresy. Meanwhile, the Legion concealed its growing paranoia, hatred, and murderous brutality behind a façade of Imperial loyalty. Its true condition remained largely hidden until the Iron Warriors were summoned to confront Horus at Isstvan V, where their treachery would finally be revealed.

### Battle of Phall

The Battle of Phall was one of many devastating engagements orchestrated by Horus during the opening stages of the Heresy. His strategy relied on coordinated betrayals, ambushes, and surprise attacks designed to cripple Loyalist forces before the Imperium could fully mobilise. Although overshadowed by the massacres at Calth and Isstvan, Phall formed part of this broader campaign to secure a swift Traitor victory. The battle's true significance lay in the enormous losses suffered by both opposing fleets. Two powerful Legiones Astartes naval forces were severely weakened at a critical moment when either might otherwise have decisively influenced the wider war. Their mutual destruction arguably helped transform what Horus intended to be a short campaign into a prolonged conflict lasting years. Beyond its strategic consequences, Phall became a symbol of extraordinary heroism, sacrifice, and tragic opportunity lost. The battle demonstrated the courage of Loyalist warriors confronting betrayal, but also the devastating role of chance in determining the fate of entire fleets. Its legacy endured as a painful reminder of the Imperium's suffering during the Horus Heresy.

Rogal Dorn became the first Primarch to learn of Horus’ betrayal when his returning fleet encountered the Eisenstein, carrying Loyalist survivors fleeing the Isstvan III atrocities. Both fleets had been caught in the same violent Warp storms, leading to their unexpected meeting. Dorn responded by dividing the Imperial Fists between the defence of Terra and a punitive expedition against Horus. Nearly one-third of his Legion’s strength was assigned to Fleet Master Yonnad, reinforced by auxiliary vessels and hunter-killer squadrons from the Sol System’s military reserves. Over 500 warships were assembled into a Retribution Fleet and dispatched towards the Isstvan system through the raging Warp storms. Its mission was to punish Horus for his treachery and deliver the Emperor’s justice, driven by Dorn’s determination to avenge his brother’s betrayal.

The Imperial Fists’ Retribution Fleet never reached Isstvan, instead becoming trapped in devastating Warp storms that scattered and crippled its forces. Forced to seek refuge in the Phall system, barely two-thirds of the original fleet survived the journey, with many remaining warships severely damaged and their crews suffering heavy casualties. The survivors found themselves completely isolated at Phall, surrounded by impassable Warp storms that prevented both escape and astropathic communication with the wider Imperium. Their messages were distorted and reflected back by the turbulent Warp, leaving them unable to request reinforcements or report their situation. The disaster was compounded by the death of Fleet Master Yonnad, one of Rogal Dorn’s most trusted and accomplished commanders, who perished during the passage. The surviving Imperial Fists were now stranded, weakened, and leaderless in a system from which they had no apparent means of escape.

Following Fleet Master Yonnad’s death, Captain Alexis Polux assumed command of the stranded Imperial Fists Retribution Fleet, having been personally designated as his successor. Although relatively young and less renowned than veteran captains such as Tyr, Pertinax, and Scallus, Polux possessed exceptional strategic potential that Yonnad had recognised long before the disaster. Polux immediately transformed the surviving fleet into a formidable defensive force. He committed supplies originally intended for the campaign against Horus to repairing damaged warships and restoring their combat readiness. He then arranged the vessels into a constantly shifting spherical formation at the edge of the Phall system, creating a heavily armed mobile fortress capable of responding rapidly to enemy attacks or escaping through the Warp if the storms subsided. Over the following months, the Imperial Fists maintained extraordinary discipline and preparedness, meticulously studying the system’s gravitational conditions, conducting extensive combat drills, and practising coordinated fleet manoeuvres. Despite repeated attempts to escape, the surrounding Warp storms remained impassable. Polux’s leadership proved decisive, turning a scattered and badly damaged fleet into a highly organised fighting force. His unexpected rise to command would ultimately have profound consequences for the future of the Imperial Fists Legion.

Alexis Polux’s decision to maintain constant defensive readiness reflected the Imperial Fists’ doctrine of disciplined vigilance, but also his growing suspicion that something was wrong with the Phall system. Its two inhabited agricultural worlds, Phall I and II, had been mysteriously depopulated, with no bodies or explanation for the disappearance of their populations. This disturbing discovery suggested that Phall might have been deliberately prepared as a trap, with unnatural Warp storms potentially engineered to strand the Retribution Fleet in a predetermined killing ground. Meanwhile, Polux’s relentless drills and defensive manoeuvres served another purpose: channeling the Imperial Fists’ anger over Horus’ betrayal into discipline and preparation. Their desire for vengeance would soon be tested by an unexpected enemy attack.

Polux’s suspicions were confirmed when the Retribution Fleet suffered a devastating psychic disturbance, spreading agonising visions and screams throughout its warships. Investigations revealed hundreds of hidden devices scattered across the Phall system, each containing a mutilated Astropath connected to powerful psychic amplifiers. When activated, the tortured psykers scanned the system for information before dying, their final screams piercing the surrounding Warp storms to transmit messages and signal the fleet’s presence to an unknown enemy. The discovery strongly suggested that Phall had been prepared as an elaborate trap long before the Imperial Fists arrived. Its vanished populations, manipulated Warp storms, and concealed psychic devices all pointed towards a coordinated plan to isolate the Retribution Fleet and prepare it for destruction.

The Iron Warriors launched a massive surprise attack on the Imperial Fists’ Retribution Fleet at Phall, with an initial vanguard of 100 warships emerging from the Warp and immediately opening fire. The veteran warship Hammer of Terra was among the first destroyed, followed by dozens of smaller vessels. More Iron Warriors ships rapidly arrived, forming an enormous assault fleet. The Imperial Fists were shocked by the betrayal, having been isolated from communications and unaware of the Dropsite Massacre or the defection of several other Legions. Their fleet maintained a constantly shifting spherical formation, designed to respond flexibly to attacks from any direction. Perturabo personally commanded the Iron Warriors from his 15-kilometre flagship, Iron Blood, directing a concentrated wedge-shaped assault intended to overwhelm a single point in the Imperial Fists’ formation. However, the attack initially penetrated rather than shattered their defences, setting the stage for a prolonged naval confrontation.

Despite the overwhelming Iron Warriors assault, Alexis Polux prevented the Imperial Fists’ fleet from collapsing through an unconventional and carefully coordinated defence. Rather than simply holding their formation, he ordered some ships to withstand enemy fire while others appeared to retreat in disorder, deliberately drawing the attackers deeper into the battle. The apparent retreat was a calculated trap. Once beyond the Iron Warriors’ firing range, the withdrawing vessels executed high-speed turns and struck the enemy’s exposed flanks with lance batteries and torpedoes, targeting slower and damaged warships. Although dozens of Imperial Fists vessels were sacrificed to execute the manoeuvre, Polux successfully disrupted Perturabo’s initial assault and transformed a potentially catastrophic defeat into a coordinated counterattack. The Iron Warriors nevertheless continued pressing forward with relentless force.

The Battle of Phall escalated into a devastating war of attrition, with Imperial Fists and Iron Warriors warships exchanging relentless macro-cannon volleys, lance fire, and torpedo barrages. Ammunition supplies intended to last hours were exhausted within minutes, while hundreds of boarding actions erupted as opposing vessels grappled together, sometimes resulting in their mutual destruction. Both fleets unleashed their full arsenals, including swarms of fighters and bombers alongside rarely deployed, proscribed weapons such as volkite implosion-spheres, vortex warheads, and phosphex munitions. Warships were destroyed by the second, and millions of crew members perished. The battle demonstrated the horrifying destructive potential of the Imperium’s own military might, turning the weapons that had conquered the galaxy during the Great Crusade against their former brothers.

Following news of the Dropsite Massacre on Isstvan V, Rogal Dorn desperately attempted to recall the Retribution Fleet to Terra. With multiple Legions destroyed, missing, or revealed as Traitors, and Mars engulfed in rebellion, Dorn feared that the Imperial capital itself might soon face attack. However, violent Warp storms prevented his urgent astropathic orders from reaching the stranded Imperial Fists. At the height of the Battle of Phall, Dorn’s messages finally broke through the storms, their psychic intensity overwhelming the receiving Astropaths. The orders were unequivocal: the Retribution Fleet was to abandon its mission and return to Terra immediately. Unaware of the wider catastrophe unfolding across the Imperium, the Imperial Fists obeyed without question, despite having gained the upper hand against the Iron Warriors and being on the verge of striking at Perturabo himself. Their absolute loyalty to Dorn would ultimately cost them a potentially decisive victory.

Although both fleets at Phall fielded comparable classes of warships, the Iron Warriors possessed a numerical advantage of potentially two to one. Their vessels were generally more heavily armoured and designed for sustained planetary assaults, making them exceptionally durable but less capable of rapid acceleration and manoeuvring. This disadvantage proved significant against Alexis Polux’s unconventional tactics. The Imperial Fists possessed one of the strongest, most diverse, and best-equipped fleets of the Great Crusade. Their Retribution Fleet contained a higher proportion of fast cruisers and battlecruisers, many equipped with powerful long-range lance batteries. They also had access to rare weapons, including vortex torpedoes supplied from Terra. These differences helped the Imperial Fists counter the Iron Warriors’ superior numbers, allowing Polux to exploit his fleet’s greater mobility. However, neither side’s technological advantages alone could determine the battle’s outcome.

Following Rogal Dorn’s orders, the Imperial Fists began withdrawing from the Battle of Phall through a temporary opening in the surrounding Warp storms. Their ships disengaged in stages, while damaged vessels and veteran warships remained behind to delay the pursuing Iron Warriors, sacrificing themselves to protect their brothers. Barely one-third of the Retribution Fleet’s original strength escaped intact, with some returning to Terra and others scattered across the galaxy. Captain Armandus Tyr and his boarding force were effectively abandoned aboard Perturabo’s flagship, the Iron Blood, when the withdrawal deprived them of reinforcements. Although their ultimate fate remains unconfirmed, accounts suggest they reached Perturabo’s own chambers before being slain by the Primarch himself. Meanwhile, Alexis Polux survived by capturing the Iron Warriors warship Contrador after his own flagship, the Tribune, was crippled. The Battle of Phall ended in a costly Imperial Fists defeat, although the Iron Warriors also suffered devastating losses. Dorn’s recall order had reversed what might have become a Loyalist victory, raising the disturbing possibility that its arrival at precisely that moment had been deliberately orchestrated by Horus, his allies, or the dark powers manipulating the Heresy.

### Early Heresy — Campaigns against Imperial Fists

- Horus dispatched Iron Warriors to devastate Imperial territories and assigned trusted Iron Warriors officers to supervise allied Traitor Auxilia operations.
- Perturabo's Legion was ordered to destroy the Imperial Fists' Castellan outposts guarding approaches to Segmentum Solar. The Warmaster exploited the rivalry between Perturabo and Rogal Dorn; Iron Warriors attacks also contributed to the dispersal of the Imperial Fists fleet.
- Before the betrayal at Port Maw, Iron Warriors vessels had held access to operational information at the naval anchorage; defensive codes were subsequently changed.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Iron Warriors, under Perturabo, concentrated on conquering Loyalist fortress worlds protecting the approaches to Segmentum Solar. Detached siege forces subsequently occupied conquered territories and became adept at countering attacks by isolated Loyalist Astartes.

The 114th Grand Battalion suffered severe losses during the Siege of Epsilon-Stranivar IX and the Siege of Vharna; at Mezoa, Autilon Skorr's Alpha Legion made extensive use of Iron Warriors as expendable siege troops. Nârik Dreygur and surviving members later joined the loyalist defence alongside Cassian Dracos.

The Siege of Epsilon-Stranivar IX exposed the growing divisions between Horus' Traitor Legions during the early Horus Heresy. Fortress Stranivar, situated near the Coronid Deeps, was one of the last Loyalist strongholds on the Warmaster's northern flank. Its defenders included a small Imperial Fists garrison reinforced by Raven Guard, Salamanders, Iron Hands, and stranded Blood Angels. Despite overwhelming odds, these forces remained united in their determination to hold the fortress for the Emperor. The attacking Iron Warriors, Death Guard, and Alpha Legion refused to cooperate effectively. Each Legion pursued its own objectives, withheld intelligence and resources, and blamed its allies for setbacks. The Iron Warriors' 114th Grand Battalion suffered particularly badly. Alpha Legion forces repeatedly allowed Perturabo's warriors to assault fortified Loyalist positions without support, exploiting the resulting confusion to strike high-priority targets and claim the glory themselves.

During the decisive Siege of Vharna, the Alpha Legion and Death Guard effectively abandoned the 114th Grand Battalion as a sacrificial rearguard. Of the 6,000 Iron Warriors committed, fewer than 2,000 survived. Their surviving senior officer, Nârik Dreygur of the Apolakron, swore vengeance for his fallen brothers. The Loyalists temporarily held Fortress Stranivar, but the planet fell to a renewed Sons of Horus assault less than a year later. Nevertheless, the campaign revealed a critical weakness in Horus' armies: their inability to overcome rivalry and distrust. Loyalist commanders subsequently exploited these divisions to resist superior Traitor forces, while resentment between the Warmaster's Legions continued to deepen.

Legionary Ker Thelon was among the few replacement Iron Warriors assigned to the severely depleted 114th Grand Battalion shortly before the Mezoa campaign. Unlike the veterans who had endured repeated betrayals by the Alpha Legion, Thelon remained fiercely loyal to Perturabo and Horus, his convictions reinforced by accelerated hypno-indoctrination. This loyalty earned him the distrust of many older warriors, who had grown increasingly resentful of their Traitor allies. Like many newly inducted Astartes during the Heresy, Thelon had never fought anything but fellow Space Marines. Nevertheless, he distinguished himself during the brutal siege of Mezoa, accumulating numerous kills and rising to senior sergeant of the 3rd Shock Assault Detachment. He also became an outspoken rival of Nârik Dreygur.

Following Dreygur's unexpected decision to join Cassian Dracos and the Loyalists during the final battle, Thelon and several similarly loyal Iron Warriors disappeared from the 114th Grand Battalion's records. No evidence confirms that they died in battle, nor is there any official record of punishment by Dreygur or his new Loyalist allies. Their ultimate fate remains unknown.

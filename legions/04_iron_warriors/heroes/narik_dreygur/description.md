## Notes

The conversation between Dracos and Dreygur remains largely unknown, although the Prophecies of the Flames suggests that Dracos possessed an extraordinary influence over technology, including enemy war machines and warriors altered by mechanical augmentation. Following their brief exchange, Dreygur addressed his Iron Warriors and apparently underwent a profound change in allegiance or purpose. His private journals ended that day, and he subsequently avoided all company except that of Dracos and his automata. The precise words that persuaded him remain unrevealed in this passage.

'Brothers, once more an oath to us stands broken. The Alpha Legion has abandoned us once again. Now we shall show that we do not renege on our vows. The dead of Stranivar and Mezoa both call for vengeance, and we shall grant it to them?’

Autilon Skorr's apparent victory at Mezoa collapsed when Cassian Dracos returned to battle alongside nearly 1,000 automata and the remnants of Nârik Dreygur's Iron Warriors, who had unexpectedly turned against the Alpha Legion. Trapped between the reactivated Mezoan defenders and advancing Loyalist forces, Skorr found his army surrounded while Solar Auxilia armoured columns attacked the Traitor landing zones. Meanwhile, Loyalist warships emerged from hiding among the outer moons and ambushed the dispersed Traitor fleet. Within hours, most of the invasion force was destroyed, with fewer than 3,000 Traitors escaping the planet. Skorr himself barely survived, suffering a shattered arm during an encounter with Xiaphas Jurr. Despite initially retaining orbital superiority, the remaining Traitor vessels were eventually driven from the system by Loyalist raids and Mezoa's volcanic bombardments.

Once a respected frontline commander of the Iron Warriors, Dreygur led his company into the fires of battle for ninety years, fearing failure of his Primarch far more than death. At Isstvan, a blank-eyed Raven Guard Moritat left him ruined and Dreygur's defeat cost him the favour of Perturabo, who discarded the broken commander as he would a flawed sidearm.

The Apolakron rebuilt Dreygur's shattered body with augmetic implants, grafting a cortex controller directly into his nervous system and inducting the veteran warrior into the ranks of their Order and its technological mysteries. Dreygur, now known as the Gravewalker, took to the field once again as a Consul Praevian. Shunned by his Primarch and brethren, he quickly came to favour the company of his automaton charges, who showed him more loyalty than those he called 'brothers'. At Epsilon-Stranivar IX, it was the loyalty of these automata that kept him alive to swear revenge upon the Alpha Legion that had abandoned him. Yet through all of these dire events, he remained loyal to his Legion, until Mezoa, where on the basalt plains before the towering Forge Spires, the Dragon Revenant tore down his unflagging loyalty and indomitable determination through some unknown artifice, replacing it with a savage new purpose.

# Blood Angels

## Legion Structure

### IX Legion organisation and the Three Spheres

#### 8. Organisation — the Three Spheres

Before Sanguinius, the IX’s structure was comparatively crude: infantry companies and individual warlords operated with considerable independence, improvising tactics locally and often without consistent strategic oversight. Its early company establishment is described as approximately 200 companies of 300 warriors. After the Primarch’s reforms, its notional structure grew to 300 companies of 500 warriors, though actual field strength fluctuated greatly due to casualties and independent deployments. Companies were grouped into larger Hosts, each assembled for a particular campaign and dissolved or reorganised according to need.

Sanguinius established Three Spheres of responsibility. The Third Sphere contained the Legion’s line companies, specialised formations, and the broad body of fighting men. These warriors were expected to exercise mastery in battle and cultivate the arts and disciplines that gave their service meaning. The Second Sphere comprised senior commanders and advisers—the captains, strategic planners, and leaders who interpreted Sanguinius’ intent and exercised independent judgement when required. The First Sphere, closest to the Primarch, consisted of distinct warrior Orders whose duties placed them directly at his side or in his personal service.

This was a hierarchy of purpose and obligation, not merely of rank. The Legion recognised the ordinary chain of command from Sanguinius through Host commanders, company captains, and junior officers, but its culture placed enormous emphasis on honour, obedience, and the responsibilities of a given Order. The title Archein was used for important captains, while subordinate officers sometimes held distinctions associated with Powers or Virtues, reflecting a system in which excellence was measured in conduct and skill as well as battlefield authority.

#### 9. The Orders of the First Sphere

**The Sanguinary Guard — Ikisat / Burning Ones**

The Primarch’s personal guard. Each individual carried the title Seraph and swore unwavering devotion to Sanguinius. They could occasionally be assigned to other commanders as a mark of the Great Angel’s special favour.

**The Crimson Paladins — Keruvim / Storm Winds**

Guardians of Sanguinius’ halls and sanctums, distinguished by disciplined resolve and enduring anger. At his command, they also served as a shield against enemies on the battlefield.

**The Burning Eyes — Ofanim / Many-Eyed**

Secret watchers and agents tasked with identifying corruption, disloyalty, and emerging madness within the IX. They guarded the Legion’s internal integrity and sometimes operated with unusual weapons and methods.

**The Angel’s Tears — Broken Blade / Dead Hand**

The IX’s Destroyer formations, entrusted with especially devastating weapons and acts of military necessity. They accepted morally burdensome work so that the wider Legion could preserve its ideal of noble warfare.

## Legion Stratagems

### IX Legion martial doctrine and gene-seed

#### 6. A hidden inheritance — gene-seed, hunger, and foresight

Sanguinius had contained, not erased, the IX’s inherited danger. The overactive omophagea and blood-oriented rituals remained embedded in the Legion’s traditions; the temptation toward fury and consumption survived beneath a veneer of discipline and elegance. Much of the Primarch’s reform was thus a continuing act of restraint: to give warriors once praised merely as killers the tools to govern themselves. The Legion was a powerful but precarious achievement, never entirely free of the monstrous impulses that had shaped its birth.

The Primarch also bore an unusual burden of foresight. Visions of possible futures, including dark and fatal outcomes, haunted Sanguinius and sometimes gave him a clarity that could not be explained by normal strategic calculation. Most of his sons did not share this gift, though some were marked by forebodings and eventually known as the Forsaken. Prophecy was an affliction as much as an advantage: knowledge that could cloud judgement and isolate its bearer.

#### 7. Battlefield characteristics and strategic doctrine

The Blood Angels became specialists in orbital insertion, shock assaults, and macro-scale decapitation strikes. Their preferred method was to strike without warning, use drop troops and fast assault craft to overwhelm a crucial position, and break an enemy’s command or cohesion before a prolonged campaign became necessary. Unlike the Revenants’ earlier attrition warfare, Sanguinius’ Blood Angels sought swift, decisive victories that minimised extended exposure to battle. Their infantry favoured close quarters, while fast armour, aircraft, and powerful supporting fire enabled the initial breakthrough.

This concentration on rapid assault gave the IX formidable offensive power but also reduced its flexibility. It excelled when it could choose the place and pace of engagement, but was less comfortable with slow siege warfare, dispersed defensive garrisons, and prolonged static actions. Its fleet consequently prioritised large assault vessels, drop-pod and dropship capacity, and weapons suited to orbital fire support over large numbers of light escort and screening craft.

In the late Great Crusade the Legion numbered roughly 120,000 warriors, operating in separate expeditionary Hosts and supported by substantial Imperial Army and Mechanicum contingents. Its naval assets included over 300 capital ships, many of them heavy cruisers or battleships, with large quantities of assault craft; by comparison it possessed fewer than 600 sub-capital ships, leaving it relatively thinly provided with smaller escorts. The Legion’s principal bases were Baal, Canopus IV, and Saiph, with vital support and equipment also provided by the Mechanicum forge of Anvillus. Baal was the symbolic and administrative heart, Canopus an important manufacturing centre, and Saiph principally a recruiting and custodial station.

### IX Legion pict-records

#### Legionary Scurn — Veteran of the Revenant Legion

Recruited from the devastated Mercian Rift of Old Alba, Scurn belonged to the earliest generation of the IX Legion. Genetic transformation gave him an appearance of almost angelic perfection, concealing the ruthless brutality characteristic of the Revenant Legion before Sanguinius' discovery. He fought in the bloodiest campaigns of the Wars of Unity throughout the Sol system, including the massacres on Venus.

His left greave bore the Pentagram of Vesper, a battle honour earned during the Venus campaigns, alongside informal acid-etched markings. The blood staining his armour was deliberate, originating from the Legion's ritual bloodletting rather than combat.

By the time of the Signus campaign, Scurn was among the few survivors of the original IX Legion, reassigned to the 94th Company as a Centurion and bearer of the Word of Wrath. He died fighting Daemonic horrors in the corrupted forests of Signus Prime, inflicting heavy losses upon the enemy. His final sacrifice earned him and his brothers greater esteem in Sanguinius' eyes than they had enjoyed during their lives.

#### Veteran Sergeant Harum — 202nd Company

Harum, a veteran sergeant of the Blood Angels' 202nd Company, wore battle armour decorated with several distinctions. Particularly notable were the embossed gold icons on his chest and helmet, representing two separate commendations for bravery awarded by an Archein of the IX Legion. His studded right pauldron was probably a battlefield replacement, forcing his rank and company insignia to be displayed on his greaves instead.

One of the few survivors of the destruction of the heavy cruiser Ifrit Nine, Harum subsequently fought alongside his brothers in the desperate defence of the Legion's armoured forces on the far flank of Signus Prime, where he was killed.

The 202nd Company was remembered as one of the few formations capable of resisting the worst effects of the supernatural rage that overwhelmed the Blood Angels. Through the Liturgy of the Blood, its warriors maintained their discipline and resisted their gene-seed's hidden flaw. Harum and his comrades died fulfilling their duty and were honoured as heroes and martyrs of the Legion.

#### Sergeant Cast — Silver Host of the Blood Angels

Sergeant Cast served in the Blood Angels' Silver Host, wearing armour typical of the Legion during the final years of the Great Crusade. His heraldry combined standard Divisio Militaris tactical markings with ornate Legion insignia on the left pauldron and informal Silver Host symbols on the right greave.

His silver helmet and gauntlets reflected a tradition adopted by the 11th Chapter following its achievements during the Absolom campaign, becoming a distinctive mark of pride among its warriors.

During the Battle of Signus Prime, Cast and his Tactical squad fought near the forefront of the Blood Angels' advance against the Daemonic legions. Their ultimate fate remains uncertain. Although fragments of Cast's decorated armour were recovered from a known battlefield, neither his body nor those of his squad were identified.

More mysteriously, Cast and his warriors were omitted from the Legion's official rolls of the fallen. Whether this exclusion represented an unusual honour or deliberate censure remains unknown, as the Blood Angels have refused to clarify their fate.

## Legion Chronicles

### The Revenant Legion and the rediscovery of Sanguinius

The Blood Angels

The IX Legion: from the Revenant Legion to Sanguinius’ reformed hosts

#### 1. Origins — the Dregs of Old Night

The early IX Legion acquired its reputation during the Unification Wars, when the Emperor’s new Legiones Astartes fought as small, highly disciplined vanguards beside larger Imperial armies. Unlike the more prestigious proto-Legions, the IX recruited heavily from the dispossessed, mutated, and socially condemned survivors of Old Earth, including the devastated regions of Alba and Indonasia. These were men exposed to rad-zones, toxic wastelands, and the worst remnants of Old Night—material other Legions often rejected.

The IX Legion’s gene-seed proved unusually capable of transforming such recruits. It could preserve viability in bodies damaged by exposure and mutation, although the Legion’s desperate recruitment system helped create its early reputation as a force of outcasts. The Emperor had assigned it an especially punishing function: campaigns of attrition in places too hazardous for more conventional formations, often with little expectation that the IX would survive intact.

The most disturbing feature of this early Legion was its overactive omophagea, the implanted organ through which Astartes could absorb memories and skills by consuming flesh. Among the IX, this ability encouraged literal consumption of fallen enemies—and, at times, of fallen comrades—alongside ritual blood-drinking. It also enabled remarkably rapid induction of inexperienced recruits, compensating for severe battlefield losses. These habits were simultaneously a tactical expedient, a source of rumours, and the origin of the names “Revenant Legion” and “Eaters of the Dead.”

#### 2. The Revenant Legion — survival through atrocity

During the Great Crusade’s opening decades, the IX was repeatedly committed to savage operations on the moons of Jupiter, the battlefields of the inner Solar System, and other war zones where victory meant surviving a prolonged slaughter. Twelve thousand of its warriors vanished in the fighting around Neptune’s moons; yet when the Emperor’s campaigns returned to that region, the Legion had reconstituted itself from the same marginal populations and was once again operational. Its numbers could grow through battlefield scavenging and relentless recruitment even while its veterans fell at appalling rates.

The Revenants favoured brutal close assaults and grinding extermination, frequently fighting to the last without thought of withdrawal, accepting losses that other Legions deemed intolerable. Their crimson armour, blood-streaked faces, and willingness to consume the dead generated fear among Imperial allies almost equal to the terror they inflicted on their enemies. They increasingly fought at night or dawn, using their appearance and reputation as weapons.

A strange institution reinforced the Legion’s sense of continuity: surviving warriors adopted the names, offices, and sometimes even the identities of dead commanders, creating the impression of immortal warlords. The best-known was Ishidur Ossuros, credited with fighting in 317 engagements and dying on at least four verified occasions. The original bearer is reported to have died in 802.M30; successors inherited his name and reputation. The last known bearer died during the Battle of Teghar Pentaurus, defending Sanguinius and his guard in a sacrificial counterattack.

Even before the Primarch’s arrival, the Legion’s excesses provoked outrage. During the Second Siege of Yarant, the 14th Company was censured by Rogal Dorn after using prisoners as living warnings on fortress walls and consuming defeated foes. Its conduct toward the wounded of the Malagant Rifles was especially infamous. For the IX, however, such actions were seen as methods of war rather than dishonour. The Legion had become effective, feared, and almost impossible to integrate with its brothers.

#### 3. The Red Brothers and the cult of rebirth

The IX’s ability to replenish itself inspired a following known as the Cult of the Reborn. Pilgrims and the desperate trailed the Legion, believing that those condemned by Old Night could be transformed through suffering into the Emperor’s angels. A priesthood called the Red Brothers collected tithes and recruits and preached that sacrifice, blood, and service to the IX offered redemption. Their belief helped fill the Legion’s ranks, but also entwined military recruitment with superstition and coercion.

When the Emperor’s Imperial Truth demanded the destruction of religious cults, the Red Brothers were denounced and their caravans burned. The IX itself turned upon the priests and their followers. The cult disappeared as an organised force, but its influence lingered in Legion rituals and in the conviction that blood, death, and rebirth defined the IX’s destiny.

#### 4. Baal and the discovery of Sanguinius

The Baal system, ruined by the long darkness of Old Night, was a desolate wasteland of irradiated deserts, warring tribes, and the shattered remains of an earlier human civilisation. On Baal Secundus, a winged child was discovered by the people of the Blood. The boy who became Sanguinius grew among them, embraced their struggle for survival, and eventually united them against hostile tribes and the cruelties of the wastes. Whether his wings resulted from Baal’s environment or the Emperor’s original design remains a mystery.

In 843.M30, the Emperor arrived to reclaim His lost son. Sanguinius acknowledged his father but asked that the lives of his followers be spared and their tribal past be forgiven. The Emperor accepted the request and left the Blood undisturbed. The reunion was followed by a gathering of the IX Legion, which assembled over four months on the storm-wracked world of Teghar. There the old, bloodstained Revenants met a Primarch whose radiant appearance seemed irreconcilable with their reputation.

Sanguinius did not repudiate his sons. He recognised their rage and hunger as a burden to be mastered, claimed them as his own, and led them in person against the enemy at Teghar Pentaurus. The spectacle of their winged Primarch fighting beside them inspired a new loyalty—no longer only to the Emperor’s war, but to the ideals and judgement of Sanguinius himself. It was the decisive turning point in the transformation of the Legion.

#### 5. A Legion reborn — from killers to exemplars

Sanguinius reshaped the IX around discipline, learning, honour, and self-control, without discarding its talent for ruthless attack. Warriors were encouraged to become scholars and craftsmen as well as soldiers. The Legion acquired a strong tradition of the fine arts, martial excellence, ceremony, and personal aspiration, bringing the old blood-hunger under conscious restraint. The once-despised outcasts gradually emerged as the Blood Angels, admired throughout the Great Crusade for their noble bearing, courage, and magnificent shock assaults.

The transformation was not instantaneous. For roughly a decade, Sanguinius assigned his companies to fight alongside formations of Horus’ Luna Wolves, learning a more varied style of warfare. At the year-long siege of Anaxis XII, the Blood Angels learned brotherhood fighting alongside the Imperial Fists against the Hrud; on Cambriole and Prehalt, discipline was sharpened against Eldar reavers; and on Kentaurus Beta, Sanguinius taught mercy during the largely bloodless pacification of human colonies. Sanguinius accepted veterans and recruits from both Terra and Baal, while blending the Legion’s separate traditions into a shared culture that could still recognise its violent origins.

Sanguinius’ growing reputation brought extraordinary prestige. His sons became paragons of the Emperor’s vision—masters of speed, close combat, and concentrated assault—while their Primarch inspired devotion even among other Legions. Such admiration carried a danger: some expected the Great Angel, rather than Horus, to be elevated above his brothers. The Blood Angels’ eventual loyalty remained unwavering, but their glory also made them an especially threatening force to the Warmaster’s rebellion.

#### 10. Why the Legion mattered

The Blood Angels’ story is the transformation of a deliberately expendable weapon into one of the Imperium’s most celebrated Legions. Sanguinius did not create their courage or martial ability; he gave those strengths discipline, moral purpose, and a vision of what they might become. Their achievements made them essential to the Great Crusade and later one of the greatest obstacles to Horus, but the same history left them dangerously vulnerable to attacks upon their pride, their love for their Primarch, and their concealed blood-hunger.

The Legion’s defining tension therefore endured: angels in armour, heirs to the Eaters of the Dead. Its magnificent organisation and military discipline did not eliminate the ancient compulsion beneath them. In the wider history of the Heresy, that contradiction would become one of the greatest tragedies—and one of the defining sources of the IX Legion’s resilience.

### Signus Campaign

- Horus directed Sanguinius and the Blood Angels to the Signus Cluster under false pretences. The trap delayed the Legion and left Dorn unaware of their fate.

#### The Battle of Signus

##### At the Gates of Hell: The Battle of Signus

###### Horus' Hatred of Sanguinius

The Battle of Signus stands as one of the most sinister episodes of the Horus Heresy, comparable to the catastrophe at Calth but driven by motives far more personal than military necessity. Rather than merely seeking a strategic advantage, Horus deliberately orchestrated the destruction of Sanguinius, the only Primarch he had ever considered superior to himself. The campaign represented the culmination of a resentment that had developed over centuries and was subsequently exploited by the malign entities allied with the Warmaster.

Horus had spent centuries earning his reputation as a brilliant commander, statesman, and the Emperor's favoured son. From his origins on Cthonia, he had fought countless wars and cultivated the loyalty of his brother Primarchs through immense personal effort. Sanguinius, however, possessed the same qualities with seemingly effortless grace, inspiring admiration and affection wherever he appeared. Where Horus commanded fear and respect, the Angel inspired genuine devotion and celebration. This contrast may have fostered a deep jealousy within the Warmaster, gradually transforming admiration into bitterness. Although the extent to which these feelings contributed to Horus' original betrayal remains uncertain, the planning of the Signus campaign revealed how completely his resentment had developed into hatred.

###### The Corruption of the Blood Angels

At Signus, Horus sought not only to kill Sanguinius but also to corrupt and enslave the Blood Angels, turning the Angel's own sons against their Primarch. To accomplish this, he deliberately unleashed Daemonic entities from the Empyrean, sacrificing the population of the Signus system to fuel the destruction and spiritual corruption of his brother's Legion.

Although some later accounts attempted to attribute these atrocities primarily to Lorgar and the Word Bearers, portraying Horus as another victim of their manipulation, the chronicler rejects this interpretation. At Signus, the Word Bearers were merely instruments of the Warmaster's intentions. Horus understood the nature of the supernatural powers he was unleashing and willingly employed them, offering an entire Imperial system to the Warp in exchange for Sanguinius' death and the enslavement of his warriors.

###### The Shattering of the Imperial Truth

Signus represented a decisive escalation in Horus' alliance with Chaos. Unlike conventional military engagements, the conflict was deliberately engineered around Daemonic intervention, ritual sacrifice, and the attempted spiritual destruction of an entire Space Marine Legion. Horus' jealousy transformed a potentially strategic operation into an act of extraordinary cruelty, leaving a lasting wound upon the Imperium.

The chronicler presents Signus as the moment Horus crossed a boundary that could never be restored. By knowingly opening the way for Daemonic forces to operate against his own brothers, he undermined the Emperor's attempts to conceal and contain the Warp's true nature.

Signus was therefore more than an attempted assassination of Sanguinius: it was an assault upon the foundations of the Imperial Truth, marking the deliberate release of the Daemon as an instrument of galactic war.

##### The Blood Angels at Kayvas

During the final years of the Great Crusade, the Blood Angels were among the largest and most accomplished Space Marine Legions, making them both indispensable allies to Horus and rivals he deeply envied. Their forces were dispersed across several major war zones, including Kayvas, Nartaba, Inconnu, and Balhaut. Sanguinius personally commanded the largest contingent, deployed to the Kayvas system alongside the Alpha Legion to eliminate Ork raiders who had escaped the destruction of Ullanor. Although Horus had not arranged this assignment himself, it suited his purposes by keeping Sanguinius isolated from his growing conspiracies and denying the Blood Angels the battlefield glory they craved.

The campaign at Kayvas was particularly frustrating for the IX Legion. While the Alpha Legion conducted the main offensive within the system's extensive asteroid fields, Sanguinius and his warriors were ordered to maintain a defensive perimeter and intercept fleeing Ork vessels. This role was poorly suited to the Blood Angels, whose greatest strength lay in decisive shock assaults and aggressive close-quarters warfare. Instead, they endured months of inactivity interrupted by sporadic void battles and brutal boarding actions. Despite these limitations, the Blood Angels performed their duties flawlessly, preventing the Orks from escaping the Alpha Legion's encirclement while suffering relatively few casualties.

However, the campaign inflicted a different kind of damage upon the Legion. Thirteen months of waiting, limited combat, and denied recognition gradually poisoned the Blood Angels with wounded pride. When the operation finally ended, the Alpha Legion claimed most of the credit, offering Sanguinius little more than a brief acknowledgement. Many Blood Angels commanders resented receiving such treatment from a Legion whose Primarch they considered beneath their own. Meanwhile, other Legions accumulated victories across the galaxy, and rumours spread that Horus was preparing a major new campaign.

By the time the Alpha Legion departed Kayvas, the Blood Angels were desperate for a worthy assignment that would restore their sense of purpose and martial prestige. This frustration made them particularly receptive to the Warmaster's intentions. When Horus' emissaries arrived with a new call to arms, few among Sanguinius' warriors questioned either its necessity or its true purpose.

The prolonged campaign at Kayvas had therefore prepared the Blood Angels for Horus' trap at Signus, exploiting their pride and hunger for glory rather than weakening them through military defeat.

##### A Hidden Flaw

###### The Warmaster's Orders

Shortly after the conclusion of the Kayvas campaign, Horus dispatched an unusual delegation to Sanguinius, choosing warriors of the Word Bearers rather than his own Sons of Horus. Led by Praetor Kreed, the emissaries delivered orders for the Blood Angels to gather as many of their scattered forces as possible and travel to the distant Signus system, supposedly to repel a xenos invasion. The assignment was welcomed by a Legion frustrated after thirteen months of inactivity, offering the opportunity for the decisive warfare and glory they had been denied at Kayvas.

Sanguinius, however, was suspicious of the mission's unusually destructive objectives. Horus demanded the complete extermination of the enemy rather than the liberation of the system's inhabitants, a task the Angel considered better suited to other Legions. Such direct interference in a Primarch's conduct of war was unusual, though Horus had already issued similar assignments to the White Scars at Chondax, Ultramarines at Calth, and Dark Angels beyond the Eastern Fringe. Despite his reservations, Sanguinius accepted the orders out of loyalty and respect for his brother.

###### The Blood Angels' Genetic Curse

A far more personal reason influenced Sanguinius' decision. The Blood Angels suffered from a concealed genetic flaw that could manifest following extreme trauma or exposure to toxic environments. Afflicted warriors developed extraordinary resistance to injuries and conditions that would normally prove fatal, but at the cost of descending into uncontrollable rage and an overwhelming thirst for blood.

Sanguinius and his senior officers had spent years concealing the defect while searching for a cure. Their efforts included falsifying casualty records and altering the gene-seed tithes dispatched to Terra, ensuring that Imperial authorities remained unaware of the Legion's deterioration.

Horus was among the few outsiders who knew the truth, having discovered the secret during a joint campaign with Sanguinius on Melchior. He had promised to protect his brother's secret and help find a cure, earning the Angel's profound trust.

###### The Trap Within the Trap

Horus and his allies now intended to exploit both the Blood Angels' wounded pride and their hidden genetic weakness. Erebus of the Word Bearers planned to amplify the Legion's flaw through Warp sorcery at Signus, forcing Sanguinius and his warriors into the service of Horus and the Chaos Gods.

However, Horus secretly pursued a different outcome. Driven by jealousy and increasingly influenced by the powers he believed himself to command, the Warmaster worked through his Davinite servants to undermine Erebus' plans and arrange another fate for Sanguinius, one directed toward the Angel's destruction rather than his corruption.

To ensure his brother would enter the trap willingly, Horus claimed that the xenos occupying Signus possessed technology that might help cure the Blood Angels' genetic defect. By invoking his earlier promise of assistance, he transformed an otherwise questionable military assignment into a chance for Sanguinius to save his sons.

The possibility of a cure overcame Sanguinius' remaining doubts. Trusting the brother who had sworn to protect his Legion's secret, the Angel began preparing the Blood Angels for the expedition to Signus, unaware that the Warmaster had deliberately turned their greatest vulnerability into the instrument of their impending destruction.

##### The Gathering of the Blood Angels

###### The Legion Reunited

Following Horus' summons, Sanguinius began assembling the scattered forces of the IX Legion around his flagship, the Red Tear. Such a concentration of Blood Angels had not occurred for years, and the prospect of a major campaign was enthusiastically welcomed by warriors eager to demonstrate their strength after the frustrations of Kayvas. Within a short period, Sanguinius gathered approximately four-fifths of his Legion, numbering more than 100,000 Space Marines, alongside their established supporting forces. The remaining 20,000 Legionaries were stationed at Baal, Saiph, and Canopus IV, or assigned to distant expeditionary fleets beyond immediate recall.

The gathering also included Praetor Kreed and his Word Bearers, travelling aboard the Dark Page as Horus' representatives. Relations between the two Legions remained uneasy following the Word Bearers' humiliation at Monarchia, and Sanguinius rarely invited Kreed to strategic discussions aboard the Red Tear. This exclusion suited the Word Bearers perfectly: Kreed already knew what awaited the Blood Angels at Signus and could conceal both his true intentions and the changes undergone by his warriors without attracting close scrutiny.

###### The Emperor's Watchers

Before the fleet departed, several unexpected Imperial delegations arrived. The first consisted of Space Wolves under Helik Redknife, who claimed to have been assigned as observers by direct order of Malcador the Sigillite. Their presence was received cautiously, given the VI Legion's reputation as the Emperor's executioners. Unknown to Sanguinius, the main Space Wolves force was already travelling towards Prospero to enforce the Emperor's judgement against Magnus. Redknife concealed this information and kept most of his warriors aboard their Stormbird, appearing publicly only with his honour guard and Wolf Priest adviser.

Another delegation brought Kalimak Sebran, a Legio Custodes Prefect bearing the title Vindex-minor, identifying him as an assassin and swordsman of Terra's Ephoroi. Officially, Sebran and his fellow Custodians were assigned to accompany and protect a new contingent of Remembrancers, although this appeared an unusually minor responsibility for the Emperor's personal guardians. Their true purpose remained unclear, and they soon disappeared into the vast assembled fleet.

Both the Space Wolves and Custodes were treated with respect but remained outsiders. Their presence caused the Blood Angels to conceal their private rituals, traditions, and discussions of the Legion's troubled past, maintaining a disciplined and formal appearance whenever these Imperial observers were nearby.

###### The Journey to Signus

The assembled fleet gathered near the pulsar Krzemin, a major Warp transit point providing access to the distant galactic east. Within three months of Horus' summons, the expedition was ready to depart. Reaching Signus required eight separate Warp passages, demanding meticulous coordination to prevent the enormous fleet from becoming scattered across space or displaced in time. Even the most experienced Navigators were pushed to their limits by the complexity of the journey.

On 327.005.M31, Sanguinius officially ordered the commencement of the Signus campaign. More than 100,000 Blood Angels, accompanied by Word Bearers, Space Wolves, Custodes, and numerous supporting forces, entered the Warp aboard their assembled fleet.

They would not be seen again within the borders of the Imperium for five years.

##### The Blood of Angels

###### The Destruction of Holst

Despite the horrors encountered at Phorus, Sanguinius refused to abandon the Signus campaign, determined to uncover the technological secret promised by Horus and rescue any surviving Imperial citizens. Ignoring his officers' warnings, he ordered the fleet toward Holst, a densely populated hive world. Initially, the planet appeared lifeless but stable. However, a reconnaissance mission involving 26 Blood Angels quickly descended into catastrophe as the cities manifested terrifying abnormalities. Survivors described animated urban structures, apparitions of ash and metal, and countless corpses filling the deserted hives. Fewer than half the deployed Legionaries returned.

During their extraction, Holst began launching enormous projectiles of rock and iron directly into orbit, despite possessing no technology capable of such feats. The bombardment appeared deliberately aimed at the Blood Angels fleet: the cruiser Pale Knight was destroyed, while the Hermia and Victus suffered severe damage. With total casualties exceeding several hundred Space Marines and nearly a thousand auxiliaries and crew, Sanguinius finally surrendered to his growing anger. He ordered Exterminatus Extremis against Holst, and the Blood Angels' fleet obliterated the planet.

Yet the destruction brought no relief. Unable to identify or confront the enemy, Sanguinius withdrew into brooding silence, while frustration and aggression spread throughout his Legion. Minor disputes escalated into violence, and among the human crews, murders and suicides became increasingly common. The mysterious influence of Signus was already undermining the Blood Angels' discipline and emotional stability.

##### Unanswered Questions

###### The Expedition to Scoltrum

Seeking answers before approaching Signus Prime, Sanguinius ordered reconnaissance missions to Scoltrum and Ta-loc, two developed inner worlds previously inhabited by millions of Imperial settlers. The expeditions were intended to investigate the catastrophe while remaining small and mobile enough to withdraw if necessary.

To Scoltrum, he dispatched Archein Nakir and the 24th Company, comprising 500 Blood Angels, supported by battle-automata of the Capita Aquilae aboard the light cruiser Alastor. Magos Domina Shurol accompanied them with specialised archival servitors, hoping to investigate the system's unnatural phenomena. The task force was instructed to retreat immediately if Scoltrum displayed planetary-scale abnormalities comparable to Phorus or Holst.

Unlike those worlds, Scoltrum showed evidence of conventional devastation rather than impossible planetary transformations. Its cities were ruined, enormous fires raged across its surface, and agricultural settlements lay abandoned. Almost no life signs remained, but a solitary distress beacon led the expedition to the wreck of the system-defence frigate Stark Dagger. The ship had been shattered across the western continent, with its stern lost at sea and its central decks badly damaged. Nakir investigated the comparatively intact prow, while Shurol secured the wreck's middle section with her automata.

###### The First Encounter

Within the frigate's ruined prow, Nakir discovered a small group of surviving civilians. Almost simultaneously, the Blood Angels recorded their first confirmed encounter with an enemy in the Signus system when a Breacher Legionary killed an unidentified xenos-like creature near the landing zone.

Further attacks followed almost immediately. Hostile entities appeared seemingly from nowhere, assaulting the Blood Angels' transports despite reconnaissance teams having detected no enemy presence. Within minutes, nearly every formation in Nakir's task force was engaged. The Legionaries fought for more than an hour while withdrawing toward their landing craft with the rescued survivors.

Descriptions of the attackers were contradictory, bearing little resemblance to known xenos species and instead evoking monsters from ancient Terran mythology. Even the Blood Angels' pict-recorders captured little reliable evidence.

One particularly significant anomaly emerged: the creatures appeared to deliberately avoid the Mechanicum's battle-automata. Shurol prioritised studying this behaviour over supporting the embattled Legionaries, but even the automata's sophisticated sensors failed to produce coherent records of the enemy. Her only reliable conclusion was that the entities seemed somehow repelled by the machines' artificial nature, despite their effectiveness in combat.

For the first time, the Blood Angels had directly confronted the mysterious enemy of Signus, yet neither their own senses nor the Mechanicum's advanced technology could adequately explain what they were fighting.

##### Shattered Hopes

###### The Disaster on Ta-loc

Sanguinius dispatched elements of the 11th and 94th Companies to investigate Ta-loc, an ocean-covered world whose cities lay partially submerged. The expedition was commanded by Archein Maulgrehn, a promising young officer favoured by the Primarch. The prestigious 11th Company provided the main assault force, while the 94th, composed largely of Terran and Canopian veterans and temporarily commanded by Centurion Aster Crohne following Archein Malad's death at Inconnu, contributed experienced reconnaissance units.

Initially, Ta-loc appeared as lifeless as the other devastated worlds. Maulgrehn dispatched the 94th to survey its submerged cities while personally leading the 11th into a surviving hive spire that showed faint signs of activity. These signals proved to be nothing more than sensor ghosts and vox echoes. Hostile entities suddenly materialised inside the supposedly secure structure, passing through defensive cordons and sealed bulkheads. Trapped approximately 40 storeys underground, the 11th Company was forced to fight its way upward through corridors packed with unnatural creatures. After eight hours of brutal combat, its survivors emerged with ammunition almost exhausted, only to find the landing zones besieged by swarms of bat-like monsters.

The 94th Company's stubborn defence enabled the evacuation, after which Imperial cruisers bombarded the area, although no evidence confirmed that the attackers had been destroyed. Maulgrehn blamed Crohne and the 94th for failing to provide warning or secure the spire, despite the enemy's inexplicable ability to appear without conventional movement. His accusations damaged relations within the Legion and reflected the growing resentment spreading among its officers. Despite considerable losses and repeated investigations, the Blood Angels had rescued only a handful of survivors from a system once inhabited by billions, while learning almost nothing about their enemy.

##### A Dark Sojourn

Conditions aboard the Blood Angels fleet deteriorated rapidly. Isolation from the Imperium, inexplicable losses, disturbing rumours, and the psychological influence of Signus produced widespread fear and aggression. The greatest concern was Sanguinius himself, who had withdrawn into his private chambers aboard the Red Tear, leaving his warriors without the reassurance of their Primarch's leadership.

Different officers attempted to confront the crisis in their own ways. Magos Domina Shurol, aboard the Mechanicum war-ark Sigma 954, studied the unnatural effects on her servitors through dissection and cortical experimentation. Raldoron, the Legion's First Captain, and Elai Jannus of the 202nd Company, known as the Crimson Oracle, searched unsuccessfully for patterns in the enemy's actions. Amit and the Fifth Company channelled their frustration into relentless combat training, while Maulgrehn attempted to restore morale through public declarations of courage and duty. Former Librarians secretly discussed increasingly disturbing dreams, and Aster Crohne, alongside members of the Judiciar and Moritat Orders, hunted for possible infiltrators.

None discovered the truth. Meanwhile, human crew members suffered escalating psychological breakdowns, suicides, violence, and accidents. Sanguinius concluded that further delay would destroy his fleet's effectiveness before the enemy could be confronted. Gathering the commanders of the Legion's Three Hundred Companies, he ordered the armada to advance toward Signus Prime, the system's principal world and the most likely location of their unseen adversary.

##### The Eye of the Storm

Once a prosperous colonial centre, Signus Prime had become a grotesque shadow of its former existence. Its atmosphere was concealed beneath thick, unnatural yellow clouds, while its surface appeared lifeless. The planet's two moons had mysteriously vanished, and its orbit was surrounded by enormous fields of wreckage. These were initially mistaken for the moons' remains but proved to be the shattered vessels of the Signus defence fleet and destroyed orbital installations, their crews reduced to frozen corpses drifting through space.

The planet itself, formerly home to almost three billion Imperial citizens, emitted no signals or indications of surviving civilisation. Every available observation suggested that its destruction had occurred long before the Blood Angels entered the system. This raised a disturbing question: why had Horus sent them to rescue a world whose population and defenders had already been annihilated? Either the Warmaster had deliberately deceived Sanguinius, or he had somehow been misinformed about Signus' fate.

Despite the evidence, Sanguinius struggled to contemplate betrayal by Horus, whom he had trusted without envy or reservation. His loyalty was so profound that he would likely have sacrificed himself and his Legion willingly had the Warmaster requested it in service to the Emperor. The unnatural Veil surrounding Signus prevented him from contacting Horus or seeking clarification, leaving the Angel trapped between his growing suspicions and his unwavering sense of honour.

As the Blood Angels assembled above Signus Prime, an inhuman shriek suddenly erupted throughout the entire fleet, emerging simultaneously from vox systems, cogitator consoles, and even autonomous servitors. It bypassed all communications security and struck every individual aboard, from bridge officers to crewmen sealed within reactor compartments. The sound caused overwhelming pain and terror, yet no recording device captured any trace of it.

The Blood Angels had finally reached the centre of Horus' trap. Their mysterious enemy had demonstrated the ability to attack the entire armada without revealing itself, and the true battle for Signus was about to begin.

##### And Hell Followed with Them

###### The Assault of the Corpse-Fleet

The Daemonic corpse-fleet attacked the scattered Blood Angels armada with unnatural flames that burned in vacuum and enormous claws of bone and metal. Ships isolated by the preceding psychic assault became its first victims. The Crest of Fire, already crippled by a riot in its engine decks, was torn apart, and eight capital ships and nearly a dozen smaller assault craft were destroyed before the Blood Angels could reorganise.

The heavy cruiser Ifrit Nine prevented even greater losses by charging into the advancing enemy formation, shielding other vessels while they recovered. Severely damaged, it withdrew under covering fire from the Mechanicum ark Sigma-954, whose servitor crew had remained operational throughout the crisis. These sacrifices allowed the surviving Blood Angels to regain control of their ships and establish a coordinated defence.

Powerful battleships including the Red Tear, Covenant of Baal, Ignis, and Golden Chalice became the anchors of the Imperial formation. Their concentrated fire destroyed enormous numbers of Daemonic vessels, while smaller cruisers and escorts established overlapping defensive screens. Despite the enemy's relentless attacks and disregard for its own survival, the Blood Angels' superior firepower, discipline, and experience gradually restored the balance. However, the corpse-ships were merely the first element of the trap.

###### The Betrayal of the Dark Page

At the height of the battle, the Word Bearers' Dark Page deliberately abandoned its assigned defensive position, opening a gap in the protective formation surrounding the Red Tear. Its weapons remained silent as Daemonic vessels passed through unopposed. Amid the confusion, many Blood Angels initially assumed their allies had suffered some misfortune rather than committed an act of treachery.

Three enemy vessels advanced upon the flagship. Archein Elai Jannus ordered the already-damaged Ifrit Nine to ram the first, sending both ships plunging toward Signus Prime while escape pods scattered behind them. The Red Tear destroyed the second attacker with its heavy guns, but the third reached the flagship. Its grotesque hull split open and fastened itself to the battleship, unleashing hordes of Daemonic creatures into its interior.

###### The Battle Aboard the Red Tear

The flagship's lower decks were overwhelmed by crawling monstrosities and heavily armoured horrors that slaughtered crew members and isolated defenders. Although several thousand Blood Angels were stationed aboard, their forces were dispersed throughout the immense vessel. The Legionaries closest to the breach sacrificed themselves to delay the attackers long enough for reinforcements to arrive.

The Custodes Ephoroi intervened to rescue several isolated Blood Angels detachments trapped among the wounded in crew compartments. Meanwhile, Thegn Helik Redknife and his Space Wolves fought through the lower decks and successfully sealed the original breach. Together, the Blood Angels, Custodes, and Space Wolves eventually repelled the Daemonic boarding forces, although casualties had been severe.

Yet the most devastating attack had occurred elsewhere.

###### The Fall of the Flagship

While the defenders fought throughout the ship, a psychological battle unfolded on the command deck. One of the Red Tear's officers, whose identity was subsequently erased from Blood Angels records, succumbed to despair, murdered fellow crew members, and destroyed the battleship's primary control systems.

With its command nexus ruined, the Red Tear lost orbital control and fell toward Signus Prime, entering the atmosphere in a trail of fire. Its survival was possible only because of its extraordinary construction. Originally built in Jupiter's orbital shipyards and enhanced by Martian technology, the flagship possessed durability far beyond that of ordinary Imperial warships.

Although the descent scorched its hull and distorted its armour, the vessel's central compartments remained intact as its enormous prow crashed into the corrupted surface of Signus Prime.

The Blood Angels had managed to withstand the corpse-fleet and repel the Daemonic boarders, but the enemy's combined use of psychological warfare, supernatural assault, and Word Bearers treachery had achieved a far greater objective: Sanguinius' flagship had been brought down upon the very world at the centre of Horus' trap.

##### The Birth of Curses and Legends

###### The Final Stage of the Signus Trap

With the Red Tear stranded on Signus Prime, Sanguinius had been drawn into the heart of Lorgar's carefully prepared trap. Isolated from the firepower of his fleet and confronted with the vulnerability of his sons, the Primarch was positioned for the final attempt to unleash the Blood Angels' hidden genetic curse. Yet the enemy's plans had not unfolded perfectly. Rather than collapsing after the loss of their flagship, the remaining Blood Angels fought even more fiercely in orbit, while Sanguinius and the survivors aboard the Red Tear remained determined to achieve an honourable victory.

The climactic battle would later be immortalised in The Angel's Fall, an epic poem by the Remembrancer Aleksandr Pontif, reportedly drawing upon Sanguinius' own writings. However, the chronicle warns that this celebrated account places too much emphasis on the Primarch and its symbolic imagery. The sacrifices and decisions of ordinary Blood Angels Legionaries were equally important in determining the fate of the Legion and the curse unleashed at Signus.

###### The Gathering of 80,000 Blood Angels

While Sanguinius rallied the survivors aboard his fallen flagship, First Captain Raldoron summoned reinforcements from the fleet still fighting above the planet. Despite the continuing Daemonic assault, commanders from across the Legion's Three Hundred Companies eagerly volunteered to descend. Those ordered to remain in orbit obeyed without protest, demonstrating the discipline that distinguished the Blood Angels despite their fierce desire for battle.

A direct planetary assault through the surrounding corpse-fleet would have been suicidal. Instead, the fastest Imperial cruisers staged a diversionary breakout, drawing enemy vessels away from the lower orbital approaches. The manoeuvre was costly: every participating ship sustained damage, while the Dragon's Scale and Zodiac Wheel were so badly crippled that they had to withdraw from combat. Nevertheless, the diversion opened a passage through which larger Imperial ships could deploy Drop Pods and assault shuttles to Signus Prime.

The arriving warriors discovered that the wrecked Red Tear had already been transformed into a fortified stronghold, with Sanguinius' crimson banner flying above its ruined command deck. Approximately 80,000 Blood Angels eventually assembled on the surface, representing almost every Company and Order of the Legion, alongside the surviving Custodes and Space Wolves.

###### The March Against the Enemy Citadel

Reconnaissance forces identified a strange fortress facing the Red Tear, its architecture reflecting the unnatural entities encountered throughout Signus. Crucially, the banners of the traitorous Word Bearers were also visible, confirming their connection to the enemy.

Several commanders advised Sanguinius to strengthen the wrecked flagship's defences and await the outcome of the orbital battle. Sanguinius rejected this cautious approach, determined to strike directly at the fortress and punish those responsible for the destruction of Signus and the suffering of his Legion.

At his command, the Blood Angels prepared for a massive ground offensive, bringing together an extraordinary concentration of the IX Legion for a decisive confrontation. The final battle would determine not only whether Sanguinius escaped Horus' trap, but also whether the Blood Angels could resist the corruption their enemies intended to awaken within them.

##### Sector 056-05RI: In the Jaws of Hell

###### The Advance on the Cathedral of the Mark

The surface of Signus Prime had become a wasteland of foul mud, shattered rock, and relentless sulphurous rain, with visibility reduced to a few dozen feet. Worse, the landscape shifted unpredictably, making conventional mapping nearly impossible. To impose some order on the battlefield, the Blood Angels divided the terrain between the wrecked Red Tear and the Cathedral of the Mark into designated sectors. The central regions, Delta-05 through Eta-08, were selected as the initial objectives, providing a staging ground for the eventual siege of the enemy stronghold.

Despite the horrors already endured, the Blood Angels began their offensive with renewed confidence. Thousands of armoured vehicles assembled around the Red Tear, and Sanguinius personally signalled the advance with his wings spread and sword raised. Land Raiders and Sicaran battle tanks spearheaded the attack, following reconnaissance units that had detected few enemy fortifications. Under normal circumstances, the force could have reached the Cathedral within an hour.

###### The Slaughter of the Survivors

Instead, tens of thousands of surviving Imperial citizens suddenly emerged from the poisoned landscape, attacking the Blood Angels with clubs, stones, and desperate ferocity. Reduced to ragged, seemingly mindless creatures by the horrors inflicted upon their world, these people appeared almost eager to die at the hands of their would-be rescuers. Their sheer numbers overwhelmed the armoured spearhead and halted the advance.

Sanguinius was devastated by the necessity of slaughtering the very population he had come to save. He withdrew from the forefront and ordered his infantry and the Angel's Tears, the IX Legion's Destroyer formations known as the Erelim, to clear the route. Their bolters, flamers, and rotor cannons devastated the attacking masses, but further waves continually emerged. Eventually, obsolete Kratos battle tanks were brought forward from the Red Tear's reserve armouries, their heavy cannons firing canister rounds that opened paths through the crowds.

After nearly three hours of fighting, the Blood Angels had advanced less than 40 kilometres, leaving an enormous trail of dead civilians behind them. The attacks had inflicted relatively little military damage but had delayed and divided the Imperial columns while forcing the Legion into acts of mass killing. For Sanguinius, every death was another reminder of his failure to rescue Signus, deepening the anger and anguish the enemy sought to exploit.

###### The Daemonic Counterattack

As the human hordes began to diminish, Daemonic creatures resembling the horrors previously encountered on Scoltrum and Ta-loc appeared among the attackers. Initially small and easily dispatched, they soon emerged in greater numbers and increasingly monstrous forms, some towering above the Legion's armoured vehicles.

With a recognisable enemy finally before him, Sanguinius returned to the front lines, cutting down enormous Warp-spawned beasts with extraordinary skill. His appearance restored the Legion's confidence, transforming its despair into battle fury. The Blood Angels surged forward to join their Primarch, competing to break through the enemy ranks.

The 5th and 9th Companies, including Terminator-armoured veterans, expended enormous quantities of ammunition, while the warriors of the 5th fought so fiercely that even their combat blades began to dull. Archein Maulgrehn's 11th Company and the veteran 24th battled alongside one another as the ground split open to release fresh hordes of monsters. Despite mounting resistance, casualties remained comparatively light, and the wounded frequently refused evacuation.

###### The Dark Reflection of Sanguinius

Six hours after the offensive began, the Blood Angels reached grid reference Zeta-07, near the centre of the battlefield between the Red Tear and the Cathedral of the Mark. There, awaiting their advance, stood an enormous, grotesque, winged entity that appeared to be a dark reflection of Sanguinius himself.

Accounts differ on the creature's precise appearance, but all agree upon its unnerving composure. As the Great Angel stepped forward and raised his sword in challenge, the monstrous figure merely smiled.

The Blood Angels had successfully pushed through waves of enslaved civilians and Daemonic horrors, but their advance had brought Sanguinius directly before an enemy seemingly prepared for his arrival.

##### Sector 0111-04L: The Rewards of Duty

###### The Left Flank Expedition

While Sanguinius led the main Blood Angels assault toward the Cathedral of the Mark, the 94th Company and the Mechanicum's Capita Aquilae were assigned to secure the army's left flank. Their objective was a dense forest of twisted trees and unnatural vegetation that could conceal enemy forces preparing to attack the main advance. Although the assignment offered little opportunity for glory, Magos Domina Shurol's battle-automata had no concern for prestige, and the reconnaissance specialists of the 94th Company, commanded by Centurion Aster Crohne, were particularly suited to the mission.

The expedition advanced with little ceremony, its mechanical forces transported to Signus Prime aboard three black Maggadon-class landers. The 94th Company still carried the stigma of its previous failures at Ta-loc and was regarded by some of Sanguinius' inner circle as an uncomfortable reminder of the IX Legion's brutal past.

Once inside the forest, the terrain itself turned against the advancing force. Roots erupted from the ground to seize warriors, while enormous thorns pierced even reinforced ceramite armour. Forced to abandon stealth, the Blood Angels employed flamers and heavy battle-automata to carve a path through the hostile vegetation. Unlike the main assault, they encountered no hordes of enslaved civilians, only small groups of monstrous entities resembling creatures from ancient mythology.

Shurol deployed Thanatar siege-automata, whose plasma bombardments incinerated large sections of the forest, while Castellax battle-automata reinforced the 94th Company's defensive formations. Vorax hunter units supported the reconnaissance troops, allowing the expedition to advance more rapidly than Sanguinius' central host.

Within hours, the left flank had reached the heart of the forest and secured its objective. Although sporadic attacks continued, the position remained defensible. As Crohne reported his success, his warriors witnessed a mysterious red light on the horizon, centred upon the hill where Sanguinius had positioned his main force.

##### Sector 09β–11γ: The Unseen Hand

###### The Right Flank Host

The Blood Angels' right flank was assigned a vital supporting role: capture sector Gamma-10 and establish an artillery position overlooking the Cathedral of the Mark. Under Archein Betric Sizal, commanding the armour reserves, and Venerable Hamonah, a Dreadnought formerly known as Lord of the Storm Winds and Principal of the Crimson Paladins, the formation consisted of approximately 300 heavy vehicles, 518 Rapier platforms and other light artillery pieces, and 73 Dreadnoughts.

Its infantry escort comprised 900 surviving warriors from the 146th and 19th Companies, both severely depleted during the orbital battle and subsequent planetary deployment. The 146th consisted primarily of Heavy Support squads, while the 19th specialised in Breacher formations and Zone Mortalis warfare.

Initially, the right flank advanced rapidly across a landscape of compressed rock and low hills, but its slow-moving artillery prevented it from matching the central assault's pace. Like the main force, it was repeatedly attacked by hordes of corrupted Signus civilians, whose purpose appeared to be delaying the advance rather than inflicting significant casualties.

###### The Word Bearers' Ambush

The 19th Company's Breachers spearheaded the effort to break through, using ceramite shields and disciplined bolter fire to push back the human thralls. Their progress was suddenly halted by Word Bearers in slate-grey armour, who emerged from prepared positions and launched repeated ambushes against the artillery columns.

The appearance of fellow Space Marines openly fighting for the creatures that had destroyed Signus shocked the Blood Angels. Despite being outnumbered, the Word Bearers successfully disrupted the right flank's advance, targeting artillery vehicles and forcing the Imperial force into prolonged skirmishes.

By the time Sanguinius confronted the dark winged entity near the centre of the battlefield, the right flank had been pinned down in the foothills of sector Beta-09. Its commanders could not break through without risking substantial losses, leaving the main Blood Angels host deprived of much of its intended artillery support.

As unnatural clouds of ash and lightning gathered over the distant battlefield, the right flank could only watch while the decisive confrontation unfolded beyond its reach.

The two flank operations demonstrated the enemy's wider strategy: the left flank was obstructed by a hostile landscape, while the right was deliberately immobilised by Word Bearers, preventing the Blood Angels from concentrating their full military strength around Sanguinius at the critical moment.

##### Sector 06: Dreams Rendered into Ash

###### Sanguinius and Ka'Bandha

At the centre of the battlefield, Sanguinius and the Blood Angels confronted the true strength of the Daemonic host. Awaiting them stood Ka'Bandha, an enormous bronze-armoured, horned Daemon whose dark wings brought torrents of corrupted blood from the skies. Behind him stretched an apparently endless army of crimson and bronze monstrosities, vastly outnumbering those encountered during the advance toward the Cathedral of the Mark.

Undeterred, Sanguinius led the charge alongside his golden-armoured Ikisat and favoured captains. The Blood Angels' disciplined formations initially smashed through the disorganised Daemonic ranks, advancing under concentrated bolter and missile fire. Yet the enemy's overwhelming numbers soon dragged the Legion into brutal close combat. Horned creatures wielding black iron cleavers attacked the infantry, while immense monsters overturned armoured vehicles and grappled with Dreadnoughts. Despite mounting casualties, the Blood Angels continued advancing, confident that their Primarch would lead them to victory.

When Sanguinius finally confronted Ka'Bandha, the battle transformed into a savage contest between two beings of extraordinary power. Their duel devastated everything nearby, while the Blood Angels increasingly abandoned coordinated tactics in favour of furious individual combat. Across the battlefield, warriors fought with blades and fists against Daemonic claws and talons, consumed by a desire for slaughter that mirrored the conflict between their leaders.

The strange influence surrounding Signus distorted the passage of time, leaving survivors uncertain whether the struggle lasted minutes or hours. All remembered, however, the moment that ended their hopes of victory: Sanguinius fell before Ka'Bandha and lay motionless upon the battlefield.

##### Sector 0111: The Last Bolt-hole of Victory

###### The Awakening of the Legion's Curse

The apparent defeat of Sanguinius unleashed uncontrollable rage throughout the Blood Angels. Some warriors were paralysed by disbelief, while others surrendered entirely to bloodlust. Those closest to Ka'Bandha hurled themselves against the Daemon, only to be slaughtered in their hundreds by a single sweep of his axe, now empowered by the Primarch's blood. The attack intensified the madness spreading through the Legion, breaking the discipline that had previously restrained its violent impulses.

Only the strongest-willed Legionaries retained sufficient control to protect their fallen Primarch. Many were members of the Ikisat, the Sanguinary Guard, who formed a defensive barrier around Sanguinius. The surviving teleportation machinery aboard the wrecked Red Tear possessed enough power for one final operation, allowing them to retrieve the unconscious Primarch and his bodyguards. However, even the flagship's advanced medical facilities and Apothecaries proved incapable of awakening him.

###### The Siege of the Red Tear

With Sanguinius incapacitated and much of the Legion consumed by rage, the Daemonic host redirected its assault toward the stranded flagship. Previously subjected only to scattered raids, the Red Tear was suddenly overwhelmed by enormous swarms of airborne monsters that darkened the sky.

The 221st Company, defending the ship's outer hull under Archein Halbeth, met the attack with entrenched weapons and operational turrets. Within the first hour, however, their ammunition reserves were largely exhausted, forcing them into desperate hand-to-hand combat. Halbeth fought at the forefront until a monstrous rotting raptor seized him and carried him into the air. Although he destroyed the creature and numerous lesser attackers, the subsequent fall killed him, leaving the remnants of his company scattered into isolated defensive pockets.

As the Daemons penetrated the wreck's damaged armour, the Crimson Paladins, known as the Keruvim, and warriors of the Legio Custodes established defensive lines at its entrances and breaches. Their shields and blades repeatedly repelled the attackers, but the overwhelming numbers gradually exhausted them. After several hours, fewer than half their original force remained.

###### The Last Defence of the Great Angel

Within the Red Tear, the former adepts of the Blood Angels Librarius desperately attempted to awaken Sanguinius, struggling against the dark visions of blood and destruction that imprisoned his consciousness. Meanwhile, the Legion's genetic curse continued spreading among the defenders, causing some to attack indiscriminately in uncontrollable fits of rage.

At the entrance to the inner halls, the surviving Crimson Paladins and Custodes rallied around Barbiel, whose hammer was stained with Daemonic blood. Outside, the last warriors of the 221st Company formed a final defensive line around Halbeth's body. Surrounded by seemingly limitless enemies, the remaining Blood Angels prepared to die rather than abandon their Primarch.

The IX Legion stood on the brink of annihilation. Its Primarch lay unconscious, its warriors were increasingly consumed by a supernatural bloodlust, and its final stronghold was collapsing under the Daemonic assault. Even if some survived, the events of Signus had already inflicted a transformation upon the Blood Angels from which they might never fully recover.

##### Sector 030: Codicil 03.23

###### The Last Stand of the Shroudmakers

Following Sanguinius' fall, the Blood Angels' left flank descended into chaos as Daemonic hordes overwhelmed the corrupted forest. Centurion Aster Crohne ordered the remaining warriors of the 95th Company, known as the Shroudmakers, to abandon static defensive formations and return to the mobile warfare practised by the IX Legion before Sanguinius' arrival. Using ambushes, traps, feigned retreats, and repeated hit-and-run attacks, they inflicted heavy casualties while avoiding encirclement.

The Capita Aquilae battle-automata, immune to the supernatural rage afflicting the Blood Angels, proved less adaptable. Their rigid formations endured attacks from multi-limbed horrors, fire-spewing monstrosities, and even maddened Blood Angels. Eventually, enormous horned Daemons broke through their ranks, threatening to collapse the entire left flank. Its destruction would expose the main Blood Angels army to encirclement and potentially annihilate the IX Legion.

###### Magos Domina Shurol's Counterattack

At the critical moment, Magos Domina Shurol personally led a counterattack, wielding a long-handled hammer against creatures capable of destroying Dreadnoughts. Although Daemonic claws severed both her legs, her intervention disrupted the assault long enough for Crohne and the Shroudmakers' elite warriors to repel the enemy.

The victory came at a devastating cost. Fewer than 100 combat-capable warriors remained in the 95th Company, while the surviving Capita Aquilae automata were badly damaged and missing limbs or weapons. The main battle continued across the plains, with Blood Angels and Daemons so thoroughly intermingled that their allegiances were almost impossible to distinguish. Meanwhile, another enormous Daemonic force gathered within the forest, preparing to overwhelm the exhausted defenders.

###### The Final Offensive

During an emergency council of war, Crohne and his sergeants struggled to formulate a plan, their judgement increasingly compromised by the rage unleashed after Sanguinius' defeat. Shurol, whose augmented cognition remained unaffected by emotion, assessed their dwindling ammunition, catastrophic casualties, and lack of reinforcements.

Her conclusion was simple: their only remaining option was to attack.

With no possibility of holding their position against another Daemonic assault, the surviving Shroudmakers and Mechanicum forces launched a final charge into the forest, seeking to strike at the enemy's concentration and delay its advance for as long as possible. Few commanders elsewhere on the battlefield could hear their final transmission, and none were able to intervene.

##### From the Depths of Hell Itself

While the left flank fought for survival, another struggle unfolded deep within the wrecked Red Tear. Sanguinius' wounds, although severe enough to kill an ordinary warrior, could not explain his continued unconsciousness. The injury inflicted by Ka'Bandha was understood to be spiritual rather than physical, leaving the Primarch trapped in a state resembling death.

The Legion's Apothecaries were powerless against such an affliction. Instead, the former members of the disbanded Blood Angels Librarius, whose psychic abilities had been forbidden under the Edict of Nikaea, undertook a desperate attempt to restore their Primarch.

Their intervention represented a third battle within the greater conflict at Signus: a struggle for Sanguinius' soul, fought not through conventional weapons but through the forbidden psychic knowledge of warriors whom the Primarch had previously been compelled to forsake.

Surviving accounts differ on whether Crohne's warriors belonged to the 94th or 95th Company.

##### Sector 10F: In the Company of Death

###### The Collapse of the Right Flank

The Blood Angels' right flank was approaching destruction as Word Bearers and Daemonic hordes systematically overwhelmed its artillery and armoured formations. A lesser Daemon prince breached the defensive perimeter, allowing Word Bearers assassins to penetrate the Imperial lines. Archein Betric Sizal, commander of the armour reserves, was surrounded and killed by Traitors wielding cursed blades, his body poisoned by Daemonic corruption. His death accelerated the collapse of discipline, with many surviving Blood Angels surrendering to uncontrollable rage and charging blindly toward the enemy.

Venerable Hamonah, the ancient Dreadnought of the Crimson Paladins, refused to abandon his oaths. Charging into the Word Bearers' assassins, he crushed several beneath his armoured limbs before confronting the Daemon prince responsible for the breakthrough. Hamonah destroyed the creature with a point-blank plasma blast, but the discharge left his right arm a ruined stump. His resistance temporarily halted the enemy, although the 146th and 19th Companies had been reduced to scattered defenders and frenzied warriors. Those who pursued the retreating Daemons were lured into further Word Bearers ambushes.

###### The Return of Elai Jannus

As the remaining defenders prepared for another assault, an unexpected sound rose above the Daemonic howls: the Liturgy of the Blood, sung by approaching Space Marines. Emerging from the ash-covered wilderness were warriors whose armour appeared black, although traces of crimson and the numerals 202 remained visible beneath the accumulated filth.

They were Archein Elai Jannus and the surviving warriors of the 202nd Company, who had escaped the destruction of the Ifrit Nine and marched across kilometres of corrupted wasteland to rejoin the battle. Their continued recitation of the Liturgy of the Blood had helped them resist the supernatural rage afflicting their brothers.

The newcomers charged directly into the Daemonic and Word Bearers formations, with Hamonah joining their chant through his Dreadnought's vox systems. Their arrival restored the defenders' discipline and fighting spirit, allowing them to repel the attackers. After slaying a Word Bearers Sorcerer-Centurion, Jannus ordered the surviving armoured vehicles to advance. Despite their devastating losses, the Blood Angels' right flank resumed its offensive.

##### Broken Angels and Fallen Wolves

###### The Dark Legacy of Sanguinius' Fall

The fall of Sanguinius had triggered a catastrophic psychological transformation among the IX Legion. Almost every Blood Angel within approximately 1,000 metres of the Primarch lost control of their reason, abandoning formations, heavy weapons, and established tactics to engage the nearest enemies in suicidal close combat. Some charged alone into overwhelming Daemonic hordes, using their bolters as improvised clubs, while entire units deserted their assigned positions and exposed neighbouring formations to attack.

More disturbing were reports of Blood Angels attacking fellow Legionaries, Imperial allies, and even unarmed civilians. These incidents recalled the brutal nature of the IX Legion before Sanguinius had transformed its culture and traditions.

###### The Disappearance of the Space Wolves

One particularly troubling mystery concerned Thegn Helik Redknife and his Space Wolves contingent. Some accounts suggest that warriors of the 5th and 11th Companies, commanded by Amit and Maulgrehn, may have been connected to their disappearance.

The Space Wolves' last recorded position lay between those two Blood Angels formations, yet Redknife and his warriors appeared on no casualty reports, and no accounts celebrated their final stand. The chronicler considers this silence suspicious, implying that the Wolves may have fallen to Blood Angels overcome by their own murderous fury. However, their actual fate remains unconfirmed.

###### The Enduring Curse

The madness was strongest among warriors nearest Sanguinius, many of whom died during their uncontrolled assaults while inflicting enormous casualties upon the Daemons. Those farther from the Primarch generally experienced less severe effects, and many on the battlefield's outskirts retained enough self-control to continue fighting as disciplined soldiers.

Nevertheless, even those who resisted the rage were permanently marked by the experience. The events of Signus exposed the ancient darkness within the Blood Angels' nature, transforming their hidden genetic weakness into a devastating force that threatened their allies as much as their enemies.

The Legion survived through courage and discipline, but Signus revealed that its greatest enemy was not necessarily the Daemonic host before it, but the murderous fury that could awaken within its own warriors.

##### Sectors 05–08: The Wrath of Angels

###### The Collapse of the IX Legion

Following Sanguinius' fall, nearly 50,000 Blood Angels who had witnessed his defeat were overwhelmed by uncontrollable rage. Military discipline collapsed across the central battlefield, reducing the fighting to a chaotic massacre in which even experienced commanders struggled to maintain control. Blood Angels and Daemons fought in sprawling melees across kilometres of wasteland, their identities obscured by blood and gore. Meanwhile, seemingly endless reinforcements poured from the Cathedral of the Mark, threatening to overwhelm the disorganised Legion through sheer numbers.

With the left flank barely holding and the central host incapable of coordinated manoeuvres, the Blood Angels appeared destined for annihilation. Yet the surviving defenders on the right flank had managed to create one final opportunity.

###### The Artillery of the Company of Death

Elai Jannus and the survivors of the 202nd Company successfully broke through the Word Bearers' positions, securing the high ground overlooking the battlefield. They established a defensive perimeter around the remaining artillery pieces and Deredeo Dreadnoughts, which unleashed a devastating bombardment against the Daemonic reinforcements advancing from the Cathedral.

The surrounding Word Bearers and Daemons repeatedly attacked the artillery position, gradually reducing its defenders. Nevertheless, Jannus' warriors continued fighting to buy time for each additional salvo, determined to inflict as much damage as possible before their inevitable destruction. As a symbol of their sacrifice, they marked their ash-blackened armour with crosses of blood in the Baalite tradition, becoming a desperate company of death fighting for one last chance at victory.

###### The March of the Damned

The concentrated artillery fire shattered the approaching Daemonic formations, creating a temporary opening in the enemy lines. Into this gap surged approximately 40,000 Blood Angels, now consumed almost entirely by their supernatural bloodlust.

For the first time since Sanguinius' defeat, the IX Legion began gaining ground. The rage that had destroyed their discipline now drove them forward with terrifying ferocity. They slaughtered the scattered Daemons and pursued the survivors directly into the continuing artillery bombardment, disregarding the explosions and shrapnel tearing through their own ranks.

Their advance was no longer a conventional military operation. The Blood Angels fought solely to kill, finding neither honour nor satisfaction beyond the spilling of blood. Behind them stretched a trail of shattered bodies and shell craters, while the remaining artillery gradually fell silent as enemy forces overwhelmed Jannus' isolated position.

Eventually, the battered Legion reached the gates of the Cathedral of the Mark, where Horus' plan for their destruction and corruption was intended to reach its conclusion. The surviving Blood Angels seemed destined to emerge permanently transformed, their noble traditions consumed by the ancient violence Sanguinius had struggled to suppress.

Yet one participant whom the Daemonic conspirators had already dismissed still had a final role to play. The apparent culmination of Horus' trap was about to be interrupted.

##### Sectors 01α and 10β: An End and a Beginning

###### The Assault on the Cathedral of the Mark

The Blood Angels reached the Cathedral of the Mark not as a disciplined army, but as a vast horde of berserkers driven by uncontrollable bloodlust. Defending its walls were the Unseen Hand, the Word Bearers' 8th Company, their once-grey armour stained with blood and covered in forbidden symbols. Alongside them stood countless Daemonic creatures, awaiting the final assault.

The Blood Angels attacked without regard for tactics or casualties, hurling themselves against the cathedral's enormous walls despite torrents of bolter fire and flames. Terminators and Dreadnoughts smashed openings in the fortifications, but the defenders immediately sealed the breaches with their own bodies or supernatural means. The walls themselves became weapons, extending blades and fleshy limbs or exuding toxic substances to repel attackers.

Hundreds of Legionaries died in futile assaults, while others climbed over the bodies of their fallen brothers. Even the greatest veterans of the Great Crusade succumbed to the madness, abandoning the discipline Sanguinius had spent decades instilling in them.

Eventually, a Leviathan Dreadnought broke through one of the cathedral's gates, sacrificing itself in combat with an enormous, shapeless Daemonic creature. The Blood Angels surged through the opening and tore the monster apart, some even devouring its corrupted flesh.

Inside, the fighting became an indiscriminate massacre. Archein Maulgrehn of the 11th Company was seen standing atop the walls, killing with wild abandon and laughing, his features rendered almost unrecognisable by blood and filth. A lone Centurion of the 271st Company attempted to restrain his maddened brothers, while others hunted Word Bearers through the cathedral with relentless determination.

###### The Scarlet Liberty: A Final Contingency

While the battle raged, First Captain Raldoron and Azkaellon had prepared a last-resort plan for the destruction of Signus Prime. The heavy cruiser Scarlet Liberty remained in geostationary orbit, ready to unleash nova cannon shells and rad-phage munitions upon a prearranged signal.

The bombardment would annihilate everything within at least 100 kilometres, including the Cathedral, the Daemonic host, and surviving Blood Angels. The commanders considered this preferable to allowing the corrupted Legion to fall permanently into the enemy's hands.

However, the cathedral's supernatural protections prevented accurate orbital targeting. Raldoron therefore personally led a veteran team aboard a Storm Eagle to deliver a targeting beacon, taken from the Red Tear's armouries. The aircraft survived the journey partly through the efforts of Elai Jannus and the right-flank defenders, whose Mortis-pattern Dreadnoughts provided vital anti-aircraft cover.

Despite fierce resistance, Raldoron and his warriors successfully breached the cathedral, positioning themselves to assist Sanguinius in the final confrontation.

###### The Return of the Great Angel

Eventually, the frenzy inside the cathedral subsided. No Daemons, Word Bearers, or surviving citizens remained, only mutilated corpses scattered throughout its halls. Yet the Blood Angels' rage had not disappeared. Unable to find new enemies, the survivors began turning against one another, ancient rivalries and imagined insults suddenly becoming reasons for murder.

The IX Legion stood on the verge of destroying itself.

Then a cry arose from the towers: white wings had been sighted above the cathedral. Sanguinius had returned.

His appearance halted the approaching fratricide. Weapons were lowered, and the remaining Blood Angels watched as their Primarch descended toward the fortress's highest tower, where the true masters of Signus awaited.

##### An Angel's Repose

###### The Awakening of Sanguinius

Meanwhile, the wrecked Red Tear had become a graveyard. Its defenders had fought almost to extinction, leaving every corridor filled with corpses. Barbiel, whose armour was stained almost black with blood, had personally claimed numerous Daemonic lives, while the remaining Crimson Paladins, Custodes, and ordinary Blood Angels continued resisting despite their desperate situation.

Their sacrifice had bought sufficient time for the former Librarians and Apothecaries to awaken Sanguinius from the supernatural coma inflicted by Ka'Bandha.

The Primarch awoke to the sounds of battle and the deaths of his sons. More disturbing still was his realisation that many Blood Angels had succumbed to the very genetic curse he had struggled to suppress. Rather than behaving as the noble warriors he had moulded them into, they had become creatures of almost uncontrollable rage and bloodlust.

Sanguinius himself resisted the same temptation. Despite his suffering and the rage stirred by his Legion's destruction, he retained control of his mind and purpose.

He immediately rallied the surviving defenders and departed for the Cathedral of the Mark. Custodes Equinox interceptors and Blood Angels Xiphon fighters cleared a path through the hostile skies, allowing the Great Angel to reach the battlefield.

##### A Laurel of Ash and Regret

###### The Second Confrontation with Ka'Bandha

Atop the cathedral's highest tower, Sanguinius confronted Ka'Bandha once again. The Daemon general remained an enormous, bronze-armoured monster, equipped with supernatural weapons and strengthened by the blood spilled during the battle.

Although Ka'Bandha had defeated him earlier, Sanguinius returned more determined than before. His injuries had not vanished, but the suffering of his sons and the devastation of Signus had hardened his resolve.

The Daemon had hoped to break the Angel through his Legion's rage and despair. Instead, its cruelty had given Sanguinius a powerful reason to fight: to avenge those who had died and save those who remained.

With his silver sword raised, the Primarch challenged Ka'Bandha to settle their struggle once and for all.

The battle for Signus had become something greater than a contest between two champions. Its outcome would determine whether the Blood Angels remained servants of the Emperor or surrendered permanently to the murderous curse awakened within them.

### After Signus and the scattered Hosts

##### Sanguinius and Imperium Secundus

Following their costly victory at Signus, Sanguinius and the Blood Angels travelled toward Ultramar, where Roboute Guilliman was establishing what would become Imperium Secundus. The IX Legion had escaped annihilation, but thousands of its warriors had died, and the survivors carried the psychological and spiritual consequences of their encounter with Chaos.

The chronicler presents Guilliman's ambitions with considerable suspicion. His attempt to preserve Imperial civilisation in Ultramar could also be interpreted as the establishment of a personal empire, and some contemporary observers saw uncomfortable similarities between his growing authority and the ambitions that had corrupted Horus. Nevertheless, Guilliman ultimately placed the reluctant Sanguinius at the head of Imperium Secundus, providing the Blood Angels with a temporary sanctuary.

This respite could not heal the Legion's deeper wounds. The curse awakened at Signus remained within the Blood Angels, and neither their kinship with the Ultramarines nor further victories could eradicate it. Eventually, Sanguinius departed Ultramar for Terra, where the final confrontation of the Heresy awaited at the Eternity Gate.

#### Of the Hosts of the Ninth

Sanguinius had taken approximately five-sixths of the Blood Angels Legion to Signus, leaving slightly more than 20,000 Legionaries scattered across distant garrisons and expeditionary fleets. These warriors remained unaware of their Primarch's fate for years, enduring the opening stages of the Heresy without the leadership of their sire.

Unlike their brothers at Signus, however, these isolated detachments had never experienced the catastrophe that awakened the Legion's hidden curse. Some survivors would later call them the Unblooded, regarding them as relics of an earlier, uncorrupted IX Legion.

##### The Scattered Blood Angels

Baal remained the Legion's principal home world, defended by approximately 3,000 Blood Angels and substantial auxiliary forces. Following Isstvan, the world received survivors of the Shattered Legions and endured growing fears that Sanguinius himself had been killed. Encircled by Traitor forces, Baal survived years of isolation and two major attempts to seize the system before receiving confirmation of the Primarch's survival.

Canopus, the Legion's second most important stronghold, maintained two full companies, thousands of auxiliaries, and industrial facilities approaching the capacity of a minor Forge World. It came under attack by the Sons of Horus around 008.M31. Its final transmission spoke of desperate resistance against overwhelming enemies. When Loyalist forces eventually returned, they discovered only ruins, with no confirmed survivors.

Anvillus hosted a small but exceptionally well-equipped Blood Angels honour guard, maintained through an ancient agreement with the local Mechanicum. During the Heresy, the Forge World was divided by a civil war between rival factions, apparently encouraged by Horus. The fate of its Blood Angels remains uncertain, although Anvillus later established an annual tradition of sending Baal a ceremonial contingent bearing the arms and armour of half a Legion company.

Saiph, a recruiting world defended by only a handful of Blood Angels under Medicae Primus Anastro Kendral, was targeted by the World Eaters. Its garrison initially appeared to have vanished, but hidden Blood Angels subsequently waged a savage guerrilla campaign that obstructed the Traitors' efforts to exploit the planet. The World Eaters eventually abandoned Saiph in 012.M31.

The 106th Expeditionary Fleet, the largest Blood Angels formation absent from Signus, contained five companies alongside a comparable contingent of Word Bearers. Operating in the distant galactic east, it initially remained outside the major battles of the Heresy. By 010.M31, however, Word Bearers outposts near Sarcossa were falling to attacks associated with the returning expedition. Reduced to roughly half its original strength, the surviving Blood Angels fought toward an Iron Hands enclave near Medusa, becoming an enduring threat to Traitor forces in the region.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Blood Angels remained missing until 010.M31. Their deployment towards Signus had been disrupted by the Ruinstorm, scattering thousands of Legionaries. Some were serving as Lamentii honour guards, while others became stranded far from their brothers and believed themselves abandoned.

After Isstvan V, Baal's defenders incorporated survivors of other Legions into provisional battalions. The 7th Battalion included Salamanders and Iron Hands Cataphractii, Salamanders infantry, Raven Guard reconnaissance troops and a Blood Angels company acting as custodians.

Legionary Dre’Haan was one of only three warriors from his cadre to escape Isstvan V, fighting through the Traitor slaughter and reaching one of the few orbital transports that survived the destruction above the battlefield.

He eventually reached Baal with other shattered Loyalist survivors, arriving months after the Dropsite Massacre aboard a battered group of Imperial Army cruisers.

The Blood Angels immediately pressed these survivors into Baal’s defence. With Sanguinius and most of the Legion absent, the Seneschal of Baal organised eight provisional battalions, totalling nearly 8,000 Legiones Astartes, to resist possible Traitor attacks.

Dre’Haan served in the 7th Battalion, a mixed formation that reflected the improvised nature of Baal’s defenders. It included:

a mixed company of Salamanders and Iron Hands Cataphractii

a line company of Salamanders infantry

a demi-company of Raven Guard reconnaissance troops

a company of Blood Angels acting as custodians

His armour had been carefully repaired after Isstvan V, restoring the ornate heraldry of his Legion.

His greave markings show recognised skill both as a smith and as a warrior, while the chest decoration marks him as a proven hunter of the volcanic drakes of Nocturne.

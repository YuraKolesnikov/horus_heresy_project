# Ultramarines

## Legion Structure

From its earliest days, the XIIIth Legion adhered closely to the Emperor's Principia Bellicosa. Its structure consisted of standardised companies of approximately 1,000 Space Marines, grouped into Chapters of ten companies each.

Unlike many other Legions, the XIIIth preserved this organisational system, regarding it as an honoured tradition. After his discovery, Roboute Guilliman refined and expanded the structure rather than replacing it.

Guilliman also developed the Legion's practice of studying and adapting other Legions' tactics. He systematically analysed their battlefield successes, identifying effective methods and improving them through careful planning and experimentation.

His determination to surpass the original practitioners occasionally created friction with other Primarchs, who resented Guilliman's conviction that even their greatest military achievements could be improved upon.

The Ultramarines favoured tactical versatility and overwhelming force, deliberately maintaining relatively few specialised companies. Instead, specialist equipment and weaponry were distributed throughout the Legion.

Every company was designed to operate independently in virtually any combat environment. Chapters maintained balanced formations of tactical and assault troops, armoured vehicles, artillery, gunships, and transports. When combined, these forces could multiply their effectiveness through coordinated operations.

As the XIIIth Legion expanded into numerous independent Crusade fleets, standardisation became increasingly important. Guilliman prioritised reliable, easily manufactured equipment that could be produced in enormous quantities, supplementing it with local designs where necessary.

This emphasis on flexible, self-sufficient formations and efficient logistics would profoundly influence Space Marine organisation after the Horus Heresy.

Although the Ultramarines generally favoured balanced, versatile formations, several Chapters maintained highly specialised capabilities.

The 4th Chapter, known as the Aurorans, specialised in armoured warfare, operating approximately five times as many armoured vehicles as a standard Ultramarines Chapter.

The 22nd Chapter, known as Nemesis, retained strong Terran traditions and unusually large numbers of Destroyer Marines. It was among the few Ultramarines formations regularly assigned to Exterminatus operations.

Guilliman personally disliked the destructive weaponry employed by Destroyer units, believing that rendering conquered territory uninhabitable undermined the purpose of conquest and liberation.

Nevertheless, unlike Vulkan, he never prohibited such weapons entirely. Guilliman recognised that extreme circumstances might require phosphex, oxygen-destroying weapons, or carbosilic cascades, and refused to deprive his Legion of these options.

The Aurorans and Nemesis Chapters demonstrated that Guilliman's commitment to standardisation still allowed specialised forces when they offered a clear strategic advantage.

The Suzerain Invictarus were among the Ultramarines' most prestigious elite formations, serving as the personal retinues of Ultramar's five Tetrarchs. They combined three responsibilities: battlefield honour guards, defenders of their assigned territories, and enforcers of Imperial law.

Their strength varied considerably between Tetrarchs. Tetrarch Amyntas commanded several thousand Suzerains to maintain order around the troubled worlds of Iax, while Tetrarch Eikos Lamiad maintained only 100, relying partly on the powerful Mechanicum forces defending Konor.

Other Invictarii served throughout the Legion's veteran formations and command hierarchy, gaining leadership experience and preparing for higher responsibilities. However, only a small proportion of Ultramarines veterans and officers belonged to this elite organisation.

The Vigil Opertii formed Ultramar's covert internal security service. Operating under the authority of the Invictarus, they suppressed internal threats and supervised the defence militias of the Five Hundred Worlds.

Their operatives were primarily augmented humans who had failed to qualify for service as Space Marines, but remained capable of serving the Imperium in other capacities.

Unlike conventional Ultramarines, the Vigil Opertii specialised in discreet operations where the presence of a Space Marine would attract unwanted attention. Their methods may have been inspired by the covert organisations of other Legions, although they operated under a strict code of conduct.

Together, the Suzerain Invictarus and Vigil Opertii demonstrated how Guilliman integrated military command, civil administration, and internal security into the governance of Ultramar.

The Ultramarines operated under a rigid but highly flexible command hierarchy, with clearly defined responsibilities for every Legionary.

Roboute Guilliman held supreme command, determining the Legion's strategic objectives and personally directing whichever fleet or war zone he occupied.

Beneath him, 25 Chapter Masters each commanded approximately 10,000 Space Marines, supported by senior officers known as the Legatii. Every Chapter contained ten companies of roughly 1,000 Legionaries, each led by a Captain responsible for combat operations, training, and readiness.

Although some Chapters preserved heraldry and traditions from the original Terran-born War-born, Guilliman gradually standardised the entire Legion. By the end of the Great Crusade, all formations followed essentially the same organisational structure.

With approximately 250,000 Legionaries across 25 Chapters, the Ultramarines functioned as a single cohesive military force. Their integrated command system allowed them to assemble enormous armies for major campaigns or divide seamlessly into smaller, independently operating detachments.

This combination of standardisation, discipline, and operational flexibility was central to the XIIIth Legion's extraordinary military effectiveness.

Ultramar's military administration operated separately from the XIIIth Legion's crusading command structure. While Chapter Masters and Captains led the Legion's expeditionary armies, five Tetrarchs governed and defended the realm itself.

Each Tetrarch was appointed by Guilliman as the military ruler of one of Ultramar's five principal worlds, exercising authority over its surrounding sub-sector. Guilliman personally ruled Macragge and Ultramar's core worlds, although his seneschal handled much of their daily administration during his campaigns.

The Legatii held ranks equivalent to Legion Praetors and commanded specialised formations outside the regular Chapter structure. Their responsibilities included directing Ultramar's garrison forces and leading the Tetrarchs' elite Suzerain Invictarus retinues.

Other Legatii controlled vital institutions of Ultramar: one commanded the twin Evocatii Chapters and the training world of Armatura, ensuring a continuous supply of recruits, while another oversaw the Vigil Opertii, Ultramar's powerful and feared internal security organisation.

This parallel hierarchy allowed Guilliman to conduct distant conquests while maintaining effective military control, recruitment, and internal order throughout the Five Hundred Worlds.

Despite being the largest Space Marine Legion, the Ultramarines possessed a comparatively modest naval fleet. Roboute Guilliman preferred to rely on the Imperial Armada for major fleet engagements and large-scale orbital bombardments, while maintaining Legion vessels optimised for planetary invasions and close assault.

The Ultramarines operated fewer than 30–35 heavy capital ships, most of them ageing vessels dating back to the early Great Crusade. Several had been lost in battle, including their original flagship during the Osiris Cluster disaster. Only a handful of newer battleships had been added under Guilliman, notably two powerful Gloriana-class vessels.

The majority of the Legion's fleet consisted of cruisers, smaller battle barges, strike cruisers, frigates, and fast patrol craft, many manufactured in Ultramar's own shipyards.

This composition provided exceptional flexibility for transporting and deploying the Legion's numerous Chapters, but limited its strength in major naval engagements.

In overall fleet tonnage and firepower, the Ultramarines ranked only in the middle tier of the Legions, considerably behind the Imperial Fists in destructive power and the Death Guard in their number of heavy capital ships.

Guilliman deliberately prioritised planetary warfare and operational mobility over naval supremacy, relying on cooperation with the Imperial Armada to compensate for his Legion's relatively limited heavy warship strength.

Before the Battle of Calth, Guilliman recalled 20 of the Ultramarines' 25 Chapters, concentrating approximately 200,000 Space Marines and vast supporting forces for the planned Ghaslakh Crusade. Fourteen Chapters assembled at Saturn before proceeding to Calth.

The betrayal inflicted catastrophic losses on the XIIIth Legion. Of the approximately 200,000 Ultramarines deployed, no more than 40,000 are estimated to have escaped the Calth system. Many others were killed, wounded, or trapped beneath the planet's irradiated surface.

Five Chapters were spared the initial massacre:

7th Chapter: Fighting a dangerous Khrave infestation in the Eastern Fringe.

10th Chapter: Operating too far away on the north-eastern frontier to be recalled.

19th, 24th and 25th Chapters: Stationed across Ultramar, with the latter two belonging to the Evocatii training organisation.

The Ultramarines fleet suffered equally devastating losses. Much of its capital ship and cruiser strength was destroyed, missing, or rendered unfit for combat, leaving the Legion heavily dependent on smaller vessels for transport and defence.

Guilliman immediately began rebuilding his naval strength, appropriating available Imperial Armada warships, modifying them for Space Marine operations, and prioritising the capture of enemy vessels through boarding actions.

The speed of this recovery was extraordinary, demonstrating the organisational resilience that would allow the Ultramarines to remain a formidable military force despite the catastrophe at Calth.

## Legion Stratagems

Observed Strategic Tendencies: Mass Assault, Targeted Decimation, Planetary Interdiction, Liberation and Limited Theatre Compliance Campaigns.

Domains: The Realm of Ultramar (a semi-autonomous administrative region of the Ultima Segmentum, accorded full rights of governance and muster by decree of the Emperor, comprising a division popularly known as the Five Hundred Worlds). Primary Legion headquarters centred on the world of Macragge (strategic command and primary armoury), secondary Legion establishments at Armatura (Legion training and indoctrination hub), Konor (forge and secondary armoury) and Calth (fleet base, under re-construction and expansion at the outbreak of the Heresy). Several outpost stations and their attendant colonies established beyond the borders of Ultramar during the Great Crusade (Honorum, Ulixis, Gathis Secondus, etc).

At the outbreak of the Horus Heresy, the Ultramarines were among the Imperium's most powerful military forces, numbering approximately 250,000 Space Marines and controlling the vast realm of Ultramar. Their greatest strength lay in their exceptional recruitment, training, and logistical infrastructure, which allowed the XIIIth Legion to recover from losses that might permanently cripple other Legions. This immense military potential made the Ultramarines a primary target of Horus' conspiracy, culminating in the devastating betrayal at Calth. Yet the Legion's celebrated reputation concealed a more complicated history. Its rise to power involved secrets deliberately withheld from Imperial records, as well as strained and sometimes openly antagonistic relationships with other Space Marine Legions. These unresolved rivalries and hidden events would ultimately contribute to the tragedy at Calth and influence the wider Horus Heresy.

## Legion Chronicles

### Early Great Crusade — Origins of the XIII Legion

During the early Great Crusade, the XIIIth Legion numbered approximately 8,000 Space Marines and participated in the Emperor's campaigns to reclaim the Segmentum Solar. They distinguished themselves during the Liberation of Diurnus, the destruction of the Scorvidian xenos empire, and independent conquests of worlds such as Lorin Sigma, Sekel's Landing, and Ne'deble.

The Legion expanded by recruiting warriors from newly conquered human worlds, incorporating diverse martial traditions alongside those of its original Terran recruits.

The XIIIth Legion's defining strengths were adaptability, discipline, and operational flexibility. Its warriors combined aggression with restraint, excelling in coordinated campaigns involving multiple military formations and theatres of war.

Unlike many other Legions, the XIIIth willingly assumed command of Imperial Army and militia formations, even assigning Space Marines as disciplinarians and battlefield leaders to maintain order among unreliable auxiliary troops.

Their military doctrine evolved through imitation and adaptation. They incorporated techniques from numerous cultures and fellow Legions, including the knife-fighting traditions of Hive Kor's Opaki gangs and the armoured warfare tactics of the Iron Hands.

Even before Guilliman's discovery, the XIIIth Legion was developing the versatility and organisational discipline that would eventually define the Ultramarines.

By the third decade of the Great Crusade, the XIIIth Legion had developed a distinct military identity, favouring direct, rapid engagements and clearly defined tactical objectives over elaborate theories or unconventional warfare.

The Legion was fiercely loyal but increasingly proud and competitive, measuring its achievements against other Space Marine Legions, especially those already reunited with their Primarchs.

Individual companies began adopting distinctive heraldry to commemorate major victories, while the Legion as a whole retained its original storm-grey and gunmetal armour.

The Nemesis companies, veterans of a campaign alongside the Night Lords against the abhuman corsairs of the Cancerai Nebula, adopted black gauntlets, midnight-blue helmets, and symbols of judgement and death.

Other formations commemorated their victory over Orks at Cypra Mundi, where their armoured forces saved a city of millions. They incorporated emerald markings inspired by the aurora that illuminated the battlefield.

The XIIIth deliberately retained its numerical designation rather than adopting a formal Legion name. First Lord Commander Gren Vosotho sought to transform the number thirteen, once associated with misfortune, into a symbol of Imperial glory.

Their greatest test, however, was still approaching, one that would challenge both their developing military doctrine and their pride.

### Septus XII and the Osiran Psybrids

By 833.M30, the XIIIth Legion had grown to approximately 33,000 Space Marines, operating primarily through the autonomous 11th Expeditionary Fleet.

Two factors enabled this rapid expansion. First, the Legion favoured efficient warfare that minimised casualties and collateral damage rather than prolonged battles of attrition. Second, its gene-seed was exceptionally stable during replication, rivalled only by that of the Ist Legion, allowing sustained recruitment even before the discovery of its Primarch.

While the Emperor, Horus, and Ferrus Manus led the Great Crusade's principal offensives, the XIIIth Legion explored the densely populated regions coreward of Terra. This placed it near the Osiris Cluster when its eleven star systems unexpectedly declared independence from the Imperium.

First Lord Commander Gren Vosotho considered the rebellion a personal affront, since the XIIIth had helped bring the cluster into Compliance years earlier. Determined to end the uprising swiftly, he dispatched approximately forty capital warships towards Septus XII, leaving the fleet's slower support vessels behind.

Led by the Goliath-class battleship Sethatn's Thunder, the Legion easily defeated the system's naval defenders and neutralised its orbital defences without suffering losses.

Vosotho then prepared a decapitation strike against Cabasset, the cluster's political and economic capital. Drawing on tactics perfected by the Luna Wolves, he intended to seize the city's government and environmental control systems, forcing a rapid surrender while preserving its infrastructure.

However, Vosotho's entire strategy rested on dangerously inaccurate intelligence. His assumptions about the rebels' numbers, capabilities, and defences were all wrong, setting the stage for the XIIIth Legion's greatest challenge yet.

The XIIIth Legion's assault on Hive Cabasset initially succeeded, but quickly became a deadly trap. Gren Vosotho's forces encountered thousands of seemingly mindless civilians armed with improvised weapons and suicide explosives. Despite heavy resistance, the Space Marines pushed deeper into the hive and captured several objectives.

Five enormous, hourglass-shaped xenos warships suddenly emerged from the system's star, revealing that the rebellion had been orchestrated by an unknown alien power. Each vessel dwarfed the XIIIth Legion's flagship, Sethatn's Thunder, and wielded devastating particle weapons and gravitational singularities.

The Imperial fleet was overwhelmed. Although it damaged one enemy vessel, approximately twelve Imperial warships were lost, and the Sethatn's Thunder was reduced to a burning wreck.

On the surface and aboard Imperial ships, mysterious xenos entities began materialising. Encased in elaborate biomechanical suits, these barely corporeal beings wielded supernatural-looking energy weapons and devastating psychic abilities. Meanwhile, the controlled civilians attempted to immobilise individual Space Marines through sheer weight of numbers.

Recognising the catastrophe, Vosotho ordered a general withdrawal and remained behind to command the rearguard. Accepting responsibility for his strategic failure, he transferred command of the XIIIth Legion to Marius Gage before sacrificing his life.

Gage's tactical skill prevented a complete disaster. He coordinated the fleet's retreat, keeping the vastly superior alien warships occupied until the surviving Stormbirds escaped from Cabasset.

The XIIIth Legion lost over 6,500 Space Marines, approximately one-fifth of its strength, including many veteran Terran officers and its Lord Commander. A quarter of its fleet was destroyed or permanently crippled, including its flagship.

The defeat at Septus XII shattered the Legion's confidence and exposed the dangers of its increasingly rigid assumptions. Although the XIIIth survived, it had suffered the greatest single-battle losses in its history, and its desire for vengeance against the mysterious xenos enemy remained unfulfilled.

Nearly a year after the disaster at Septus XII, the XIIIth Legion returned to the Osiris Cluster, reinforced by the Solar Armada, elements of the XVIIIth Legion, and specialist anti-psyker units from Terra.

The mysterious enemy, now designated the Osiran Psybrids, had vanished. The Imperials discovered devastated worlds whose populations had been mentally enslaved, abducted, or abandoned to starvation. Other planets had descended into rebellion through the psychic manipulation of their rulers and populations.

Under Marius Gage, the XIIIth Legion reclaimed the cluster and swore vengeance against the Psybrids. However, the defeat had deeply damaged the Legion's confidence. Its warriors became increasingly withdrawn, pursuing victories with relentless determination while avoiding the recognition they had once sought.

### Rediscovery of Roboute Guilliman and the Five Hundred Worlds

The Legion's greatest hope was reunion with its missing Primarch. Unknown to its warriors, the Emperor had already discovered evidence of Roboute Guilliman's existence more than two years before the disaster at Septus.

The Emperor learned of Guilliman while visiting Espandor, a world connected to Macragge. However, powerful Warp storms prevented his fleet from reaching the Primarch for approximately five years.

During this isolation, Guilliman transformed Macragge into a prosperous, highly organised civilisation. He rebuilt its cities, strengthened its armies, established interstellar trade routes, and conquered neighbouring systems to restore order.

Guilliman had already conceived the foundations of Ultramar before meeting the Emperor, drawing inspiration from ancient records of humanity's lost interstellar domains.

When the Emperor finally reached Macragge, Guilliman immediately recognised his origins and pledged loyalty. His exceptional analytical intelligence, administrative abilities, and talent for large-scale organisation impressed Imperial observers.

The reunion promised to transform the XIIIth Legion, giving its warriors the Primarch they had long sought and providing the Great Crusade with one of its most capable strategists and empire-builders.

Roboute Guilliman completely reorganised the XIIIth Legion, combining Macragge's military traditions with lessons drawn from the histories and doctrines of other Space Marine Legions. The result was a highly structured, meritocratic force designed for maximum efficiency and adaptability.

His reforms established two equally important principles: the Practical and the Theoretical. The Practical encompassed courage, discipline, combat skill, and adaptability, while the Theoretical emphasised planning, precedent, analysis, and assessment. Together, they became the foundation of Ultramarines military doctrine.

Guilliman demanded absolute discipline while placing great value on individual Legionaries. Merit and achievement earned promotion and responsibility, but every warrior was expected to obey the chain of command. Unnecessary casualties were unacceptable because each loss weakened the entire Legion.

The XIIIth officially became the Ultramarines, adopting deep-blue armour with gold detailing and the ancient Ultima symbol, linking the Legion to Guilliman's expanding realm.

With Macragge as its new base, Guilliman received independent Crusade command. His reformed 12th Expeditionary Fleet, reinforced with modern Martian warships, launched nearly a century of campaigns across the eastern and northern reaches of the galaxy.

The Ultramarines reportedly liberated more worlds than any other Primarch's forces during this period. Their success rested not merely on military victories but on Guilliman's systematic reconstruction of conquered planets.

Every compliant world received organised government, sustainable defences, and functioning infrastructure, strengthening Imperial control while expanding the Legion's recruitment, industrial, and logistical networks.

This combination of conquest, administration, and long-term development enabled the Ultramarines' extraordinary expansion, transforming them into one of the Great Crusade's most effective military forces.

Under Roboute Guilliman, the Ultramarines experienced unprecedented numerical expansion, combining their exceptionally stable gene-seed with the Primarch's organisational expertise and genetic material.

The Fortress of Hera on Macragge became the centre of the Legion's recruitment system. Candidates were drawn not only from Ultramar but from scores of conquered worlds. Rigorous physical and mental selection standards remained in place, yet the enormous recruitment network produced more initiates than any other Legion.

Guilliman's strategy created a cycle of increasing military strength. Larger armies could overwhelm enemies more efficiently, reducing casualties and allowing further expansion. Even devastating losses against Orks, the Tanaril, and other powerful xenos were generally replaced within a few years.

By Horus' appointment as Warmaster, the Ultramarines were officially the largest Space Marine Legion by a considerable margin. Their immense 12th Expeditionary Fleet was divided into approximately twenty smaller Expeditionary and Persecution fleets, each commanding dozens of warships and thousands, sometimes tens of thousands, of Legionaries.

Only the Word Bearers' secret expansion and uncertain estimates of the Alpha Legion's strength potentially rivalled their numbers.

Ultramar had meanwhile grown into the Five Hundred Worlds, effectively forming a powerful, largely autonomous empire within the Imperium.

This extraordinary strength ultimately made Guilliman and his Legion an unavoidable target for Horus. The Warmaster needed the Ultramarines neutralised, while Lorgar sought revenge for Monarchia. Together, these ambitions would culminate in the betrayal at Calth.

### The Prohibited Zone of Ultramar

The 12th Expeditionary Fleet discovered a mysterious forbidden region beyond Ultramar, containing numerous resource-rich paradise worlds resembling Eldar Maiden Worlds. However, Navigators and Astropaths detected an ancient darkness and an enormous disturbance in the Warp, possibly originating from an event even older than the Fall of the Eldar.

Grand Master Indias Cavalerio of Legio Tempestus insisted on conquering the worlds, deploying Titans alongside Solar Auxilia and Imperial Army forces. Initially encountering no resistance, the Imperials discovered enormous alien gateways hidden among the forests.

A mysterious figure emerged from one gateway and unleashed a devastating plague by shattering a chalice filled with an unknown substance. The infection specifically targeted human genetics, killing Imperial soldiers within minutes and penetrating even the sealed armour of Solar Auxilia. The plague simultaneously appeared across multiple worlds and inexplicably spread aboard orbiting warships.

Only the Titans of Legio Tempestus survived on the planetary surfaces, their protective systems resisting the infection. The expedition withdrew, destroying alien gateways wherever possible. Despite heavy losses during evacuation, the fleet's decisive response reportedly saved more than three million soldiers.

The Council of Terra subsequently declared the entire region forbidden, establishing a quarantine extending thirty astronomical units beyond the affected systems.

Roboute Guilliman later assumed responsibility for enforcing the quarantine, establishing warning beacons and ordering the immediate destruction of any vessel entering or leaving the prohibited region.

The worlds remain uninhabited and strictly forbidden, their mysterious human-killing plague still posing an existential threat to Imperial exploration and settlement.

### Battle of Calth [007.M31]

#### The Shadow War before Calth

Ultramar, ruled by Roboute Guilliman and defended by the XIIIth Legion, was one of the Imperium’s greatest military and industrial strongholds. Comprising over 500 worlds, it possessed enormous manufacturing capacity, hundreds of Imperial Army regiments, and one of the largest Space Marine Legions.

Horus regarded Ultramar as a critical threat to his rebellion. If Guilliman had time to mobilise, the Ultramarines could potentially resist the Traitor armies or attack their rear during an advance on Terra. Destroying Ultramar and crippling the XIIIth Legion was therefore essential to Horus’ strategy.

Lorgar and the Word Bearers had a personal motive for attacking Ultramar. Their humiliation at Monarchia had created a lasting hatred of Guilliman and his Legion. Although the precise architect of the Calth betrayal remains uncertain, Lorgar is widely suspected of devising the plan.

The trap was prepared as early as 005.M31. Under Horus’ orders, the Blood Angels were dispatched to Signus, the Dark Angels to Tsagualsa, and the Ultramarines ordered to assemble alongside the Word Bearers at Calth for a supposed campaign against the Orks of Ghaslakh.

Guilliman gathered his scattered forces at Saturn before departing for Calth, months before Terra learned of Horus’ betrayal. Severe Warp disturbances delayed the XIIIth Legion and prevented Imperial warnings from reaching them.

The Word Bearers arrived at Calth only after most Ultramarines had assembled, having participated in the atrocities at Isstvan. The stage was now set for one of the most devastating betrayals of the Horus Heresy.

#### Battle for Calth Near-Space

The Battle of Calth began with the mysterious capture of the Campanile, an ageing Imperial fleet auxiliary vessel, in the outer reaches of the Veridia System. Its entire crew disappeared or was killed, and the ship fell under the control of an unknown Warp-related force, possibly as part of a ritual orchestrated by Lorgar.

Shortly afterwards, the first Word Bearers warships arrived at Calth, ostensibly to join the Ultramarines for their planned campaign against the Orks.

Hundreds of Ultramarines vessels were concentrated around Calth, with many docked, undergoing maintenance, loading supplies, or operating with their reactors powered down.

The XIIIth Legion was dangerously vulnerable to a surprise attack, its fleet assembled for cooperation rather than combat, while the captured Campanile remained unnoticed.

The Word Bearers deliberately kept their forces separate from the Ultramarines at Calth, positioning their warships beyond the main orbital platforms and landing troop transports at designated camps across the planet.

Contact between the Legions was minimal, limited to an honour delegation aboard the Ultramarines cruiser Samothrace and a brief hololith exchange between their Primarchs.

This separation was a calculated preparation for betrayal, allowing the Word Bearers to position their forces without raising suspicion before launching their surprise attack.

The Battle of Calth erupted when the hijacked fleet tender Campanile accelerated to nearly half its maximum realspace velocity, becoming a devastating relativistic weapon aimed at the densely packed Ultramarines fleet.

The Campanile destroyed approximately twelve Ultramarines warships before striking Calth Veridian Anchor, obliterating the orbital platform and every vessel docked to it. Crucially, the impact also destroyed the cogitator systems controlling Calth's planetary defence grid, leaving the entire system dangerously vulnerable.

The destruction triggered a catastrophic chain reaction. The Ultramarines warship Antrodamicus broke free of its orbital dock and crashed into Numinus City, detonating with atomic force and destroying the Kalkas Fortalice Watchtower, the heart of the Mechanicum's command network.

Within seconds, tens of thousands were dead, and the Ultramarines' naval infrastructure was crippled. Communications failed across more than 900 orbital platforms, thousands of ground installations, and numerous warships. Surviving commanders found themselves blinded, isolated, and unable to coordinate their forces.

The Word Bearers had effectively decapitated Calth's military command and defensive network before firing a single shot, creating ideal conditions for their planned surprise assault.

The Word Bearers' betrayal at Calth became unmistakable as their fleet opened fire on the crippled Ultramarines. The destruction of Calth Veridian Anchor had disabled the Mechanicum's command network, while Traitor Mechanicum agents had secretly sabotaged its backup systems. Many Ultramarines commanders initially believed the disaster was an Ork attack.

Word Bearers warships systematically destroyed the unprepared XIIIth Legion fleet. The Act of Convocation annihilated the cruiser Star of Paramenio, while the Sanctifying Grace crippled the Judicious Truth, sending part of its wreckage crashing into Calth. The Rapture of the Void destroyed the Trials of Anteias using forbidden weaponry, and the battleship Abyssal Communion devastated several Ultramarines frigates. Within minutes, dozens of Traitor vessels were attacking ships whose reactors, shields, and weapons were not fully operational.

Roboute Guilliman initially struggled to believe the Word Bearers were deliberately attacking his Legion. Some officers suspected confusion or accidental retaliation following the destruction of Veridian Anchor. Guilliman eventually established communication with Lorgar, demanding that he cease fire. Lorgar responded with a curse and terminated the transmission, confirming the betrayal.

Guilliman immediately authorised the Ultramarines to return fire. Several XIIIth Legion vessels, including the Gladius, Hope of Narmenia, Valediction, Vosperus, and Antropheles, launched desperate rearguard actions to buy time for the surviving fleet to regroup. All were destroyed.

Meanwhile, a second phase of the Traitor plan unfolded aboard the Ultramarines cruiser Samothrace. The Word Bearers honour delegation that had boarded earlier seized control of the bridge with the assistance of Mechanicum infiltrators.

Ten minutes later, the captured Samothrace accelerated towards the orbital platform Zetsun Verid Yard. Dark Apostle Kor Phaeron personally directed this critical operation, threatening to unleash another catastrophe upon the already devastated Veridia System.

The Word Bearers launched a devastating orbital bombardment of Calth, deploying seventeen bombardment monitors led by Destiny's Hand. With the planetary defence grid under Traitor control, the vessels approached unopposed and began systematically destroying cities, military installations, and population centres.

The southern island cities were completely annihilated, while concentrated energy bombardments vaporised enormous quantities of seawater, creating lethal waves of superheated steam. Within hours, Calth was enveloped in a dense atmospheric shroud that obscured much of its sun's light.

The destruction followed a deliberate ritual pattern. Certain regions were obliterated from orbit, while others, including Numinus City and Ithraca, were preserved for subsequent ground assaults. Later analysis suggested that the Word Bearers were selecting victims and locations according to occult symbolism, using the massacres to fulfil Lorgar's sorcerous designs.

Meanwhile, the Word Bearers assaulted Calth's network of more than 900 orbital defence platforms. Although heavily armed, these installations had been rendered largely helpless by the Traitors' seizure of their central command systems. Some were destroyed outright, while others were deliberately selected for boarding actions.

Word Bearers assault forces penetrated dozens of platforms using boarding torpedoes and assault claws, engaging the defending Solar Auxilia in brutal close-quarters combat.

The Solar Auxilia fought with exceptional discipline, using their sealed armour, carefully prepared defensive positions, and extensive experience in Zone Mortalis warfare. Lasrifle sections conducted organised withdrawals while elite Veletaris troops held corridors with volkite weapons and flamers.

Despite inflicting significant losses, the Solar Auxilia were gradually overwhelmed by the Word Bearers' superior resilience and close-combat capabilities. Their resistance bought valuable time, but the Traitor Legionaries continued advancing deeper into the stations, transforming their interiors into bloody battlegrounds.

The assault demonstrated that Calth's destruction was more than a conventional military operation: the Word Bearers were systematically dismantling Ultramar's defences while conducting massacres according to an elaborate, seemingly supernatural design.

The Word Bearers' boarding assaults revealed the ritualistic nature of the Battle of Calth. Some Legionaries advanced chanting ominous hymns, others mutilated their victims, while many captured defenders for unknown purposes. Their attacks were designed to inflict suffering as well as secure military objectives.

A few Solar Auxilia garrisons managed to destroy their attackers through desperate sacrifices. On Platform Principia-Veridia 27/K, the 222nd Calth Solar Auxilia flooded a boarding chamber with lethal neutron radiation before charging the weakened Word Bearers. Every combatant died. On Elipsia-Veridia 09/Q, Terminator-armoured Word Bearers teleported directly into the command centre, bypassing the defenders. Lord Marshal Turnus of the 255th Solar Auxilia responded by ordering the station's plasma reactor overloaded, destroying the entire platform and its attackers.

The true purpose of many boarding operations was revealed when the Word Bearers captured station commanders alive. Dark Apostles forced these prisoners to witness Calth's destruction before killing them, believing their final memories would serve as supernatural testimony to the powers of the afterlife.

Two hours into the battle, Lorgar contacted Roboute Guilliman aboard the Macragge's Honour. Enraged by the betrayal, Guilliman swore vengeance against his brother. Lorgar, however, appeared transformed, displaying an unsettling confidence and revealing that the attack was part of a galaxy-wide rebellion involving Horus and half the Primarchs, rather than mere revenge for Monarchia.

Lorgar also claimed that three Loyalist Primarchs were already dead, although this would later prove false. Guilliman nevertheless recognised the scale of the rebellion and resolved to kill Lorgar personally.

The conversation ended in a horrifying supernatural attack. Lorgar's hololithic projection transformed into a monstrous entity that somehow manifested physically aboard the Ultramarines flagship. The creature detonated, destroying the bridge's armoured observation dome and blasting its occupants into space.

With Guilliman apparently lost and the bridge of the Macragge's Honour devastated, the Ultramarines suffered another catastrophic blow to their command structure.

#### Betrayal on Calth

The massacre on Calth's surface was a carefully orchestrated genocide, planned years before the Word Bearers' arrival. Rather than a spontaneous act of revenge, the attack formed part of a deliberate campaign serving the Legion's increasingly sinister objectives.

Two years earlier, Guilliman had received orders while his fleet was gathered near Saturn, directing him to assemble the XIIIth Legion at Calth for a supposed campaign against the Orks of Ghaslakh. These orders prevented the Ultramarines from remaining in the Sol System, where their presence might have significantly altered the course of the Heresy.

Gathering the Ultramarines was an immense logistical undertaking. Hundreds of thousands of Legionaries were scattered across distant campaigns. Some formations, including elements of the 10th Chapter and companies of the 7th and 22nd Chapters, could not reach Calth because of their ongoing operations.

Guilliman's journey to Macragge took almost eighteen months, severely delayed by increasingly violent Warp storms. His fleet was stranded twice, and the heavy cruiser Tiger's Heart disappeared with all hands.

Other Ultramarines formations suffered similar disasters. Four companies of the 14th Chapter experienced repeated Gellar field failures, while reinforcements from Accatran and Tigrus were delayed until after the Battle of Calth.

These disturbances were later suspected to be the earliest manifestations of the Ruinstorm, or supernatural echoes of the approaching catastrophe. Whatever their origin, they delayed and dispersed the XIIIth Legion, helping ensure that Guilliman's forces would arrive at Calth isolated and vulnerable.

Calth underwent enormous preparations to accommodate the approaching Ultramarines muster. Facilities were constructed for over 100,000 Legionaries and their war machines, nearly a million labourers were reassigned, and the planet's industries concentrated on producing weapons, ammunition, and military supplies for the planned Ghaslakh Crusade.

By Guilliman's arrival, the Veridia System had become a formidable fortress, protected by minefields, automated weapons platforms, patrol fleets, and the Veridia Maximus starfort. However, these defences were useless against the Word Bearers, who entered freely under the guise of trusted allies.

Erebus arrived almost a month behind schedule, accompanied by warriors of the Flayed Hand Chapter. Unaware of the Isstvan massacres, the Ultramarines attributed the delay to Warp storms. Over the following day, approximately 50,000 Word Bearers and 500,000 auxiliary troops arrived, positioning their warships near vulnerable Ultramarines vessels.

Guilliman had established 68 muster camps and deliberately billeted Ultramarines alongside Word Bearers, hoping to strengthen relations between the Legions after Monarchia. Instead, this placed Traitor forces directly among his unsuspecting warriors.

Under cover of routine military preparations, the Word Bearers stockpiled ammunition, studied Ultramarines deployments, and conducted occult rituals and sacrifices intended to unleash the Ruinstorm.

Some Ultramarines officers noticed suspicious chanting and unusual combat drills, but their warnings were dismissed or punished, as Guilliman's commanders refused to question their supposed allies.

The Word Bearers had infiltrated Calth's defences, positioned their forces for betrayal, and begun preparing a supernatural catastrophe while the XIIIth Legion remained completely unaware.

The Word Bearers began massacring Ultramarines on Calth even before the destruction of the Campanile. In remote muster camps, unsuspecting Legionaries were murdered and their remains used to construct grotesque monuments. Elsewhere, Word Bearers assassination teams eliminated XIIIth Legion officers, disrupting the Ultramarines' rigid command structure before the main attack.

When the Campanile struck, the betrayal became a planet-wide slaughter. Thousands of Ultramarines were killed in their barracks or while assembling for battle. Falling warship debris devastated cities and severed communications, while the compromised Mechanicum network left surviving formations isolated and unable to coordinate their defence.

At Komesh, Foedral Fell's Word Bearers overwhelmed the Ultramarines 9th Chapter. Approximately 15,000 Ultramarines were killed, while 5,000 survivors eventually escaped under Tetrarch Tauro Nicodemus after nearly twenty hours of continuous fighting.

At Erud, Titans of Legio Suturvora devastated elements of the 1st, 2nd and 3rd Chapters. Ultramarines armoured companies counterattacked with Shadowsword and Falchion super-heavy tanks, severely damaging several Titans. Only two Shadowswords and one damaged Falchion escaped with Captain Sydance's retreating column.

At Dainhold, the Word Bearers used waves of mortal auxiliaries to exhaust Ultramarines ammunition. Once the defenders were weakened, Nur Asoktan led Cataphractii Terminators of the Flayed Hand into the manufactoria. The surviving Ultramarines continued fighting with combat blades and improvised explosives, deliberately cutting power to exploit the darkness.

Not every Traitor assault succeeded. At Sylator Province, autonomous Legio Cybernetica battle-automata attacked the advancing Word Bearers after losing contact with their Mechanicum controllers. The 14th Garnide Heavy Infantry subsequently secured the bunker complex.

In northern Thrascias, the Gal Vorbak massacred civilians near the site of Erebus' planned Ruinstorm ritual. However, the Word Bearers failed to neutralise approximately 2,000 Ultramarines Destroyers of the 22nd Chapter stationed nearby.

Unlike other Loyalists, these veterans were accustomed to fighting monstrous enemies and were not intimidated by the Gal Vorbak's unnatural appearance. Believing the Word Bearers had suffered some form of xenos contamination, the Destroyers unleashed phosphex and radiation weapons, turning the Thrascian wilderness into a lethal battlefield.

Despite the catastrophic surprise attack, scattered Ultramarines formations began mounting determined resistance. The Word Bearers' obsession with ritual slaughter also created tactical opportunities that some Loyalist units were able to exploit.

Within twelve hours of the betrayal at Calth, nearly 100,000 Ultramarines had been killed, and the Word Bearers controlled almost every major city and strategic position. Millions of civilians also perished beneath orbital bombardments, falling wreckage, and the destruction caused by battles between the Legions.

The slaughter was part of a vast psychic ritual rather than merely a military campaign. As the death toll increased, Calth experienced unnatural storms, strange auroras, and other Warp phenomena. Eventually, daemonic entities began manifesting, spreading terror even among the normally disciplined Space Marines.

The Word Bearers appeared to regard these manifestations as objectives in themselves. Daemons emerged particularly in areas of intense fighting and mass death, although their attacks rarely contributed significantly to the conventional military campaign.

Meanwhile, the Word Bearers turned Calth's orbital weapons and their own warships against the Veridian star. The resulting surge in solar radiation began burning and poisoning those on the planet's surface. Even survivors who escaped the fighting faced a slow death from radiation exposure.

By nightfall, the Ultramarines faced near-annihilation. Their forces were scattered, their enemies controlled the planet, daemons were emerging from the Warp, and Calth's own sun was becoming a weapon against its inhabitants.

Only five complete Ultramarines Chapters escaped the Battle of Calth, alongside a handful of smaller detachments. These formations became informally known as the Unmarked, while survivors of the massacre carried the psychological scars of the Word Bearers' betrayal.

The Unmarked comprised the 7th, 10th, 19th, 24th and 25th Chapters. The 7th and 10th were engaged in distant campaigns, while the 19th, 24th and 25th, the latter two known as the Evocatii, performed garrison and training duties throughout Ultramar.

A lasting divide emerged between Calth veterans and the Unmarked. Although Guilliman never officially recognised this distinction, survivors increasingly formed their own groups, becoming distrustful, suspicious, and exceptionally cautious in battle.

During the later Scouring, the Unmarked were frequently assigned separate operations, and even Guilliman appeared reluctant to campaign alongside them.

Many were among the first formations separated during the Second Founding, preserving traditions and attitudes closer to those of the Ultramarines before the Horus Heresy.

#### Battle for the Macragge's Honour

The daemonic explosion aboard the Macragge's Honour tore open its bridge and hurled Roboute Guilliman into the void. The Primarch was caught in the creature's writhing remains as he was violently expelled through the breached hull.

The bridge crew suffered catastrophic losses. A dozen senior officers were swept into space, while Shipmaster Zedoff was killed by flying shards of armoured glass.

Chapter Master Vared attempted to rescue Guilliman, abandoning standard void-breach protocols and launching himself into the torrent of debris. He was never seen again.

With Guilliman missing and the flagship's command staff devastated, the Ultramarines faced the apparent loss of their Primarch at the height of the Battle of Calth.

First Chapter Master Marius Gage survived the destruction of the Macragge's Honour bridge, escaping through a sealing blast door while rescuing the grievously wounded Chapter Master Banzor, who died shortly afterwards. The flagship's command tower was collapsing, forcing the few survivors to retreat into the ship's interior.

The Macragge's Honour had been overrun by daemonic entities despite remaining in realspace. Horned, sword-wielding creatures, bloated plague-ridden monstrosities, and deceptively human figures with deadly claws slaughtered the crew. Unfamiliar with the true nature of Chaos, many Ultramarines believed the Word Bearers had unleashed some previously unknown xenos weapon.

The Ultramarines' attempts to organise a defence collapsed. Their command systems were crippled, senior officers were dead, and surviving formations were isolated. Conventional countermeasures proved ineffective, while even concentrated bolter fire frequently failed to stop the attackers.

The daemons displayed no recognisable military strategy, seeking only slaughter. Their supernatural resilience and overwhelming numbers rendered the XIIIth Legion's renowned tactical discipline and analytical methods almost useless.

With Guilliman missing, its command structure shattered, and daemons rampaging throughout its decks, the Macragge's Honour faced destruction from within.

As daemons ravaged the Macragge's Honour, a force of Word Bearers cruisers approached the crippled flagship. They matched its uncontrolled drift and launched boarding grapnels, preparing to breach its heavily armoured hull and finish off the surviving Ultramarines.

Meanwhile, organised resistance began emerging inside the flagship. Chapter Master Klord Empion of the 9th Chapter gathered scattered Legionaries, naval armsmen, and Solar Auxilia, leading a counterattack along Deck Thirty-five. Captain Heutonicus of the 16th Company similarly organised a group of inexperienced Ultramarines on Deck Twenty, although only a quarter of his young warriors survived.

Sergeant Aeonid Thiel of the 135th Company became instrumental in turning the battle. Previously censured for unconventional thinking and marked by a red-painted helmet, Thiel had been awaiting judgement when the daemons appeared. He armed himself with Guilliman's electromagnetic longsword and Kehletai friction axe, discovering that the creatures were far more vulnerable to melee weapons than bolter fire.

Thiel correctly identified the attackers as daemons, recognising them as supernatural entities rather than an unknown xenos species. Gathering several dozen survivors, he coordinated his forces with Empion and Heutonicus, establishing a combined counteroffensive towards the ruined command tower.

During the advance, Thiel rescued First Chapter Master Marius Gage, who had lost his right arm and was suffering from a daemonic wound. Recognising the effectiveness of Thiel's tactics, Gage endorsed his findings and placed him in tactical command while an Apothecary treated his injuries.

The surviving Ultramarines began regaining coordination under Thiel's leadership, developing effective methods against the daemons just as the Word Bearers prepared to board the flagship.

With resistance aboard the Macragge's Honour stabilising, Marius Gage devised a plan to regain control of the flagship. His forces would secure the auxiliary bridge, but they also needed an experienced shipmaster, since Captain Zedoff and most of the original command crew had been killed.

Their best hope was Shipmaster Hammed, captain of the destroyed Sanctity of Saramanth, whose escape craft had been recovered by the flagship shortly before the daemonic incursion.

Sergeant Aeonid Thiel led a rescue mission to the starboard launch deck, while Gage and Chapter Master Empion advanced towards the auxiliary bridge. Despite the diminishing number of daemons, Thiel's force suffered further casualties fighting creatures as powerful as, or stronger than, Space Marines.

Thiel discovered Hammed and the other survivors surrounded by a horde of horned daemons. Acting immediately, he ordered his Ultramarines to open sustained bolter fire, deliberately drawing the creatures' attention away from the trapped shipmaster.

While the daemons attacked his position, Thiel lowered the loading platform carrying Hammed to safety. His warriors maintained fire and withdrew gradually until the survivors were secure, then retreated behind a reinforced blast door.

Hammed's rescue gave the Ultramarines a crucial opportunity to restore command of the Macragge's Honour and potentially bring its formidable weapons back into the battle.

First Chapter Master Marius Gage led his Ultramarines through the Mechanicum-controlled decks of the Macragge's Honour, discovering that Tech-Priests, Skitarii, and battle-automata had suffered heavy losses fighting daemonic invaders.

Inside a sacred machine fane, Gage encountered a group of Mechanicum adepts surrounded by daemons. Their volkite weapons and radiation projectors proved almost useless against the creatures.

Applying Aeonid Thiel's discovery, Gage ordered his warriors to abandon ranged weapons and engage in close combat. Wielding Guilliman's own friction axe, he led a disciplined assault. Although twelve Ultramarines fell almost immediately, the Mechanicum joined the counterattack with ceremonial weapons, servitors, and Castellax battle-automata. Together, they surrounded and destroyed the daemons.

Gage rescued the senior Mechanicum adepts, while Thiel successfully recovered Shipmaster Hammed. The two forces reunited at the auxiliary bridge, bringing together the personnel necessary to restore the flagship's command systems.

Approximately ten hours after the destruction of the main bridge, the Ultramarines regained control of the Macragge's Honour. Shipmaster Hammed activated the auxiliary bridge, restored communications, and re-established contact with Loyalist forces on Calth.

The restored sensors revealed the catastrophic scale of the defeat. Guilliman was still missing, the Ultramarines on Calth had suffered devastating casualties, and only around one-fifth of the original fleet remained battle-worthy.

Although the flagship was operational again, the full extent of the Word Bearers' boarding assault was only beginning to become clear.

Chapter Master Klord Empion organised a counter-boarding assault to free the Macragge's Honour from the Word Bearers. Forty squads of up to thirty Ultramarines each deployed across the flagship's outer hull, tasked with destroying boarding equipment, severing grapnels, and eliminating enemy assault teams.

Fierce fighting erupted across the enormous vessel's exterior. Although the Ultramarines inflicted casualties, their primary objective was sabotage. Three Legionaries in each squad carried melta charges to destroy the Word Bearers' breaching machinery, often sacrificing themselves to complete their missions.

Sergeant Aeonid Thiel's Squad Six was nearly overwhelmed when a much larger Word Bearers force counterattacked. Just as defeat seemed inevitable, Roboute Guilliman suddenly appeared from the void, slaughtering Traitor Legionaries by the dozen and rescuing his sons.

Remarkably, Guilliman had survived approximately ten hours in open space without a helmet or apparent oxygen supply. Even the extraordinary physiology of a Primarch could not fully explain his survival, leaving later Imperial scholars unable to account for the feat.

Guilliman's return transformed the Ultramarines' morale. His warriors eventually persuaded him to abandon his relentless slaughter of the Word Bearers and return aboard the flagship.

For the first time since the betrayal began, the XIIIth Legion had reason to believe it could survive the Battle of Calth.

#### Ashes of Calth

Despite suffering devastating losses, the Ultramarines began organising a coordinated resistance across Calth. The Word Bearers controlled orbital space, most strategic objectives, and the major cities, but their obsession with ritual slaughter and seemingly pointless destruction gave the XIIIth Legion valuable opportunities to regroup.

Tetrarch Tauro Nicodemus led the remnants of the 9th Chapter in a fighting withdrawal across Erud Province. Avoiding major enemy concentrations, his warriors systematically destroyed isolated Word Bearers detachments under Foedral Fell, eventually advancing towards a fragmentary signal originating near Leptius Numinus.

In the northern deserts, Tetrarch Eikos Lamiad, the Iron Warden of Konor, assembled another Loyalist force. His troops included Ultramarines, Imperial Army survivors, Solar Auxilia of the 41st Espandor High Guard, a damaged Contemptor Dreadnought, and a Thanatar siege-automaton. They advanced towards the same rallying point at Lanshear.

In Sharud Province, several thousand survivors of the 11th and 12th Chapters waged a disciplined guerrilla campaign against Word Bearers, Traitor Mechanicum forces, and Titans of Legio Suturvora. Using ambushes, improvised fortifications, and successive defensive positions, they inflicted heavy casualties while gradually withdrawing through the devastated forests.

These scattered formations eventually began converging on Lanshear, forming a Loyalist army numbering in the thousands. Their forces even included a Titan maniple from Legio Oberon, the Deathbolts, giving the survivors a chance to launch a meaningful counteroffensive.

However, the Word Bearers still possessed overwhelming numerical superiority. In Lanshear City, Hol Beloth, commander of the Graven Star, controlled nearly 10,000 Word Bearers, supported by numerous auxiliary troops and several maniples of Legio Suturvora Titans.

The Ultramarines could not hope to defeat this concentration in a conventional battle. Their survival depended on identifying a strategically decisive objective before committing their remaining forces.

The Word Bearers had lost some of their tactical momentum, but the XIIIth Legion had only begun transforming scattered resistance into an organised counterattack.

The fighting across Calth continued without respite, with battles raging through ruined cities, frozen tundra, and burning forests. Even when the Ultramarines achieved local victories, their losses were devastating and fresh Traitor forces soon attacked.

Entire regions were consumed by destruction. Daemonic entities overran Sanachi Province, while Kor Phaeron ordered the Korvel Islands devastated by nuclear bombardment. At Komesh, more than 20,000 Ultramarines lay dead, their bodies and wounded survivors displayed as trophies on Word Bearers vehicles.

At Sylator, the battle-automata of Legio Cybernetica Magenim continued fighting despite exhausting their ammunition, crushing Traitor auxiliaries and mutated Word Bearers with their mechanical limbs.

The defence of Dainhold finally collapsed after nearly twelve hours of resistance. Fewer than 1,000 survivors of the Ultramarines' 8th and 15th Chapters remained, isolated among the ruined manufactoria after enduring repeated assaults by Word Bearers auxiliaries.

The Flayed Hand then deployed 200 fresh Cataphractii Terminators against the exhausted defenders. With ammunition almost depleted, the Ultramarines resorted to improvised traps, industrial equipment, and their remaining power weapons, managing to destroy several Terminators.

The defenders were ultimately annihilated. By dawn, only their corpses remained, mounted on the manufactoria walls as gruesome trophies by the victorious Word Bearers.

#### Battle of Ithraca

The Battle of Ithraca was one of the most devastating engagements of the Battle of Calth, foreshadowing the supernatural horrors that would engulf the Imperium during the Horus Heresy.

Unlike many other battles, Ithraca involved far more than Ultramarines fighting Word Bearers. Titans, colossal war machines, and other legendary Imperial forces participated, suffering catastrophic losses alongside the XIIIth Legion.

The battle ultimately descended into supernatural carnage, as previously unimaginable Warp-born entities appeared alongside the immense war engines, demonstrating the terrifying new nature of the conflict.

Ithraca City was one of Calth's most important military and logistical centres, second only to Lanshear in the scale of its orbital transshipping facilities. Its three enormous landing zones, extensive industrial infrastructure, and millions-strong population made it a crucial staging ground for the planned Ghaslakh Crusade.

The city's primary defensive stronghold was the Ithraca Heliokon, a heavily fortified command centre protected by capital ship-grade void shields, missile batteries, and turbo-laser emplacements. It also served as a backup control station for Calth's planetary defence network.

Ithraca hosted more than 200 Titans from two Legios:

Legio Praesagius (True Messengers), stationed at Landing Zone Macro-Alpha.

Legio Suturvora (Fire Masters), stationed at Landing Zone Macro-Beta.

These forces were supported by extensive repair facilities, ammunition reserves, and specialised machinery capable of maintaining the Imperium's largest war engines.

Two Ultramarines Chapters were also stationed beneath the city: the 4th Chapter (Aurorans), specialising in heavy armoured warfare and possessing its own super-heavy tank division, and the 24th Chapter (Exitium), a strategic reserve formation equipped with substantial artillery and logistical support.

The largest accompanying Imperial Army formation was the Calaq War Host, comprising three divisions of mechanised infantry, five artillery companies, and two cohorts of super-heavy tanks. Another 250,000 Imperial soldiers and Mechanicum units were also gathered around Ithraca.

The concentration of two Titan Legions, two Ultramarines Chapters, and enormous supporting armies made Ithraca one of the most powerful military positions on Calth. Its importance as both a logistical hub and a reserve command centre ensured that the coming battle would involve some of the largest and most destructive war engines deployed during the Heresy.

The Battle of Ithraca began while Legio Praesagius was preparing to embark its Titans for the Ghaslakh Crusade. Approximately 70 god-engines were assembled at Landing Zone Macro-Alpha, with the heaviest Battle Titans already loaded aboard the enormous Titan-conveyor Arutan.

The destruction of Calth's command network by the Campanile provided the signal for the Traitors to attack. Imperial communications collapsed and were replaced by corrupted scrapcode and ominous chanting, while Traitor forces received encrypted orders to begin their assault.

Word Bearers warships launched a precisely coordinated orbital bombardment against Macro-Alpha. Lance strikes and plasma weapons devastated the unprepared Titans of Legio Praesagius, many of which had not activated their void shields.

The Arutan was struck repeatedly while ascending above Ithraca. Although its battleship-grade armour initially withstood the bombardment, a direct hit destroyed one of its four enormous thrusters. The crippled Titan-conveyor crashed through the city's buildings before coming down in the central parklands.

The bombardment's principal objective was the destruction of Ithraca's transport infrastructure. Scores of orbital lifters, gunships, fighters, and shuttles were destroyed on the ground, triggering massive secondary explosions throughout the dockyards.

Simultaneously, Traitor Imperial Army formations turned against their unsuspecting Loyalist comrades. Across Ithraca's barracks, checkpoints, assembly grounds, and vehicle depots, soldiers were murdered by supposed allies. Entire battalions were annihilated by point-blank gunfire, grenades, and armoured vehicles.

Within minutes, Ithraca's carefully assembled invasion force had been thrown into chaos, its Titans bombarded, transport capacity devastated, and hundreds of thousands of soldiers engulfed in a coordinated massacre.

The betrayal at Ithraca intensified as Legio Suturvora, the Fire Masters, launched a surprise attack against their fellow Titans of Legio Praesagius. More than 100 Traitor Titans advanced in a seven-kilometre-wide formation across the Spur Wastes, crushing everything in their path as they approached Landing Zone Macro-Alpha.

The True Messengers remained unaware of the betrayal. Communications were overwhelmed by corrupted scrapcode and disturbing chants, while Imperial forces struggled to distinguish enemies from allies. Meanwhile, the Ithraca Heliokon's Mechanicum personnel desperately attempted to restore control of the city's defences.

The Traitor Mechanicum warship Dirac's Lament then entered orbit above Ithraca, launching precision siege missiles against the city's critical infrastructure. Its principal target was the Ithraca Heliokon, whose void shields had been disabled by scrapcode.

The missiles completely annihilated the Heliokon, destroying Ithraca's main command centre in an immense thermal explosion. Other strikes targeted power generators and security fortresses, further crippling organised resistance.

As the Praesagius Titans turned towards the devastation of the Heliokon, Legio Suturvora struck their exposed flanks. The Fire Masters unleashed a concentrated barrage from over 100 god-engines.

Approximately 20 Praesagius Titans were destroyed in the opening salvo, with another 20 severely damaged. Reactors ruptured, armour shattered, and colossal war engines collapsed under the overwhelming firepower.

Within moments, the True Messengers had lost much of their fighting strength. The attack marked one of the most rapid and devastating massacres of Titans since the Imperium's founding, and the Fire Masters continued their advance.

The Calaq War Host joined the betrayal at Ithraca, emerging from Landing Zone Macro-Gamma to massacre the city's population. Having already murdered all outsiders within their deployment area, the Traitor Imperial Army formations advanced through the streets, crushing civilians beneath their tanks and slaughtering survivors with bayonets and cleavers.

Unlike a conventional invading army, the Calaq made no attempt to capture territory or establish supply lines. Their sole objective was mass slaughter, accompanied by ritualistic mutilations and strange symbols painted in their victims' blood.

Organised Loyalist resistance was rapidly overwhelmed by concentrated assaults from super-heavy tanks, Avenger bombers, and modified gunships.

Meanwhile, Word Bearers reinforcements began descending from orbit. Crimson gunships, drop pods, and Ordo Reductor war machines filled the skies above Ithraca, signalling the next phase of the Traitor assault.

With the Calaq War Host devastating the city and the Word Bearers arriving in force, Ithraca faced destruction from both within and above.

Despite devastating losses, Legio Praesagius maintained its discipline and established a defensive formation known as the Trinity Shield. Fifteen Warlord Titans formed an overlapping wall of reinforced void shields, protecting their damaged comrades from the overwhelming firepower of Legio Suturvora.

Command fell to Master-Princeps Rhiko Trieste aboard the Warlord Titan Auric Pegasus. Recognising that her forces were hopelessly outnumbered, she ordered approximately 33 surviving Titans and 20 Knights to retreat into Ithraca City, where the narrow streets could negate some of the Fire Masters' numerical advantage.

Trieste then led nine Warlord Titans in a suicidal countercharge to cover their withdrawal. Two were disabled before reaching the enemy, but the remaining seven smashed into the Traitor formation, disrupting its advance.

All nine Warlords were ultimately destroyed. According to later accounts, Auric Pegasus was the last to fall, still clutching the severed head of an enemy Reaver Titan. Their sacrifice nevertheless allowed the remaining True Messengers to escape into the city.

Other Praesagius battlegroups experienced different fates. Battlegroup Peregrine was annihilated by missiles from the Dirac's Lament, while Battlegroup Argentus, led by the Reaver Invigitator, survived largely intact. Unable to warn the main force because of communications interference, Argentus withdrew into Ithraca and would later play an important role in the battle.

The Loyalist Taghmata Xerxes also survived the initial assault, retreating into Ithraca's southern industrial district. Supported by allied Knights, its battle-automata and armoured vehicles destroyed thousands of lightly equipped Mordechai Occlusion Traitor troops attempting to block their advance.

The first phase of the Battle of Ithraca ended with the Loyalists badly mauled but still fighting. Legio Praesagius had sacrificed its heaviest Titans to preserve its remaining forces, while surviving Mechanicum units prepared to continue resistance from within the city.

Within the first hour of the Battle of Ithraca, the western districts became a vast battlefield between Legio Praesagius and Legio Suturvora. The surviving True Messengers used the city's towering buildings and narrow avenues to evade the numerically superior Fire Masters, forcing the Titan battle into scattered ambushes, counterattacks, and close-range engagements.

The fighting devastated entire districts. Titans demolished skyscrapers, unleashed inferno weapons across populated streets, and suffered catastrophic reactor explosions. Civilian casualties became incalculable as smoke and toxic vapours plunged the city into an artificial night.

Battlegroup Argentus, comprising approximately twelve relatively intact Praesagius Titans under the Reaver Invigitator, advanced towards the crashed Arutan. Its commanders hoped that something within the enormous Titan-conveyor could provide a means of salvation.

Meanwhile, Ultramarines of the 4th and 24th Chapters began resisting the Calaq War Host in eastern Ithraca. Many Legionaries had been trapped beneath the destroyed Heliokon, but survivors gradually reopened underground access tunnels and returned to the battle. Others had been killed by the initial explosion or buried in collapsed bunkers.

The Ultramarines launched disciplined counterattacks against the Calaq's rampaging infantry. Their power armour rendered most small arms ineffective, allowing them to inflict devastating casualties with bolters and chainswords.

Against enemy armour and combat walkers, the XIIIth Legion exploited elevated positions within the city's buildings, concentrating missile launchers and lascannons against vulnerable targets rather than charging recklessly.

Although the Ultramarines began containing the Calaq War Host locally, the outcome of the larger battle remained uncertain. In western Ithraca, the increasingly destructive clash between Loyalist and Traitor Titans threatened to determine the fate of the entire city.

An unnatural bloodlust spread throughout the Traitor forces at Ithraca, driving Imperial Army units, cultists, and eventually even Titans into increasingly reckless slaughter.

The Taghmata Xerxes came under relentless assault in the southern industrial district. Waves of Traitor auxiliaries overwhelmed prepared killing zones through sheer numbers, exhausting the defenders' ammunition and overheating their weapons.

Word Bearers of the Chapter of the Unspeaking joined the attack, bringing heavy weapons and unfamiliar destructive technologies. Their armour bore disturbing modifications, including bladed horns and censers releasing psychotropic incense. Under mounting pressure, the Taghmata gradually withdrew into smaller defensive positions.

Legio Suturvora also succumbed to the growing madness. Many Fire Masters abandoned coordinated tactics and began massacring civilians instead of pursuing the surviving Titans of Legio Praesagius. They used their powerful war sirens to drive terrified populations from shelter before annihilating them with their weapons.

This loss of discipline inadvertently saved the remaining True Messengers. Rather than concentrating their forces around the crashed Arutan, the Fire Masters scattered across Ithraca in pursuit of slaughter.

Battlegroup Argentus consequently reached the Arutan with approximately twelve Titans, accompanied by fewer than a company of Ultramarines and a tercio of the 312th Solar Auxilia.

Only three enemy Titans defended the crash site: two Warhound Scouts and the formidable Nemesis-class Heavy Battle Titan Revoka, supported by Traitor Skitarii.

Although Argentus enjoyed numerical superiority, the Revoka possessed enough firepower to destroy almost any opposing Titan in a single volley. Recognising that delay would only invite additional Traitor reinforcements, the Loyalists immediately launched an all-out assault to reach the Arutan.

By the sixth hour of the Battle of Ithraca, the Loyalists began recovering ground. Surviving Ultramarines of the 17th and 18th Chapters entered the city despite attacks by the Word Bearers' Black Comet Chapter. Meanwhile, Sub-captain Mantargo of the 4th Chapter established an artillery stronghold along the Trident Aqueduct, directing counter-battery fire and coordinating reinforcements despite severe communications interference.

At the wreck of the Arutan, Battlegroup Argentus defeated the formidable Traitor Titan Revoka, but suffered devastating losses. The Warlord Evocatus and Warhound Deathrunner were destroyed, while the surviving Invigilator was badly damaged. The remaining Titans desperately defended the crash site as engineers attempted to open the transport's sealed assault bay.

In southern Ithraca, the Taghmata Xerxes faced annihilation. Despite assistance from Loyalist Knights of House Vornherr, they were trapped between Word Bearers-led cultist hordes and the Traitor Ordo Reductor forces of Covenant Mormoth-Null.

Recognising that victory was impossible, the Taghmata converted an industrial plasma reactor into an enormous bomb. At approximately 7.6 hours into the battle, their remaining Castellax and Thanatar automata delivered the device into the enemy formations. Its detonation vaporised everything within roughly half a kilometre and devastated another 23 city blocks, destroying the Loyalist position alongside its attackers.

Meanwhile, the sacrifice of Battlegroup Argentus finally paid off. Shortly before the seventh hour, the Arutan's assault doors opened, releasing the surviving heavy Titans of Legio Praesagius.

Among them was the Immortalis Domitor, a Warmonger-class Emperor Titan, accompanied by two complete maniples of Warlord Titans. Their opening salvo annihilated four Fire Masters Battle Titans: Deus Vassago, Orias, Consecratus Inferna, and Tyrant's Fist.

The arrival of the Immortalis Domitor decisively reversed the Titan battle. The True Messengers advanced through western Ithraca as a coordinated formation, overcoming communications interference by using their war sirens to transmit signals. Against the increasingly disorganised and blood-crazed Fire Masters, their concentrated firepower proved overwhelming.

The Ultramarines exploited the shifting battle, launching targeted counterattacks and beginning limited civilian evacuations through underground routes to arcologies west of the city.

By the eighth hour, the Loyalists were unexpectedly retaking Ithraca. The Traitor forces were descending into disorder, while Legio Praesagius and the Ultramarines had regained the initiative.

Just as the Loyalists began regaining control of Ithraca, the unnatural vox interference abruptly ceased. Communications resumed across the city, allowing isolated Ultramarines and Titan formations to reconnect with their commanders.

The Legio Praesagius Warhound Titan Lacardio was dispatched to investigate the devastated industrial district, where the Taghmata Xerxes had detonated their improvised plasma bomb.

Its crew discovered deeply disturbing phenomena. Burned bodies and twisted metal had fused into unnatural, tree-like structures, while the Titan's sensors registered impossible fluctuations in life signs and energy readings.

The Lacardio's final transmission described an enormous winged figure emerging from a blood-red fog, taller than a Titan and seemingly alive despite its impossible appearance. Its crew reported thousands of anomalous readings and failing armour integrity before their signal abruptly ended.

The Lacardio was never heard from again. Its disappearance marked the beginning of a new supernatural catastrophe at Ithraca, threatening to overturn the Loyalists' hard-won recovery.

After the eighth hour, the Battle of Ithraca descended into a supernatural catastrophe. Multiple breaches opened between reality and the Warp, unleashing thousands of daemonic entities across the city. The largest rupture appeared where the Taghmata Xerxes had detonated their plasma bomb.

The Word Bearers' earlier massacres had apparently been designed to create these Warp breaches. The locations of the incursions corresponded to sites of particularly intense bloodshed, revealing the ritual purpose behind the Traitors' seemingly senseless slaughter.

The exhausted Loyalists were completely overwhelmed. Daemons ranged from swarms of monstrous creatures to entities capable of matching Space Marines and towering horrors powerful enough to destroy Titans. At Avaris Plaza, an enormous serpentine entity crushed the Reaver Titan Knossos, while the remaining 312th Solar Auxilia sacrificed themselves protecting fleeing civilians.

Even some Traitor forces were attacked by the daemons. The Mechanicum forces of Covenant Mormoth-Null withdrew aboard the Dirac's Lament, while the Word Bearers reportedly moved among the creatures unharmed.

At the Ultramarines' Trident command post, a daemonic entity emerged from the body of a captured Word Bearer. Growing to the size of an Imperial Knight, it slaughtered Legionaries before being destroyed by Fire Raptor gunships. Sub-captain Mantargo was wounded during the attack.

By the thirteenth hour, Mantargo recognised that Ithraca could no longer be defended and ordered a general evacuation. Survivors in the northern districts escaped through underground tunnels and surviving transports, but the southern and eastern sectors were apparently overrun completely.

The surviving Titans of Legio Praesagius chose to make their final stand. With the Arutan destroyed and no available means of transporting their remaining dozen god-engines, the True Messengers elected to hold back the daemonic hordes rather than attempt an impossible retreat.

Every remaining Titan of Legio Praesagius was lost at Ithraca. Their sacrifice nevertheless allowed more than 4,000 Ultramarines to escape the ruined city and continue the war against the Traitors.

The Battle of Ithraca ended in a catastrophic Loyalist defeat, but the True Messengers' final resistance preserved thousands of warriors who would carry the memory of their sacrifice into the wider Horus Heresy.

#### Calth — Epilogue

By the twentieth hour of the Battle of Calth, the Ultramarines faced near-annihilation, yet continued fighting both in orbit and on the surface. Guilliman, First Chapter Master Marius Gage, and Captain Remus Ventanus emerged as key figures in organising the Legion's recovery.

The key to reversing the battle was regaining control of Calth's planetary defence grid, whose thousands of weapons had been seized by the Traitor Mechanicum through corrupted machine code.

The Ultramarines launched two coordinated operations. Captain Ventanus led an assault on the Lanshear Guildhall, seeking access to an uncontaminated data-engine that Loyalist Mechanicum adepts could use to purge the Traitors' code. Simultaneously, forces in orbit needed to disable the overseer data-engine aboard the Zetsun Verid Yard station.

Ventanus advanced with a powerful armoured force, including Land Raiders, Shadowsword super-heavy tanks, elements of the Ultramarines 4th Company, Skitarii, and surviving Imperial Army troops.

The column encountered a Legio Mortis Warhound Titan, whose void shields were overwhelmed by concentrated fire from Predator, Whirlwind, and Sicaran tanks before a Shadowsword delivered the killing blow.

The Ultramarines then stormed the Word Bearers' positions around the Guildhall. Even the intervention of the daemonically empowered Gal Vorbak failed to halt their furious advance, although both Legions suffered heavy casualties.

For the first time, the Ultramarines were close to breaking the Word Bearers' control of Calth's defences, creating an opportunity to reverse the catastrophic battle.

The Battle of Calth turned decisively when the Ultramarines regained control of the planetary defence grid. Captain Remus Ventanus held the Lanshear Guildhall against overwhelming Word Bearers forces, reinforced by Sergeant Anchise, Captain Aethon of the 19th Company, Tetrarch Eikos Lamiad, and the Dreadnought Telemechrus.

The decisive breakthrough came when Guilliman's forces captured the Zetsun Verid Yard orbital station. Loyalist Mechanicum adepts at the Guildhall immediately purged the Traitor code and reactivated Calth's enormous defensive network.

The restored weapons devastated the Word Bearers. Orbital bombardments annihilated Traitor formations across Calth, allowing the surviving Ultramarines to launch a planet-wide counterattack. In orbit, dozens of Word Bearers warships were destroyed, their wreckage eventually forming a hazardous ring around the planet.

Kor Phaeron survived his confrontation with Guilliman despite suffering a near-fatal wound. With their fleet being destroyed, Shipmaster Sire Antonius Antwark ordered the Word Bearers flagship, Infidus Imperator, to retreat.

Guilliman dispatched Marius Gage aboard the Macragge's Honour to pursue the fleeing flagship. The resulting confrontation eventually destroyed the Infidus Imperator, although Guilliman would not learn the fate of Gage and his flagship for many years.

Despite winning the immediate battle, the Ultramarines faced three escalating crises. The Veridia star had been poisoned, making Calth's surface increasingly uninhabitable. Reports revealed simultaneous Traitor invasions across dozens of Ultramar's worlds. Meanwhile, an immense Warp storm was closing around the system, threatening to trap Guilliman's fleet for years.

Guilliman ordered an immediate evacuation, prioritising surviving Titans, armoured formations, and other forces that could be transported. Infantry and civilians unable to escape were directed into Calth's subterranean arcologies.

Those left behind would continue fighting the Word Bearers in the decade-long Calth Underworld War.

Before departing, Guilliman swore to return and liberate Calth, recognising that the Word Bearers' betrayal had been intended to destroy his Legion and leave the Five Hundred Worlds defenceless.

The Ultramarines had survived the attempt to annihilate them, but the Shadow Crusade against Ultramar had only begun.

### The Ultima Taskforce

#### The Ultima Taskforce

Following the catastrophic Battle of Calth, Roboute Guilliman recognised that the Ultramarines were dangerously unprepared for the supernatural weapons employed by the Word Bearers. Although he had previously obeyed the Emperor's restrictions on psychic warfare, the devastation demonstrated that conventional military doctrine alone could not counter the growing Daemonic threat.

Guilliman therefore secretly assembled thirteen senior Librarians, placing them within the secure Asteria Wing of the Fortress of Macragge. Alongside selected warriors, they formed the Ultima Taskforce, a clandestine organisation dedicated to studying the Warp and developing practical countermeasures against supernatural enemies.

Drawing upon captured Word Bearers, their knowledge of forbidden rituals, and the experience gained during Calth, the taskforce conducted systematic investigations into Daemonic phenomena. This represented a deliberate departure from the Edict of Nikaea, with Guilliman prioritising the survival of his Legion over continued obedience to restrictions that had left his warriors vulnerable.

Eventually, widespread supernatural disturbances forced the taskforce to relocate to a concealed laboratory on Prandium, where its members received greater resources and continued their research into weaponising captured Warp entities. The ultimate fate of the Ultima Taskforce remains uncertain, although later Imperial accounts suggest that its activities were eventually suppressed or concealed.

### Shadow Crusade

- Lorgar and Angron attacked Guilliman's Ultramarines in the Shadow Crusade. The Ruinstorm and the surrounding campaigns isolated the Legion; Guilliman eventually came to believe Terra had fallen.

### Sanguinius and Imperium Secundus

##### Sanguinius and Imperium Secundus

Following their costly victory at Signus, Sanguinius and the Blood Angels travelled toward Ultramar, where Roboute Guilliman was establishing what would become Imperium Secundus. The IX Legion had escaped annihilation, but thousands of its warriors had died, and the survivors carried the psychological and spiritual consequences of their encounter with Chaos.

The chronicler presents Guilliman's ambitions with considerable suspicion. His attempt to preserve Imperial civilisation in Ultramar could also be interpreted as the establishment of a personal empire, and some contemporary observers saw uncomfortable similarities between his growing authority and the ambitions that had corrupted Horus. Nevertheless, Guilliman ultimately placed the reluctant Sanguinius at the head of Imperium Secundus, providing the Blood Angels with a temporary sanctuary.

This respite could not heal the Legion's deeper wounds. The curse awakened at Signus remained within the Blood Angels, and neither their kinship with the Ultramarines nor further victories could eradicate it. Eventually, Sanguinius departed Ultramar for Terra, where the final confrontation of the Heresy awaited at the Eternity Gate.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Ultramarines spent much of 007–008.M31 defending Ultramar against Angron and Lorgar. However, detached XIIIth Legion forces remained stationed on distant worlds such as Molech or accompanied Rogue Trader expeditionary fleets. Some operated independently until contact with Guilliman could be restored.

Ultramarines Assault-Captain Roscius led an independent company which captured a Sons of Horus strike cruiser at Plutol and subsequently operated it against traitors. Knights-Errant Tylos Rubio joined Malcador's clandestine force after renouncing the Edict of Nikaea.

## Notes

Horus diverted Guilliman's Ultramarines to the Eastern Fringe under the pretext of fighting the Ghaslakh Orks. The Word Bearers betrayed the XIII Legion at Calth; after the ensuing isolation and Shadow Crusade, Guilliman came to believe the Throneworld had fallen.

The Legion's greatest hope was reunion with its missing Primarch. Unknown to its warriors, the Emperor had already discovered evidence of Roboute Guilliman's existence more than two years before the disaster at Septus.

The Emperor learned of Guilliman while visiting Espandor, a world connected to Macragge. However, powerful Warp storms prevented his fleet from reaching the Primarch for approximately five years.

During this isolation, Guilliman transformed Macragge into a prosperous, highly organised civilisation. He rebuilt its cities, strengthened its armies, established interstellar trade routes, and conquered neighbouring systems to restore order.

Guilliman had already conceived the foundations of Ultramar before meeting the Emperor, drawing inspiration from ancient records of humanity's lost interstellar domains.

When the Emperor finally reached Macragge, Guilliman immediately recognised his origins and pledged loyalty. His exceptional analytical intelligence, administrative abilities, and talent for large-scale organisation impressed Imperial observers.

The reunion promised to transform the XIIIth Legion, giving its warriors the Primarch they had long sought and providing the Great Crusade with one of its most capable strategists and empire-builders.

Roboute Guilliman completely reorganised the XIIIth Legion, combining Macragge's military traditions with lessons drawn from the histories and doctrines of other Space Marine Legions. The result was a highly structured, meritocratic force designed for maximum efficiency and adaptability.

His reforms established two equally important principles: the Practical and the Theoretical. The Practical encompassed courage, discipline, combat skill, and adaptability, while the Theoretical emphasised planning, precedent, analysis, and assessment. Together, they became the foundation of Ultramarines military doctrine.

Guilliman demanded absolute discipline while placing great value on individual Legionaries. Merit and achievement earned promotion and responsibility, but every warrior was expected to obey the chain of command. Unnecessary casualties were unacceptable because each loss weakened the entire Legion.

The XIIIth officially became the Ultramarines, adopting deep-blue armour with gold detailing and the ancient Ultima symbol, linking the Legion to Guilliman's expanding realm.

With Macragge as its new base, Guilliman received independent Crusade command. His reformed 12th Expeditionary Fleet, reinforced with modern Martian warships, launched nearly a century of campaigns across the eastern and northern reaches of the galaxy.

The Ultramarines reportedly liberated more worlds than any other Primarch's forces during this period. Their success rested not merely on military victories but on Guilliman's systematic reconstruction of conquered planets.

Every compliant world received organised government, sustainable defences, and functioning infrastructure, strengthening Imperial control while expanding the Legion's recruitment, industrial, and logistical networks.

This combination of conquest, administration, and long-term development enabled the Ultramarines' extraordinary expansion, transforming them into one of the Great Crusade's most effective military forces.

Under Roboute Guilliman, the Ultramarines experienced unprecedented numerical expansion, combining their exceptionally stable gene-seed with the Primarch's organisational expertise and genetic material.

The Fortress of Hera on Macragge became the centre of the Legion's recruitment system. Candidates were drawn not only from Ultramar but from scores of conquered worlds. Rigorous physical and mental selection standards remained in place, yet the enormous recruitment network produced more initiates than any other Legion.

Guilliman's strategy created a cycle of increasing military strength. Larger armies could overwhelm enemies more efficiently, reducing casualties and allowing further expansion. Even devastating losses against Orks, the Tanaril, and other powerful xenos were generally replaced within a few years.

By Horus' appointment as Warmaster, the Ultramarines were officially the largest Space Marine Legion by a considerable margin. Their immense 12th Expeditionary Fleet was divided into approximately twenty smaller Expeditionary and Persecution fleets, each commanding dozens of warships and thousands, sometimes tens of thousands, of Legionaries.

Only the Word Bearers' secret expansion and uncertain estimates of the Alpha Legion's strength potentially rivalled their numbers.

Ultramar had meanwhile grown into the Five Hundred Worlds, effectively forming a powerful, largely autonomous empire within the Imperium.

This extraordinary strength ultimately made Guilliman and his Legion an unavoidable target for Horus. The Warmaster needed the Ultramarines neutralised, while Lorgar sought revenge for Monarchia. Together, these ambitions would culminate in the betrayal at Calth.

By the twentieth hour of the Battle of Calth, the Ultramarines faced near-annihilation, yet continued fighting both in orbit and on the surface. Guilliman, First Chapter Master Marius Gage, and Captain Remus Ventanus emerged as key figures in organising the Legion's recovery.

The key to reversing the battle was regaining control of Calth's planetary defence grid, whose thousands of weapons had been seized by the Traitor Mechanicum through corrupted machine code.

The Ultramarines launched two coordinated operations. Captain Ventanus led an assault on the Lanshear Guildhall, seeking access to an uncontaminated data-engine that Loyalist Mechanicum adepts could use to purge the Traitors' code. Simultaneously, forces in orbit needed to disable the overseer data-engine aboard the Zetsun Verid Yard station.

Ventanus advanced with a powerful armoured force, including Land Raiders, Shadowsword super-heavy tanks, elements of the Ultramarines 4th Company, Skitarii, and surviving Imperial Army troops.

The column encountered a Legio Mortis Warhound Titan, whose void shields were overwhelmed by concentrated fire from Predator, Whirlwind, and Sicaran tanks before a Shadowsword delivered the killing blow.

The Ultramarines then stormed the Word Bearers' positions around the Guildhall. Even the intervention of the daemonically empowered Gal Vorbak failed to halt their furious advance, although both Legions suffered heavy casualties.

For the first time, the Ultramarines were close to breaking the Word Bearers' control of Calth's defences, creating an opportunity to reverse the catastrophic battle.

The Battle of Calth turned decisively when the Ultramarines regained control of the planetary defence grid. Captain Remus Ventanus held the Lanshear Guildhall against overwhelming Word Bearers forces, reinforced by Sergeant Anchise, Captain Aethon of the 19th Company, Tetrarch Eikos Lamiad, and the Dreadnought Telemechrus.

The decisive breakthrough came when Guilliman's forces captured the Zetsun Verid Yard orbital station. Loyalist Mechanicum adepts at the Guildhall immediately purged the Traitor code and reactivated Calth's enormous defensive network.

The restored weapons devastated the Word Bearers. Orbital bombardments annihilated Traitor formations across Calth, allowing the surviving Ultramarines to launch a planet-wide counterattack. In orbit, dozens of Word Bearers warships were destroyed, their wreckage eventually forming a hazardous ring around the planet.

Kor Phaeron survived his confrontation with Guilliman despite suffering a near-fatal wound. With their fleet being destroyed, Shipmaster Sire Antonius Antwark ordered the Word Bearers flagship, Infidus Imperator, to retreat.

Guilliman dispatched Marius Gage aboard the Macragge's Honour to pursue the fleeing flagship. The resulting confrontation eventually destroyed the Infidus Imperator, although Guilliman would not learn the fate of Gage and his flagship for many years.

Despite winning the immediate battle, the Ultramarines faced three escalating crises. The Veridia star had been poisoned, making Calth's surface increasingly uninhabitable. Reports revealed simultaneous Traitor invasions across dozens of Ultramar's worlds. Meanwhile, an immense Warp storm was closing around the system, threatening to trap Guilliman's fleet for years.

Guilliman ordered an immediate evacuation, prioritising surviving Titans, armoured formations, and other forces that could be transported. Infantry and civilians unable to escape were directed into Calth's subterranean arcologies.

Those left behind would continue fighting the Word Bearers in the decade-long Calth Underworld War.

Before departing, Guilliman swore to return and liberate Calth, recognising that the Word Bearers' betrayal had been intended to destroy his Legion and leave the Five Hundred Worlds defenceless.

### The Ultima Taskforce

The Ultima Taskforce

Following the catastrophic Battle of Calth, Roboute Guilliman recognised that the Ultramarines were dangerously unprepared for the supernatural weapons employed by the Word Bearers. Although he had previously obeyed the Emperor's restrictions on psychic warfare, the devastation demonstrated that conventional military doctrine alone could not counter the growing Daemonic threat.

Guilliman therefore secretly assembled thirteen senior Librarians, placing them within the secure Asteria Wing of the Fortress of Macragge. Alongside selected warriors, they formed the Ultima Taskforce, a clandestine organisation dedicated to studying the Warp and developing practical countermeasures against supernatural enemies.

Drawing upon captured Word Bearers, their knowledge of forbidden rituals, and the experience gained during Calth, the taskforce conducted systematic investigations into Daemonic phenomena. This represented a deliberate departure from the Edict of Nikaea, with Guilliman prioritising the survival of his Legion over continued obedience to restrictions that had left his warriors vulnerable.

Eventually, widespread supernatural disturbances forced the taskforce to relocate to a concealed laboratory on Prandium, where its members received greater resources and continued their research into weaponising captured Warp entities. The ultimate fate of the Ultima Taskforce remains uncertain, although later Imperial accounts suggest that its activities were eventually suppressed or concealed.

# Iron Hands
Renowned for their pride, cold-hearted brutality and remorselessness in the battle, the Iron Hands were among the most powerful and famed Legions of the Great Crusade. They were the hammer that had shattered countless foes of the Emperor’s quest to liberate Mankind from the darkness of Old Night. Their Primarch was Ferrus Manus, a bellicose and uncompromising warlord and peerless weapon-smith, from whom the Xth Legion took their name and whose command was to them inviolate law. Caught up in the machinations of the Warmaster’s treachery, they were undone by their own hubris and the blade of the Traitors, and paid a heavy price in the brutal slaying of their Primarch and the near-crippling of their strength. Though left a shattered Legion, the cold fury of the Iron Hands’ bitter vengeance would afterwards be writ across the stars.  

## Legion Structure
The Iron Hands legion was a highly structured military force, with numerous tactical and strategic divisions of power and organisation within its ranks. The Legion was deliberately composed from a series of interlocking components, each with its own specialisation, duties and chain of command, beholden only to itself and immediate superiors. Each of these components, be they Legionary squad, armoured vehicle squadron or support elements, came together first as companies. Each company was a part of a larger grouping with its own independent command, support, logistical network, armoury and ships. These formations were referred to as Orders and were superficially equivalent to a specialized battalion in general Legiones Astartes terms, but far more concrete in makeup and isolated in operation. In battle each Order was given a specific task which it was expected to fulfil without needing assistance and succeed it would at any cost. Orders were often formed from large single Clan grouping, and sometimes units from different clans fought together within them, often as rivals for glory and achievement.  

Clans were notionally the Iron Hands’ equivalent of the chapters of other Legions, but were in practice of distinct character, being patterned to a larger extent on the Medusal feudal system and were linked to planet’s nomadic barbarian populations as recruiting bases. These Clans were in fact ‘pocket Legions’, fully self-sufficient and self-supplied, and each had a single Chieftain or Iron Lord to rule them by Primarch-given right.  

The level of integration and strength the Iron Hands Legion’s system manifested was extraordinary if somewhat inflexible. Whether deployed as a single company or a full Legion in scale, it was a crushing leviathan when in action; extraordinarily difficult to stop, but also at times brutally intractable and slow to change course through its single-minded pursuit of its starting objectives. This was Ferrus Manus’ deliberate intention, and as part of that design he invested authority and initiative with the few, not the many.  

Within the Iron Hands Legion, command carried with in the power of both life and death; Each Shield-Centurion was empowered to enact at will both reward and preferment, as well as immediate punishment - including execution, over any Legionary beneath, without any recourse, hearing or trial. Likewise, at the higher echelons of the Legion, an Iron-Captain had the same rights over their War Leaders and other subordinate officers within their Order, and the ranks beneath them, Iron-Lord over Iron-Captains in their Clan, and so on.  

Clan is commanded by the Iron Lord with retinue of Iron-Captains;  
Iron-Captain commands the Order with his War Leaders;  
War Leader is basically a Company Captain, Company is divided into Squads, each led by Shield-Centurion.  

The Iron Hands Legion known to maintain a particularly extensive and sophisticated arsenal of war engines - tanks, armoured vehicles and Dreadnoughts - the equal of any Legion save perhaps for Perturabo’s IV. Thanks to long-standing ties with the Mechanicum and the technological aptitude displayed by many in their ranks, they possessed access to numerous cybernetic implant systems seldom seen outside the Machine Cult, and they were also able to widely reproduce and maintain large stockpiles of grav-weapons, conversion beams and laser-destroyer systems, and other arcane and esoteric machines of death. This range of materiel extended both to extensive numbers of servitors, and advanced super-heavy tanks like Fellblade. In addition, battle-automata and troops from the Mechanicum were preferred by the Iron Hands Legion for strategic support when available as the Legion was largely dismissive of most Imperial Army units as unfit for frontline work. It is also worthy of note that the Iron Hands Legion and its master Ferrus Manus was at the forefront of the introduction of a number of weapons systems and armour patterns, such as Stormbreaker pattern thunder hammer and Indomitus Terminator Armor.  
The strength of the Iron Hands pseudo-feudal system of organisation created a highly disciplined and motivated force which both despised and punished failure and weakness, and rewarded and glorified strength, accomplishment and success. The most competent and strong-willed rose quickly within the ranks, others quickly weeded out. It also had the effect of not inconsiderable friction and direct competition between the Clans, both to attain glory and praise in their Primarch’s eyes and to outdo each other in battle. The most successful Clans were favoured by their Primarch with a greater proportion of recruits and resources. For the rest the message was clear - it was a matter of the survival of the fittest; adapt, grow strong, overcome - or fail and wither to destruction.  

At the outset of the Horus Heresy, the Iron Hands Legion is commonly estimated to have had an operational strength just in excess of 113 000 Legiones Astartes, with a huge operating reserve of wargear and supplies, and a diverse fleet with several capital ships. This placed the Iron Hands within the mid-tier of Legion strengths recorded at this date by their numbers alone.  

### The Keys of Hel
The Iron Hands’ obsession with strength and elimination of weakness led them to revere the machine and distrust the failings inherent in flesh. To them their Emperor-augmented bodies were neither more nor less than a living machine of blood and gristle made for war, a machine which could be repaired and improved upon as opportunity allowed and need commanded.  

The Legion favoured cybernetic replacements, allowing for fallen or injured Astartes to get back in the fight with frightening speed. As a result no Iron Hands who had served in battle for any length of time was wholly without augmentation, and such implants were marks of honour, and often improved and augmented away from the battle’s pressing need. Some of the most veteran among the Iron Hands went so far that they were little more than machines cradling shreds of their former humanity. To be interred within a Dreadnought body and therefore be allowed to fight for their Primarch and the great cause of unifying humanity forever was the highest honour any could aspire to.  

But there were limits to what even Ferrus Manus would countenance and certain mysteries of cybermancy such as true rites of cybernetic resurrection remained prescribed as nightmare sciences of the Dark Age of Technology. Such elements [for example, Agesine Protocols, the Eight Sleepers, Sarcosan Formulae], during the Great Crusade, were seized and suppressed by their Primarch’s order, being put beyond reach but not destroyed.  

After the death of Ferrus Manus, however, some saw virtue in what had once been forbidden and, it is said, turned the Keys of Hel.

## Legion Chronicles
### Unification Wars
The martial history of the Xth Legion is a relatively well-documented one, about which a great deal still survives on record to the present. Alongside the other proto-Legions created during the latter days of the Unification Wars on Ancient Terra, they were prepared and armed in preparation for the great work ahead, and their initial outfitting and organisation followed closely to the standard patterns set of the Legiones Astartes at the start of the Great Crusade.  

Their recruitment base is noted as being relatively widespread, with elements drawn from all over Ancient Terra, both from areas of longstanding Imperial support, such as the Qavinite Place cities, and from former enemy blocs such as Solus Stellax. In particular, the warlike culture and renowned brutality of the population of Old Albia, which supplied substantial numbers of recruits for the Xth, had a great deal to do with Legion’s early character, lending them a fierce pride in a warrior ancestry that predated the Legion’s founding.  

### Great Crusade
Its first individual battle honours are recorded in the wider Sol system against the mutant ‘Scythers’ of the deep-orbiting Mican Aggregation and the extermination of the xenos Lyasx enclave hidden beneath the ice of Oberath. The Xth proved itself to be a remorseless and fiercely disciplined attacking force, fully capable of operating with remarkable coordination and tightly focused wrath to achieve tactical and strategic aims.  

[During the battle of Planet 02-34] The massed forces of Xth Legion, under the generalship of their Lord Commander Amadeus DuCaine, formulated a plan of attack which owed much of the feared and pitiless battle-craft of Old Albia: a tactic known as the Hammer and the Storm. After forcing a landing via the brute power of their ships from orbit, DuCaine ordered the Urshan soldiery to make planetfall in full strength. In this they had ‘raised the storm’ - the Orks, bestirred in their millions by the attack from above and, ever-warlike, poured from their warrens to attack the invaders. Within hours the plain was a bloodbath of shot and shell, mangled machinery and bomb craters swollen with broken bodies, both human and Ork. DuCaine waited until he was sure the bulk of the Ork forces were fully committed - swarming like a tide of black ants in numbers large enough to be visible from orbit like a spreading stain - before answering with the might of his Legion. They would be the hammer. The Xth deployed in full armored might in a single massed spearhead, comprised in the main of heavy Malcador, Mauler and Land Raider tanks. In the shadow of these armored giants, the Legion’s squads and dreadnoughts formed in close and disciplined ranks. Moving forward as a single, slow-moving mass, lashing out the relentless torrent of firepower, the Legion went on in unison like some immense harvesting machine, destroying everything in their path.  

After days of near-continuous fighting, the waves of the alien assault grew fewer and further between, until they guttered and stopped at last. The Orks of Rust had not lost the will to fight, but had simply run out of blood and machinery to spend. At the end of the battle, the Legion had suffered less than a fifth of its number in fatalities.  

The Xth developing strategies were studied and indeed copied by several other Legions, such as the IVth and XIIIth in the campaign against the Krooked Klaw Orks and thereafter. Very quickly, with its victories and developing tactics, the Xth Legion was forging an identity for itself in battle, and a name ‘storm walkers’ had been coined for them by the Urhsan who had come to view them with an almost superstitious awe.  

### Rediscovery of Ferrus Manus
In a short span of years after the sundering of the Krooked-Klaw empire, news came that the Xth Legion Primarch was found on a cold, merciless world named Medusa. The Primarch of the Iron Hands, who had taken unto himself the name of Ferrus Manus, was among the first of the Emperor’s lost sons to be discovered and, like Horus and Russ before him, had risen to become a warlord in his own right on the world on which he had been cast.  

Medusa was a cold, barren realm, driven for countless generations to incessant warfare through privation and hardship, but like much of Old Terra before the ascension of the Emperor, while its culture had regressed into barbarity and darkness, it had maintained a great deal of mechanical and technological lore. So it was that Ferrus Manus’ transition from planetary warlord to general of the Great Crusade was a swift one, aided by his evident hunger for the task set before him and the uncompromising intelligence and diligent application of his greater calling he displayed.  

In a scant few years, Ferrus Manus was transferred full control of the Xth Legion which he took command of body and soul, renaming it and remaking it in his image. The Primarch took the Xth apart with the precision and intent with which an artisan might deconstruct a mechanical chronograph. Under his direction, the Legion’s already evident preference for order and systematised combat was refocused and increased. In response to their Primarch’s own values, the newly renamed Iron Hands valued superiority in terms of weaponry and wargear as much as they did zeal, valour or discipline, and the Legion saw a concentration of technological expertise seldom matched by other Legions.  

From the beginning, it was clear Ferrus Manus would prove a harsh but constant overlord. The Primarch was utterly intolerant of failure and swift to anger, but also rigidly and rationally controlled in the application of the wellspring of wroth that was never far from the surface of his thoughts and actions. Quick to scorn and rare to praise, Ferrus Manus set utterly uncompromising standards for his Legion, which he saw as an extension of his own body and will. In truth the Primarch also held himself to those same unmatchable standards and despised duplicity and dissemblance as much as he did weakness, and for these reasons, more than any other, the warriors of the Xᵗʰ loved him as sons love a hard-hearted father, with a scrap of his praise worth more to them than any laurel of victory. Very quickly because of the Primarch’s influence, a mind-set was crystallised in the Legion that meant that failure to the Iron Hands was intolerable. Failure was weakness, and weakness was the greatest sin. If weakness was found in their own ranks, it would be purged so that only the strong remained and so were further strengthened.   

Building on the talents and tactics of the Xth Legion had already displayed, Ferrus Manus and the Iron Hands excelled at high-intensity warfare both against technologically advanced opponents and xenos species such as the Orks. As a consequence of this, the Legion’s common opponents in battle often included intransigent human factions who actively resisted unification and had retained some fragment of the technological might of the lost Dark Age of Technology. In such conflicts it was common,  due the strategic necessity, for the Iron Hands to fight closely alongside elements of the Mechanicum and the Titan Legions, with whom a cultural relationship inherited from Medusa was shared, and over the years of battle these ties and allegiance grew.  

### Later Great Crusade
As the years passed, Iron Hands also gained an increasingly belligerent reputation with their fellow Legions as lacking both patience with others and subtlety of action, both in the person of their Primarch and the character of the Legion’s rank and file. Iron Hands’ ruthlessness in battle and their seemingly callous indifference to the price of victory made them conquerors, not liberators. It is also consequently true that Ferrus Manus and his Legion cared little for the minutiae of the Great Crusade, they saw their task as a clear one: to expand the borders of the Imperium and destroy its enemies. When once asked about his Legion’s role in the Great Crusade, Ferrus Manus said: “Make war and move on, and again, and again, until nothing breathes which stands against us. Everything else is sophistry and pretty lies.”  

As the Great Crusade progressed, the Iron Hands Legion was in constant service at the front line of expansion, both at the head of its own battle groups, compliance and extermination taskforces, and wielded as a part of larger Expeditionary fleets, fighting alongside other Legions where needed. After more than a century under its Primarch’s command, the victories of the Iron Hands could not easily be counted and, indeed, it is believed many of its battles went unrecorded, as the Legion itself became increasingly insular in many ways, and uninterested in sharing the details of its independent operations in anything but the most perfunctory way. To the wider Imperium it presented a cold, brutal façade; the Legion as a calculating, prideful and powerful engine of war that did not stoop to deal with petty concerns or affairs of state. Such secrets the Iron Hands Legion did keep, it kept well.  

### Dawn of Heresy
Iron Hands’ fleet was badly injured by the Emperor’s Children treachery, but whose Primarch, Ferrus Manus, was also proceeding to the Istvaan System at best speed with the warriors of his chosen Avernii clan. At the time of events on Istvaan III were taking place, the major part of the Legion was under the direct command of their Primarch, forming a 52nd Expeditionary Fleet.

### Istvaan V
For Ferrus Manus, the absence of the Traitor Legions’ fleets in the Istvaan system was not so much a cause of suspicion as it was an opportunity that should not be wasted. Through the bungled attempt of Fulgrim and his Emperor’s Children to sway Manus to the Traitor’s cause and the flight of the ‘Eisenstein’ escaping the betrayal at Istvaan III, the Imperium had been made aware of the Warmaster’s plot long before the arch-traitor had been ready, and to the grim mind of the master of Iron Hands, it was an error for which the enemy would be made to pay.  

The decision for immediate and all-out attack upon the Traitors positions on the planet was taken by Ferrus Manus. As senior battle-leader among his fellow brothers, the darkly aggrieved master of the Iron Hands would brook no delay. Some surviving reports make note of the Ferrus Manus overriding Vulkan’s counsel of caution to await the bulk of the Loyalists before mounting the attack, and taking Corax’s silence for agreement. His warlike intelligence formulating a hundred possible scenarios of attack and defence already, then refining them further as the data from Ad Temperesta intelligence had been received.  

Owing of the perfidious treachery of Fulgrim, and the uncontrollable wrath of Ferrus Manus in response, the Iron Hands Primarch broke ranks from this force and reached Istvaan V with only the elite cadre of chosen from his then-favoured Avernii Clan alongside him, almost all of whom were annihilated with their slain Primarch during the Dropsite Massacre.  

First descended hundreds of drop pods and assault rams, falling like a steel rain upon the world, with Ferrum’s own deadly cargo the first to fall. In this first onslaught came Ferrus Manus himself, hungry for the blood of those who betrayed their father.  Such was the relentlessness of this first strike wave and the precision and timing with which it had been executed, that for every drop pod or landing claw smashed in the descent, a dozen or more made planet fall intact, spilling their Space Marine cargo into the teeth of Traitor’s defence lines.  

The Gorgon’s precisely calculated attack patterns that brought them to the very edge of the shielding overlays of the fortress, and well forward of the projected killing ground so carefully crafted in the foothills of the depression by the Traitors. The battle was joined at close quarters, a fight as savage and pitiless as any the Great Crusade had seen, brother against brother, superhuman against superhuman. Bolters thundered, chainblades howled, spraying sparks of armor plates or biting deep in suit seems.  

The Loyalist attack formations had succeeded in forming up according to Ferus Manus’ pre-battle plan.  

In the center of the line, where the enemy line was at its worst furious, indefatigable and roaring his defiance was Ferrus Manus. The Gorgon led a grinding wedge of Terminators from Avernii clan, numbering a thousand strong.. Whilst around him rallied the vanguard survivors and six maniples of Iron Hands Legion battle-automata.. The Primarch crushed and killed with his bare silver hands, ripping apart the foe with inhuman, cold rage against which even the Legiones Astartes could not stand. Where the Gorgon struck the enemy, warriors of the Sons of Horus and Emperor’s Children alike broke before him. As the wrathful Primarch pressed on, his dark-iron clad Legionaries followed relentlessly with him through the unceasing hail of shellfire..  

After 3 hours of battle, Salamanders and Raven Guard began to fall back to regroup and rearm, having expended their supplies.. Ferrus Manus, however, would have none of it. Retreat was unthinkable to him, and, heedless to the fact, the enclave of Traitors had formed around him, he ordered his forces not merely to hold the ground but to press on, becoming dangerously separated from the Loyalist lines. There are unconfirmed reports that what so provoked the Gorgon was the sight of Fulgrim. Once a friend, now most hated enemy, at the sight of the taunting Fulgrim Ferrus Manus’ rage had overflowed, and he broke from his own ranks in order to duel with his fellow Primarch, seeking to slay the Phoenician no matter the cost.  

Ferrus Manus fell, slain by arch-traitor Fulgrim. Betrayed, entrapped and outnumbered, for Loyalists on the Istvaan V there was no hope of salvation or victory. In the hearts of surviving Salamanders, Raven Guard and Iron Hands, outraged hatred roared into savage life. Spitting defiance to the last they fought on; until the last shell and the last drop of blood, they fought like legendary heroes of old and they did not falter, but this was no longer a battle, it was extermination.  

A great many vessels of the 52nd Expeditionary Fleet were destroyed, along with tens of thousands Iron Hands on board as they later transited into the Istvaan system, rushing unknowingly into a death trap from which only few ships were to escape.  

Taking together, these losses were great, although this left more survivors from the Iron Hands in number that could be ascribed to the Dropsite Massacre’s other victim Legions overall. This was largely the case of large groups of Iron Hands were presently engaged elsewhere and unable to heed, or ignorant of, their Primarch’s rushed call to assault the traitors at the Istvaan system.  

It is known that some Clans re-organised a council of Iron-Fathers on Medusa to take charge of the ravaged Legion, and speeded the recruitment and creation of new Space Marines from the extensive stores of gene-seed kept there with the goal to rebuild the Legion. In the aftermath some Iron Hands units and, in some cases, entire Clans shunned the Medusan Council’s assumed authority and went their own way, consumed by their own hatred and need of revenge. These fractured forces fought their own war and it is widely believed some delved into the use of dark and forbidden forces from humanity’s ancient past to do so, although this has been strenuously denied since by the Iron Hands Clan Council. Other lone Iron Hands cast off their Legion’s livery entirely and either fought on alone or went renegade - Iron Hands no more.

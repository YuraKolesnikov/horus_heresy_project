# Iron Hands
Renowned for their pride, cold-hearted brutality and remorselessness in the battle, the Iron Hands were among the most powerful and famed Legions of the Great Crusade. They were the hammer that had shattered countless foes of the Emperor’s quest to liberate Mankind from the darkness of Old Night. Their Primarch was Ferrus Manus, a bellicose and uncompromising warlord and peerless weapon-smith, from whom the Xth Legion took their name and whose command was to them inviolate law. Caught up in the machinations of the Warmaster’s treachery, they were undone by their own hubris and the blade of the Traitors, and paid a heavy price in the brutal slaying of their Primarch and the near-crippling of their strength. Though left a shattered Legion, the cold fury of the Iron Hands’ bitter vengeance would afterwards be writ across the stars.  

## Legion Structure
The Iron Hands legion was a highly structured military force, with numerous tactical and strategic divisions of power and organisation within its ranks. The Legion was deliberately composed from a series of interlocking components, each with its own specialisation, duties and chain of command, beholden only to itself and immediate superiors. Each of these components, be they Legionary squad, armoured vehicle squadron or support elements, came together first as companies. Each company was a part of a larger grouping with its own independent command, support, logistical network, armoury and ships. These formations were referred to as Orders and were superficially equivalent to a specialized battalion in general Legiones Astartes terms, but far more concrete in makeup and isolated in operation. In battle each Order was given a specific task which it was expected to fulfil without needing assistance and succeed it would at any cost. Orders were often formed from large single Clan grouping, and sometimes units from different clans fought together within them, often as rivals for glory and achievement.  

Clans were notionally the Iron Hands’ equivalent of the chapters of other Legions, but were in practice of distinct character, being patterned to a larger extent on the Medusal feudal system and were linked to planet’s nomadic barbarian populations as recruiting bases. These Clans were in fact ‘pocket Legions’, fully self-sufficient and self-supplied, and each had a single Chieftain or Iron Lord to rule them by Primarch-given right.  

The level of integration and strength the Iron Hands Legion’s system manifested was extraordinary if somewhat inflexible. Whether deployed as a single company or a full Legion in scale, it was a crushing leviathan when in action; extraordinarily difficult to stop, but also at times brutally intractable and slow to change course through its single-minded pursuit of its starting objectives. This was Ferrus Manus’ deliberate intention, and as part of that design he invested authority and initiative with the few, not the many.  

Within the Iron Hands Legion, command carried with in the power of both life and death; Each Shield-Centurion was empowered to enact at will both reward and preferment, as well as immediate punishment - including execution, over any Legionary beneath, without any recourse, hearing or trial. Likewise, at the higher echelons of the Legion, an Iron-Captain had the same rights over their War Leaders and other subordinate officers within their Order, and the ranks beneath them, Iron-Lord over Iron-Captains in their Clan, and so on.

The Iron Hands Legion known to maintain a particularly extensive and sophisticated arsenal of war engines - tanks, armoured vehicles and Dreadnoughts - the equal of any Legion save perhaps for Perturabo’s IV. Thanks to long-standing ties with the Mechanicum and the technological aptitude displayed by many in their ranks, they possessed access to numerous cybernetic implant systems seldom seen outside the Machine Cult, and they were also able to widely reproduce and maintain large stockpiles of grav-weapons, conversion beams and laser-destroyer systems, and other arcane and esoteric machines of death. This range of materiel extended both to extensive numbers of servitors, and advanced super-heavy tanks like Fellblade. In addition, battle-automata and troops from the Mechanicum were preferred by the Iron Hands Legion for strategic support when available as the Legion was largely dismissive of most Imperial Army units as unfit for frontline work. It is also worthy of note that the Iron Hands Legion and its master Ferrus Manus was at the forefront of the introduction of a number of weapons systems and armour patterns, such as Stormbreaker pattern thunder hammer and Indomitus Terminator Armor.  
The strength of the Iron Hands pseudo-feudal system of organisation created a highly disciplined and motivated force which both despised and punished failure and weakness, and rewarded and glorified strength, accomplishment and success. The most competent and strong-willed rose quickly within the ranks, others quickly weeded out. It also had the effect of not inconsiderable friction and direct competition between the Clans, both to attain glory and praise in their Primarch’s eyes and to outdo each other in battle. The most successful Clans were favoured by their Primarch with a greater proportion of recruits and resources. For the rest the message was clear - it was a matter of the survival of the fittest; adapt, grow strong, overcome - or fail and wither to destruction.  

At the outset of the Horus Heresy, the Iron Hands Legion is commonly estimated to have had an operational strength just in excess of 113 000 Legiones Astartes, with a huge operating reserve of wargear and supplies, and a diverse fleet with several capital ships. This placed the Iron Hands within the mid-tier of Legion strengths recorded at this date by their numbers alone.  

### The Keys of Hel
The Iron Hands’ obsession with strength and elimination of weakness led them to revere the machine and distrust the failings inherent in flesh. To them their Emperor-augmented bodies were neither more nor less than a living machine of blood and gristle made for war, a machine which could be repaired and improved upon as opportunity allowed and need commanded.  

The Legion favoured cybernetic replacements, allowing for fallen or injured Astartes to get back in the fight with frightening speed. As a result no Iron Hands who had served in battle for any length of time was wholly without augmentation, and such implants were marks of honour, and often improved and augmented away from the battle’s pressing need. Some of the most veteran among the Iron Hands went so far that they were little more than machines cradling shreds of their former humanity. To be interred within a Dreadnought body and therefore be allowed to fight for their Primarch and the great cause of unifying humanity forever was the highest honour any could aspire to.  

But there were limits to what even Ferrus Manus would countenance and certain mysteries of cybermancy such as true rites of cybernetic resurrection remained prescribed as nightmare sciences of the Dark Age of Technology. Such elements [for example, Agesine Protocols, the Eight Sleepers, Sarcosan Formulae], during the Great Crusade, were seized and suppressed by their Primarch’s order, being put beyond reach but not destroyed.  

After the death of Ferrus Manus, however, some saw virtue in what had once been forbidden and, it is said, turned the Keys of Hel.

Morkarch was an Iron Hands survivor of the Isstvan V Dropsite Massacre, one of the few warriors of Clan Vurgaan to escape after being outflanked by World Eaters and Death Guard forces. He retained his Mk III power armour from Isstvan V, although his destroyed left arm had been replaced with Mk IV components. This technically difficult modification required the expertise of Iron Hands Techmarines because the two armour marks were normally incompatible.

The Medusan Immortals were Iron Hands warriors who had suffered defeat and been saved from death, only to be sent back into battle to redeem their failures. Their numbers increased dramatically after Isstvan V, when the Legion lost Ferrus Manus and much of its leadership. Unlike traditional Immortals, those of Clan Morragul retained their clan affiliation and fought alongside their brothers, seeking redemption through service.

During the assault on Tredecimmia, one such Immortal participated in a heavy assault aboard a Mastodon transport, tasked with breaking through World Eaters auxiliary lines before the XII Legion could counterattack. He bore extensive cybernetic modifications, unusual shoulder armour reinforcement studs, and the red talon insignia of Clan Morragul, marking a significant departure from the traditional practice of stripping Immortals of their clan heraldry.

## Legion Chronicles

### Unification Wars
The martial history of the Xth Legion is a relatively well-documented one, about which a great deal still survives on record to the present. Alongside the other proto-Legions created during the latter days of the Unification Wars on Ancient Terra, they were prepared and armed in preparation for the great work ahead, and their initial outfitting and organisation followed closely to the standard patterns set of the Legiones Astartes at the start of the Great Crusade.  

Their recruitment base is noted as being relatively widespread, with elements drawn from all over Ancient Terra, both from areas of longstanding Imperial support, such as the Qavinite Place cities, and from former enemy blocs such as Solus Stellax. In particular, the warlike culture and renowned brutality of the population of Old Albia, which supplied substantial numbers of recruits for the Xth, had a great deal to do with Legion’s early character, lending them a fierce pride in a warrior ancestry that predated the Legion’s founding.  

### Great Crusade
Its first individual battle honours are recorded in the wider Sol system against the mutant ‘Scythers’ of the deep-orbiting Mican Aggregation and the extermination of the xenos Lyasx enclave hidden beneath the ice of Oberath. The Xth proved itself to be a remorseless and fiercely disciplined attacking force, fully capable of operating with remarkable coordination and tightly focused wrath to achieve tactical and strategic aims.  

[During the battle of Planet 02-34] The massed forces of Xth Legion, under the generalship of their Lord Commander Amadeus DuCaine, formulated a plan of attack which owed much of the feared and pitiless battle-craft of Old Albia: a tactic known as the Hammer and the Storm. After forcing a landing via the brute power of their ships from orbit, DuCaine ordered the Urshan soldiery to make planetfall in full strength. In this they had ‘raised the storm’ - the Orks, bestirred in their millions by the attack from above and, ever-warlike, poured from their warrens to attack the invaders. Within hours the plain was a bloodbath of shot and shell, mangled machinery and bomb craters swollen with broken bodies, both human and Ork. DuCaine waited until he was sure the bulk of the Ork forces were fully committed - swarming like a tide of black ants in numbers large enough to be visible from orbit like a spreading stain - before answering with the might of his Legion. They would be the hammer. The Xth deployed in full armored might in a single massed spearhead, comprised in the main of heavy Malcador, Mauler and Land Raider tanks. In the shadow of these armored giants, the Legion’s squads and dreadnoughts formed in close and disciplined ranks. Moving forward as a single, slow-moving mass, lashing out the relentless torrent of firepower, the Legion went on in unison like some immense harvesting machine, destroying everything in their path.  

After days of near-continuous fighting, the waves of the alien assault grew fewer and further between, until they guttered and stopped at last. The Orks of Rust had not lost the will to fight, but had simply run out of blood and machinery to spend. At the end of the battle, the Legion had suffered less than a fifth of its number in fatalities.  

The Xth developing strategies were studied and indeed copied by several other Legions, such as the IVth and XIIIth in the campaign against the Krooked Klaw Orks and thereafter. Very quickly, with its victories and developing tactics, the Xth Legion was forging an identity for itself in battle, and a name ‘storm walkers’ had been coined for them by the Urhsan who had come to view them with an almost superstitious awe.  

### Rediscovery of Ferrus Manus
In a short span of years after the sundering of the Krooked-Klaw empire, news came that the Xth Legion Primarch was found on a cold, merciless world named Medusa. The Primarch of the Iron Hands, who had taken unto himself the name of Ferrus Manus, was among the first of the Emperor’s lost sons to be discovered and, like Horus and Russ before him, had risen to become a warlord in his own right on the world on which he had been cast.  

Medusa was a cold, barren realm, driven for countless generations to incessant warfare through privation and hardship, but like much of Old Terra before the ascension of the Emperor, while its culture had regressed into barbarity and darkness, it had maintained a great deal of mechanical and technological lore. So it was that Ferrus Manus’ transition from planetary warlord to general of the Great Crusade was a swift one, aided by his evident hunger for the task set before him and the uncompromising intelligence and diligent application of his greater calling he displayed.  

In a scant few years, Ferrus Manus was transferred full control of the Xth Legion which he took command of body and soul, renaming it and remaking it in his image. The Primarch took the Xth apart with the precision and intent with which an artisan might deconstruct a mechanical chronograph. Under his direction, the Legion’s already evident preference for order and systematised combat was refocused and increased. In response to their Primarch’s own values, the newly renamed Iron Hands valued superiority in terms of weaponry and wargear as much as they did zeal, valour or discipline, and the Legion saw a concentration of technological expertise seldom matched by other Legions.  

From the beginning, it was clear Ferrus Manus would prove a harsh but constant overlord. The Primarch was utterly intolerant of failure and swift to anger, but also rigidly and rationally controlled in the application of the wellspring of wroth that was never far from the surface of his thoughts and actions. Quick to scorn and rare to praise, Ferrus Manus set utterly uncompromising standards for his Legion, which he saw as an extension of his own body and will. In truth the Primarch also held himself to those same unmatchable standards and despised duplicity and dissemblance as much as he did weakness, and for these reasons, more than any other, the warriors of the Xᵗʰ loved him as sons love a hard-hearted father, with a scrap of his praise worth more to them than any laurel of victory. Very quickly because of the Primarch’s influence, a mind-set was crystallised in the Legion that meant that failure to the Iron Hands was intolerable. Failure was weakness, and weakness was the greatest sin. If weakness was found in their own ranks, it would be purged so that only the strong remained and so were further strengthened.

Building on the talents and tactics of the Xth Legion had already displayed, Ferrus Manus and the Iron Hands excelled at high-intensity warfare both against technologically advanced opponents and xenos species such as the Orks. As a consequence of this, the Legion’s common opponents in battle often included intransigent human factions who actively resisted unification and had retained some fragment of the technological might of the lost Dark Age of Technology. In such conflicts it was common,  due the strategic necessity, for the Iron Hands to fight closely alongside elements of the Mechanicum and the Titan Legions, with whom a cultural relationship inherited from Medusa was shared, and over the years of battle these ties and allegiance grew.

##### Medusa: The Ruins of Ancient Power

Medusa, the fourth planet of the Sthenelus system in the outer Segmentum Obscurus, was a cold, barely habitable world several times larger than Terra. During the Dark Age of Technology, its mineral wealth had made it a place of considerable importance, remembered by both Mars and Old Earth even after the Age of Strife. Ancient machines had mined its rare ores, while the colossal Telstarax orbital ring transported its resources into space. When a Mechanicum Warp-runner rediscovered Medusa more than a year ahead of the main expeditionary fleets, it encountered a devastated system littered with wrecked ships, shattered planetoids, dust and still-active ancient defences. The ruined Telstarax hung above a surface scarred by exhausted mines, immense craters and near-constant tectonic upheaval. Yet amid the desolation, the vessel detected functioning reactors, vast mobile machines and scattered human survivors. Most importantly, it discovered a Primarch.

##### The Techno-Barbarian Clans

The origins of Medusa’s inhabitants are uncertain. Genetic evidence suggests that several distinct populations survived there, including possible descendants of Dark Age settlers, shipwrecked crews and pre-Navigator Mechanicum expeditions launched during the Age of Strife. The hostile climate, unstable geology and scarcity of native life forced them into a nomadic existence, as entire mountain ranges and seas could change within a season. Their society retained fragments of the Machine Cult and uneven knowledge of weapons, cybernetics and engineering, but little of its former civilisation. The largest clans lived aboard enormous fortified land-crawlers derived partly from ancient mining and harvesting STC designs, sending out raiders and foragers to seize resources and wage perpetual war against their neighbours. Survival depended on strength, and slavery and killing were commonplace. Although historians compared Medusa to Vulkan’s home world of Nocturne, their societies were profoundly different despite their similarly harsh environments.

##### The Gorgon and the Wyrm Asirnoth

Ferrus Manus rarely spoke of his formative years, and much of what is known comes from Medusan folklore rather than reliable records. According to those traditions, his arrival shattered Karaashi, the Pinnacle of Black Ice, in the northern polar regions, far from the inhabited clan territories. These forbidden lands were believed to harbour restless dead and ancient iron-skinned monsters. Legends describe the young Primarch wandering the north, overcoming storm giants and destroying monstrous machines left buried beneath the ice. The most famous account concerns Asirnoth, a seemingly deathless great wyrm whose quicksilver-like skin permanently coated the Primarch’s hands and forearms during its death throes, giving rise to his common name, Ferrus Manus.

##### Warlord, Inventor and Teacher

Known as the Gorgon, Ferrus emerged from the forbidden regions and imposed his authority upon the scattered clans. The Medusans regarded him as a living god, but he neither demanded nor encouraged worship; obedience was what mattered, and resistance was crushed. Unlike a ruler seeking to civilise his subjects through peace, Ferrus deliberately allowed inter-clan warfare to continue. He exchanged his inventions with the Iron-Fathers, the priest-shamans responsible for the clans’ spiritual and technological needs, in return for the ancient knowledge they had preserved. His teachings enabled the clans to manufacture superior weapons and stronger machines, intensifying their struggle to survive. He also led their bravest warriors into sealed underground vaults and buried fragments of ancient machine-works in search of metal and salvage. There they confronted degenerate mutants, decaying cyborgs and surviving engines of forgotten ages. By the Emperor’s arrival, Ferrus had become Medusa’s warlord, sage and demigod. Tradition holds that he was expecting the Emperor and already suspected the purpose for which he had been created.

##### Forging the Iron Hands

Upon assuming command of the Terran-founded X Legion, Ferrus established Medusa as its principal base and forcibly merged the Legion with the native clan society. Chapters became Clans, and Terran Space Marines displaced the existing secular and spiritual leaders through force. The Iron Hands thus became the dominant Medusan caste, while the population’s traditional struggle acquired a new purpose: proving that its children deserved a place in the Legion. Imperial Compliance did not end hardship, conflict or superstition, because Ferrus intended these conditions to eliminate the weak and produce physically resilient, warlike and psychologically suitable recruits. To compensate for Medusa’s small population, he imposed a perpetual tithe of strong adolescent males on resistant human worlds conquered by the Iron Hands. Selected by itinerant Mechanicum priests, these youths were sent to Medusa to live among the clans, survive their trials and potentially become Legion aspirants upon reaching maturity. In this way, the Gorgon ensured that the ruthless survival culture of his adopted world would shape successive generations of the X Legion.

### Later Great Crusade
As the years passed, Iron Hands also gained an increasingly belligerent reputation with their fellow Legions as lacking both patience with others and subtlety of action, both in the person of their Primarch and the character of the Legion’s rank and file. Iron Hands’ ruthlessness in battle and their seemingly callous indifference to the price of victory made them conquerors, not liberators. It is also consequently true that Ferrus Manus and his Legion cared little for the minutiae of the Great Crusade, they saw their task as a clear one: to expand the borders of the Imperium and destroy its enemies. When once asked about his Legion’s role in the Great Crusade, Ferrus Manus said: “Make war and move on, and again, and again, until nothing breathes which stands against us. Everything else is sophistry and pretty lies.”  

As the Great Crusade progressed, the Iron Hands Legion was in constant service at the front line of expansion, both at the head of its own battle groups, compliance and extermination taskforces, and wielded as a part of larger Expeditionary fleets, fighting alongside other Legions where needed. After more than a century under its Primarch’s command, the victories of the Iron Hands could not easily be counted and, indeed, it is believed many of its battles went unrecorded, as the Legion itself became increasingly insular in many ways, and uninterested in sharing the details of its independent operations in anything but the most perfunctory way. To the wider Imperium it presented a cold, brutal façade; the Legion as a calculating, prideful and powerful engine of war that did not stoop to deal with petty concerns or affairs of state. Such secrets the Iron Hands Legion did keep, it kept well.  

### Dawn of Heresy
Iron Hands’ fleet was badly injured by the Emperor’s Children treachery, but whose Primarch, Ferrus Manus, was also proceeding to the Istvaan System at best speed with the warriors of his chosen Avernii clan. At the time of events on Istvaan III were taking place, the major part of the Legion was under the direct command of their Primarch, forming a 52nd Expeditionary Fleet.

### Istvaan V
For Ferrus Manus, the absence of the Traitor Legions’ fleets in the Istvaan system was not so much a cause of suspicion as it was an opportunity that should not be wasted. Through the bungled attempt of Fulgrim and his Emperor’s Children to sway Manus to the Traitor’s cause and the flight of the ‘Eisenstein’ escaping the betrayal at Istvaan III, the Imperium had been made aware of the Warmaster’s plot long before the arch-traitor had been ready, and to the grim mind of the master of Iron Hands, it was an error for which the enemy would be made to pay.  

The decision for immediate and all-out attack upon the Traitors positions on the planet was taken by Ferrus Manus. As senior battle-leader among his fellow brothers, the darkly aggrieved master of the Iron Hands would brook no delay. Some surviving reports make note of the Ferrus Manus overriding Vulkan’s counsel of caution to await the bulk of the Loyalists before mounting the attack, and taking Corax’s silence for agreement. His warlike intelligence formulating a hundred possible scenarios of attack and defence already, then refining them further as the data from Ad Temperesta intelligence had been received.  

Owing of the perfidious treachery of Fulgrim, and the uncontrollable wrath of Ferrus Manus in response, the Iron Hands Primarch broke ranks from this force and reached Istvaan V with only the elite cadre of chosen from his then-favoured Avernii Clan alongside him, almost all of whom were annihilated with their slain Primarch during the Dropsite Massacre.  

First descended hundreds of drop pods and assault rams, falling like a steel rain upon the world, with Ferrum’s own deadly cargo the first to fall. In this first onslaught came Ferrus Manus himself, hungry for the blood of those who betrayed their father.  Such was the relentlessness of this first strike wave and the precision and timing with which it had been executed, that for every drop pod or landing claw smashed in the descent, a dozen or more made planet fall intact, spilling their Space Marine cargo into the teeth of Traitor’s defence lines.  

The Gorgon’s precisely calculated attack patterns that brought them to the very edge of the shielding overlays of the fortress, and well forward of the projected killing ground so carefully crafted in the foothills of the depression by the Traitors. The battle was joined at close quarters, a fight as savage and pitiless as any the Great Crusade had seen, brother against brother, superhuman against superhuman. Bolters thundered, chainblades howled, spraying sparks of armor plates or biting deep in suit seems.  

The Loyalist attack formations had succeeded in forming up according to Ferus Manus’ pre-battle plan.  

In the center of the line, where the enemy line was at its worst furious, indefatigable and roaring his defiance was Ferrus Manus. The Gorgon led a grinding wedge of Terminators from Avernii clan, numbering a thousand strong.. Whilst around him rallied the vanguard survivors and six maniples of Iron Hands Legion battle-automata.. The Primarch crushed and killed with his bare silver hands, ripping apart the foe with inhuman, cold rage against which even the Legiones Astartes could not stand. Where the Gorgon struck the enemy, warriors of the Sons of Horus and Emperor’s Children alike broke before him. As the wrathful Primarch pressed on, his dark-iron clad Legionaries followed relentlessly with him through the unceasing hail of shellfire..  

After 3 hours of battle, Salamanders and Raven Guard began to fall back to regroup and rearm, having expended their supplies.. Ferrus Manus, however, would have none of it. Retreat was unthinkable to him, and, heedless to the fact, the enclave of Traitors had formed around him, he ordered his forces not merely to hold the ground but to press on, becoming dangerously separated from the Loyalist lines. There are unconfirmed reports that what so provoked the Gorgon was the sight of Fulgrim. Once a friend, now most hated enemy, at the sight of the taunting Fulgrim Ferrus Manus’ rage had overflowed, and he broke from his own ranks in order to duel with his fellow Primarch, seeking to slay the Phoenician no matter the cost.  

Ferrus Manus fell, slain by arch-traitor Fulgrim. Betrayed, entrapped and outnumbered, for Loyalists on the Istvaan V there was no hope of salvation or victory. In the hearts of surviving Salamanders, Raven Guard and Iron Hands, outraged hatred roared into savage life. Spitting defiance to the last they fought on; until the last shell and the last drop of blood, they fought like legendary heroes of old and they did not falter, but this was no longer a battle, it was extermination.  

A great many vessels of the 52nd Expeditionary Fleet were destroyed, along with tens of thousands Iron Hands on board as they later transited into the Istvaan system, rushing unknowingly into a death trap from which only few ships were to escape.  

Taking together, these losses were great, although this left more survivors from the Iron Hands in number that could be ascribed to the Dropsite Massacre’s other victim Legions overall. This was largely the case of large groups of Iron Hands were presently engaged elsewhere and unable to heed, or ignorant of, their Primarch’s rushed call to assault the traitors at the Istvaan system.  

It is known that some Clans re-organised a council of Iron-Fathers on Medusa to take charge of the ravaged Legion, and speeded the recruitment and creation of new Space Marines from the extensive stores of gene-seed kept there with the goal to rebuild the Legion. In the aftermath some Iron Hands units and, in some cases, entire Clans shunned the Medusan Council’s assumed authority and went their own way, consumed by their own hatred and need of revenge. These fractured forces fought their own war and it is widely believed some delved into the use of dark and forbidden forces from humanity’s ancient past to do so, although this has been strenuously denied since by the Iron Hands Clan Council. Other lone Iron Hands cast off their Legion’s livery entirely and either fought on alone or went renegade - Iron Hands no more.

### Assault on Bodt [008.M31]

Autek Mor was a Terran-born Iron Hands warlord and Iron-Father of Clan Morragul, a formation composed largely of outcasts and warriors who struggled to conform to the Legion's rigid traditions. Having earned command by killing his clan's previous leader, Mor remained an outsider even under Ferrus Manus. His clan was held in orbital reserve during the Isstvan V Dropsite Massacre, sparing most of his warriors from the initial slaughter. Arriving aboard his flagship, the Red Talon, Mor discovered the Loyalist fleet in ruins and learned of Ferrus Manus' death. Despite attempting to break through the Traitor blockade, he was ultimately forced to withdraw with his surviving ships.

Unlike Shadrak Meduson, who favoured guerrilla warfare against Horus' armies, Mor developed a strategy of overwhelming retaliation. He gathered independent Loyalist forces and allowed the Traitors to conquer worlds before striking them at their most vulnerable, when their strength had been depleted by invasion. His intention was to create a devastated defensive barrier separating Traitor-held sectors from those still loyal to Terra. Mor willingly sacrificed entire populations to achieve this objective, regarding such losses as a necessary part of his ruthless calculations of war.

Within a year of the Dropsite Massacre, most of the northern Imperium had fallen to Horus, while scattered Loyalist armies struggled to retreat and regroup. Against this backdrop, Autek Mor launched an independent campaign of devastating counterattacks from his flagship, the Red Talon. One of his earliest known targets was Gethsamaine Colonus in the Cyclops Cluster, which had joined the Warmaster a year earlier. Mor's Iron Hands fleet emerged from the Grail Abyss, captured key Traitor prisoners, and annihilated the planet's major cities with nuclear weapons, leaving the world uninhabitable for generations.

Unlike Shadrak Meduson's covert guerrilla campaign, Mor pursued open, overwhelming destruction against worlds that had submitted to Horus. Operating without Terra's oversight, he allowed the Traitor advance to pass before striking its vulnerable rear. Although remembered as the last Loyalist to leave the Coronid Deeps, Mor never truly abandoned the region. His relentless campaign likely prevented numerous additional worlds from falling to the Warmaster.

Autek Mor's growing Iron Hands force was reinforced by Imperial Fists survivors and the Agathon Solar Auxilia, while captured Traitor officers provided valuable intelligence. Interrogations revealed that Bodt, a World Eaters recruitment and muster world, remained operational. Imperial Fists reports of Mechanicum transports travelling between Sarum and Bodt reinforced Mor's suspicions that the planet was supporting a major Traitor military project. Bodt was believed to contain substantial Astartes wargear reserves and potentially thousands of uncommitted World Eaters recruits. Mor also suspected that the Sarum Mechanicum was involved in forbidden modifications to Legionary implantation and indoctrination procedures, a suspicion later proven correct.

Rather than continue his usual strategy of attacking newly conquered worlds, Mor decided to strike directly at Bodt and destroy its military infrastructure before its resources could reinforce Horus. Meanwhile, Imperial commanders were attempting to organise a defensive line between Paramar and Chondax, and fragmentary records suggest that Mor may have received orders to join them. If so, he ignored those orders, remaining determined to pursue his independent campaign. Twelve days after departing Iod Binary, the Red Talon fleet emerged at the edge of the Bodt system, prepared for a full-scale assault against one of the most brutal Legions in the Imperium.

Autek Mor began his assault on Bodt's capital, Tredecimmia, with an audacious plan to destroy the entire World Eaters recruitment world. As the Red Talon fleet approached, Mor identified Bodt's lone moon, which was passing unusually close to the planet along its elliptical orbit. He ordered his warships to expend their remaining high-yield void ordnance to alter the moon's trajectory by a fraction of a degree, redirecting it towards Bodt.

Instead of passing the planet at a distance of 500,000 kilometres, the moon was now destined to collide with it within hours, unleashing destruction far beyond the capabilities of Mor's conventional weapons. The enormous detonation used to redirect the moon was visible from Bodt's surface, although its inhabitants had no idea that it signalled their world's impending destruction.

The sight of Bodt's burning moon plunged the planet's feral gun-clans into chaos. Terrified by the approaching celestial catastrophe, they turned their weapons against one another, with some even firing at the moon itself. Autek Mor likely intended this psychological effect to neutralise the gun-clans before his invasion. Meanwhile, the Red Talon fleet divided into twelve assault squadrons and launched a precision orbital bombardment against the surface-to-orbit defences surrounding Tredecimmia, destroying entire chains of weapons emplacements within an hour.

Despite the devastation, Bodt's principal defenders remained largely intact. The World Eaters overlord cadre, supported by Mechanicum Taghmata and Titan forces, were protected within subterranean fortifications and powerful void shields. Mor understood that orbital bombardment alone could not defeat such opponents, and by the third hour of the assault, he initiated a full-scale planetary landing to eliminate the surviving defenders before the moon struck.

As Bodt's burning moon approached the planet, Autek Mor launched a massive planetary assault against Tredecimmia. Waves of Iron Hands and Imperial Fists drop pods descended through intense anti-aircraft fire, suffering heavy losses but establishing multiple landing zones within four hours. Simultaneously, Xiphon fighters, Storm Eagles, and Fire Raptors attacked the planet's strongest fortifications, destroying a heavily shielded bastion in the poisoned inner sea. Iron Hands and Imperial Fists Thunderhawk formations then assaulted a fortified mountain complex, fighting through hundreds of defenders before the Imperial Fists breached its subterranean command bunker and crippled the Traitors' command structure.

At Tredecimmia's star port, Iron Hands Legionaries encountered World Eaters displaying extraordinary ferocity, providing the first evidence of the XII Legion's forbidden implantation experiments. The battle descended into vicious close combat before Imperial Fists reinforcements attacked the defenders from behind, although the World Eaters continued fighting to the last. Elsewhere, the Agathon Solar Auxilia struggled to contain previously unseen Traitor Mechanicum war machines equipped with powerful Warp-based shielding, losing dozens of super-heavy tanks. Frenzied gun-clans also attacked the Loyalists in overwhelming numbers, forcing Astartes Tactical Support squads to burn them down with flamers. With the star port, command bunker, and several strategic facilities neutralised, Mor initiated the decisive phase of his operation, directing attention towards the heavily defended macro-provender silos in north-western Tredecimmia, where the true purpose of his invasion would become apparent.

In the fifth hour of the assault on Bodt, Autek Mor launched an operation against the heavily defended macro-provender silos near Tredecimmia. Anticipating the intervention of Legio Audax Titans, he deployed Iron Hands and Imperial Fists blocking forces north and east of the complex, including Mastodon assault transports and Mauler battle tanks. The World Eaters responded as Mor had intended, abandoning their defensive positions to attack the eastern Iron Hands force, leaving the silos' western approaches exposed.

Mor then personally led the decisive strike, teleporting from the Red Talon with an elite force of Morragul Clan Gorgon Terminators, whose specialised armour technology he had managed to reproduce despite his isolation. The heavily armoured Iron Hands overwhelmed the remaining automated gun-towers supplied by the Sarum Mechanicum and advanced through the silos towards the entrance to the underground stasis chambers, the true objective of Mor's assault.

Autek Mor and his Gorgon Terminators encountered unexpected resistance inside Bodt's macro-provender silos, where insect-like Mechanicum sentries detached from the surrounding structures and attacked with rad-cleansers and lightning weapons. Fighting through the mechanical defenders, the Gorgons breached the heavily fortified entrance to the underground stasis chambers. Mor then ordered his warriors to hold the entrance and descended alone, forbidding anyone to follow him regardless of what happened.

Elsewhere, the World Eaters launched ferocious counterattacks using waves of rapidly indoctrinated Inductii, whose lack of experience was offset by extraordinary aggression and physical strength. More disturbingly, the Iron Hands encountered Red Butchers Terminators who were revealed to be former Loyalist World Eaters, psychologically shattered during the betrayal at Isstvan III and now incapable of distinguishing friend from foe. Their assault nearly overwhelmed the Iron Hands until Mastodon transports unleashed cyclonic melta fire, incinerating the Red Butchers alongside some of the Loyalists trapped in combat with them.

As Bodt's burning moon approached its final descent, the battle for Tredecimmia grew increasingly desperate. The World Eaters defending the planet were largely composed of rapidly implanted and indoctrinated Legionaries, many reduced to uncontrollable berserkers far removed from the Emperor's original vision for the Astartes. Wearing mismatched armour manufactured by the Crimson Brotherhood of Sarum and wielding unfamiliar experimental weapons, they inflicted severe casualties on the Iron Hands and Imperial Fists. Nevertheless, the experienced Loyalist veterans adapted their tactics and continued advancing despite mounting losses.

By the sixth hour, the battle had begun turning against Autek Mor's forces, while the Iron-Father remained inside the subterranean vaults beneath the macro-provender silos, pursuing an objective whose nature remained unknown. His prolonged absence endangered the entire operation as the moon drew closer to impact. The situation became critical when twelve Warhound Titans of Legio Audax emerged from the northern wastes, having bypassed or overwhelmed the Loyalist blocking forces. Their weapons devastated everything in their path as they advanced directly towards the silos, leaving only the Gorgon Terminators of Clan Morragul to oppose them. Even their formidable armour could not withstand such overwhelming firepower for long, and by the seventh hour, with planetary destruction imminent, Mor's mission appeared on the verge of failure.

The destruction of Bodt in 008.M31 left the World Eaters' recruitment world a volcanic wasteland that might remain uninhabitable for centuries or eventually disintegrate entirely. Autek Mor's invasion force included over 5,000 Iron Hands, fewer than 800 Imperial Fists, 300–400 additional Loyalist Astartes, and approximately 12,000 Solar Auxilia. Opposing them were around 3,500 established World Eaters, as many as 11,000 rapidly produced Inductii, fewer than 100 Legion command personnel, and substantial Mechanicum and Titan support under Tribune Karak and the Crimson Brotherhood of Sarum.

Although Mor had already guaranteed Bodt's destruction by redirecting its moon, he risked thousands of warriors to recover a mysterious stasis casket bearing the insignia of the Emperor's Terran gene-wrights. Its contents remain unknown, but some historians speculate that Mor intended to use forbidden genetic technology to transform or expand Clan Morragul beyond the established limitations of the Legiones Astartes. His true ambitions remain uncertain, reinforcing his reputation as a ruthless warlord whose power rested as much on dangerous secrets as on military strength.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Iron Hands were shattered by the death of Ferrus Manus at Isstvan V. Their Legion fragmented into independent warfleets, many joining Shadrak Meduson's Shattered Legions or fighting under Autek Mor. Others pursued campaigns of vengeance against the Traitors at Gagia, Sacrissan, and Agromis.

The Shattered Legions included Iron Hands survivors under Shadrak Meduson, alongside Salamanders and Raven Guard. Their combat organisations and independent raids are described in the Age of Darkness overview.

The Shattered Legions emerged from the Isstvan V Dropsite Massacre, where the Iron Hands, Salamanders, and Raven Guard were nearly annihilated by the betrayal of the Word Bearers, Night Lords, Iron Warriors, and Alpha Legion. Survivors escaped aboard scattered warships, often forming mixed groups of warriors from all three Legions. Some attempted to return to their home worlds, while others remained together, united by their shared losses and determination to fight Horus.

After the remaining Iron Hands Clan-Council was destroyed at Oqueth Minor, Shadrak Meduson of Clan Sorrgol assumed command of the largest surviving formations. He united more than 20,000 Legionaries into independent, mixed-Legion strike cells and dispersed them throughout Traitor-controlled territory. For three years, these forces conducted thousands of raids, assassinations, and sabotage operations, gradually weakening Horus' armies and delaying their advance towards Terra. Their campaign was so carefully coordinated that even Horus initially failed to recognise that the seemingly unrelated attacks were directed by a single commander.

The Ruinstorm subsequently created countless other isolated Astartes formations across the galaxy. Some had no knowledge of their Primarchs' allegiances, while others deliberately rejected both sides and continued following their original orders. Unlike Meduson's coordinated forces, many pursued independent objectives, particularly vengeance, sometimes fighting throughout the Heresy and into the Great Scouring. Even without a unified strategy, their actions frequently disrupted major military operations and influenced the wider civil war.

The Shattered Legions depended heavily on strong commanders capable of uniting warriors from different Legions after losing contact with their Primarchs. Leaders such as Shadrak Meduson achieved this through charisma, tactical ability, or personal example, while others relied on intimidation and brutal discipline. Despite their mixed composition, these forces generally preserved the standardised rank structures established during the Great Crusade, allowing officers from one Legion to command warriors from another. A praetor might therefore lead hundreds of Legionaries from different Legions despite having only a handful of his own warriors under his command.

Most Shattered Legion formations numbered several hundred Astartes, although some reached approximately 2,000 warriors, commonly drawn from three different Legions. Their organisation varied considerably, and isolation from regular supply lines forced them to rely on whatever equipment and specialist units they possessed, including formations such as Iron Hands Medusan Immortals or Salamanders Pyroclasts. This adaptability made their attacks difficult to predict, while combining different Legions' fighting doctrines often produced unusually effective battlefield tactics. Similar formations also emerged among the Traitors, including isolated detachments continuing earlier orders and disgraced warriors sent on near-suicidal missions against Loyalist survivors. Thus, both sides fielded independent, mixed-Legion forces throughout the Horus Heresy.

### Battle of Perditus [009.M31]

One notable confrontation was the Battle of Perditus in 009.M31, where the 98th Clan Company of the Iron Hands intercepted a Death Guard force led by its First Company near a forbidden Mechanicum research facility. The Death Guard sought an artefact believed capable of calming the Ruinstorm, whose power had engulfed the Ultima Segmentum less than two years earlier. The two Legions fought for more than a month before Lion El'Jonson intervened, ordering both forces to withdraw. According to Iron Hands accounts, the Dark Angels subsequently seized the artefact, considering it essential for navigating the Warp during the Ruinstorm.

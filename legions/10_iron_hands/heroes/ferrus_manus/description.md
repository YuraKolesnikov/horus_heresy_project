## Notes

Ferrus Manus was killed at Isstvan V. His death was followed by the fragmentation of the Iron Hands and campaigns undertaken separately by Autek Mor, Shadrak Meduson and other survivors.

### Ferrus Manus — The Medusan Inheritance

#### Medusa: The Ruins of Ancient Power

Medusa, the fourth planet of the Sthenelus system in the outer Segmentum Obscurus, was a cold, barely habitable world several times larger than Terra. During the Dark Age of Technology, its mineral wealth had made it a place of considerable importance, remembered by both Mars and Old Earth even after the Age of Strife. Ancient machines had mined its rare ores, while the colossal Telstarax orbital ring transported its resources into space. When a Mechanicum Warp-runner rediscovered Medusa more than a year ahead of the main expeditionary fleets, it encountered a devastated system littered with wrecked ships, shattered planetoids, dust and still-active ancient defences. The ruined Telstarax hung above a surface scarred by exhausted mines, immense craters and near-constant tectonic upheaval. Yet amid the desolation, the vessel detected functioning reactors, vast mobile machines and scattered human survivors. Most importantly, it discovered a Primarch.

#### The Techno-Barbarian Clans

The origins of Medusa’s inhabitants are uncertain. Genetic evidence suggests that several distinct populations survived there, including possible descendants of Dark Age settlers, shipwrecked crews and pre-Navigator Mechanicum expeditions launched during the Age of Strife. The hostile climate, unstable geology and scarcity of native life forced them into a nomadic existence, as entire mountain ranges and seas could change within a season. Their society retained fragments of the Machine Cult and uneven knowledge of weapons, cybernetics and engineering, but little of its former civilisation. The largest clans lived aboard enormous fortified land-crawlers derived partly from ancient mining and harvesting STC designs, sending out raiders and foragers to seize resources and wage perpetual war against their neighbours. Survival depended on strength, and slavery and killing were commonplace. Although historians compared Medusa to Vulkan’s home world of Nocturne, the source stresses that their societies were profoundly different despite their similarly harsh environments.

#### The Gorgon and the Wyrm Asirnoth

Ferrus Manus rarely spoke of his formative years, and much of what is known comes from Medusan folklore rather than reliable records. According to those traditions, his arrival shattered Karaashi, the Pinnacle of Black Ice, in the northern polar regions, far from the inhabited clan territories. These forbidden lands were believed to harbour restless dead and ancient iron-skinned monsters. Legends describe the young Primarch wandering the north, overcoming storm giants and destroying monstrous machines left buried beneath the ice. The most famous account concerns Asirnoth, a seemingly deathless great wyrm whose quicksilver-like skin permanently coated the Primarch’s hands and forearms during its death throes, giving rise to his common name, Ferrus Manus.

#### Warlord, Inventor and Teacher

Known as the Gorgon, Ferrus emerged from the forbidden regions and imposed his authority upon the scattered clans. The Medusans regarded him as a living god, but he neither demanded nor encouraged worship; obedience was what mattered, and resistance was crushed. Unlike a ruler seeking to civilise his subjects through peace, Ferrus deliberately allowed inter-clan warfare to continue. He exchanged his inventions with the Iron-Fathers, the priest-shamans responsible for the clans’ spiritual and technological needs, in return for the ancient knowledge they had preserved. His teachings enabled the clans to manufacture superior weapons and stronger machines, intensifying their struggle to survive. He also led their bravest warriors into sealed underground vaults and buried fragments of ancient machine-works in search of metal and salvage. There they confronted degenerate mutants, decaying cyborgs and surviving engines of forgotten ages. By the Emperor’s arrival, Ferrus had become Medusa’s warlord, sage and demigod. Tradition holds that he was expecting the Emperor and already suspected the purpose for which he had been created.

#### Forging the Iron Hands

Upon assuming command of the Terran-founded X Legion, Ferrus established Medusa as its principal base and forcibly merged the Legion with the native clan society. Chapters became Clans, and Terran Space Marines displaced the existing secular and spiritual leaders through force. The Iron Hands thus became the dominant Medusan caste, while the population’s traditional struggle acquired a new purpose: proving that its children deserved a place in the Legion. Imperial Compliance did not end hardship, conflict or superstition, because Ferrus intended these conditions to eliminate the weak and produce physically resilient, warlike and psychologically suitable recruits. To compensate for Medusa’s small population, he imposed a perpetual tithe of strong adolescent males on resistant human worlds conquered by the Iron Hands. Selected by itinerant Mechanicum priests, these youths were sent to Medusa to live among the clans, survive their trials and potentially become Legion aspirants upon reaching maturity. In this way, the Gorgon ensured that the ruthless survival culture of his adopted world would shape successive generations of the X Legion.

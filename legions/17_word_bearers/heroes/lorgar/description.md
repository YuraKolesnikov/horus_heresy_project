Lorgar and Angron were dispatched to confront Guilliman's Ultramarines. Their Shadow Crusade devastated Imperial worlds; Lorgar's mass sacrifices and pact with Warp powers generated the Ruinstorm, disrupting communications and Warp travel.

Before their reunion with Lorgar in 857.M30, the XVIIth Legion was known as the Imperial Heralds, or the Iconoclasts. They were among the most fanatical enforcers of the Imperial Truth, specialising in destroying religious cults, psychic sects, and xenos tyrannies. Their uncompromising brutality earned countless victories, but Imperial authorities feared they would leave entire worlds devastated rather than pacified.

Lorgar's arrival transformed the Legion into the Word Bearers. He encouraged his warriors to rebuild conquered worlds and spread his belief that the Emperor was a living god. Although this initially appeared to soften the Legion's character, its growing religious devotion increasingly conflicted with the Emperor's secular vision.

In 963.M30, the Emperor finally intervened. Frustrated by the Word Bearers' slow conquests and construction of religious monuments, he sent Malcador and Roboute Guilliman to enforce his will. The Ultramarines destroyed Monarchia, the sacred city on Khur, and the entire XVIIth Legion was forced to kneel in its ashes while their devotion was publicly condemned.

Following this humiliation, Lorgar dramatically accelerated the Word Bearers' conquests, bringing dozens of worlds into Imperial Compliance and apparently regaining the Emperor's favour.

In retrospect, this sudden obedience concealed the earliest preparations for the Legion's betrayal. What appeared to be renewed loyalty was the beginning of a transformation that would ultimately make the Word Bearers central architects of the Horus Heresy.

Lorgar and the Word Bearers had a personal motive for attacking Ultramar. Their humiliation at Monarchia had created a lasting hatred of Guilliman and his Legion. Although the precise architect of the Calth betrayal remains uncertain, Lorgar is widely suspected of devising the plan.

Following the Dropsite Massacre, Lorgar and Angron assembled an enormous invasion force to devastate Ultramar. The Word Bearers commanded over 100 capital warships, including nine Gloriana-class battleships and three immense Abyss-class vessels: Furious Abyss, Blessed Lady, and Trisagion. Alongside the World Eaters' numerous smaller warships, the combined fleet carried nearly 300,000 Space Marines, supported by vast numbers of mortal soldiers. Their principal flagships were Lorgar's Fidelitas Lex and Angron's Conqueror.

By mid-007.M31, the Word Bearers and World Eaters had secretly assembled near the Xersinia Prohibited Zone, a quarantined region north of Calth. Its isolation allowed the Traitor fleet to regroup unnoticed after its turbulent journey through the Warp.

Lorgar divided the Traitor armada into at least six separate fleets, each assigned a different objective:

Calth: Erebus and Kor Phaeron commanded the principal Word Bearers assault force, comprising nearly 70 capital ships and numerous escorts. Their flagships were Destiny's Hand and the Infidus Imperator, formerly the Raptorous Rex.

Armatura: Lorgar and Angron led the largest concentration of destructive firepower, including the Abyss-class battleships Trisagion and Blessed Lady, alongside the Fidelitas Lex and Conqueror. Their objective was to destroy one of Ultramar's most important military strongholds.

Macragge: Word Bearers Captain Zadkiel launched an ultimately unsuccessful assault aboard the Furious Abyss.

The wider Five Hundred Worlds: Smaller Word Bearers and World Eaters squadrons conducted rapid raids against Imperial shipping, agricultural colonies, and industrial centres, including Espandor and Latona.

The Shadow Crusade was designed to cripple Ultramar militarily while spreading terror and destruction throughout Guilliman's realm. By attacking multiple worlds simultaneously, Lorgar intended to prevent the Ultramarines from concentrating their forces or recovering from the destruction at Calth.

These massacres also served a darker purpose: the suffering of Ultramar's population would fuel Lorgar's sorcerous plans. Angron and his World Eaters, previously restrained by the Word Bearers, were now free to unleash their full brutality.

### The Primordial Truth

Decades before the Heresy, Lorgar had already begun questioning the Emperor's denial of divinity. Following his humiliation at Monarchia, where the Emperor condemned the Word Bearers' religious devotion, Lorgar embarked upon a pilgrimage into the galaxy's darkest regions. Guided by his desire to prove that gods genuinely existed, he eventually encountered the powers that would become central to his doctrine of the Primordial Truth. His correspondence with Kor Phaeron and the teachings preserved in the Book of Lorgar suggest that this revelation was the culmination of a deliberate search. Unlike his brothers, who often encountered the Warp through circumstance or military necessity, Lorgar actively pursued its mysteries and ultimately embraced the entities the Emperor had forbidden humanity to worship.

Lorgar and the Primordial Truth

Following his discovery of the entities inhabiting the Warp, Lorgar Aurelian underwent a profound psychological and spiritual transformation. Later revisions of the Book of Lorgar, some written in blood rather than ink, adopted increasingly disturbing ritualistic language, while Kor Phaeron's correspondence began openly discussing supernatural powers in terms of religious worship. Rather than merely proving the Emperor's Imperial Truth false, Lorgar became a devoted prophet of the Chaos Gods. By the Horus Heresy, his ultimate ambition was the union of humanity and the Primordial Annihilator, creating a symbiotic relationship between mankind and the powers of the Warp. He pursued this vision methodically, spreading the religious traditions of Colchis throughout his Legion before establishing selective alliances with Warp entities sympathetic to his objectives.

Horus learned much of the Warp's deeper mysteries through Lorgar, but their ambitions differed fundamentally. Where Lorgar envisioned spiritual transformation and worship, Horus regarded Daemons as strategic resources to exploit in his campaign for galactic domination. He sought neither religious enlightenment nor a replacement for the Imperial Truth, but absolute political and military power. In his calculations, sacrificing warriors, worlds, and entire populations in exchange for Daemonic assistance appeared profitable. Horus believed that victory would allow him to renegotiate any obligations owed to the Warp, failing to understand the true cost of these bargains. Lorgar attempted to warn him against excessive arrogance and reckless exploitation of supernatural powers, but Horus increasingly dismissed his brother's advice, creating a growing philosophical rift between them.

# White Scars

## Legion Structure

### V Legion organisation, command and auxiliary orders

#### 7. Structure: From Companies to Hordes and Brotherhoods

The Legion did not reproduce the Principia Bellicosa’s standard chapter and company hierarchy. Jaghatai reorganised the old Pioneer Companies into Hordes, large independent war hosts under Noyan-khans. The earliest reorganisation probably created five Hordes, though some accounts give seven. Their sizes varied dramatically, from a little more than 5,000 to approximately 20,000 warriors, depending on a commander’s needs and strategic role. Transfers between Hordes and the creation of new ones were common; the aim was effective leadership rather than numerical symmetry.

Within each Horde were Brotherhoods, closer to traditional companies but even more variable in size—ranging from fewer than a few hundred to several thousand. A Brotherhood typically included a core of mounted shock troops, with scouts, close-assault specialists and other units reflecting the preferences of its Khan. Some specialised in heavy fire support or siege operations, though such formations were uncommon. Each Brotherhood was expected to work independently for long periods and could vary its internal structure without awaiting approval from a distant central staff.

Many Brotherhoods maintained a Keshig, a body of the most experienced fighters who served as both elite reserve and their Khan’s personal guard. Because leadership depended upon judgement and achievement rather than a fixed table of ranks, the White Scars could quickly improvise new formations. Conversely, the inconsistency of their rosters and force strengths made them difficult for allies or the Divisio Militaris to classify and supply.

#### 8. Command and the Meaning of Loyalty

Jaghatai was the unquestioned Great Khan, but beneath him command was deliberately decentralised. A Noyan-khan led a Horde; individual Khans commanded Brotherhoods. A Khan might nominate a Kavkhan as principal lieutenant and designated successor. Additional specialist officers included Terni-khans responsible for voidcraft, Gan-khans for armouries, and respected Stormseers as spiritual and strategic advisers. The prestige of these roles rested on earned trust and practical ability rather than adherence to a uniform Imperial rank ladder.

The White Scars rarely maintained conventional military police or rigid disciplinary offices. They relied instead on personal oaths, the authority of commanders who fought beside their men and the belief that a warrior must fulfil an accepted duty without hesitation. Although their culture placed absolute weight on obedience to the Khan, it did not equate that bond with automatic obedience to every order from the wider Imperium. Their loyalty to their father and their insistence on independent judgement were inseparable—a strength that became a political danger when the Heresy divided the Primarchs.

#### 9. The Stormseers and the Question of the Warp

The White Scars’ psykers, often described by outsiders as “weather-witches,” descended from the Legion’s early Librarius personnel but were shaped equally by Chogorian shamans and tribal law. Stormseers advised Khans and Brotherhoods, mediated disputes and interpreted signs, while using psychic power sparingly and with considerable finesse. On the battlefield they obscured movement with fog, wind, hail and storms, and disrupted enemies without relying exclusively on destructive displays of force.

Their ritual language and visible mysticism appeared superstitious to advocates of the Imperial Truth. Mortarion was a particularly vocal opponent of their privileged position. Their discipline and knowledge of the Warp suggest a more measured understanding of psychic danger than that of many nominally more secular practitioners. They were an influential council within the Legion, and certain weapons and specialist orders could be deployed only with the assent of both the Great Khan and the Stormseers.

#### 10. The Five Principal Auxiliary Orders

The White Scars maintained specialised groups outside the normal Horde and Brotherhood system. Some reported directly to Jaghatai; others worked on their own initiative. Their organisation reflected the Legion’s preference for self-directed service rather than a single prescribed structure.

Karaoghlanlar · “Dark Sons of Death”. Black-clad Destroyer formations employing proscribed weaponry for total annihilation and certain post-campaign rituals. Answered to the Council of Seers rather than a single Brotherhood; used only by agreement of the Great Khan and Stormseers.

Burgediin Sarhvu · “Falcon’s Claws”. An elite scouting and hunting order whose veterans underwent Chogorian initiation. Served as reconnaissance specialists and quiet eliminators of enemy leaders; also cared for servo-raptors used as symbolic and practical scouts.

Kharash. A volunteer shock or diversionary force formed for missions often considered near-suicidal. Entry could be a form of penance or an extraordinary honour. One of the few White Scars bodies to employ Tactical Dreadnought Armour regularly.

Uhaan Solban. The Legion’s relatively few Dreadnoughts, known as guardians of the Morning and Evening Stars. Their chief duty was to protect gene-seed repositories and sacred sites on Chogoris and Terra; many were seldom seen in battle.

Akoghlanlar. The medicae order, dedicated to preserving Legionaries and Jaghatai’s legacy. Apothecaries operated across many Brotherhoods rather than concentrating as one force, gathering together only for rare ceremonial purposes.

#### 12. War Disposition at the End of the Great Crusade

The White Scars were not one of the numerically largest Legions. Their strength was approximately 70,000 by 865.M30 and approached 95,000 at its high point shortly before 007.M31, making them among the smaller Legiones Astartes. Their warriors, however, were dispersed across immense distances, attached to roaming fleets and isolated outposts. Only the Iron Warriors had a higher proportion committed to fleets and garrisons. In consequence, even Imperial strategists often had only a partial picture of the Legion’s true strength or location.

The reunited Legion’s main domains were Chogoris, limited rights on Terra and several tributary territories within the Kolarne Cluster. Its many fleets and independent Hordes crossed the fringes of known space with relatively little bureaucratic oversight. Five Hordes fought in Horus’ Ullanor campaign, while other elements remained dispersed. During the approach to the Heresy, some of those formations were gathered for Chondax; others were far beyond communication or operated with forces whose loyalty would later be questioned.

Their unmatched freedom of action also exposed them to isolation, misinformation and manipulation when civil war broke out. Jaghatai’s judgement could never be replaced by simple orders.

## Legion Stratagems

### V Legion martial culture and doctrine

#### 5. The First Blooding and the Chogorian Identity

The first great gathering on Chogoris also established the ritual known as the Blooding or Ascension. On the plains of the Empty Quarter, more than 50,000 Legionaries took blades and marked their faces, the depth and pattern of each scar declaring personal vows or the traditions of a chosen brotherhood. The blood shed in this rite also symbolised the abandonment of earlier divisions, while many Terran warriors accepted new names and identities within the Ordu of Jaghatai. Such scars later helped distinguish Brotherhoods and shared lineages, although no strict universal marking system existed.

The Khan encouraged study of the “Noble Pursuits”: calligraphy, poetry, storytelling, hunting, philosophy and the keeping of Chogorian custom. These pursuits were not an alternative to warfare but a means of disciplining the fierce spirits of his sons. The Legion became renowned for artistic accomplishment and reflection as well as violent swiftness. Some older Terran superstitions and independent warrior lodges survived beneath this Chogorian identity, ensuring that the White Scars never became culturally uniform in every detail.

#### 6. Martial Character: Speed, Surprise and Control

The White Scars specialised in sudden shock assaults, mounted warfare, encirclement, rapid redeployment, deep raids and extended operations beyond the protection of friendly lines. Jetbikes, speeders and other fast vehicles commonly formed the core of Brotherhoods, supported by equally mobile infantry. The Khan preferred attacking an exposed weakness, breaking an enemy’s cohesion and disappearing before a counterstroke, rather than sacrificing warriors in prolonged sieges. Their most effective victories combined fierce local action with a larger design concealed from the opponent.

Outsiders often dismissed the Legion as barbaric or impulsive. Its finest warriors were formidable strategists, highly skilled artisans and scholars, and governed by a stern code of personal honour. White Scars could be ruthless in the act of war, but they were capable of mercy to a defeated foe and could condemn unnecessary cruelty. Their contempt was directed particularly toward rigid doctrine, hollow rank, and orders detached from the realities of combat. Such attitudes encouraged creativity but often strained relations with other Imperial commanders.

The Legion’s extreme mobility had costs. Static heavy weapons, specialised siege formations and large artillery contingents were relatively rare; where necessary, the White Scars either adapted their own flexible forces or borrowed auxiliaries to fill those roles. Their unpredictable movement also repeatedly confused Imperial logistics, which sometimes prepared supplies for conventional formations only to find that a White Scars Horde was much larger, smaller or elsewhere than expected.

#### 11. Dreadnoughts, Battle Culture and the Price of Service

Dreadnought interment was exceptionally uncommon among the White Scars. Warriors famed for speed and open-air combat often regarded enclosure within a metal sarcophagus as an existence between honour and punishment. The Uhaan Solban therefore served mainly as vigilant guardians of sacred repositories, emerging to fight only when the needs of the Legion or the visions of the Stormseers demanded it. On such occasions they sought a final worthy stand rather than continued preservation at any cost.

The Kharash embodied a contrasting attitude: accepting a dreadful assignment voluntarily could transform near-certain death into a matter of reputation and duty. The Legion’s discipline was built on such bonds. Outsiders saw loose formations and individual freedom; its own warriors saw an obligation to give everything once an oath had been made. This combination made them dangerous both as raiders and in desperate defensive actions.

Erased from official Legion records, remnants of the Brotherhood resurfaced over sixty years later, fighting for the Loyalists in the Pale Stars and other distant war zones. #### Legionary Nidun — Veteran of the Golden Star

Legionary Nidun exemplified the distinctive heraldry of the later White Scars Legion, which largely abandoned standard Divisio Militaris identification markings in favour of complex glyphs derived from Chogorian culture. His newly issued Mk VI power armour displayed the Legion emblem on his pauldron, while symbols on his right greave identified his Brotherhood, rank, specialisation, and commanding officer. His left greave bore a modified Tactical designation. Such markings were often difficult for warriors of other Legions to interpret.

Nidun had served with the Golden Star for 27 years before the Battle of Chondax, participating in numerous campaigns commemorated by glyphs on his forearm and pauldron. His most notable engagement was the disastrous drop assault at Anthalmax, where his Brotherhood and its Khan defended the landing grounds for six hours while the Iron Warriors established fortified positions.

During the battle, Nidun achieved 18 confirmed kills, earning commendations from both his own Khan and the Iron Warriors commander. His decorations reflected the White Scars' emphasis on personal achievement, Brotherhood identity, and distinctive Chogorian traditions.

#### Legionary Daumas (Munokhoi) — Stalker of the Grey Ghosts

Legionary Daumas served in the 731st Pioneer Company, informally known as the Grey Ghosts, a White Scars formation attached to the 98th Expeditionary Fleet alongside Rogal Dorn's Imperial Fists. Operating from the cruisers Cold Shroud and Sorrow Vector, the Grey Ghosts specialised in reconnaissance, forward scouting, and covert disruption. They identified enemy strongholds, assessed their defences, and either withdrew from overwhelming opposition or weakened vulnerable targets through sabotage and assassination before the Imperial Fists arrived to conduct the main assault.

Daumas belonged to the company's elite Stalker cadres, equipped with modified anti-materiel rifles adapted for sniping and advanced camouflage systems. These specialists eliminated priority targets and prepared enemy positions for subsequent Imperial offensives.

During the initial assault on Altus-coriola, Daumas and his cadre destroyed 34 grounded fighter aircraft, significantly reducing casualties during the Imperial Fists' subsequent drop assault.

After the rediscovery of Jaghatai Khan and the reorganisation of the V Legion, Daumas adopted the name Munokhoi, symbolising his loyalty to the Great Khan and his integration into the reborn White Scars.

#### Legionary Surentai — Ebon Keshig of the White Scars

Legionary Surentai originally served as a line infantryman in the Brotherhood of the Sable Wolf. Although an experienced and capable warrior, he had earned little distinction before the assault on Black-blight, when he volunteered to join the Ebon Keshig at the Great Khan's call.

The Ebon Keshig were a specialised formation of the White Scars equipped with rare, black-painted Terminator armour, employed for particularly dangerous assaults and desperate last stands. Unlike conventional Terminator formations, their armour was generally issued temporarily for a single battle and returned to the Legion's vaults afterward. Individual deployments were known as Kharash, and many suits possessed long histories of sacrifice and extraordinary bravery.

Surentai received the Cataphractii-pattern armour Monqara Erden, a Chogorian name roughly translated as “Eternal Dark Jewel.” During the assault on Black-blight, he killed numerous enemies but suffered grievous wounds.

Personally commended by Jaghatai Khan for his courage, Surentai survived and returned to the Brotherhood of the Sable Wolf with the rank of Kavkhan, his service in the Ebon Keshig having transformed an otherwise unremarkable Legionary into an honoured veteran.

## Legion Chronicles

### Origins and reunification of the V Legion

The White Scars

#### 1. Terra’s Forgotten Sons: The Legion Before Jaghatai

The Emperor intended the V Legion to be the Great Crusade’s far-ranging pathfinders rather than a ponderous army of conquest. Its earliest recruits came in part from the techno-nomadic tribes of Terra’s frozen Thulean Basin, survivors whose machinecraft, independence and ability to endure terrible environments suited them to scouting, raiding and war beyond reliable Imperial support. Initial strength was only a few hundred Legionaries, often operating in detachments of fewer than a dozen. They infiltrated ahead of larger armies, drew enemies into the open, cut lines of communication and identified weaknesses that other Legions would exploit.

Their earliest campaigns were often fought in the most hazardous theatres of Old Night’s aftermath. The V suffered bitter attrition while other forces claimed the more visible honours of conquest. The dangerous work made them skilled at independent decisions and unconventional tactics, but also gave them a reputation as ill-disciplined outsiders. Some of their old epithets—including Star Hunters, Vanguard, Blood Debt and Grey Ghosts—reflected the fragmented identities developing among far-separated units.

The Legion’s guiding principle was already visible: survival and victory through initiative, adaptability and the ability to fight without reinforcement. Such freedom made the V invaluable on the frontier but difficult to incorporate into the hierarchies and schedules of the wider Imperium. Their commanders sometimes ignored orders from officers who had not earned their personal respect, even while accepting the Emperor’s distant authority.

#### 2. The Pioneer Companies: Strength Without Unity

As the Great Crusade expanded, the V became a collection of autonomous Pioneer Companies rather than a unified host. Each company recruited, trained and resupplied largely on its own, often incorporating soldiers and customs from the worlds it crossed. Units could number from roughly 500 to 3,000 warriors; by 800.M30 records identified perhaps 800 such companies and an estimated total strength of about 80,000. Even so, they rarely assembled more than a few thousand in one place, except in unusual engagements such as the Battle of Thapsis in late 744.M30.

This arrangement preserved extraordinary operational flexibility while creating serious problems of discipline, logistics and identity. Their opportunistic recruitment sometimes infringed worlds reserved for other Legions. Ferrus Manus and Leman Russ were among those who complained; Horus is credited with preventing a formal censure. Individual companies developed their own heraldry, rituals and command practices, borrowing as readily from nearby Legions as from their Terran ancestry. Many such subcultures endured even after the Legion’s later reformation.

Contemporary examples show the variety: the 224th Pioneer Company under Lieutenant Apion Hansa, the 666th “Void Devils” of Captain Theon Juoksa, the 3rd “Lions of Thapsis” of Captain Kornelius Dure and the 99th “Enders” of Captain Ikem Aghur. The V Legion’s problem was never lack of courage or skill, but the absence of a common identity and command structure capable of directing those talents on a larger scale.

#### 3. Jaghatai Khan: Conquest and Freedom on Chogoris

The legends of Jaghatai’s youth are strongly embellished, but their consistent core places him among the horse-riding tribes of Chogoris’s Empty Quarter. Adopted into a nomadic clan, he became a formidable rider, hunter and leader. A rival tribe, identified in some accounts as the Kurayed, murdered his adoptive father and his companions. Jaghatai avenged them and then broke the recurring cycle of tribal vendetta: defeated enemies could join his growing people rather than perpetuate endless retaliatory war.

His talent for uniting rival tribes developed into a campaign that overthrew the settled Palatine civilisation dominating much of Chogoris. Through speed, surprise, alliances and relentless campaigning, he became the Khan of Khans and brought the planet under his rule. When the Emperor eventually found him, Jaghatai had already won his world. He acknowledged the Emperor as father and accepted a place in the wider Imperial endeavour, but was never naturally inclined to treat sovereign obedience as the highest virtue. His bond was personal and founded upon respect rather than surrender of judgement.

Chogoris—also called Mundus Planus—became the Legion’s primary home world. The culture of its nomads, their horsemanship, oaths, poetry, feuds and ritual practices would become the basis of a new identity for a Legion previously scattered across the stars. Yet Jaghatai’s loyalty to the wider Imperium remained inseparable from his conviction that he and his warriors must retain the freedom to choose their own course.

#### 4. Calling the V Home: Reunification and the Kolarne Circle

After his discovery, Jaghatai summoned the scattered Pioneer Companies by astropath and courier, but distances and long silences meant that almost a decade passed before most had assembled. By approximately 865.M30 the emerging host was large, yet its constituent companies mistrusted one another, carried incompatible traditions and scarcely recognised a shared Legion. The Khan mixed Terran veterans and recruits from Chogoris into new formations, replacing the old system without eliminating its useful independence.

He first secured a common culture through Chogorian traditions, then tested that identity in war. His campaigns around the Kolarne Circle forced the former companies to cooperate as one army, even as he deliberately gave different detachments opportunities to prove themselves by their own methods. The fighting lasted about five years and involved approximately 80,000 warriors; the text estimates that one in ten died. Against the Ork forces of Sengar Mar and Vorghaist, the Khan integrated speed, dispersal, independent action and coordinated decisive attacks into a coherent Legion-wide doctrine.

The Kolarne campaign was therefore more than a conquest. It made former strangers into comrades who had bled for the same cause, produced leaders trusted across company boundaries and gave the V a shared military identity. At its conclusion the Legion was no longer a miscellany of wandering Pioneer Companies. The White Scars had emerged.

#### The Betrayal of Chondax

The Betrayal
of Chondax

##### 1. The Campaign Nobody Was Supposed to Remember

Unlike Isstvan or Calth, Chondax rarely appears among the defining disasters of the Horus Heresy. No single atrocity made it infamous; its significance lay in how thoroughly it removed the White Scars from the developing civil war. Here Horus' rebellion was prepared through isolation, misleading orders, manipulated enemies and the deliberate wasting of years. The historical account portrays Chondax as the first major success of a quieter strategy: denying loyal forces the information and freedom of movement needed to intervene elsewhere. The precise responsibility for every act remains disputed, particularly where the Alpha Legion is concerned.

The scheme began in the aftermath of Ullanor. Horus, newly confirmed as Warmaster, redistributed expeditionary armies, Titan Legions and brother Legions under the guise of completing the Great Crusade. Trusted formations were moved into the most intense war zones, while Primarchs whose loyalty was less secure were sent far from the centres of Imperial power. The Blood Angels were directed toward Signus, the Ultramarines toward Calth, and others away from the wars that would determine Terra's fate. The White Scars, although not natural enemies of Horus, had to be removed just as carefully.

Jaghatai Khan was independent, difficult to intimidate and unimpressed by the ceremony and hierarchy that surrounded Imperial command. He had not coveted Horus' title, but neither would he obey an unjust or suspicious demand simply because it came from his brother. His affinity with Magnus the Red also made him a potential danger to plans involving Prospero. Horus therefore chose not to force his allegiance: he offered the Khan a campaign that appealed to his martial pride and allowed the Legion to believe it was serving the Great Crusade on its own terms.

##### 2. A False Trail into Chondax

The assigned objective was ostensibly straightforward: eliminate remnants of the Ork empire of Urlakk Urg, shattered at Ullanor, which had fled into the remote Chondax system. Chondax lay only a few Warp passages from Ullanor yet had little strategic or economic value to the Imperium. It was a tangled arrangement of stars, planets, moons, isolated colonies and asteroid fields, ideal for a mobile war of pursuit but far from the main routes of Imperial communication. The Khan accepted the assignment, viewing it as an opportunity to complete the victory at Ullanor and demonstrate his Legion's skill.

The White Scars were not alone in receiving orders to the region. An Alpha Legion contingent had already been directed toward Chondax, purportedly to prepare the system and prevent the Orks' escape. The exact timing of Alpharius' orders is unclear, although the account places his instructions before the conclusion of the Ullanor triumph. Rather than destroy the remaining xenos swiftly, the XX Legion built observation posts, supply installations and covert positions from which it could shape the campaign. The Alpha Legion also seems to have been charged with preventing outside communications and prolonging the White Scars' stay until larger events had overtaken them.

The provenance of the surviving records remains uncertain. Much of its detailed reconstruction derives from a document called the Unbalanced Scales, recovered from Terran archives after the Traitor retreat from Terra and linked to those who had served Malcador. It purports to preserve Alpha Legion deployments, personnel, orders and images, but its provenance cannot be fully established. Historical accounts warn that an Alpha Legion record may be an elaborate falsehood even when parts can be corroborated. Its statements about motive, fleet strength and clandestine command must therefore be treated as reconstruction rather than indisputable fact.

##### 3. The White Scars Host

The force committed to Chondax was formidable. The roster reproduced in the account gives approximately 67,600 White Scars Legionaries, including limited armour and support units: more than two-thirds of the Legion. Eight major Hordes were involved, those of Krenak (about 7,900), Asudai (7,300), Hasik (9,800), Jemulan (8,300), Nogai (9,200), Sangjar (8,600), Gansukh (7,000) and Orbaatar (9,500). The White Scars identified these formations by their current Noyan-khan rather than by a fixed numerical sequence, which made them difficult for outside observers to catalogue. Five had fought at Ullanor; others arrived from operations near the Pale Stars and the Yarath Pacification.

The naval contingent included 472 capital ships of the V Legion, principally vessels chosen for speed rather than extreme mass, and roughly 600 lighter strike craft and destroyers. It was supported by 21 Imperial Navy capital ships and a Silent Sisterhood light cruiser. Attached Imperial forces included approximately 4,000 void hoplites of the 13th Solar Auxilia, 8,000 troops of the 42nd Seraphine Guard, 2,000 augmented siege personnel of the 8th Fane of the Charonid Sentinels, and a small detachment of 63 Sisters of Silence. Jaghatai Khan commanded the expedition, while Knight-Centura Calistis Merovin represented the Silent Sisterhood and Argus Gygan led a Saturnyne auxiliary cohort.

The composition already hinted at contradictory aims. The White Scars excelled at mobile warfare, sudden raids, reconnaissance and decisive assaults, and their Primarch favoured independent groups acting with broad discretion. A mechanised Saturnyne contingent and siege engineers, by contrast, fitted a slower style of war. The Khan also showed interest in the Sisters of Silence, in part because of their unusual jetbikes, even though their presence alongside a Legion opposed to rigid restrictions on psykers sat uneasily with the politics of Nikaea.

##### 4. The Hydra Waiting in the Dark

The Alpha Legion's preparations were more extensive than any ordinary Ork-cleansing operation required. The Unbalanced Scales distinguishes an early local force of no more than about one hundred smaller warships from a later reserve fleet supposedly numbering at least 600 capital ships and more than 1,000 lighter craft. It also suggests fewer than 30,000 Alpha Legion ground troops were present, many dispersed in small detachments across the system. Those figures conflict with other accounts and may include disguised civilian craft, wrongly attributed vessels or deliberate deception; the chronicle cautions against accepting them literally. Even the identity of the Alpha commander remains uncertain. A codename, Desidero, appears, but records suggesting Alpharius himself commanded every operation are treated sceptically.

The XX Legion's most consequential preparations were hidden from the White Scars. It established a network of fortified stations and outposts, including the Tenebrae installations positioned along the system's heliopause and between its stars. These bases maintained communications, supplied covert detachments, monitored traffic and could interfere with astropathic transmissions. Some may also have manipulated the Warp by methods not disclosed in Imperial records. The result was an isolation system that made it extremely difficult for the Khan to receive reliable news or for his forces to leave in coherent strength.

Several accounts even implicate Word Bearers or other servants of the Ruinous Powers in making the surrounding Warp storms so persistent. The wider motives are equally uncertain. Horus clearly benefited from the Khan's absence, yet the document speculates that the Alpha Legion might have pursued an additional agenda: serving a secret patron, seeking an advantage over Horus, or exploiting the Heresy for purposes unknown. The record offers no conclusive resolution.

##### 5. The War Against the Orks

Chondax initially seemed to justify Horus' orders. The system contained a sprawl of Ork populations, from ruined refugee enclaves to entrenched clan strongholds, and enough bodies and raw materials to sustain protracted fighting. The White Scars began with Chondax Prime, where Orks occupied the fortress called Black-light. While other Legions might have preferred a long siege, Jaghatai used the style of war for which his sons were renowned: fast armoured advances, sudden deployments and direct attacks against the strongest enemy positions. The Ebon Keshig, his black-armoured Terminator companions drawn from warriors seeking honour through dangerous service, helped breach the fortress in a fierce close-quarter assault. Black-light fell after little more than six hours of fighting, and the surviving Orks scattered into the wastelands.

The apparent victory did not end the campaign. Orks on Chondax Prime refused to remain concentrated long enough for another decisive battle, while other worlds sheltered independent clans. The Khan consequently divided his Hordes into autonomous commands, directing some to herd the Orks into the northern ranges, others to eliminate concentrations on the plains and additional formations to clear worlds outward from the system's centre. This dispersal played to the White Scars' tactical strengths, but it also made them difficult to coordinate and easy to isolate once communications began to fail.

The struggle expanded across the planets and moons of the wider Chondax system. The White Scars won brilliant local actions: Krenak's riders fought prolonged chases over the ice plains of Teras; Asudai Noyan-khan pursued a Warp-seer among Orks on Alkonost; and heavy armour supported the Saturnyne Rams in battles against towering scrap-built monsters. The Khan's reputation for fearless attacks spread among his sons. Yet the remaining Orks repeatedly escaped extinction, and unexpected developments kept returning victories into new campaigns.

##### 6. Seven Years of Deliberately Prolonged War

What should have been a manageable cleansing campaign became a seven-year war of diminishing returns. Ork clans that ought to have been broken regrouped with inexplicable speed, appeared where they could disrupt carefully planned encirclements, or escaped just as the White Scars closed in. Sometimes unrelated xenos groups displayed an unusual awareness of the Legion's deployments. Other times earthquakes, subsidence and unpredictable Warp-related phenomena disrupted otherwise decisive operations. White Scars formations had been scattered across a dozen worlds, and every attempt to unite them or withdraw the last garrisons was delayed by fresh reports of enemy activity.

The Alpha Legion's hidden hand is clearest in the recorded campaign on Shaln. Nogai Noyan-khan led a highly successful eradication effort, exploiting reconnaissance specialists and veteran hunters to separate and destroy Ork groups. His swift victories threatened to bring that part of the campaign to a close. During the battle of the great western reef, however, Nogai was assassinated. The death was officially attributed to the Orks, but the later record assigns it to an Alpha Legion headhunter cadre led by a warrior bearing the title Interfector. The assassination both removed a capable commander and forced the White Scars to renew operations against an enemy they had nearly cornered.

Such measures were not isolated. Infiltrators murdered xenos warlords whom the White Scars were about to destroy, ensuring that the Orks would scatter rather than be decisively defeated. Alpha Legion agents diverted raiding parties away from White Scars traps, launched covert attacks against patrols and suppressed attempts to investigate suspicious events. On the fringes of the system, Ork seers became afflicted by Warp-born disturbances that the White Scars could not explain. The campaign was never allowed to reach a natural conclusion: every success opened another demand for pursuit, supplies and time.

The isolation became as damaging as the fighting. No reliable messages arrived from the wider Imperium, and small vessels dispatched through the storms to carry reports or seek information often failed to return. Legion officers became increasingly uneasy, but the Khan continued to honour his assignment. The Unbalanced Scales later alleges that the Alpha Legion was under orders not merely to delay the White Scars, but also to avoid damaging them so severely that they would become uncontrollable. That limitation, apparently imposed by Horus, was not always obeyed.

##### 7. An Unquiet Legion

By the end of the seventh year, the Khan's commanders could no longer ignore the campaign's contradictions. The Orks had been reduced to scattered remnants, but losses and unexplained delays persisted; ammunition and other essential stores were steadily exhausted. With no orders from Horus, no clarification from Terra and almost no contact beyond Chondax, Jaghatai began to suspect that events of much greater significance were being concealed from him. He had fulfilled the Warmaster's command and had earned the right to demand answers.

The White Scars' internal discipline gradually deteriorated. The Legion's independence, normally a strength, permitted Brotherhoods to operate for long periods without oversight, and they could not easily compare the anomalies each had experienced. Veteran officers were angered by being made to chase insignificant remnants while decisive campaigns elsewhere were apparently proceeding without them. Others began to question whether the seclusion was a result of natural Warp storms or human design. The delayed realisation made the eventual arrival of contradictory messages particularly destructive.

The scale of the attrition was substantial even before the Alpha Legion openly attacked. By the beginning of 007.M31, one summary estimated that nearly 6,000 White Scars had been killed and another 15 percent of the deployed force needed medical treatment serious enough to limit combat effectiveness. Taken together, the losses rendered nearly 16,500 Legionaries combat-ineffective. Their many separate outposts also lacked supplies and could not be withdrawn swiftly. In a campaign supposedly against a defeated enemy, such losses were difficult to justify.

##### 8. The Trap Exposed on Phemus IV

The Brotherhood of the Talon was assigned to search Phemus IV, a cold, mountainous world at the system's edge and the site of one of the Alpha Legion's covert strongholds. On discovering an extensively prepared base, its commander faced an ambush by warriors who were not Orks at all, but Alpha Legion Space Marines. The fight became one of the earliest recorded direct clashes between the two Legions in this campaign. The Talon's veterans fought their way into the enemy's lines with a ferocity that surprised the attackers, but their numbers were too small to resist an organised garrison indefinitely. Their reports were restricted, and the fuller meaning of their discovery was not yet understood across the White Scars fleet.

Elsewhere the most important Alpha Legion infrastructure began to fail. The destruction of Tenebrae 9-50, a station linked to the network that had obstructed messages entering Chondax, appears to have caused the abrupt end of the astropathic blackout. The station may have been destroyed by a dissident Alpha Legion faction, rather than by the White Scars themselves, although this remains unverified. Suddenly the fleet's astropaths were flooded with years of accumulated messages, warnings and contradictory reports. The return of communication was thus almost as destabilising as its absence.

##### 9. Messages from a Broken Imperium

News arrived of events that no loyal Primarch could have expected: the massacre at Isstvan, Word Bearers treachery, the disaster at Prospero and accusations that Horus had turned against the Emperor. Messages from loyal commanders, some incomplete and others long delayed, pointed toward a civil war that had already engulfed the Imperium. The accounts were inconsistent. White Scars officers argued over whether Magnus or Leman Russ had been deceived, whether the reports about Horus could be trusted, and whether the Legion should obey a summons to Terra. Their longstanding relationship with the Warmaster and the Khan's friendship with Magnus made these questions especially painful.

Jaghatai's instinct was to verify the truth before taking sides. The Unbalanced Scales depicts him wrestling with a choice between loyalty to Horus, obedience to the Emperor's representatives and the responsibility owed to his Legion. Rogal Dorn called him to Terra; other accounts concerned Russ and the Space Wolves in need of aid. The Khan knew his brothers well enough to recognise that even a purportedly authoritative order might be false or manipulated. His uncertainty created exactly the opening the Alpha Legion needed to force a decision by arms.

##### 10. The Veil Lifts and the Hydra Appears

The first Alpha Legion attacks struck isolated White Scars scouts and patrol craft while the Khan attempted to reassemble his scattered host. Communications were again disrupted locally, and individual units were captured, killed or impersonated. Then the concealed XX Legion fleet emerged in strength. White Scars witnesses described well over a thousand vessels bearing the Alpha Legion's insignia, compared with roughly seven hundred White Scars ships that could answer the recall. These totals may include disguised transports and armed merchantmen, but the White Scars unquestionably faced an enormous, coordinated threat.

Rather than launch an immediate extermination attack, the Alpha Legion established a blocking cordon, mirrored the White Scars' formations and refused to explain its presence. Jaghatai sent demands and signals; none brought a trustworthy answer. His commanders responded by preparing for battle, but the Khan continued to hesitate, aware that an attack on another Legion could make reconciliation impossible. The Alpha Legion exploited his restraint to occupy critical exits and move against the Hordes still scattered through the system.

One of the crucial openings lay at a Mandeville point between the stars of the system, where precise navigation was needed to make a safe Warp transition. The hidden Tenebrae stations had made that route unusually treacherous and forced vessels to depend upon controlled alignments and beacons. A fleet that could reach this narrow exit could leave Chondax; one that failed would be trapped among the Alpha Legion's much larger reserve.

##### 11. The Chisel: Jaghatai's Breakout

Once his decision was made, Jaghatai employed a plan known as the Chisel. Rather than allowing his fleet to be enclosed, the White Scars created an armoured wedge led by the flagship Swordstorm, using the speed of their lighter vessels and intense concentrated fire to break through a narrow sector of the blockade. Simultaneously, detachments made sudden assaults upon the Alpha Legion's ground positions, obliging the XX Legion to defend multiple locations rather than concentrate entirely upon the Khan's line of escape. The manoeuvre relied on precisely the aggressive, decentralised warfare that had made the White Scars formidable during the Great Crusade.

The naval fighting was furious. Alpha Legion squadrons feigned retreats, opened carefully prepared crossfires and tried to split the White Scars' forward echelons from their supports. The Swordstorm drove toward the weakest part of the cordon while its escorts screened its flanks. At several points White Scars vessels accepted fatal damage to prevent the enemy from closing the escape route. Yet the Alpha Legion did not press every advantage available to it; even when its larger fleet could have massed to destroy the Khan's ships, some squadrons held back or moved in ways that seemed intended to prolong the struggle.

The open Chondax Engagement reportedly lasted four days from the Alpha Legion fleet's appearance to the Khan's withdrawal, in striking contrast to the preceding seven years of concealed manipulation. These four days were only the visible end of a campaign whose decisive operations had occurred much earlier.

##### 12. Phemus IV: The Blade Against the Hydra

On Phemus IV, the White Scars launched a daring assault against an Alpha Legion stronghold, using agile Scimitar jetbikes and fast-moving infantry to approach through broken terrain. The black-armoured Keshig and other close-assault specialists smashed into the garrison while supporting units sought to prevent counterattacks. The Alpha Legion attempted to turn the White Scars' speed against them, drawing them into prepared corridors, hidden firing positions and successive ambushes. Fighting spread through the fortress and its tunnels, and the White Scars suffered heavily as they forced their way toward the enemy command centre.

A decisive feature of the engagement was the presence of the Silent Sisterhood. The Alpha Legion deployed warriors using forbidden psychic powers, including a sorcerer identified as Malek Striga, whose Warp-born attacks devastated the assaulting White Scars. The Sisters of Silence, led by Knight-Centura Calistis Merovin, advanced despite terrible losses and suppressed the powers of the psykers they faced. Their intervention made a breach possible for the White Scars. Tsolmon Khan confronted Striga in the fighting and struck him down with a thunder hammer, but the Sisters' detachment was devastated; Merovin's fate is bound up with the final defence of the position.

The fighting on Phemus IV was not a simple victory. Alpha Legion counterattacks repeatedly separated the attackers, and their commander eventually withdrew into an inner sanctum where the Khan's warriors could not easily pursue. This officer is identified as Praetor Sirdor Vhen, although many reports mistakenly described him as Alpharius. Jaghatai himself encountered Vhen near the end of the battle. The Alpha Legion commander taunted him with references to Magnus and his brothers' divided loyalties; the Khan killed him before the confrontation could develop into a prolonged duel. His death did not end the enemy's operations, but it deprived the defenders of their most visible leader.

##### 13. The Cost of Speed and the Rescue from Byfrust

Beyond Phemus, isolated White Scars detachments tried to answer the recall while under attack from Alpha Legion ships and ground forces. Some fought through prepared ambushes; others were lured into abandoned installations, deceived by false-colour armour or trapped behind the changing war lines. On Herkon, for example, White Scars units were turned against seemingly friendly auxiliaries during attacks that relied upon disguises and manipulated identification protocols. In several sectors, the White Scars could hold their ground only by sacrificing the possibility of a timely withdrawal.

The end of the fighting at Byfrust left Tsolmon Khan and a few hundred warriors surrounded by an Alpha Legion force they could not defeat in a sustained campaign. A small fleet picket, previously thought destroyed, made an unexpected return. The cruiser Hawkstar emerged from concealment and attacked the Alpha Legion vessel Phi-Hekator in orbit, allowing a landing craft to reach the surface and extract survivors. The strike did not save all the stranded forces, but it broke the immediate blockade. Before departure, Tsolmon and his warriors erected a cairn of stones and broken weapons to honour those who had died side by side, from ordinary Legionaries to members of the Silent Sisterhood.

Another shadow accompanied the fighting. Accounts of the Hound and the Huntsman record six officers slain by a mysterious sniper during the final battle, including two Alpha Legionaries and four White Scars. The victims among the White Scars were associated with opposition to the spread of warrior lodges. At least one report attributes the killings to the Interfector, but the purpose of the pattern and the sniper's precise allegiance remain unresolved. The episode illustrates how the Alpha Legion's clandestine conflicts continued inside the conventional battle.

##### 14. The Fleet Escapes; the Legion Does Not

The White Scars' breakout depended on the Swordstorm's pressure at the centre of the Alpha Legion blockade and on the commitment of smaller ships willing to absorb losses in its wake. The Khan refused to turn the escape into an attempt to destroy the enemy fleet: the objective was to leave Chondax before the main XX Legion force could close every route. A handful of vessels deliberately remained to keep the Alpha Legion occupied, while the surviving cruisers drove for the narrow Warp exit. The maneuver exploited an enemy that had expected the Khan either to fight blindly for personal honour or to remain paralysed by conflicting orders.

Estimates suggest that about 6,000 White Scars had been killed during the larger Chondax campaign and that close to 16,500 Legionaries were combat ineffective when the Alpha Legion finally forced them to withdraw. Approximately thirty White Scars warships were lost during the final transition and associated fighting; others were left behind damaged and might later have been captured, repaired or used in Alpha Legion deception operations. Only a fraction of the 60,000 or so combat-capable White Scars still in the wider expeditionary force assembled for the sudden four-day engagement, with fewer than 10,000 Legionaries estimated to have participated directly in the decisive action. These figures must be read with caution, because the archival accounts are incomplete and the definitions of wounded, missing and detached troops vary.

For the scattered White Scars, the breakout brought no immediate rescue. Some detachments were killed by Alpha Legion forces within weeks; others remained hidden on abandoned worlds for years, scavenging wrecks, ambushing patrols and avoiding detection. These isolated bands preserved the Legion's resistance in Chondax long after the Khan's departure, but their experience was almost erased from the official histories.

##### 15. Alaxxes, Prospero and the Choice of the Khan

The Khan's escape did not resolve his dilemma. Even as the Battle of Chondax unfolded, Leman Russ and the Space Wolves were fighting a separate Alpha Legion force in the Alaxxes Nebula. Their appeals for assistance finally reached the White Scars, but Jaghatai declined to divert his already battered fleet to rescue Russ. His allegiance remained uncertain at this point. The Khan had heard conflicting reports about Prospero and Magnus, was unsure which of his brothers had betrayed the Emperor, and had to decide whether further intervention without verified knowledge would only serve his enemies.

Horus had counted upon precisely such uncertainty. He hoped to chain the White Scars to his cause through personal ties and a carefully managed version of events, just as he had tried to manipulate other Legions. Yet the Khan had never been an obedient instrument. Remembering the old traditions of Chogoris, he refused to become a servant of a cause he did not understand. Rather than submit to Horus' summons or adopt Dorn's order without question, he turned toward Prospero to discover what had happened to Magnus and the Thousand Sons for himself.

The decision left the White Scars distrusted by multiple factions, but it preserved Jaghatai's agency. When the Legion finally departed Chondax, its warriors formed up not to fight on the basis of rumour, but to seek the truth. Their ultimate allegiance would be determined by what they found, not by the lie that had held them imprisoned.

##### 16. Aftermath: A Victory That Was Not a Victory

The final direct Chondax Engagement cost far fewer lives than Isstvan or Calth, yet it accomplished an equally important strategic function: it kept a large and highly mobile Loyalist Legion out of the civil war's opening campaigns. The White Scars had spent seven years defeating Orks whose remnants should never have required such a prolonged effort, then lost many of their remaining warriors and ships to a former brother Legion. The Alpha Legion's refusal to deliver a quick annihilating blow remains one of the most difficult aspects of the campaign to explain.

The fate of the last isolated survivors gives the episode its bitter coda. The Lost and the Forsaken account describes White Scars who remained on Chondax for years after the withdrawal. Most were eventually killed, but a small band of the Brotherhood of the Black Axe survived until 017.M31, when a Raven Guard patrol found them on Irra Minor. The eight exhausted warriors, led by Sengur One-arm, initially treated the rescuers as enemies, having endured Alpha Legion headhunters, Dark Mechanicum hunter-killer servitors and manifestations of the Warp. Offered passage and recognition at Terra, they instead chose to return quietly to Chogoris.

The chronicler also questions whether the Alpha Legion's Chondax force was truly unified. Some actions were coherent attempts to delay the Khan; others contradicted Horus' apparent instructions by provoking open battle or killing warriors he had demanded remain intact. Possible answers range from rival commanders and competing agendas to a deliberate attempt by Alpharius to shape the Heresy's duration rather than simply secure Horus' victory. None is proved by the surviving evidence. The Alpha Legion also operated at Alaxxes, apparently fielding forces of a scale larger than contemporary Imperial estimates could explain.

Chondax therefore cannot be reduced to a straightforward tale of Traitors defeated by the White Scars. The Khan escaped, but his Legion was mauled, divided and too late to influence many decisive events. Horus benefited from that delay, yet the Alpha Legion's contradictory operations invite questions about whom its commanders actually served. the Heresy's gravest danger was not only Daemonic corruption or the ambition of a single Warmaster, but the human capacity for calculated betrayal, manipulation and cruelty.

### After Chondax and the scattered Brotherhoods

##### The Khan's Unanswered Questions

Jaghatai Khan emerged from Chondax with a similarly bitter victory. His Legion had survived the Alpha Legion's trap, but the conflict had exposed the unreliability of former allies and the dangerous uncertainty surrounding the rebellion.

The Khan's search for answers led him toward Prospero, where the fate of Magnus and the Thousand Sons would further complicate his understanding of loyalty and betrayal. Rather than immediately surrendering his judgement to any faction, Jaghatai sought to discover the truth independently, determined that his Legion would choose its future on its own terms.

#### Of Brotherhoods Near and Far

Although most White Scars had accompanied Jaghatai Khan to Chondax, numerous independent Hordes and Brotherhoods remained active throughout the galaxy. Their isolation produced radically different experiences of the Heresy, with some formations remaining loyal, others joining the Traitors, and many fighting for years without understanding the wider conflict.

Dutar Sokhat Noyan-khan's Horde, approximately 2,000 warriors, operated near the northern Imperial frontier and close to the Isstvan system. Despite their proximity to the rebellion's earliest atrocities, they initially remained unaware of Horus' treachery. Upon returning to Imperial space, they were ambushed by Traitor forces. Sokhat and his surviving warriors escaped and subsequently conducted a prolonged campaign against Traitor supply convoys and isolated outposts.

Jirghadai Noyan-khan's Horde, numbering approximately 6,000 warriors, was attacked during the Isstvan massacres by a combined force dominated by World Eaters and Death Guard. The attack devastated the Horde, but the Traitors unexpectedly withdrew before completing its destruction. Jirghadai's survivors eventually reached the vicinity of Nocturne, where they received assistance from elements of the Salamanders. Later evidence suggested that their attackers had acted on Horus' orders, contradicting his apparent intention to keep the White Scars neutral.

The Brotherhood of the Great Eye, serving alongside the Sons of Horus in the 905th Expeditionary Fleet, took a different path. Its leader, Nayaga Khan, pledged allegiance to Horus when the rebellion was declared, possibly without understanding the events at Chondax. His warriors subsequently fought for the Traitors until encountering Dutar Sokhat's loyalists. No members of the Brotherhood of the Great Eye are known to have survived.

The Brotherhood of the Broken Shield, commanded by Bujir Khan, had been isolated on deep reconnaissance missions since approximately 002.M31. Returning near Nostramo, they discovered the region engulfed in conflict between the Dark Angels and Night Lords. Misled by rumours that Jaghatai had joined Horus, Bujir initially sought contact with the Traitors. Nevertheless, his Brotherhood eventually played an important role in the Loyalist campaign during the Thramas Crusade.

##### The Khan's Hidden Wars

For much of the Heresy, Jaghatai Khan deliberately kept the White Scars outside the great concentrations of Imperial and Traitor power, pursuing independent campaigns while seeking the truth behind the rebellion. This isolation did not prevent his warriors from influencing the wider war.

White Scars formations struck vulnerable infrastructure across the Imperium, including the promethium yards of Orythane, the Forge of Martius-Maximus, and the outer districts of Lamrys. These operations denied resources to opposing forces but also forced the Legion to sustain itself without the normal administrative and logistical support of the Great Crusade.

The Khan's uncertain allegiance made him an object of intense interest to both sides. Once overlooked by many of his brothers, he became a potential ally whose support could alter the balance of the civil war. Yet he remained difficult to influence, refusing to surrender his independence to either intimidation or political manipulation.

### The Brotherhood of the Shattered Shield at Thramas

#### The Brotherhood of the Shattered Shield

The final major addition to Curze's forces came from an unexpected quarter: the Brotherhood of the Shattered Shield, a White Scars detachment under Zhenjin Khan. Having been campaigning in distant regions when the Heresy began, the Brotherhood arrived at Nostramo seeking supplies and repairs, only to discover the ruined world and receive no response from nearby Imperial stations. The Night Lords received them as guests and claimed that Horus had ordered the pacification of the Eastern Fringe against rebellious forces. Knowing the First Legion largely through tales of its pride and secrecy, and remembering Horus as a patron and friend of the V Legion, Zhenjin Khan accepted their account. In gratitude for the supplies provided, he joined Curze with approximately 3,000 Great Crusade veterans and six capital warships. The VIII Legion thereby gained sufficient strength to challenge the Dark Angels in open war.

Meanwhile the Lion's attempts to contact the former Great Crusade bases at Tigrus and Honourum had failed, and the growing Ruinstorm obstructed communications with his loyal brothers. The First Legion could neither reach Thramas nor withdraw without abandoning the liberated worlds. Curze launched preliminary raids using expendable slave soldiers and abhuman auxiliaries to probe the Dark Angels' defences and deplete their resources. On Yrrdek and Chenro IX, conscripts were sacrificed to reveal defensive positions; on Sedricce, the Thunguard volunteers survived their failed attack only through the intervention of the White Scars, who also helped evacuate surviving Loyalists. The Night Lords mockingly named Zhenjin Khan the Saviour, an insult understood by the White Scars, who were increasingly disturbed by their allies' contempt for life and the honour shown by their supposed enemies.

In the aftermath, Zhenjin Khan knelt before the Lion and offered both the recovered blade and his life in atonement for his mistaken allegiance. El'Jonson refused to treat the survivors solely according to their former loyalties, declaring that history would judge the dead and that the living must bear responsibility for their choices through the fighting still ahead. The First Legion had held Crucible, the Night Lords had been driven back, and the Brotherhood of the Shattered Shield had joined the Loyalist cause. The struggle for Thramas, however, was not yet concluded.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The White Scars were trapped in a prolonged war against the Orks at Chondax through Alpha Legion manipulation. With Jaghatai Khan's allegiance initially uncertain, distant formations had to determine their own loyalties. Some disgraced warriors were later dispatched as Sagyar Mazan, conducting near-suicidal infiltration and assassination missions.

White Scars veteran Legionary Khasar belonged to the disgraced Foresworn Brotherhood after heavy losses fighting the Barghesi in 955.M30. During the Pale Stars campaign White Scars formations resisted Alpha Legion operations under Ijax (details in the campaign record).

### War Without End [087.M31]

Decades after the great battles of the Heresy, the Brotherhood of the Blue Hawk, numbering approximately 132 White Scars, remained active near the southern frontier of Imperial space. Its warriors had been charting remote regions for future Compliance operations and were returning toward Terra when they encountered renegade Alpha Legion forces near the watch-station of Theograth.

The Brotherhood successfully repelled the attack but suffered substantial casualties, making the engagement one of the last recorded battles between warriors of the original Legiones Astartes, long after the Second Founding had divided most surviving Legions into Chapters.

Following their victory, Yeke Negurin Khan ordered the survivors to continue their journey toward Terra. Yet no record confirms their arrival. Their final declaration promised that they would return to the Great Crusade beyond the mapped boundaries of the Imperium, continuing the Emperor's work in the unexplored darkness.

Whether they perished or remained at war beyond Imperial territory is unknown. Their disappearance serves as a final reminder that the Horus Heresy never ended simultaneously for all who fought it, and that some warriors continued the struggles of the Great Crusade long after the Imperium itself had been irrevocably transformed.

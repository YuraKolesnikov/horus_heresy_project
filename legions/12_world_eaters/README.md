# World Eaters
Monsters long before Horus became corrupted and monsters they remained, only now with what little remained of their restraint and their humanity stripped away. Of al the Space Marine Legions of the Great Crusade, none were so savage and dreaded. For while others such as the Night Lords could justly claim to have brought worlds into compliance by fear alone, and others such as the White Scars and the Space Wolves could descend without warning and leave a world burning in their wake, for the World Eaters to be assigned to a campaign meant only one thing for the enemy extermination. Extermination not by virus bomb or atomic firestorm, but by chainaxe and bolt shell-worlds drowned one-by-one in the blood of their inhabitants.

## Legion Structure and Customs
As with almost all the Space Marine Legions, the XII at its creation followed the so-called Terran Pattern' of organisation as, but even in this earliest period the Legion's procurement and outfitting showed a considerable bias towards direct assault and operations within the close and deadly confines of the kinds of battlefields designated as 'Zone Mortalis’ in strategic doctrine. Tactics consisted of a hybrid of tactical/close assault troops for the main part, supported by dedicated heavy assault units such as Terminators and specialised units such as Land Speeder squadrons.

Past a certain point in training, gladiatorial contests and battle-exercises became real life-and-death combat with live rounds and wetted blades, with the goal to raise the skill and strength of the warrior to the greatest extent before they could be deemed worthy of joining the World Eaters' numbers. In order to cope with the rigours of their training and ceaseless campaigning, under Angron's direction recruitment processes were streamlined and accelerated, and recruits were drawn from a number of Feral and Feudal worlds scattered across the Segmenta in order to meet the Legion’s demands. The Apothecaries of the Legion embraced the use of the Primarch's own genetic material to stabilise and speed up the progression and implantation procedures — the so-called Grabiya's Theorem. However as time progressed, viable technology was replicated and steadily improved (although it was never fully stable or constant between subjects), and entire newly-formed companies of recruits were implanted, as well as large numbers of existing World Eaters who volunteered for the dangerous operation. The majority of these were absorbed back into the Legion’s line units, while those deemed perhaps too unstable for such tasks joined a growing number of near-berserker assault units known a s Rampager squads, and within these those too far gone to be anything but restrained between battles simply became known as the Caedere or the 'Butchers’ — a frightening portent of what was to come for the Legion.

The World Eaters were known to maintain a strong Destroyer cadre in their ranks, the Legion favouring their tactical use against monstrous xeno-forms and during extermination campaigns. This Destroyer carries the 'Red Hand' across his faceplate, denoting an award for bloody victory by his commanding officer in his previous battle.

Command within the Legion was gained through a mixture of martial prowess on the battlefield and displays of leadership on the front line, with specialists singled out by aptitude early on. No rank or role within the Legion was exempt from the expectation that they would fight as hard as the rest however, nor was the desire to grapple with a foe and cut them down by blade-stroke discouraged if the opportunity arose, be the Space Marine in question an Apothecary or Artillerist rather than a line fighter. Compared to many other Legions, order and discipline did not come as naturally to those of this gene-seed as might be expected-tempers seethed, slights perceived or real were met with anger and more often than not violence should their sense of honour be impugned. Any officer of the Legion knew they were expected to back up their commands by force if needed, and punish infractors b y their own hands as was the Legion's way to disobey an order in battle was a death sentence to be carried out without delay.

The arrival of the Primarch Angron brought a primitive, almost tribal unity to the newly renamed World Eaters, and he quickly became the pinnacle of warrior-hood to be aspired to for his Legion. Under his influence, the competitive, hot-blooded tension that had always roiled under the surface of the Legion's psyche was channelled and given form.

The rank structure of the World Eaters under Angron remained simple and direct, the Primarch having little but scorn for the trappings of elites and pointless accolades and titles, refusing it is said to be addressed even as 'Lord', but he did see the virtue of a reliable and transparent chain of command in war. Such honours that the Legion and their master did believe in were warrior-marks of brotherhood and the scars of battle; these things transcended rank and spoke to the worth of the Space Marine beneath the armour.

Trial by combat was also the Legion's preferred route of settling disagreements within its ranks, and bloodletting by warriors in open discord was an honourable thing both in Angron’s eyes and that of his Legion. Here also could one of higher rank be challenged for the right to command, although such rare contests were always to the death. The most revered measure of this latter practice was membership of the Devourers. This cadre within the Legion was a dedicated bodyguard unit for the World Eaters' Primarch (whether he needed one is another matter). In battle they fought encased in Terminator armour and carried the most savage weapons the Legion possessed. This warrior band numbered only twelve members, and access to its ranks was only attained by defeating a Devourer in single combat to the death or, should one of its number fall in battle against an enemy, a successor was selected by a contest open to all in the Legion, only one of which would survive.

## Legion Stratagems
Observed Strategic Tendencies: Shock Assault, Planet-kill and Exterminatus Operations, Close-quarters Actions (Space Hulk Purgation, Boarding Operations, Line-breaker Attacks).

In this the Legion quickly embraced the use of mass-drop pod attack from orbit in invasion situations to deliver themselves directly to the heart of the enemy without manoeuvre or preamble as a first wave attack. This sudden overwhelming blow was designed to keep the enemy pinned in place fighting this vanguard (which often suffered heavy losses), while a second wave of armour and heavier its followed in its wake and smashed into areas of high resistance revealed b y the first wave. In these attacks the Legion pressed its assault with murderous fury, often fighting in conjunction with dangerously close supporting barrages from warships in low orbit, or with Titans and other siege units right in the thick of the fighting with them. By these tactics the World Eaters’ onslaught overcame any resistance through sheer fury, hurling themselves again and again at the foe until their enemy broke and was cut down, fleeing in terror.

Hand-to-hand combat was always the Legion's preferred form of warfare, even before it took the Emperor-given name of the War Hounds for itself. This did not mean that the Legion lacked ability and competency in ranged engagements or armoured warfare and supporting artillery attack-indeed no lesser luminary of mechanised warfare than Ferrus Manus himself praised the War Hounds' armoured assault at Aldebaran Septus as the "epitome of iron-clad rage given form", but for the War Hounds such things were a tactical means to an end.

## Legion Armory
In addition to the ubiquitous combat blades or gladius, even Legionaries attached to reconnaissance squads and vehicle crews commonly carried chainblades, flay-cutters and mono-serrated bayonets, back-up knives, hatchets and cleavers. While in dedicated assault units particularly this profusion of bloody killing tools was added to by a weapon that dated back to the techno-barbarian tribes of Terra, the broad-bladed chainaxe. With the coming of Angron as their commander, the World Eaters’ predilections for hand-to-hand bloodletting reached even greater heights, as the master-gladiator taught them new weapons and new ways to kill, and what can only be described as a cult of personal combat took hold of the Legion at a fundamental level. The chainaxe was further refined under Angron’s direction, and such was the reputation it gained (particularly given its effectiveness against brute beasts such as the Orks), that its use spread to several other Legions. But as an abstract the weapon could be seen as a symbol of the World Eaters themselves: brutal and savage, remorseless and unsubtle, a machine with but one purpose — to kill.

## Legion Chronicles

### Unification Wars
Of the Terran origins of the XII it would appear that there was no particular bias as to the tribe or city state from which the initial influx of recruits was taken as there was in the case of several of the other Legions. There are though tantalising hints towards psychological screening being used to single out the most inherently aggressive and obsessively competitive recruit in an experimental pre-selection program that may have also informed the intake of a handful of other Legions as well. Whether this is true or merely supposition later formed to fit established facts, it is apparent from such records that survive that the XIIth were from the outset deemed a highly aggressive force, its warriors hot-blooded and savage.

The Legion's first recorded engagement saw them used in the Sa'afrik Liberation as a spearhead of shock troops, mounting direct annihilation assaults on enemy forces, both in open battle and fortified positions, and able to carry the attack despite their then relatively small numbers b y sheer courage and the fury of the violence they could unleash. The nascent Legion however, after its initial battles, seems to have been largely held in reserve by the Emperor during the latter Unification Wars and right through the re-conquest of the Sol system, perhaps in case of a sudden reversal of the fortunes of war, or as veiled evidence, implies as a weapon to be unleashed in case of disloyalty among the Emperor's own. During this me the XII was kept in a state of constant readiness, training relentlessly and steadily growing in numbers and on the occasions it was brought into battle it performed with almost gleeful savagery, tearing apart whatever enemy it was given to fight without mercy or falter, regardless of its own losses and heedless of risk. It is believed that it was during this period that the Emperor himself dubbed the XII Legion his War Hounds’ as a tribute to the savage and tenacious way they fought to pacify the narco-sprawls of the Cephic Hives, and in pride of this the red bound became the Legion's badge of war.

### Early Great Crusade
As the early decades of the Great Crusade progressed, and the first of the Primarchs were found, the XII Legion was broken up temporarily into a number of independent sub-commands, each several thousand Space Marines strong. The largest of which, at some 8,000 War Hounds, along with dedicated assault and fleet support elements was designated the 13th Expeditionary Fleet, or the 'Bloody 13"h as it became quickly known. They served as frontline assault troops in glorious campaigns alongside the Space Wolves, Iron Warriors and Dark Angels Legions. Elsewhere they would often provide the killing-strike for larger Imperial Army formations in war zones where an impasse had been reached, breaking a strategic deadlock in a single furious attack which sent an enemy reeling. very assault they conducted ended in only one of two ways: victorious slaughter or simple slaughter.

Increasingly the warriors of the XIIth were deemed by the War Council as being more suitable for use against targets where annihilation was the goal rather than compliance or liberation, a task to which they seemed eminently suited.

### Rediscovery of Angron
[...After rediscovery of Angron] culture within the Legion shifted to ever more violent and bloodthirsty mores and values, which became quickly echoed in the shifting skein of the Legion's own rites and ceremonies, and the martial traditions of Old Terra, never strong in the War Hounds who had prided themselves in their fury and courage above all else, disappeared altogether and were replaced by Angron's own red code of butchery and savage competition. The planets upon which the World Eaters fell were not merely crushed - they were destroyed utterly. Where once resistance had been found, now were left only grave worlds in their stead — planets not merely burned or blasted from on high but slaughtered in their streets and palaces, factories and fortresses.

Under Angron's command the Ork empires of Blitzklaw and Neverlight were exterminated by the World Eaters, as were the Khrave of Serreak-17 where others had failed before them. In one of the most famous triumphs of its time, the Legion entrapped the Eldar Craftworld of Tuonoetar which had endured for a hundred millennia and scoured it of life in a brutal attrition campaign, its vast and empty wraithbone carcass sent hurtling at last into a bloated ancient sun. Not least of their detractors was Roboute Guilliman, Primarch of the Ultramarines, who fought beside Angron and his Legion during the Cleansing of Ariggata and saw at first hand the bloodbath they had left in the wake of their attack on the Basalt Citadel, where the last resistance of this non-compliant human world had made their stand. Guilliman had seen the ramp of World Eaters corpses that had been used to finally mount a breach in the mighty fortress and the vengeful horror the Space Marines had wrought within and been sickened. But Angron's Legion, while friendless, nevertheless had its supporters, most vocal of which were Horus Lupercal of the Luna Wolves who defended their martial record and named them an invaluable asset to the Great Crusade, while Jaghatai Khan of the White Scars more cryptically praised their strength of purpose and purity, and challenged his fellow Primarchs: "...not to judge those on a path on which the winds of fate decreed that they had never themselves walked, nor ever could.

### Shadow Crusade

- Horus sent Angron's World Eaters and Lorgar's Word Bearers against Guilliman's Ultramarines. Their campaigns devastated hundreds of worlds and obstructed loyalist reinforcements.

Following the Dropsite Massacre, Lorgar and Angron assembled an enormous invasion force to devastate Ultramar. The Word Bearers commanded over 100 capital warships, including nine Gloriana-class battleships and three immense Abyss-class vessels: Furious Abyss, Blessed Lady, and Trisagion. Alongside the World Eaters' numerous smaller warships, the combined fleet carried nearly 300,000 Space Marines, supported by vast numbers of mortal soldiers. Their principal flagships were Lorgar's Fidelitas Lex and Angron's Conqueror.

The Traitor Legions left a trail of massacred worlds and destroyed warships across the eastern Imperium. The World Eaters, often personally led by Angron, slaughtered entire populations without attempting conquest or accepting surrender. Meanwhile, the Word Bearers conducted darker operations, including the mass abduction of hundreds of thousands of civilians from the Aglaean Colonies and mysterious rituals amid the ruins of Gareva. These atrocities may have been preparations for Erebus' great ritual at Calth.

One of their most significant targets was Honourum, a fortified Ultramarines outpost. The planet was defended by a full company of the XIIIth Legion under Captain-Praetor Arcaes Odenathus, alongside several Solar Auxilia regiments. Despite this resistance, Angron's forces overwhelmed the defenders in less than a day.

The massacre on Honourum continued for weeks, as the World Eaters and the Word Bearers' Gal Vorbak hunted surviving Ultramarines and Imperial soldiers through the ruined cities. The planet was left so devastated that it would not fully recover.

The combined Traitor fleet then disappeared into the Dominion of Storms, a notoriously dangerous region of Warp space along the Imperium's eastern frontier. Despite the increasingly violent Warp storms, Lorgar and Angron's armada passed through seemingly unharmed, continuing its destructive advance towards Ultramar.

Angron's relentless pursuit of slaughter severely delayed the Traitor fleet during its passage through the Dominion of Storms. Despite unusually favourable Warp travel, the World Eaters' repeated massacres threatened to ruin Lorgar's timetable for reaching Calth and preserving the element of surprise.

Following Angron's destruction of Garalon Prime, tensions between the two Primarchs nearly erupted into open conflict. According to the Chronicle of Ashes, their confrontation was prevented only by the sudden arrival and attack of a xenos fleet from the Xersinia Prohibited Zone.

This unexpected intervention may have prevented a disastrous split between the Word Bearers and World Eaters, allowing their campaign against Ultramar to continue.

By mid-007.M31, the Word Bearers and World Eaters had secretly assembled near the Xersinia Prohibited Zone, a quarantined region north of Calth. Its isolation allowed the Traitor fleet to regroup unnoticed after its turbulent journey through the Warp.

While preparing their invasion, the Traitor Legions raided merchant vessels and Imperial patrols, slaughtering crews and extracting intelligence about Ultramar's defences. Some accounts suggest the Word Bearers employed sorcery to lure ships into traps and unleash daemonic entities aboard them. Information gathered from captured commanders helped the Traitors refine their plans for attacking Calth.

The Ultramarines were dangerously exposed. Approximately 250,000 Legionaries served in the XIIIth Legion at the outbreak of the Heresy, making it the largest Legion. Its naval strength relied heavily on numerous versatile cruisers and strike craft manufactured at Konor and Calth, while its older battleships were less numerous. Much of this fleet, along with substantial Legion forces, had assembled at Calth awaiting the supposedly loyal Word Bearers.

The Traitors identified two critical defensive installations for destruction: the immense orbital fortress above Ischara and the neutron-pulsar cannon on Ravishol's fourth moon. Although the Veridia system was heavily fortified, the Ultramarines remained unaware of Horus' betrayal, giving the attackers a crucial advantage.

Lorgar divided the Traitor armada into at least six separate fleets, each assigned a different objective:

Calth: Erebus and Kor Phaeron commanded the principal Word Bearers assault force, comprising nearly 70 capital ships and numerous escorts. Their flagships were Destiny's Hand and the Infidus Imperator, formerly the Raptorous Rex.

Armatura: Lorgar and Angron led the largest concentration of destructive firepower, including the Abyss-class battleships Trisagion and Blessed Lady, alongside the Fidelitas Lex and Conqueror. Their objective was to destroy one of Ultramar's most important military strongholds.

Macragge: Word Bearers Captain Zadkiel launched an ultimately unsuccessful assault aboard the Furious Abyss.

The wider Five Hundred Worlds: Smaller Word Bearers and World Eaters squadrons conducted rapid raids against Imperial shipping, agricultural colonies, and industrial centres, including Espandor and Latona.

The Shadow Crusade was designed to cripple Ultramar militarily while spreading terror and destruction throughout Guilliman's realm. By attacking multiple worlds simultaneously, Lorgar intended to prevent the Ultramarines from concentrating their forces or recovering from the destruction at Calth.

These massacres also served a darker purpose: the suffering of Ultramar's population would fuel Lorgar's sorcerous plans. Angron and his World Eaters, previously restrained by the Word Bearers, were now free to unleash their full brutality.

### Destruction of Bodt [008.M31]

Rather than continue his usual strategy of attacking newly conquered worlds, Mor decided to strike directly at Bodt and destroy its military infrastructure before its resources could reinforce Horus. Meanwhile, Imperial commanders were attempting to organise a defensive line between Paramar and Chondax, and fragmentary records suggest that Mor may have received orders to join them. If so, he ignored those orders, remaining determined to pursue his independent campaign. Twelve days after departing Iod Binary, the Red Talon fleet emerged at the edge of the Bodt system, prepared for a full-scale assault against one of the most brutal Legions in the Imperium.

Autek Mor began his assault on Bodt's capital, Tredecimmia, with an audacious plan to destroy the entire World Eaters recruitment world. As the Red Talon fleet approached, Mor identified Bodt's lone moon, which was passing unusually close to the planet along its elliptical orbit. He ordered his warships to expend their remaining high-yield void ordnance to alter the moon's trajectory by a fraction of a degree, redirecting it towards Bodt.

Instead of passing the planet at a distance of 500,000 kilometres, the moon was now destined to collide with it within hours, unleashing destruction far beyond the capabilities of Mor's conventional weapons. The enormous detonation used to redirect the moon was visible from Bodt's surface, although its inhabitants had no idea that it signalled their world's impending destruction.

The sight of Bodt's burning moon plunged the planet's feral gun-clans into chaos. Terrified by the approaching celestial catastrophe, they turned their weapons against one another, with some even firing at the moon itself. Autek Mor likely intended this psychological effect to neutralise the gun-clans before his invasion. Meanwhile, the Red Talon fleet divided into twelve assault squadrons and launched a precision orbital bombardment against the surface-to-orbit defences surrounding Tredecimmia, destroying entire chains of weapons emplacements within an hour.

Despite the devastation, Bodt's principal defenders remained largely intact. The World Eaters overlord cadre, supported by Mechanicum Taghmata and Titan forces, were protected within subterranean fortifications and powerful void shields. Mor understood that orbital bombardment alone could not defeat such opponents, and by the third hour of the assault, he initiated a full-scale planetary landing to eliminate the surviving defenders before the moon struck.

As Bodt's burning moon approached the planet, Autek Mor launched a massive planetary assault against Tredecimmia. Waves of Iron Hands and Imperial Fists drop pods descended through intense anti-aircraft fire, suffering heavy losses but establishing multiple landing zones within four hours. Simultaneously, Xiphon fighters, Storm Eagles, and Fire Raptors attacked the planet's strongest fortifications, destroying a heavily shielded bastion in the poisoned inner sea. Iron Hands and Imperial Fists Thunderhawk formations then assaulted a fortified mountain complex, fighting through hundreds of defenders before the Imperial Fists breached its subterranean command bunker and crippled the Traitors' command structure.

At Tredecimmia's star port, Iron Hands Legionaries encountered World Eaters displaying extraordinary ferocity, providing the first evidence of the XII Legion's forbidden implantation experiments. The battle descended into vicious close combat before Imperial Fists reinforcements attacked the defenders from behind, although the World Eaters continued fighting to the last. Elsewhere, the Agathon Solar Auxilia struggled to contain previously unseen Traitor Mechanicum war machines equipped with powerful Warp-based shielding, losing dozens of super-heavy tanks. Frenzied gun-clans also attacked the Loyalists in overwhelming numbers, forcing Astartes Tactical Support squads to burn them down with flamers. With the star port, command bunker, and several strategic facilities neutralised, Mor initiated the decisive phase of his operation, directing attention towards the heavily defended macro-provender silos in north-western Tredecimmia, where the true purpose of his invasion would become apparent.

In the fifth hour of the assault on Bodt, Autek Mor launched an operation against the heavily defended macro-provender silos near Tredecimmia. Anticipating the intervention of Legio Audax Titans, he deployed Iron Hands and Imperial Fists blocking forces north and east of the complex, including Mastodon assault transports and Mauler battle tanks. The World Eaters responded as Mor had intended, abandoning their defensive positions to attack the eastern Iron Hands force, leaving the silos' western approaches exposed.

Mor then personally led the decisive strike, teleporting from the Red Talon with an elite force of Morragul Clan Gorgon Terminators, whose specialised armour technology he had managed to reproduce despite his isolation. The heavily armoured Iron Hands overwhelmed the remaining automated gun-towers supplied by the Sarum Mechanicum and advanced through the silos towards the entrance to the underground stasis chambers, the true objective of Mor's assault.

Autek Mor and his Gorgon Terminators encountered unexpected resistance inside Bodt's macro-provender silos, where insect-like Mechanicum sentries detached from the surrounding structures and attacked with rad-cleansers and lightning weapons. Fighting through the mechanical defenders, the Gorgons breached the heavily fortified entrance to the underground stasis chambers. Mor then ordered his warriors to hold the entrance and descended alone, forbidding anyone to follow him regardless of what happened.

Elsewhere, the World Eaters launched ferocious counterattacks using waves of rapidly indoctrinated Inductii, whose lack of experience was offset by extraordinary aggression and physical strength. More disturbingly, the Iron Hands encountered Red Butchers Terminators who were revealed to be former Loyalist World Eaters, psychologically shattered during the betrayal at Isstvan III and now incapable of distinguishing friend from foe. Their assault nearly overwhelmed the Iron Hands until Mastodon transports unleashed cyclonic melta fire, incinerating the Red Butchers alongside some of the Loyalists trapped in combat with them.

As Bodt's burning moon approached its final descent, the battle for Tredecimmia grew increasingly desperate. The World Eaters defending the planet were largely composed of rapidly implanted and indoctrinated Legionaries, many reduced to uncontrollable berserkers far removed from the Emperor's original vision for the Astartes. Wearing mismatched armour manufactured by the Crimson Brotherhood of Sarum and wielding unfamiliar experimental weapons, they inflicted severe casualties on the Iron Hands and Imperial Fists. Nevertheless, the experienced Loyalist veterans adapted their tactics and continued advancing despite mounting losses.

By the sixth hour, the battle had begun turning against Autek Mor's forces, while the Iron-Father remained inside the subterranean vaults beneath the macro-provender silos, pursuing an objective whose nature remained unknown. His prolonged absence endangered the entire operation as the moon drew closer to impact. The situation became critical when twelve Warhound Titans of Legio Audax emerged from the northern wastes, having bypassed or overwhelmed the Loyalist blocking forces. Their weapons devastated everything in their path as they advanced directly towards the silos, leaving only the Gorgon Terminators of Clan Morragul to oppose them. Even their formidable armour could not withstand such overwhelming firepower for long, and by the seventh hour, with planetary destruction imminent, Mor's mission appeared on the verge of failure.

The destruction of Bodt in 008.M31 left the World Eaters' recruitment world a volcanic wasteland that might remain uninhabitable for centuries or eventually disintegrate entirely. Autek Mor's invasion force included over 5,000 Iron Hands, fewer than 800 Imperial Fists, 300–400 additional Loyalist Astartes, and approximately 12,000 Solar Auxilia. Opposing them were around 3,500 established World Eaters, as many as 11,000 rapidly produced Inductii, fewer than 100 Legion command personnel, and substantial Mechanicum and Titan support under Tribune Karak and the Crimson Brotherhood of Sarum.

Although Mor had already guaranteed Bodt's destruction by redirecting its moon, he risked thousands of warriors to recover a mysterious stasis casket bearing the insignia of the Emperor's Terran gene-wrights. Its contents remain unknown, but some historians speculate that Mor intended to use forbidden genetic technology to transform or expand Clan Morragul beyond the established limitations of the Legiones Astartes. His true ambitions remain uncertain, reinforcing his reputation as a ruthless warlord whose power rested as much on dangerous secrets as on military strength.

Bodt was one of the World Eaters' oldest recruitment and training worlds, serving as Angron's muster ground after he assumed command of the XII Legion. Its capital, Tredecimmia, contained vast underground storage facilities, gladiatorial training arenas, and ancient weapons stockpiles. The Legion recruited heavily from Bodt's savage gun-clans, whose warriors fought brutal selection battles to earn the right to become Astartes. The planet also maintained a secret alliance with the Crimson Priests of Sarum, a renegade Mechanicum faction that supplied forbidden weapons and experimental technologies.

Following Isstvan III, World Eaters Apothecaries, assisted by specialists from the Emperor's Children and Word Bearers, began conducting horrific experiments to accelerate Astartes recruitment. Thousands of aspirants were subjected to unstable implantation procedures, with many dying or suffering catastrophic mutations. The survivors received modified Butcher's Nails and artificial memories of hundreds of battles, convincing inexperienced recruits that they were seasoned veterans. These rapidly produced Inductii became uncontrollably violent, often incapable of distinguishing friend from foe. By the time Autek Mor attacked Bodt, the planet had become a centre for mass-producing psychologically unstable World Eaters, sacrificing quality, sanity, and human life to replenish Angron's Legion.

### Loyalist Counter-Invasion of Numinal

- A Pillum-class corvette bearing faded blue-and-white World Eaters heraldry attacked an astropathic relay and deployed grey-armoured jump-pack Legionaries (their identities and precise allegiance remain uncertain in the notes).

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The World Eaters fought alongside the Word Bearers in the Shadow Crusade, while maintaining recruitment worlds such as Bodt. These centres rapidly produced new Legionaries known as Inductii. Loyalist Shattered Legion forces raided these facilities, provoking a prolonged shadow war against World Eaters hunter-killer formations.

Bodt continued producing rapidly indoctrinated Inductii after Isstvan III; their experiments drew on work by Apothecaries from the World Eaters, Emperor's Children and Word Bearers.

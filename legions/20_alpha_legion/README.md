# Alpha Legion

## Legion Structure

The Alpha Legion’s internal organisation remains deeply mysterious and contradictory. Some accounts describe an extremely hierarchical structure governed by layers of secrecy, while others portray a surprisingly egalitarian Legion in which warriors could freely contribute to strategic discussions regardless of rank. Despite these conflicting reports, the Alpha Legion consistently emphasised collective discipline, coordination, and unity of purpose. Alpharius designed his Legion around flexible battlefield tactics that demanded exceptional cooperation and the ability to adapt rapidly to changing circumstances. This philosophy began during recruitment and training. Aspirants were trained and tested as cohesive units rather than individuals, with each warrior’s survival dependent on the success of his comrades. Their trials were exceptionally dangerous and deliberately designed to be impossible to overcome alone, requiring intelligence, teamwork, and absolute mutual reliance.

The Alpha Legion excelled at espionage, sabotage, infiltration, and assassination, using these methods to cripple enemies before launching decisive assaults. Unlike Legions that relied on attrition or static defences, the Alpha Legion avoided prolonged warfare whenever possible, preferring to fight on its own terms. Despite its preference for covert operations, the Legion was exceptionally dangerous in open battle. Drawing inspiration from commanders such as Horus and Sanguinius, its warriors demonstrated remarkable adaptability, coordination, and mastery of complex manoeuvres. Their formations operated almost like a single organism, responding fluidly to changing battlefield conditions. The Alpha Legion’s organisational structure was equally flexible and unconventional. Although outwardly resembling standard Legion formations established by the Principia Belicosa, its internal hierarchy appears to have been far more specialised and adaptable. Individual Legionaries could be reassigned to different roles or positions according to operational needs, suggesting a command system built around tactical flexibility rather than rigid ranks.

The Alpha Legion maintained an exceptionally fluid organisational structure, constantly reorganising its companies, battalions, and chapters, sometimes designated as Harrows, Cohorts, Hosts, or Instruments. Unit numbers, personnel, and resources were frequently reassigned, even during active campaigns, making the Legion’s true strength, deployments, and intentions almost impossible for allies or enemies to determine. This deliberate ambiguity extended to the identities and appearances of individual Legionaries. Some accounts describe Alpha Legion warriors exhibiting unusual physical similarities, while others suggest considerable genetic diversity. Reports also mention unnatural grey skin, unusual pigmentation, and shimmering serpentine tattoos. The origins of these characteristics remain unknown. Possible explanations include surgical alterations, advanced implantation procedures, psychological conditioning, or forbidden technologies. Whether Legionaries willingly underwent such modifications—or were even aware of them—remains another mystery surrounding the XXth Legion.

The Alpha Legion maintained an exceptionally diverse arsenal, allowing it to conduct everything from armoured assaults and artillery warfare to precision strikes and close-quarters combat. Its extensive military resources were partly supplied by Forge Worlds such as Lucius, Incadion, and Phaeton, with Horus personally facilitating some arrangements to strengthen the Legion’s loyalty. The Legion also possessed rare and experimental equipment unavailable to most other Legions, suggesting access to secret technologies and independent procurement networks. A notable example was the Corvus-Alpha power armour, a unique variant resembling Corvus-pattern armour, already in use by the Dropsite Massacre and the First Battle of Paramar. Evidence suggests the Alpha Legion may have stolen early design schematics and developed its own version without Mechanicum involvement. Long before the Horus Heresy, the Alpha Legion had apparently established independent manufacturing capabilities and a vast network of concealed supply depots and weapons caches, both inside and beyond Imperial territory. These preparations allowed it to operate independently of conventional Imperial logistics, although the full extent of its hidden infrastructure remains unknown.

The Alpha Legion favoured mobility, deception, and concentrated firepower, typically opening engagements with reconnaissance and infiltration units before rapidly deploying fast armour, mechanised infantry, and close air support. Through coordinated attacks from multiple directions, feints, and ambushes, they exposed enemy weaknesses before delivering decisive blows with shock troops, heavy armour, and artillery. The Legion maintained numerous specialised formations, often equipped with rare or experimental weaponry. Among these were autonomous units known as Splinters, which operated independently on secret missions, sometimes without the knowledge of allied forces or even other Alpha Legion detachments. Their objectives frequently took precedence over their own survival. The Alpha Legion also specialised in elite assassination and sabotage units. Its Seeker and Reconnaissance squads targeted high-value enemies, while the feared Headhunters, also known as Effrit, conducted precision strikes behind enemy lines. Destroyer squads and covert operatives received similar recognition within the Legion, reflecting its preference for small, highly trained formations capable of inflicting disproportionate damage.

The Lernaean were among the Alpha Legion’s most formidable shock assault formations, consisting primarily of elite Terminator units, but sometimes including breacher squads and Dreadnoughts. Equipped with advanced teleportation systems, conversion beam weapons, and phase-field generators, they specialised in rapidly isolating and destroying the enemy’s strongest frontline troops and most valuable battlefield assets. Their overwhelming brutality was deliberately intended to shatter enemy morale as well as their defences. The Legion also maintained several mysterious specialised formations, including the Rhatosthaen, Shayatan, Quaryn, and Echidna, whose exact purposes remain unknown. Another designation, Chrythsaor, may have referred to the Alpha Legion’s Librarius or its wider intelligence network. The Legion apparently maintained extensive psychic and intelligence capabilities before the Council of Nikaea. Although Alpharius outwardly complied with the Council’s restrictions on battle-psykers, this obedience ended during the Horus Heresy.

The Alpha Legion relied extensively on human and augmented-human operatives, unlike most Legions that primarily used Imperial Army forces for conventional military support. These agents were trained in espionage, infiltration, and sabotage, subjected to psychological conditioning and sometimes enhanced through surgical or biochemical modifications. They operated individually or in compartmentalised cells, often unaware of one another’s identities or even their true masters. Known as the Sparatoi, these operatives infiltrated human worlds long before the Alpha Legion arrived, spreading misinformation, encouraging rebellion, manipulating insurgent groups, and sabotaging infrastructure. Their objective was to destabilise entire societies, leaving them vulnerable to conquest. During the Legion’s assault, they launched coordinated sabotage and diversionary attacks, frequently sacrificing their own lives. By the outbreak of the Horus Heresy, the Sparatoi network had already penetrated the Imperium’s civilian population and military institutions. Unconfirmed reports even suggested that Alpha Legion Space Marines had secretly infiltrated other Legions, impersonating their warriors and operating undetected within their ranks.

The Alpha Legion maintained a highly decentralised and flexible command structure, combining strict discipline with considerable independence at every level. Authority was determined by expertise rather than rank alone, with specialists such as siege masters exercising absolute control within their fields. Individual units operated as autonomous cells, expected to demonstrate initiative and adapt independently while pursuing the Legion’s wider objectives. This system gave the Alpha Legion exceptional battlefield coordination and responsiveness. Alpha Legion commanders rarely sought personal glory or direct confrontation. Instead, they observed battles from a distance, analysing developments and intervening only when their decisions or personal actions could inflict maximum damage. One of the Legion’s most distinctive command titles was Harrowmaster, also known as Iharuk. These commanders exercised overall authority over Alpha Legion forces within a war zone, constantly adapting strategies and coordinating complex operations. Their exceptional battlefield control earned considerable respect, even among Legions that otherwise distrusted them. The Alpha Legion also employed shifting ranks and titles, sometimes imitating other Legions’ command structures. The true identities of its Harrowmasters remained largely concealed, with even their recorded names potentially representing aliases or identities shared by multiple warriors.

The Alpha Legion’s true strength at the outbreak of the Horus Heresy remains uncertain. Contemporary estimates placed its numbers at 120,000–130,000 Legionaries, while lower assessments suggested around 90,000. However, evidence of numerous simultaneous operations across distant regions indicates that its actual strength may have reached 180,000 Space Marines, potentially making it one of the largest Legions. The Legion possessed extensive stocks of advanced military equipment, including Maximus and Tartaros-pattern armour, Sicaran battle tanks, plasma weapons, conversion beam weapons, and specialised munitions. Its large and diverse fleet favoured fast cruisers, intermediate warships, and escorts over heavy capital ships, reflecting its preference for mobility and flexible operations. There were also unproven suspicions that the Alpha Legion employed captured xenos vessels and incorporated forbidden alien technology into its warships. Remarkably, the Alpha Legion appeared capable of rapidly replacing its losses throughout the Horus Heresy. While other Legions steadily declined in strength, the XXth seemed to grow stronger despite suffering serious defeats. Its hidden recruitment networks, manufacturing capabilities, and concealed reserves made its true military potential almost impossible to assess, perfectly reflecting its symbol: the Hydra, growing new heads whenever one was severed.

## Legion Stratagems

The Alpha Legion, the XXth Legion of the Legiones Astartes, was shrouded in secrecy, deception, and contradiction from its very foundation. Its true origins, purpose, and activities were deliberately concealed with the apparent approval of the Emperor himself, making any reliable reconstruction of its history extraordinarily difficult. This secrecy gradually concealed a darker transformation within the Legion. Its growing fascination with manipulation, covert warfare, and destruction became increasingly dangerous, particularly after Horus became Warmaster. The Alpha Legion evolved into an unpredictable and autonomous force that neither the Emperor nor Horus could fully control. Much of the Alpha Legion’s history remains uncertain, reconstructed from fragmented intelligence, eyewitness accounts, and contradictory evidence. Even testimony from its own warriors is unreliable, deliberately obscured by lies and misdirection. Its true nature remains hidden beneath layers of deception.

Observed Strategic Tendencies: Surprise Assault, Sabotage, Infiltration, Insurgency and Counter-Insurgency Warfare, Multi-vector Attack, Interplanetary Pursuit and Decimation Campaigns, and Deep-range Raiding Operations.

Domains: Unknown / Unconfirmed

### The Confessions of the Hydra

#### The Confessions of the Hydra

Much of the available evidence concerning the Alpha Legion's relationship with the Warp originated from the testimony of an unidentified Legionary who surrendered to the VI Legion shortly after the Siege of Terra. Recorded under the designation LXX and subsequently executed for treason, the prisoner revealed that the Alpha Legion had secretly continued studying and employing psychic powers despite the restrictions established at Nikaea.

According to his testimony, Alpha Legion operatives infiltrated Warrior Lodges within several Traitor Legions, particularly those influenced by the Word Bearers and Sons of Horus. Their objective was to gather intelligence about Daemonic summoning, binding, possession, ritual symbols, and the extraction of supernatural power through sacrifice. Some agents experimented directly with these practices, although many died or disappeared. Unlike the Word Bearers, however, the Alpha Legion regarded the Warp primarily as a weapon and source of intelligence rather than an object of worship. Even after learning of the Chaos Gods, most apparently continued approaching supernatural forces through calculation and experimentation, accepting the risks as part of their clandestine operations.

The testimony also described dangerous experiments involving direct exposure to the Immaterium. In one incident, several Alpha Legionaries entered the Warp without protection; most perished, but two reportedly survived through a form of Daemonic symbiosis, emerging with unnatural abilities. Other operatives studied possession and summoning rituals before returning to share their discoveries with their Primarch. These accounts suggest that the Alpha Legion had developed considerable expertise in forbidden psychic practices while publicly maintaining a reputation for conventional covert warfare.

#### Alpha Legion Fifth Column — The Hetenborg Hill Massacre

During the final stages of the Chondax Cleansing, the Alpha Legion deployed operatives of its Fifth Column, disguising them as White Scars to infiltrate and betray Imperial forces. One such Legionary participated in the Hetenborg Hill Massacre on Eshmun, the third planet of Chondax's mid-system belt. His armour had been hastily repainted white, with battle damage exposing the Alpha Legion's turquoise beneath. The imitation Chogorian glyphs and tribal markings were crude but convincing enough at a distance.

The massacre began when the 42nd Seraphine Guard Regiment, commanded by Colonel Naeth, crossed the desert and discovered a vast crater inhabited by Orks at Hetenborg Hill. Naeth requested reinforcements from nearby White Scars formations, and two apparent Brotherhoods responded. The first arrived on jetbikes to support the Imperial Army, while the second positioned itself on the opposite ridge, its commander signalling readiness.

Confident that both flanks were secured, the Seraphine Guard and their White Scars allies charged into the Orks. However, once the Imperial forces were committed to close combat, the promised support from the second Brotherhood never arrived. Instead, Alpha Legion warriors appeared along the surrounding ridge, aiming their weapons at the Imperial troops rather than the Orks.

The incident exemplified the Alpha Legion's strategy of deception during the Chondax campaign: exploiting the distinctive appearance of the White Scars to infiltrate Imperial formations, manipulate their battlefield decisions, and expose them to devastating ambushes.

The supplied account ends as the Alpha Legion reveals itself; it does not establish the final fate of Colonel Naeth or the 42nd Seraphine Guard.

## Legion Chronicles

### Unification Wars and Secret Origins

The Alpha Legion’s secrecy originated at its very creation, near the end of the Unification Wars. Its gene-seed and development were subjected to exceptional security, even by the Emperor’s standards. Alongside the XVIIIth (Salamanders) and VIth (Space Wolves) Legions, the XXth was established largely in isolation from the other proto-Legions, apparently to fulfil highly specialised purposes known only to the Emperor. These three Legions were sometimes collectively referred to as the “Trefoil”, their unusual origins and separation from their brothers fostering lasting suspicion. While the Salamanders eventually overcame this distrust, the Alpha Legion became increasingly secretive and enigmatic. The Emperor’s original intentions for the XXth Legion remain unknown, including its earliest missions and the purpose behind its unusual development. This mystery would only deepen as the Alpha Legion evolved into one of the Imperium’s most unpredictable military forces.

Almost nothing is reliably known about the XXth Legion’s earliest operations on Terra or during the reconquest of the Sol System. Surviving records contain only scattered references to unidentified Space Marine units conducting targeted assassinations, abductions, and covert strikes, often operating under exceptionally high security clearances and without any recognised Legion allegiance. Some evidence suggests that the designation “Alpha” was associated with the XXth Legion from its earliest existence, potentially originating in the Emperor’s secret genetic laboratories. However, the true meaning of the name, the Legion’s original chain of command, and the purpose of its clandestine missions remain uncertain.

During the creation of the Legiones Astartes, each proto-Legion underwent several stages of genetic testing and gradual expansion, culminating in the “Alpha” induction. This final preliminary stage involved the first non-experimental implantation of gene-seed, producing a small but combat-capable force for battlefield testing. Records indicate that at least one gene-seed strain failed during this phase. The XXth Legion successfully passed its initial combat trials and received the Emperor’s approval, yet, unlike the other Legions, it was apparently never authorised for full-scale recruitment or assigned a dedicated recruitment region. Consequently, its strength remained limited to approximately 1,000–2,000 Legionaries, while the other Legions expanded rapidly during the early Great Crusade. The reason for this deliberate restriction remains unknown, raising further questions about the Emperor’s original intentions for the Alpha Legion.

The XXth Legion’s restricted expansion was unlikely to have resulted from genetic instability or poor battlefield performance. Its gene-seed implantation success rate was among the highest recorded, surpassing even several other Legions, while its combat effectiveness was consistently rated highly. Three theories have been proposed to explain why the Legion remained so small. The first suggests a shortage of suitable recruits, since other Legions had already claimed most viable candidates. The second proposes an undiscovered genetic or developmental flaw, although this seems unlikely given the Emperor’s willingness to correct or eliminate failed experiments. The third and most intriguing theory suggests that the Emperor deliberately kept the XXth Legion isolated for a special purpose. It may have served as a strategic gene-seed reserve, a control group for monitoring genetic degradation in other Legions, or a force undergoing unique conditioning and development. However, no evidence connects the XXth Legion to the Emperor’s secret Custodes project, Labryk Polaris, or other classified genetic experiments.

The XXth Legion disappeared from official Imperial records for several decades during the early Great Crusade, although scattered evidence suggests its involvement in mysterious, classified operations. Some historians theorise that the Legion, still without its Primarch, was secretly employed by the Emperor to protect the Imperium from internal threats. Despite the success of the Unification Wars, the emerging Imperium continued to face terrorist attacks, rebellions, and conspiracies. These threats included remnants of defeated Terran factions seeking revenge, ambitious renegades, and individuals corrupted by dangerous forces beyond the Imperium. The Alpha Legion may have served as the Emperor’s clandestine weapon against these hidden enemies, conducting operations too sensitive for conventional Imperial forces. However, its actual activities during this period remain largely undocumented, and the theory has never been conclusively proven.

The near-destruction of the IIIrd Legion (Emperor’s Children) during a mysterious gene-seed crisis was suspected by some to be the work of hidden enemies operating within the Imperium. During the fourth decade of the Great Crusade, reports emerged of unidentified Space Marine units conducting clandestine operations far from the main battlefronts. These small detachments carried no recognisable Legion insignia or operated under false identities, sometimes impersonating other Legions without their knowledge. Their missions included assassinations, espionage, sabotage, targeted strikes, artefact recovery, and abductions. These operations were exceptionally precise and deadly, rarely leaving witnesses. Although their true allegiance remained unknown, the accounts suggest the possible involvement of the mysterious XXth Legion.

The mysterious “Ghost Legion” conducted rare but highly secretive operations across the Imperium, with complaints from local commanders routinely dismissed or ignored. Some within Imperial High Command suspected the XXth Legion was responsible, but as organisations such as the Legio Custodes and Officio Assassinorum expanded their influence, reports of the Ghost Legion gradually faded into legend. If the Ghost Legion was indeed the Alpha Legion, it may have spent more than a century conducting a secret war alongside the Great Crusade, developing unparalleled expertise in infiltration, manipulation, and clandestine operations. Such an unusual history would explain the Legion’s distinctive methods and its extensive hidden networks. Why this covert role eventually ended remains unknown. Possible explanations include replacement by more specialised Imperial agencies, concerns that the Legion had become uncontrollable, the growing need for additional frontline troops, or the discovery of its Primarch. Whatever the reason, the XXth Legion eventually emerged from the shadows and entered the wider Imperial military structure.

The exact date of the Alpha Legion’s emergence as an openly recognised military force remains disputed. Some Imperial records place it shortly before the Farinatus Extermination, while others suggest it first appeared during the Third Rangdan Genocides, a discrepancy of approximately thirty sidereal years. Regardless, the XXth was the last Legion to receive official recognition and the last to reunite with its Primarch. Under its mysterious Primarch, the Alpha Legion quickly developed a reputation for exceptional discipline, organisation, and coordination. Its warriors operated with remarkable unity, prioritising collective objectives over individual achievement and combining rapid, overwhelming assaults with sophisticated sabotage, ambushes, assassinations, and psychological warfare. Despite its effectiveness, the Alpha Legion remained secretive and deliberately distant from its brother Legions, even during joint campaigns. Its preference for deception and manipulation created an atmosphere of distrust, with other Imperial forces increasingly perceiving something sinister beneath its disciplined exterior.

### Great Crusade — Emergence of the Alpha Legion

The Alpha Legion’s name carried deliberate symbolism and possible contradictions. Although the XXth was the last Legion created, “Alpha” traditionally signified “the first” or “the beginning,” while also suggesting supremacy and ambition. Its Primarch’s name, Alpharius, reinforced this symbolism, potentially expressing the Legion’s belief in its own exceptional status. The Legion’s early use of the combined Alpha and Omega symbols, known as the Aeternus, represented unity, continuity, and indestructibility. These symbols also carried ancient associations with serpents, knowledge, power, secrecy, and deception. The Hydra eventually became the Alpha Legion’s dominant emblem, representing a mythical serpent that grew two heads for every one severed. Together, these symbols reflected the Legion’s layered identity: ambition, resilience, hidden knowledge, manipulation, and the ever-present threat of destruction.

Unlike other Space Marine Legions, the Alpha Legion appeared as a fully formed military force with no clearly documented origins or recruitment history. Even the Imperialis Logistica could not determine its recruitment territories, heraldry, or actual strength. It seemingly emerged from nowhere with tens of thousands of well-equipped Legionaries and an experienced war fleet, already operating with remarkable coordination and discipline. The Legion deliberately cultivated confusion about its identity. Besides “Alpha Legion,” it operated under alternative names such as the Unbroken Chain and the more ominous Harrowing, used during the Perditian Gap Extermination. Individual Legionaries frequently identified themselves as “Alpharius” or “One of Many,” deliberately obscuring their identities and confusing allied forces. Whether this practice reflected the Legion’s emphasis on unity, an obsession with secrecy, or an effort to conceal its true numbers remains unknown.

Contradictory evidence further complicates the Alpha Legion’s unusual naming practices. Some records indicate that Legionaries adopted names and code words from ancient, long-forgotten dialects. During a campaign against the Khrave at Memlock, investigators discovered that the names used by Alpha Legion warriors matched those of inhabitants of a rebellious human world the Legion had previously exterminated during the Neverlight Contagion. The purpose of this disturbing practice remains unknown, whether psychological warfare, deliberate deception, or something more obscure. Remarkably, the Legion’s extensive use of secrecy and misinformation appears to have been tolerated by both the Emperor and later Horus, although their reasons remain unclear.

The Alpha Legion developed an exceptional reputation for combining covert warfare with overwhelming military force. Like the Raven Guard, they excelled at espionage, infiltration, and sabotage, but their objectives were far more ruthless. While the Raven Guard sought efficient victories with minimal unnecessary destruction, the Alpha Legion deliberately cultivated fear, confusion, and chaos, sometimes seemingly for their own sake. Their signature tactic, known as the “Harrowing,” involved systematically weakening an enemy before delivering a devastating final assault. Through deception, psychological warfare, and carefully orchestrated attacks, they manipulated opponents into vulnerable positions, stripped away their effective strength, and then struck simultaneously from multiple directions with overwhelming force. To their enemies, the Alpha Legion was a terrifying and unpredictable opponent, capable of exploiting seemingly invisible weaknesses and attacking without warning or mercy. To their allies, however, the Legion's secrecy, shifting identities, questionable loyalty, and apparent enjoyment of destruction made it deeply untrustworthy, despite its undeniable military effectiveness.

The Alpha Legion rapidly accumulated victories during the Great Crusade, seeking to prove itself superior to its brother Legions. Its commanders carefully studied the strengths and weaknesses of other Legions, adopting and adapting their tactics and weaponry. However, their unnecessarily elaborate, ruthless methods and obsession with proving their superiority increasingly earned them distrust rather than admiration. Relations with other Legions deteriorated significantly, particularly because the Alpha Legion frequently disregarded orders from commanders placed above it. Alpharius repeatedly clashed with Roboute Guilliman and Mortarion, notably following the Ullanor Campaign and during the Vassalius Uprisings. Rogal Dorn and the Imperial Fists were especially hostile, regarding the Alpha Legion as dishonourable assassins unworthy of bearing the Emperor’s symbol. Following the fall of the Fortress of Jakalla, only the Emperor’s intervention prevented bloodshed between their senior officers. Even Konrad Curze condemned the Alpha Legion for concealing its sins behind lies, while the Thousand Sons reportedly avoided contact with them whenever possible. By this point, the Alpha Legion’s military successes had been overshadowed by its growing isolation and rivalry with its fellow Legions.

Horus was perhaps the Alpha Legion’s closest ally among the Primarchs, recognising its unique strategic value and appreciating its ambition and unconventional tactics. The Legion also cooperated successfully with the Dark Angels and Iron Hands, demonstrating exceptional skill in coordinating campaigns with non-Astartes forces, although often sacrificing allied troops with ruthless efficiency. Its victories included suppressing rebellions, enforcing Imperial Compliance, and exterminating dangerous psyker-led raiders, xenos, and renegades. After becoming Warmaster, Horus granted the Alpha Legion considerable independence, allowing it to expand rapidly and establish several Expeditionary fleets and numerous deep-range raiding forces. These operated alongside Rogue Traders and ventured far beyond the established frontiers of the Great Crusade. However, even Horus gradually began questioning the Alpha Legion’s true motives and loyalty. Secret reports suggested that the Legion was becoming increasingly uncontrollable, perhaps even developing a dangerous obsession with elaborate warfare. Rather than pursuing efficient victories, it increasingly sought challenging battles and orchestrated unnecessarily complex, destructive operations simply to demonstrate its superiority. By the eve of the Horus Heresy, both the Warmaster and the Imperial Court feared that the Alpha Legion was slipping beyond their control.

By the final years of the Great Crusade, the Alpha Legion had developed a reputation for unnecessary destruction and increasingly elaborate warfare. Imperial commanders accused it of deliberately devastating civilizations that could have been brought into Compliance through negotiation or limited military action. Some suspected this behaviour reflected a growing psychological obsession with demonstrating its superiority, although Horus publicly dismissed such accusations. With distrust mounting, the Alpha Legion appeared to withdraw towards the distant frontiers of Imperial space, expanding its covert networks and adopting increasingly numerous false identities.

When the Horus Heresy began, Alpharius and approximately 50,000 Alpha Legionaries were stationed at Lucius, having recently returned from campaigns beyond the north-eastern Galactic fringe. Their apparent isolation from Imperial affairs convinced Rogal Dorn that they remained loyal, prompting him to include them in the punitive expedition against Horus at Isstvan V. In reality, the Alpha Legion had already established an extensive network of spies, infiltrators, and saboteurs throughout the Imperium. Its participation in the Dropsite Massacre exposed its allegiance to Horus, while unconfirmed accounts even suggested Alpharius had helped plan the betrayal. The Legion's clandestine operations would subsequently devastate countless Imperial worlds, causing billions of deaths. Yet even Horus never fully trusted Alpharius or believed he could control the Alpha Legion once unleashed, a suspicion that later events would prove justified.

##### The Primarch of Paradoxes

Of the Primarchs who fought in the Horus Heresy, Alpharius is among the least understood. Alpha Legion warriors repeatedly assumed his name and identity, even before allied Legions and representatives of the Imperial Court and Warmaster. Some accounts suggest that the XX Legion possessed more than one Primarch, or that Alpharius could somehow appear in multiple places simultaneously; others associate these mysteries with the three-headed hydra. Reports describe him either as indistinguishable from his Legionaries or as a towering figure in ornate hydra-decorated armour, wielding a double-headed spear. His strategic abilities were compared with those of Perturabo and Guilliman, and his extraordinary learning was accompanied by a cold, vigilant intellect. He apparently sought little fellowship with his brothers except perhaps Horus, although reports place him on Terra and Caliban at various times. His commanders enjoyed exceptional independence while remaining united in purpose, a contradiction that reflected the character of their Primarch.

##### 1. The Encounter with Horus

A tale circulated privately within the Imperial Court claims that Alpharius ruled a confederation of human systems. Its inferior fleet defeated an isolated Luna Wolves battleship through deception, provoking Horus into a pursuit marked by repeated ambushes. During an attack on his flagship, a lone intruder reached Horus’ command chamber and overcame his bodyguards. Rather than killing the attacker, Horus recognised him as his brother Alpharius.

##### 2. The Dead World of the Mandragoran Stars

A version extracted from an Alpha Legion Centurion captured by the Legio Custodes after the Fall of Seraphina places the infant Primarch on a nameless dead world near the Mandragoran Stars, amid the ruins of a civilisation destroyed before humanity arose. Alone for years, he survived its hostile environment and spectral terrors until a corsair vessel carrying renegades and alien mercenaries arrived to loot the ruins. Alpharius killed its occupants, acquired their knowledge, weapons and ship, and departed to seek his creator.

##### 3. The Captive of the Slaugth

One account encrypted within the suppressed Transit of the Human Soul through Strife, also called the Codex Hydra, describes Alpharius arriving on the prosperous tech-oligarchy of Bar’Savor. Before he was ten, the Slaugth invaded, captured him and subjected him to mental corruption, training him to sow discord before their predations. The Emperor eventually rescued him by attacking their immense stone vessel and spent years undoing the damage inflicted upon his son.

##### 4. The Emperor’s Hidden Hand

A contradictory account in the same work claims that Alpharius, or some part of him, remained behind when the other Primarchs were scattered, gravely injured and in some sense unfinished. Raised secretly by the Emperor near Terra, concealed even from His closest associates, he eventually became His covert agent and protector before departing to fulfil his destiny.

##### The Unresolved Enigma

Every one of these accounts is explicitly described as false. They are only a selection of the competing stories surrounding Alpharius; none establishes his true origins. Yet each may preserve a fragment of truth. The contradictions are integral to the mystery of the Primarch and his Legion, rather than inconsistencies that can be confidently resolved.

### Invasion of Paramar

Paramar was a dangerous target, defended heavily by both the Armada Imperialis and the Mechanicum, and to send too minor a force would be to court disaster, while to strike with the full weight of the Traitors’ power would risk committing to full scale engagement with the forces of the Segmentum Solar if matters went awry or a shock assault transformed into a protracted siege. With customary dispatch and conviction, Horus made his decision; the taking of Paramar would fall to the Alpha Legion.

Horus chose the Alpha Legion to invade Paramar not only for their expertise in covert operations and precise strikes, but also to test their loyalty. Despite Horus' relatively close relationship with Alpharius, neither he nor the other Traitor Legions fully trusted the Alpha Legion, whose true motives remained unclear. Their limited and cautious participation in the Dropsite Massacre had raised further suspicions. At Paramar, Horus intended to force the Alpha Legion to demonstrate its commitment through open warfare and the sacrifice of its own warriors.

The Alpha Legion secretly entered the Paramar system, using radiation from its three stars to conceal their fleet. They established blockades and ambushes in nearby systems to prevent reinforcements and communications from reaching Paramar. Although delaying the assault increased the risk of discovery or news of their betrayal arriving, it also allowed them to refine their plans, move warships closer to their targets, and infiltrate the system’s defences with covert operatives.

Before the assault on Paramar, the Alpha Legion hijacked transport ships and patrol vessels to secretly infiltrate the system with operatives and gather intelligence. Their Spartoi agents marked key personnel, particularly aboard the Pharaeon of Paramar, for assassination and planted sabotage devices throughout the defence network. Meanwhile, delayed-action toxins and biological contaminants were introduced into the water supplies of the system’s prison hives, intended to cause chaos when activated. These coordinated preparations ensured that Paramar’s defences would be crippled from within when the Alpha Legion launched its attack.

Approximately 38 days after the Dropsite Massacre on Isstvan V, eleven Alpha Legion warships entered the Paramar system, posing as loyalist forces under Rogal Dorn’s authority. Claiming to have been separated from the Isstvan V expedition by Warp turbulence, they requested urgent repairs and resupply. The battle barge Sigma-Pythonus and its escorts approached the Pharaeon of Paramar, while the heavy assault cruiser Anax-Rho headed for Paramar V. The defenders accepted their deception without suspicion, allowing the Alpha Legion to position its forces for a surprise attack while additional warships remained concealed at the system’s outskirts.

As the Alpha Legion fleet approached its targets at Paramar, five unexpected Astartes warships emerged from the Warp, threatening to expose their carefully planned deception. Despite the danger, the Alpha Legion maintained its course and challenged the newcomers. The vessels were heavily modified and reinforced Iron Warriors warships, led by the Tyche’s Lament, flagship of the 77th Grand Battalion under Kyr Vhalen. Vhalen responded dismissively, declaring that he cared nothing for the Alpha Legion’s orders, only his own.

The unexpected arrival of five Iron Warriors warships threatened to disrupt the Alpha Legion’s invasion of Paramar. Although both Legions served Horus, they had a history of hostility, and the Iron Warriors’ presence was entirely unanticipated. The Alpha Legion could not determine whether Horus had sent them as reinforcements, as watchdogs to ensure their loyalty, or whether they were acting independently. Uncertain of their intentions or whether more ships were approaching, the Alpha Legion nevertheless continued its operation, which had already progressed too far to abandon.

The Alpha Legion warships silently advanced deeper into Paramar’s defences, their fully armed Legionaries awaiting the order to attack. Their weapons remained concealed from Imperial sensors, allowing them to pass unchallenged between the massive defensive gun bastions. The lead vessel approached the Pharaeon of Paramar itself, while its escorts moved towards the protected fleet anchorage platforms, carefully positioning themselves for a devastating surprise assault.

The Alpha Legion battle barge Sigma-Pythonus took position near the Pharaeon of Paramar and dispatched a Thunderhawk carrying a figure believed to be Primarch Alpharius. His imposing appearance convinced the station’s officers and Mechanicum representatives of his identity, and he was immediately escorted to the main command centre. There, the Commodore-Intendant, Magos-Shipmaster, Preceptor of Astropaths, and other senior officials knelt before him. As the station’s alarm sirens suddenly sounded, Alpharius immediately slaughtered the assembled leadership, decapitating Paramar’s command structure at the opening of the assault.

The Alpha Legion launched its surprise assault on the Pharaeon of Paramar, with the Sigma-Pythonus devastating the station’s defences and void shields at point-blank range. Simultaneously, other Alpha Legion warships attacked the anchored Imperial fleet, deploying boarding parties through assault rams, Dreadclaws, and teleportation. Pre-planted sabotage crippled communications, power systems, and targeting networks, causing confusion and friendly fire, while assassinations and poisonings eliminated key officers. The defending forces were quickly overwhelmed, and within an hour the Pharaeon was effectively captured. Its surrounding gun bastions could only watch helplessly, as their weapons were designed to repel external threats rather than enemies already inside their defensive perimeter.

Above Paramar V, the Alpha Legion cruiser Anax-Rho and the Iron Warriors flagship Tyche’s Lament became locked in a dispute over priority access to the Paramar Nexus, while the Mechanicum refused to intervene. The Alpha Legion discovered that Kyr Vhalen’s 77th Grand Company had spent over forty years fighting xenos in the Therikon Wastes and remained completely unaware of the Horus Heresy, the Dropsite Massacre, or their own Legion’s betrayal of the Emperor. Vhalen had merely returned to Paramar for routine resupply and refused to yield his rights to the Alpha Legion. The standoff remained unresolved as events elsewhere in the system overtook them.

As the Alpha Legion’s attack on the Pharaeon became detectable, the Anax-Rho abandoned its standoff with the Iron Warriors and launched a surprise salvo of fusion torpedoes against the unsuspecting Tyche’s Lament. It then deployed a wave of Kharybdis Assault Claws against the Paramar Nexus primary terminal on Paramar V. Despite the Mechanicum’s rapid response, the assault craft penetrated the station’s partially activated void shields and crashed into the upper towers of the Terminus Panopticon, beginning the Alpha Legion’s ground assault.

The Anax-Rho continued its assault on Paramar V, scattering the Iron Warriors’ escort ships while bombarding the Terminus Panopticon’s shields with heavy macro-cannons. Preparing additional torpedoes and a second wave of assault craft, the Alpha Legion hoped to overwhelm the Mechanicum stronghold before its defences fully activated. Meanwhile, the Tyche’s Lament survived the surprise fusion torpedo attack thanks to its extraordinarily thick armour, suffering mostly superficial damage. Despite being caught with its weapons and shields unprepared, the Iron Warriors battleship rapidly accelerated towards the enemy, preparing to retaliate.

Despite blinded sensors and a burning bridge, Warsmith Kyr Vhalen personally seized control of the seven-kilometre-long Tyche’s Lament and accelerated directly towards the Anax-Rho. Preoccupied with bombarding Paramar V and attacking the Iron Warriors’ escorts, the Alpha Legion cruiser failed to react in time. The Tyche’s Lament rammed its amidships from below, shattering its void shields and inflicting catastrophic damage. Although the Anax-Rho barely survived, it was thrown out of control and forced to retreat from orbit, while Vhalen’s battleship began turning for another attack.

Within the Terminus Panopticon, Alpha Legion Terminators launched a brutal assault, smashing through Mechanicum defenders, barricades, and automated weapons. Their objective was the Panopticon Narthex, a central control chamber capable of commanding the entire Paramar Nexus facility and its defences. However, the Alpha Legion encountered something entirely unexpected. The Panopticon was not a conventional fortress but an immense, constantly shifting mechanical labyrinth designed by Archmagos Suyria Nihhon. Under her control, the installation began actively fighting the invaders. Thousands of expendable thralls were sent against the Terminators, sacrificing themselves to slow their advance and exhaust their ammunition. Meanwhile, the fortress itself became a weapon. Walls shifted to separate squads and trap Legionaries, corridors collapsed, electrical systems overloaded, mechanical tendrils attacked intruders, and floors opened into deadly shafts. The Alpha Legion suffered mounting casualties as the Panopticon systematically attempted to destroy them.

Despite these obstacles, the Legionaries adapted, regrouping and carving their way through the shifting structure with their weapons. Although the fortress's automated countermeasures alone were unlikely to stop the Alpha Legion, they had delayed and weakened the attackers. More importantly, they were merely the beginning of the Mechanicum's defence. Deep beneath the Panopticon, powerful war machines were awakening, preparing to confront the invaders directly.

The Mechanicum unleashed waves of Castellax and Vorax Battle-automata against the Alpha Legion Terminators inside the Panopticon. Emerging from hidden passages, the war machines attacked at close range with devastating claws, bolt cannons, and plasma weapons, tearing apart even heavily armoured Legionaries. The Alpha Legion fought back fiercely, destroying numerous automata with their own weapons, but the battle descended into brutal close-quarters combat throughout the fortress. Gradually, the Mechanicum gained the upper hand. For every automaton destroyed, another emerged to replace it, while the Alpha Legion's original force of more than 200 Terminators suffered mounting casualties. Despite inflicting heavy losses on the defenders, the Legionaries were increasingly overwhelmed. Their assault, initially slowed and contained by the Panopticon's defences, was now in danger of being completely crushed.

As Paramar V fully mobilised its defences, the heavily damaged Anax-Rho and Tyche’s Lament continued their battle in orbit, while the surviving Iron Warriors escorts waited for an opportunity to intervene. Suddenly, a barrage of powerful energy weapons fired from the planet’s surface, striking the Alpha Legion cruiser and completely destroying it. The Mechanicum and Iron Warriors had successfully repelled the initial assault on Paramar V, but their victory was short-lived. Long-range sensors revealed hundreds of additional enemy vessels approaching the planet, signalling that a far greater attack was imminent.

With Paramar V fully alerted and preparing for war, the Alpha Legion faced the prospect of a full-scale planetary invasion. A prolonged siege would favour the defenders, allowing the Mechanicum to activate its enormous reserves of weapons and war machines while potentially devastating the very facilities the Alpha Legion sought to capture intact. The approaching second wave observed the rapid activation of vast planetary void shields and powerful Titan reactors hidden beneath the southern polar mountains. Meanwhile, the Iron Warriors’ 77th Grand Battalion had deployed to the Paramar Nexus before their damaged ships withdrew from the system. The Alpha Legion now faced a rapidly strengthening Mechanicum fortress reinforced by experienced Iron Warriors, making a swift and decisive victory increasingly difficult.

With speed essential, Alpha Legion Harrowmaster Armillus Dynat ordered his fleet to accelerate beyond safe engine limits, even using faster warships to tow massive Mechanicum vessels that could not keep pace. Several ships were damaged or lost in the dangerous manoeuvre, but Dynat succeeded in rapidly closing the distance to Paramar V. The Alpha Legion began its assault with continuous torpedo barrages, while the planet's defences responded with powerful lasers and interceptor missiles. Although nearly all incoming ordnance was destroyed before reaching the surface, the bombardment served as a diversion, concealing the attackers' precise movements from the defenders. Under cover of the overwhelming exchange of fire, three Alpha Legion strike frigates descended directly into Paramar V's upper atmosphere at extreme speed, exploiting the confusion to penetrate the planet's defensive perimeter and begin the next phase of the invasion.

Three Alpha Legion strike frigates flew dangerously low over Paramar V’s southern polar region, unleashing their powerful ship-to-ship weapons against ground installations and planetary defence lasers. The defenders struggled to target the fast-moving vessels, whose devastating bombardment tore through fortifications and created widespread destruction. However, the extreme manoeuvres proved catastrophic for the attackers. One frigate disintegrated under immense gravitational stress, while another suffered engine failure and crashed into a mountain. Only the third escaped back into space, heavily damaged. Despite losing two warships, the suicidal attack achieved its objective, opening a gap in Paramar V’s planetary defence network approximately 40 kilometres south of the Paramar Nexus. This breach would serve as the entry point for the Alpha Legion’s main invasion force.

As orbital bombardment continued, the Alpha Legion exploited the breach in Paramar V’s defences to launch its main ground invasion. Heavy Argo-class landers, Stormbird carriers, and Thunderhawk transports deployed hundreds of tanks onto the planet’s rocky deserts. They were followed by enormous mass conveyors carrying the Titans of Legio Fureans, the Tiger Eyes, and the war machines of the Taghmata Satarel, including numerous Battle-automata. Rather than conduct a prolonged siege, Harrowmaster Armillus Dynat committed his entire force to a single, decisive armoured assault. With no reserves or defensive positions prepared for retreat, the Alpha Legion relied on overwhelming firepower, speed, and precise coordination. Its fully mechanised army rapidly advanced across the desert towards the Paramar Nexus, staking the entire invasion on one devastating offensive.

The Titans of Legio Fureans (Tiger Eyes) spearheaded the Alpha Legion’s advance towards the Paramar Nexus, followed by two massive formations of Predator and Sicaran tanks, including the new Scorpius Whirlwinds. Behind them advanced Fellblade and Glaive super-heavy tanks, while approximately a hundred Land Raiders and Spartans transported the Legion’s infantry. Gunships and speeders provided close air support. Further behind came the Taghmata Satarel, advancing in a separate formation protected by a mobile power field generated by a hovering Ordinatus-class platform. They were accompanied by the bronze-and-crimson Knights of House Perdaxia and the ghost-grey Knights of House Rajha. Together, this enormous combined-arms force of Titans, tanks, Knights, infantry, and Mechanicum war machines advanced at full speed across the desert, forming a devastating armoured spearhead aimed directly at the Paramar Nexus.

The first major clash of the invasion erupted when the Titans of Legio Fureans (Tiger Eyes) encountered the loyalist Legio Gryphonicus (War Griffons), positioned across a five-kilometre defensive line at Landing Zone Secundus, guarding the main approach to the Paramar Nexus. Both Titan Legions advanced towards one another and unleashed an almost unprecedented exchange of firepower, devastating the surrounding landscape. Void shields collapsed, armour disintegrated, and entire war machines were annihilated in explosions visible from orbit. Among the first casualties was the Tiger Eyes Warlord Titan Lyakarri, whose reactor explosion also destroyed the nearby Warhound Blood Hunger. The War Griffons' Reaver Titan Southron Shield was likewise torn apart by concentrated laser fire. These were only the opening losses in what would become a catastrophic battle between the two Titan Legions.

The devastating clash between Legio Fureans and Legio Gryphonicus created an impassable no-man’s-land directly before the Paramar Nexus. Anticipating this, the Alpha Legion divided its armoured spearhead into two forces, bypassing the Titan battle on opposite sides. One advanced towards the main landing and supply complex southwest of the Terminus, while the other followed a longer route to attack the Terminus itself from the southeast. Following behind, the super-heavy tanks and mechanised infantry also split into separate formations, while smaller detachments moved to seize strategic positions and deploy supporting weapons. The resulting battle became an exceptionally brutal confrontation across largely open terrain, with little cover for either side. The Alpha Legion’s massive armoured offensive collided with heavily fortified Mechanicum defences, further strengthened by newly constructed Castellum Strongholds manned by the Iron Warriors’ 77th Grand Battalion, who had rapidly reinforced the Terminus complex.

By the third hour of the battle, both Titan Legions had suffered devastating losses, leaving the battlefield covered in wreckage, smoke, and radioactive fallout. In the southwest, Harrowmaster Armillus Dynat personally led the Alpha Legion to capture the Provender and supply complexes, securing their most decisive victory so far. The ruined installations became a forward firebase for attacks against the Terminus, while Alpha Legion Headhunter squads eliminated survivors and searched for entrances to the underground facilities. Elsewhere, however, the Alpha Legion suffered a major setback. Iron Warriors super-heavy tanks, including Stormblades, Falchions, and Typhons, emerged from concealed underground positions and ambushed an advancing armoured column from the rear, effectively splitting the attacking force in two. Their concentrated fire devastated the lighter Alpha Legion tanks. Simultaneously, Iron Warriors Dreadnoughts supported by Legio Gryphonicus Battle-automata assaulted the Alpha Legion's super-heavy vehicles at close range. Despite heavy losses during their approach, those that reached the tanks tore them apart in melee, exploiting the inability of their massive weapons to engage enemies at such close distances. Both the Alpha Legion and Iron Warriors suffered heavy casualties and were eventually forced to withdraw from the immediate engagement. During the fighting, Glaive super-heavy tanks accidentally ruptured buried promethium pipelines with their Volkite weapons, triggering a massive eruption of burning fuel. The resulting inferno temporarily split the battlefield and separated the opposing forces.

By the fifth hour of battle, the Alpha Legion had gained the upper hand despite suffering heavy casualties, systematically overwhelming the Mechanicum’s defensive lines until only the Terminus remained. The siege-automata of Taghmata Satarel, protected by the Cerastus-Lancer Knights of House Perdaxia, finally broke through the Iron Warriors’ fortified Castellum Strongholds. The defenders fought to the last, refusing to retreat even as their positions collapsed. Warsmith Kyr Vhalen was gravely wounded and evacuated from the ruins by his Apothecaries. With the defensive perimeter destroyed, twelve surviving battle-ready Titans of Legio Fureans advanced on the Terminus. Their overwhelming firepower sealed the fortress’s fate, while the Taghmata Satarel’s Myrmidons breached the Panopticon and massacred its remaining defenders. Harrowmaster Armillus Dynat watched the final assault from atop his damaged Fellblade. However, neither the wounded Kyr Vhalen nor Archmagos Suyria Nihhon could be found, having apparently disappeared into the underground depths. Nevertheless, the Alpha Legion had achieved its primary objective: the Paramar Nexus and Paramar V were captured, with their vast industrial facilities and military stockpiles intact and now under Horus’ control.

Horus received news of the conquest of Paramar V with frustration rather than satisfaction. The Alpha Legion had departed almost immediately after their victory, taking valuable supplies and equipment from Paramar’s stockpiles but leaving the system severely undermanned. The remaining Taghmata Satarel lacked sufficient forces to maintain control and feared both uprisings among the population and attacks by surviving Loyalists. Consequently, Horus was forced to divert additional troops to defend Paramar, undermining his plans elsewhere. These concerns eventually proved justified when the system faced a Loyalist counter-invasion two years later, followed by further devastation during the Scouring. According to an unverified account, Horus was furious with Alpharius and demanded an explanation for his Legion’s actions. Alpharius reportedly replied that he had fulfilled his orders precisely: he had been commanded to capture Paramar, not to garrison it. His response allegedly concluded with an ancient Terran proverb: "The Devil is not Mocked."

### Chondax Campaign

- A powerful Alpha Legion fleet attacked Jaghatai Khan's White Scars while they were campaigning against Orks near Chondax.

#### Alpha Legion operations at Chondax

##### 4. The Hydra Waiting in the Dark

The Alpha Legion's preparations were more extensive than any ordinary Ork-cleansing operation required. The Unbalanced Scales distinguishes an early local force of no more than about one hundred smaller warships from a later reserve fleet supposedly numbering at least 600 capital ships and more than 1,000 lighter craft. It also suggests fewer than 30,000 Alpha Legion ground troops were present, many dispersed in small detachments across the system. Those figures conflict with other accounts and may include disguised civilian craft, wrongly attributed vessels or deliberate deception; the chronicle cautions against accepting them literally. Even the identity of the Alpha commander remains uncertain. A codename, Desidero, appears, but records suggesting Alpharius himself commanded every operation are treated sceptically.

The XX Legion's most consequential preparations were hidden from the White Scars. It established a network of fortified stations and outposts, including the Tenebrae installations positioned along the system's heliopause and between its stars. These bases maintained communications, supplied covert detachments, monitored traffic and could interfere with astropathic transmissions. Some may also have manipulated the Warp by methods not disclosed in Imperial records. The result was an isolation system that made it extremely difficult for the Khan to receive reliable news or for his forces to leave in coherent strength.

Several accounts even implicate Word Bearers or other servants of the Ruinous Powers in making the surrounding Warp storms so persistent. The wider motives are equally uncertain. Horus clearly benefited from the Khan's absence, yet the document speculates that the Alpha Legion might have pursued an additional agenda: serving a secret patron, seeking an advantage over Horus, or exploiting the Heresy for purposes unknown. The record offers no conclusive resolution.

##### 6. Seven Years of Deliberately Prolonged War

What should have been a manageable cleansing campaign became a seven-year war of diminishing returns. Ork clans that ought to have been broken regrouped with inexplicable speed, appeared where they could disrupt carefully planned encirclements, or escaped just as the White Scars closed in. Sometimes unrelated xenos groups displayed an unusual awareness of the Legion's deployments. Other times earthquakes, subsidence and unpredictable Warp-related phenomena disrupted otherwise decisive operations. White Scars formations had been scattered across a dozen worlds, and every attempt to unite them or withdraw the last garrisons was delayed by fresh reports of enemy activity.

The Alpha Legion's hidden hand is clearest in the recorded campaign on Shaln. Nogai Noyan-khan led a highly successful eradication effort, exploiting reconnaissance specialists and veteran hunters to separate and destroy Ork groups. His swift victories threatened to bring that part of the campaign to a close. During the battle of the great western reef, however, Nogai was assassinated. The death was officially attributed to the Orks, but the later record assigns it to an Alpha Legion headhunter cadre led by a warrior bearing the title Interfector. The assassination both removed a capable commander and forced the White Scars to renew operations against an enemy they had nearly cornered.

Such measures were not isolated. Infiltrators murdered xenos warlords whom the White Scars were about to destroy, ensuring that the Orks would scatter rather than be decisively defeated. Alpha Legion agents diverted raiding parties away from White Scars traps, launched covert attacks against patrols and suppressed attempts to investigate suspicious events. On the fringes of the system, Ork seers became afflicted by Warp-born disturbances that the White Scars could not explain. The campaign was never allowed to reach a natural conclusion: every success opened another demand for pursuit, supplies and time.

The isolation became as damaging as the fighting. No reliable messages arrived from the wider Imperium, and small vessels dispatched through the storms to carry reports or seek information often failed to return. Legion officers became increasingly uneasy, but the Khan continued to honour his assignment. The Unbalanced Scales later alleges that the Alpha Legion was under orders not merely to delay the White Scars, but also to avoid damaging them so severely that they would become uncontrollable. That limitation, apparently imposed by Horus, was not always obeyed.

##### 10. The Veil Lifts and the Hydra Appears

The first Alpha Legion attacks struck isolated White Scars scouts and patrol craft while the Khan attempted to reassemble his scattered host. Communications were again disrupted locally, and individual units were captured, killed or impersonated. Then the concealed XX Legion fleet emerged in strength. White Scars witnesses described well over a thousand vessels bearing the Alpha Legion's insignia, compared with roughly seven hundred White Scars ships that could answer the recall. These totals may include disguised transports and armed merchantmen, but the White Scars unquestionably faced an enormous, coordinated threat.

Rather than launch an immediate extermination attack, the Alpha Legion established a blocking cordon, mirrored the White Scars' formations and refused to explain its presence. Jaghatai sent demands and signals; none brought a trustworthy answer. His commanders responded by preparing for battle, but the Khan continued to hesitate, aware that an attack on another Legion could make reconciliation impossible. The Alpha Legion exploited his restraint to occupy critical exits and move against the Hordes still scattered through the system.

One of the crucial openings lay at a Mandeville point between the stars of the system, where precise navigation was needed to make a safe Warp transition. The hidden Tenebrae stations had made that route unusually treacherous and forced vessels to depend upon controlled alignments and beacons. A fleet that could reach this narrow exit could leave Chondax; one that failed would be trapped among the Alpha Legion's much larger reserve.

The chronicler also questions whether the Alpha Legion's Chondax force was truly unified. Some actions were coherent attempts to delay the Khan; others contradicted Horus' apparent instructions by provoking open battle or killing warriors he had demanded remain intact. Possible answers range from rival commanders and competing agendas to a deliberate attempt by Alpharius to shape the Heresy's duration rather than simply secure Horus' victory. None is proved by the surviving evidence. The Alpha Legion also operated at Alaxxes, apparently fielding forces of a scale larger than contemporary Imperial estimates could explain.

Chondax therefore cannot be reduced to a straightforward tale of Traitors defeated by the White Scars. The Khan escaped, but his Legion was mauled, divided and too late to influence many decisive events. Horus benefited from that delay, yet the Alpha Legion's contradictory operations invite questions about whom its commanders actually served. the Heresy's gravest danger was not only Daemonic corruption or the ambition of a single Warmaster, but the human capacity for calculated betrayal, manipulation and cruelty.

### Siege of Mezoa [early Age of Darkness]

Consul-Delegatus Autilon Skorr was appointed overall commander of the Mezoan expedition, using his seniority to seize most available supplies and reinforcements for the Alpha Legion. This left Dreygur's already depleted Iron Warriors struggling to recover from their losses at Stranivar, with little hope of support from the distant Perturabo or other Traitor commanders. Despite these internal divisions, the invasion force comprised 9,000 Legionaries, twelve capital warships, including two Mangonel-class bombardment cruisers, three Taghmata from M'Pandex, and 20,000 auxiliary troops. It was among the largest independent Traitor battlegroups entrusted to a commander other than a Primarch during the early Heresy. Victory promised Skorr considerable favour from Horus, making the expedition both a military opportunity and a means of advancing his personal ambitions. With Davinite priests assuring the Navigators of a safe passage, the armada departed Port Maw, poised to attack Mezoa within days of the Ebon Drake's arrival.

The recovery of Cassian Dracos restored hope and unity among the Salamanders, Raven Guard, and Iron Hands aboard the Ebon Drake. Although Dracos could offer no assurance about the fate of Ferrus Manus or Corvus Corax, his survival and conviction that Vulkan still lived inspired the entire company. Once installed aboard the cruiser, his ancient Dreadnought chassis, forged by Vulkan himself, began repairing its damaged systems without assistance. Despite more than a year of conscious entombment beneath Isstvan V, Dracos refused to enter the restorative sleep customary for ancient Dreadnoughts. Instead, he became an increasingly influential figure, delivering cryptic prophecies and recounting memories of centuries of warfare. Many Legionaries adopted new devotional heraldry in his honour, and those who gathered around him became known as the Disciples of the Flames.

Dracos' influence extended beyond the Astartes. Servitors and automata aboard the Ebon Drake began exhibiting inexplicable behaviour, with some abandoning their duties to attend him without receiving orders. These disturbances were apparently never investigated. Within days, the ancient warrior, now known as the Dragon Revenant, had effectively assumed command of the expedition. Although its original mission was complete and many expected to return to Nocturne, Dracos insisted that the cruiser instead travel to the Forge World of Mezoa. Few questioned his authority, and the Ebon Drake departed Isstvan V under his direction, leaving the ruined system behind forever.

The Ebon Drake arrived at Mezoa to find the Loyalist Forge World already under siege by a powerful Traitor armada. Iron Warriors and Alpha Legion warships were battling a heavily outnumbered defensive fleet composed of scattered Legion vessels, Imperial Navy ships, and Mezoan warcraft. Emerging unexpectedly into the middle of the engagement, the Salamanders cruiser briefly confused the Traitors, giving its crew enough time to identify friendly vessels and open fire. The Ebon Drake then joined the Loyalist defenders, attempting to prevent Horus' forces from securing control of the planet's orbital approaches.

The battle continued for nine hours, with the Traitors sacrificing warships to gradually overwhelm the defenders. The Loyalists suffered from divided command, as their various contingents answered to separate leaders and lacked effective coordination. Eventually, they were forced to abandon Mezoa's orbit and retreat towards the system's outer reaches. Several Loyalist vessels attempted desperate final approaches to deploy reinforcements onto the planet, but only three of the six ships involved survived. With the defensive fleet driven away, the Iron Warriors and Alpha Legion were free to position their troop transports and heavy cruisers above Mezoa, systematically destroying its orbital fortifications and defence satellites. The final invasion of the Forge World had begun.

As the Iron Warriors and Alpha Legion prepared their invasion of Mezoa, Cassian Dracos, whose Dreadnought body had partially regenerated through Vulkan's extraordinary craftsmanship, entered the planet's central forge alongside his Disciples of the Flames. There he met the Norn-regents, three ancient magos-sisters whose interconnected minds governed the Forge World as a single intelligence. Even their formidable Domitar automata withdrew at his approach. Dracos recounted his suffering beneath Isstvan V and his visions concerning Vulkan, finding unexpected common ground with Mezoa's religious beliefs. The Norn-regents proclaimed him a living avatar of the Machine God, granted him a high position within their defensive forces, and interpreted his arrival as a sign of approaching victory. The remarkable influence Dracos exerted over both machines and people later led scholars to speculate that his prolonged entombment within Isstvan's Warp disturbances had altered him in ways not yet understood.

While Mezoa's finest artificers took Dracos into the planet's hidden forges to restore his damaged Dreadnought chassis, Xiaphas Jurr assumed command of the Disciples of the Flames in the coming defence. The Loyalist garrison included the battle-hardened Taghmata Mezoa, the 891st Lethe Cohort of the Solar Auxilia, Knights of House Hermetika, and 48 Imperial Fists stranded after the fighting at Manachea. Together with combat automata and adsecularis troops, approximately 12,000 defenders prepared to resist the overwhelming Traitor invasion as enemy transports filled the skies above Mezoa.

The third invasion of Mezoa began with Autilon Skorr sacrificing thousands of Moabite conscripts and renegade Imperial Army troops in an expendable first wave. The defenders responded by using immense subduction furnaces to launch magma and bedrock into orbit, destroying most of the approaching transports. Those who reached the surface were slaughtered by Vorax battle-automata. Anticipating that Mezoa could not sustain such bombardment without destabilising its own crust, Skorr committed the Iron Warriors' 114th Grand Battalion under Nârik Dreygur as the second wave. Once they landed, Skorr threatened to destroy any vessel attempting evacuation, effectively forcing the Iron Warriors to fight to the death. Despite fierce Loyalist resistance, Dreygur's warriors established a beachhead using their transports as improvised fortifications. During the first day's fighting, Dreygur personally killed Imperial Fists champion Valtus Moran, whose body was subsequently torn apart by battle-automata under his command.

With the landing zone secured, the Traitors deployed their main army beneath a protective network of void shields generated by Sokar-class Stormbirds. The Iron Warriors bore the brunt of attacks against Mezoa's strongest defences, suffering mounting casualties while battling Mechanicum automata. Meanwhile, the Alpha Legion conducted precision raids against vulnerable command centres and isolated officers, exploiting the distractions created by their allies. The Loyalists responded with aggressive counterattacks: Xiaphas Jurr's Disciples of the Flames operated in demi-companies of fifty, supported by the surviving Imperial Fists, while Solar Auxilia and siege-automata defended the forge-fanes. Mezoan tech-adepts deliberately collapsed sections of the planetary crust, burying Traitor formations beneath magma and isolating enemy detachments for destruction. Suicide units of automata and adsecularis also lured attackers into areas targeted by devastating volcanic bombardments, regardless of friendly casualties. Despite these successes, the defenders' meticulous casualty calculations revealed that their smaller army was losing the war of attrition, and Mezoa's eventual defeat appeared increasingly likely.

For nine days, Autilon Skorr directed an unrelenting siege against Mezoa, combining orbital bombardment with devastating ground assaults. The defenders retaliated by launching molten rock from the planet's mantle, turning entire battlefields into wastelands of ash, steam, and magma. Although Xiaphas Jurr and the Disciples of the Flames watched Mezoa's fortifications gradually collapse, the Norn-regents refused to surrender, ordering their forces to fight until the last automaton was destroyed. Recognising that a prolonged siege would delay his ambitions of joining Horus' advance on Terra, Skorr devised a decisive assault intended to destroy Mezoa's leadership. He ordered Nârik Dreygur and the depleted Iron Warriors 114th Grand Battalion to attack the heavily fortified Tertial-05 bastion, promising Alpha Legion support. The Iron Warriors advanced through murderous fire and drew substantial Loyalist reinforcements into the battle, but eventually became trapped in a devastating crossfire. Dreygur fortified his captured positions and repeatedly requested the promised reinforcements, receiving no response.

Skorr had deliberately used the Iron Warriors as a diversion while his relatively untouched Alpha Legion headhunters infiltrated Mezoa's central forge-spires. With most defenders committed to Tertial-05, the infiltrators overwhelmed the remaining automata and advanced towards the chambers of the Norn-regents. Approximately fifty Disciples of the Flames were guarding Cassian Dracos, who remained sealed within the fabrication vaults, and had been ordered not to abandon their posts. Nevertheless, Raven Guard Legionary Kirhane led a small contingent to defend the Norn-regents alongside the surviving Domitar automata. Despite their resistance, Skorr and twenty of his personal guards penetrated the chamber and killed one of the three interconnected magos-sisters using lascutters and meltaguns. The destruction of a single Norn-regent crippled Mezoa's command network, causing battle-automata to collapse or attack one another while Skitarii and Thallax formations lost central direction. Traitor forces surged forward across the planet, and only at Tertial-05 did the Solar Auxilia and Disciples of the Flames continue holding the Iron Warriors at bay. With its leadership shattered and defences disintegrating, Mezoa appeared on the verge of defeat.

Cassian Dracos emerged from Mezoa's fabrication vaults with his Dreadnought chassis fully restored, but instead of confronting Autilon Skorr and the Alpha Legion attacking the central forge-spires, he led the remaining Disciples of the Flames towards Tertial-05. As he advanced, the malfunctioning battle-automata encountered along his path inexplicably recovered from their madness and submitted to his command. By the time he reached the Iron Warriors' fortified positions, Dracos had gathered nearly 1,000 automata under his control. Rather than attacking, he approached the enemy alone, passing unharmed through Iron Warriors automata that refused orders to engage him, and demanded an audience with Nârik Dreygur.

The conversation between Dracos and Dreygur remains largely unknown, although the Prophecies of the Flames suggests that Dracos possessed an extraordinary influence over technology, including enemy war machines and warriors altered by mechanical augmentation. Following their brief exchange, Dreygur addressed his Iron Warriors and apparently underwent a profound change in allegiance or purpose. His private journals ended that day, and he subsequently avoided all company except that of Dracos and his automata. The precise words that persuaded him remain unknown.

'Brothers, once more an oath to us stands broken. The Alpha Legion has abandoned us once again. Now we shall show that we do not renege on our vows. The dead of Stranivar and Mezoa both call for vengeance, and we shall grant it to them?’

Autilon Skorr's apparent victory at Mezoa collapsed when Cassian Dracos returned to battle alongside nearly 1,000 automata and the remnants of Nârik Dreygur's Iron Warriors, who had unexpectedly turned against the Alpha Legion. Trapped between the reactivated Mezoan defenders and advancing Loyalist forces, Skorr found his army surrounded while Solar Auxilia armoured columns attacked the Traitor landing zones. Meanwhile, Loyalist warships emerged from hiding among the outer moons and ambushed the dispersed Traitor fleet. Within hours, most of the invasion force was destroyed, with fewer than 3,000 Traitors escaping the planet. Skorr himself barely survived, suffering a shattered arm during an encounter with Xiaphas Jurr. Despite initially retaining orbital superiority, the remaining Traitor vessels were eventually driven from the system by Loyalist raids and Mezoa's volcanic bombardments.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Alpha Legion remained one of the most mysterious forces of the Heresy. Following Chondax, elements of the XXth Legion specialised in hunting Loyalist Shattered Legions through espionage, infiltration, and disinformation, undermining isolated Loyalist forces without relying primarily on direct confrontation.

The Alpha Legion hunted Shattered Legions warbands after Chondax and fielded independent hunter-killer formations, including those operating against the Dark Brotherhood in the Pale Stars.

### Pale Stars Campaign [008–010.M31]

During the Pale Stars campaign of 008–010.M31, Alpha Legion Strike Master Ijax attempted to conquer the region for Horus but encountered fierce resistance from the Dark Brotherhood, an independent Blackshield warband. Its mysterious leader, the Nemean, was rumoured to be a Terran veteran of the Dark Angels who had fought in the Third Rangdan Xenocide. The Brotherhood rejected both Horus and the Emperor, conducting raids against Alpha Legion fleets, stealing supplies, and establishing its own dominion. Their conflict devastated several systems, while Ijax deliberately sacrificed local forces to lure the Blackshields into ambushes.

The campaign also attracted the attention of Malcador's Knights-Errant. Nathaniel Garro, formerly of the Death Guard, travelled to the Pale Stars alongside Zaven of the Emperor's Children and Rubio of the Ultramarines. Their mission was to contact the Nemean and potentially recruit his Blackshields into Malcador's service. At the Conclave of Optera, they encountered representatives of other isolated Legions, including Blood Angels survivors stranded far from their Primarch.

At the Conclave of Optera, Nathaniel Garro and his fellow Knights-Errant, Rubio and Zaven, attempted to recruit the Nemean, mysterious leader of the Dark Brotherhood Blackshields, into Malcador's service. The gathering also included Captain Serron of the Blood Angels' 121st Company, whose warriors had been scattered by Warp storms while travelling to Nartaba, and Nirun, a White Scars commander leading approximately 500 Legionaries. Garro revealed the truth of Horus' betrayal at Isstvan III and invited all three leaders to join the Knights-Errant. Serron refused, determined to reunite with his Legion, while Nirun remained committed to Jaghatai Khan's secret orders. The Nemean appeared willing to consider Garro's offer.

Before negotiations could conclude, Alpha Legion forces under Strike Master Ijax launched a surprise assault on Optera. The resulting battle scattered the assembled Astartes, forcing Garro, Rubio, and Zaven to escape by teleportation. Serron's Blood Angels eventually escaped into the Warp towards Segmentum Solar, with rumours placing survivors at the Siege of Terra. Nirun's White Scars fought the Alpha Legion at Phargos Rex before disappearing into a violent Warp storm, their fate unknown. The Nemean survived and apparently joined Malcador's Knights-Errant, with later accounts suggesting that he fought during Terra's final defence.

# The Thramas Crusade

## The Thramas Crusade

The Horus Heresy · The opening war for the Eastern Fringe

## The Strategic Importance of Thramas

By 007.M31, the rebellion of Horus Lupercal had engulfed much of the young Imperium's core sectors. The Eye of Horus flew across its northern reaches, Ultramar had been devastated, and the forces loyal to the Emperor had been scattered. Yet the efforts of loyalist warriors had alerted Terra to the danger, allowing Rogal Dorn to marshal the forces still under his command in defence of the approaches to the Imperial throne. Horus no longer had a swift route to victory. Instead, he faced a prolonged war of attrition that would demand enormous reserves of manpower, armaments and industrial resources. Recognising the importance of the eastern sectors to this struggle, the Warmaster committed the vast majority of the VIII Legion and its supporting troops to their conquest, granting the Night Lords virtually unrestricted authority over the populations they were to subjugate.

The principal objective was the capture of the three closely linked Forge Worlds of Triplex: Galatia, Phall and Thule. Their manufactoria could provide sufficient munitions and engines of war to overwhelm Dorn's defences around Terra through sheer weight of numbers. The secondary objective was Thramas and its heavily populated neighbouring worlds, whose inhabitants could be forcibly conscripted into the armies of the Warmaster. Lesser forges and frontier colonies were of comparatively little strategic importance, serving instead as spoils for the Night Lords and outlets for their cruelty. Horus cared little for the fate of these populations so long as their resources flowed into his war effort. Far more than a single campaign or battlefield, the conflict ultimately encompassed four incorporated Imperial sectors, dozens of worlds and outlying frontier territories, involving millions of warriors and leaving destruction that the chronicle describes as exceeding the infamous massacres of the Heresy's opening years. The loyalist Legions were initially too scattered, weakened or otherwise occupied to intervene effectively. Nevertheless, news of the rebellion had already reached the Lion and his army, whose eventual arrival promised to alter the course of the war.

## The Path of Tears and the Opening Conquest

Following the atrocities at Isstvan, the Night Lords returned to their former domains: the ruins of Nostramo and the hive worlds of Cairn and Kruun. These populations had long endured the rule of the VIII Legion, under which the Night Haunter's harsh laws had gradually deteriorated into a system of honour killings and corruption. They surrendered their sons, daughters and resources to the Warmaster out of fear rather than loyalty. However, these systems had already been largely exhausted. Having replenished their ranks of thralls and their fleets' munition stores, the Night Lords advanced towards Thramas along a chain of inhabited worlds known as the Tithe Road, or Path of Tears. During the Great Crusade, the Legion had regularly exacted tribute from these worlds in exchange for protection, taking recruits from those who survived its brutal initiation trials.

The first world on this route was Tsagualsa, which had paid tribute to the VIII Legion for almost a century. In the closing months of 007.M31, the Night Lords returned under the terms of Horus' Dark Compliance, demanding every inhabitant capable of bearing arms or serving as a thrall, together with the world's meagre resources. Unwilling to sacrifice their homes and families for the Warmaster's distant war, the settlers refused. Their few militia regiments were powerless against the Legion, and Tsagualsa was devastated. A few hundred survivors were permitted to escape aboard decrepit ships so that they might spread news of the consequences of resistance. Upon the ruins of its cities, the Night Lords established the foundations of a new citadel to replace their former holdings, signalling their intention to become permanent overlords of the Eastern Fringe. Independent detachments, sometimes comprising little more than a cruiser and several companies, spread along the Path of Tears and imposed the same demands upon isolated worlds. Within months, refugee vessels crowded the borders of the Thramas sector and the Gulgorahd Protectorate.

## The Madness of Konrad Curze and the Fracturing of the VIII Legion

By the outbreak of the Heresy, Konrad Curze had already destroyed Nostramo, suffered incarceration on Terra and assaulted his brother Rogal Dorn. The chronicle traces his tragedy to the flawed gift of foresight inherited from the Emperor. Whereas the Emperor could perceive multiple possible futures and navigate between them, Curze saw only a single course of events leading towards failure and death. Neither his achievements during the Great Crusade nor his escape from the horrors of his childhood could alter these visions. By the Crusade's final years he stood on the brink of insanity. His motives for joining Horus remain uncertain: he may have sought to escape the fate he foresaw, or to drown his visions in bloodshed. At the beginning of the Thramas campaign, he was a diminished version of his former self, increasingly neglecting his responsibilities and allowing individual warlords to consolidate their own power.

Originally, the terror employed by the VIII Legion had served a calculated military purpose, breaking the enemy's will before large-scale warfare became necessary. As recruits drawn from the criminal underclasses of Nostramo and neighbouring worlds came to dominate its ranks, however, terror increasingly became an end in itself. The Legion's strict hierarchy gave way to a loose association of warbands and gangs. Two informal factions emerged: warriors who remembered the Legion's former purpose and wished to preserve both it and its Primarch, and those who sought freedom to indulge their brutality without the Night Haunter's interference. The campaign thus became a struggle for the identity of the Legion as well as for control of the Eastern Fringe. Among the leading figures of the latter faction was Nakrid Thole, a charismatic Nostraman warlord and outspoken critic of Curze who commanded a substantial following among the newer recruits.

Thole's warriors led the devastation of the Thramas border worlds. These territories were defended by the Thramassi Nightwatch, regiments of the Solar Auxilia accustomed to guarding the Imperium's distant frontier. Fearing that resistance would bring destruction upon their populations, many garrisons abandoned their bases and withdrew to Thramas, leaving planetary militia and governors to fend for themselves. Some accepted the Warmaster's authority, while others concealed their equipment and disappeared among the civilian population. Within a few months, the Night Lords controlled almost half the sector, from Tsagualsa in the galactic north to the strategic crossroads world of Crucible. On Serk, the governor's refusal to deface monuments honouring loyal Legions provoked a massacre, during which Thole desecrated a statue of Dorn. Curze himself descended upon the hive world of Makator V to exact a bloody tithe from its wealthy merchants, in a grim repetition of his youth on Nostramo that failed to relieve the visions tormenting him.

## Triplex: The Hidden War and the Traitor Victory

While Thole's followers ravaged the frontier, those who still remembered their duty advanced upon Triplex, the key to Horus' strategic ambitions. The system's military strength was sufficient to resist almost anything short of the entire VIII Legion, and an assault on that scale threatened the very industry the Warmaster wished to capture. A force of four full Chapters entered the system under Sevatar, lord of the Atramentar, and several veteran commanders, many of Terran origin. They discovered that Triplex had already been torn apart by its own civil war. Its outward unity concealed a longstanding rivalry between the three forges. Phall had prospered through the Great Crusade, employing its portion of Legio Victorum at Horus' behest; Galatia accepted a subordinate alliance with its wealthier sibling; and Thule, loyal to the Emperor, had attempted to undermine Phall through covert sabotage and by courting its neighbours. Horus' rebellion enabled Phall to abandon intrigue and unleash its military superiority. Thule's allies deserted it, Galatia ignored its destruction, and by the time the Night Lords arrived the conflict was almost over.

The last stronghold of the magi of Thule lay on Nehren, the outermost world of the system. Its underground fortress was protected by the surviving maniples of Legio Victorum III, the Foe Breakers, as well as orbital defence batteries, automata and thralls of the fallen forge. The Titan landers of the traitor Legio Victorum I and II could not safely deploy while the planetary defences remained operational. Sevatar led the Terminators of the Atramentar in a teleport assault into the fortress, disabling its principal defence cannon. The traitor Titans were then free to engage the remaining Foe Breakers, who fought against forces five times their own strength. Within the fortress, Sevatar personally overcame the automata guarding the Archmagos of Thule and captured him alive, declining any reward. With Triplex secured, the archmagi of Galatia and Phall formally allied themselves with the Warmaster and the Night Lords, offering their armies and the productive capacity of their manufactoria.

Curze arrived in Triplex after the victory, having entrusted the conquest of Thramas itself to Nakrid Thole. Like Sevatar, he rejected the archmagi's rewards and flattery, but claimed the captive Archmagos of Thule as a living trophy aboard his flagship, Nightfall. Although a new fortress was under construction on Tsagualsa, Triplex would serve as the base of the Primarch and the core of the VIII Legion for much of the campaign. Curze spent much of his time secluded in his private sanctum, leaving individual warlords to direct the Legion's operations. At this stage the traitors appeared to have gained an overwhelming advantage, encountering little organised resistance beyond scattered uprisings.

## The Collapse of Command and the Sheol Incident

Nakrid Thole and his allies expected the remaining worlds, terrified by the fate of their neighbours and burdened by refugees, to surrender without significant resistance. A loose alliance of Chapters moved against the Gulgorahd Protectorate, while Thole claimed the right to receive the surrender of Thramas himself. With Curze increasingly absent from command, the Legion's warlords pursued personal territories and spoils rather than the common objectives of Horus. On the young hive world of Sheol III, two newly founded Chapters, the Dark Eyes and the Boneless, came into conflict. Both were composed almost entirely of the final intake of Nostraman recruits, were understrength and lacked heavy armour, making the world's population and industrial capacity attractive prizes. Their praetors attempted to settle their competing claims, but the dispute erupted into a savage internecine war. The Dark Eyes destroyed most of their rivals; the surviving Boneless were forced to adopt the victors' colours and join their ranks. The incident demonstrated how far the VIII Legion had deteriorated without a unifying leader or even a common enemy.

## Gulgorahd: Surrender Is Death

Gulgorahd was a minor Forge World on the edge of Imperial space that had survived the Long Night through determination, sacrifice and resilience. When Imperial fleets returned to the Eastern Fringe, its magi expected renewed prosperity, only to find that both Terra and Mars favoured the more powerful Triplex. Situated closer to the turbulent warp storms of the Ghoul Stars, Gulgorahd regarded Triplex's prosperity as having been purchased through its own hardships and sacrifices. A one-sided rivalry endured for roughly a century: Triplex scarcely acknowledged its lesser neighbour, while Gulgorahd's resentment deepened. Assuming the smaller forge would follow Triplex into submission, the Night Lords underestimated both its concealed military strength and its implacable hatred of their new allies.

When the Night Lords demanded an answer from the fortress world of Bastion-019, long-dormant automata and Knight forces were mobilised and its ancient weapons opened fire. The Night Lords cruiser Callow Flame was destroyed. Gulgorahd's resistance was motivated less by devotion to the Emperor than by hatred of anyone allied with Triplex. A battered frigate subsequently reached the Thramas system and broadcast the forge's declaration: war ended in victory or death, and surrender merely prolonged the inevitable. Gulgorahd would never submit. This message became a turning point in the defence of Thramas.

## The Uprising of Thramas

Thole entered the Thramas system with little more than a single Chapter and a squadron of three cruisers, having delayed his arrival to raid several smaller worlds. Thramas itself was a major staging point for Great Crusade expeditions bound for the eastern limits of the galaxy. Its orbital defences could challenge a conventional fleet, several small expeditionary fleets waited at its dry-docks and muster stations, and its hive cities were protected by extensive ferrocrete fortifications, void shields and numerous Imperial Army regiments. Yet fear had paralysed the world. Refugees carried accounts of neighbouring planets' destruction, Terra remained silent, and Horus' agents had spread news of the loyalist massacre at Isstvan. Mayvin Khelen, Regent of Thramas, weighed the demands of loyalty against the lives of her people. It appeared that even Thole's small fleet could secure the planet's surrender without a battle.

Gulgorahd's declaration transformed the defenders' resolve. When Thole entered the Regent's palace and demanded unconditional surrender in the name of Horus, Curze and the Night Lords, Khelen refused. Arcturus Morhde, Captain-general of the Nightwatch and a Terran veteran of the Great Crusade, grievously wounded the praetor, while the Midnight Host, the elite of the Nightwatch, attacked his honour guard. The surviving Night Lords fought their way out of the palace carrying their crippled commander. An uprising spread across Thramas: loyal regiments attacked those that supported Horus, while civilians and refugees took up arms. At the spaceport, the Antikaan Hussars, whose banners bore the insignia of the Imperial Fists with whom they had once fought, overwhelmed Thole's remaining detachment. His surviving warriors reached their cruiser, but the planetary defences and expeditionary fleets had already joined the conflict. Some vessels, including the ancient Visigoth-class battlecruiser Crimson Tyrant, supported the traitors; most rallied to the Regent. Unable to contest orbital supremacy, the Night Lords launched phage warheads at the planet and fled, abandoning their former allies.

## Total War and the Return of the Night Haunter

News of the uprising spread throughout the Eastern Fringe. Concealed Nightwatch soldiers emerged as guerrillas and saboteurs, attacking supply installations and isolated garrisons, while the armies of Thramas and Gulgorahd went on the offensive. The scattered Night Lords warbands were initially caught off guard, but retaliated with brutal reprisals and drew their enemies into urban warfare, where the superior numbers of the Nightwatch were offset by the skill of the Legiones Astartes. The conflict engulfed the sector, halting trade and bringing famine and disease even to worlds spared direct combat. The loyalists failed to capitalise on their initial advantage and became bogged down defending the few worlds they had secured. Qetesh Prime and Crucible became battlefields of relentless attrition; Thramas endured repeated raids ordered by the disfigured Nakrid Thole; and Gulgorahd, caught between the Night Lords and traitor worlds of the Aegis sector, struggled to hold its frontier. Even after the surface of Bastion-019 was scorched and irradiated, its Knights and automata continued fighting from subterranean fortifications.

For a time, the loyalists hoped that by holding their worlds they could deny Horus the resources of the Eastern Fringe and force the enemy into an exhausting stalemate. The return of Curze, accompanied by the armies of Triplex and the most loyal elements of his Legion, shattered this balance. Sheol III and Yaelis fell within a week. The defenders of Sheol destroyed their central hive spire rather than surrender. On Yaelis, survivors of a dozen decimated Auxilia regiments withdrew into the manufactoria of the lower hive, where Curze and his forces eliminated the remaining resistance. Bastions 011 and 009 fell in some of the largest Titan engagements of the campaign: the traitor Legio Victorum I and II and Legio Phasma, long bound to the Night Lords, opposed the bulk of Gulgorahd's Legio Adamantus and elements of the loyal Legios Atrox and Saevus. Nearly a hundred god-engines fell in a month of fighting. Legio Adamantus lived up to its name, the Unbroken, as many maniples fought to the last rather than retreat.

At this point in the chronicle, the loyalist cause stood on the brink of destruction. Curze's intervention had restored the traitors' decisive superiority, and defeat appeared to be a matter of time. Yet the introduction foreshadows the arrival of the Lion's army: if Thramas could endure long enough, the balance of the war might be reversed. The supplied excerpt ends before that intervention and does not describe the subsequent stages of the campaign.

## Midnight in the Kingdom of Ashes

By the middle of 008.M31, the war for the Thramas sector and the Gulgorahd Protectorate appeared almost won by the Traitors. With Konrad Curze personally involved in the campaign, the prospect of surrender no longer offered the defenders any hope of survival. The Night Haunter had abandoned Horus' intention to acquire the sector intact and allowed the most depraved elements of the VIII Legion to pursue a war of annihilation. On Kenrac, regiments of the Imperialis Militia killed their own people rather than leave them to the Night Lords, before seeking death in a hopeless assault. On Crucible, the strategic crossroads leading into the heart of the sector, Captain-general Morhde's eight Solar Auxilia regiments were reduced to a fraction of their strength. Unable to withdraw, he armed the underhive gangs and conscripted every capable citizen, placing Nightwatch veterans among them to maintain discipline. Bastion-019, meanwhile, had endured almost six months of siege: a few hundred tech-thralls and a handful of Knights continued to resist from its underground tunnels. Though the devastated world no longer threatened the Traitor fleets, the Night Lords refused to leave its defenders alive. Across the warzone, both sides abandoned negotiations, while the VIII Legion repeatedly sacrificed opportunities for rapid conquest in favour of prolonged terror and destruction.

Triplex remained the industrial engine of Horus' eastern campaign. The manufactoria of Phall and Galatia produced cohorts of automata, new Titans, munitions and war engines on an immense scale, drawing upon the wealth extracted from Thramas. Some of this output was committed locally, but much was reserved for the Warmaster and transported towards the distant fronts at Paramar and Beta-Garmon. Amid the traffic between the forges and the system's Mandeville points, the arrival of a new formation initially escaped the attention of the Mechanicum's sentinel barges. These vessels were neither transports nor familiar Night Lords warships, but ancient black-clad relics dating back to the Golden Age of Mankind, each marked with a winged sword. The First Legion had returned to the Imperium.

## The Diamat Incursion and the Lion's Return

Horus had dispatched the Dark Angels to conquer the distant Shield Worlds, an enclave of humanity in the darkness between galaxies associated with an unknown xenos species. This assignment kept Lion El'Jonson away from the Imperium during the opening stages of the rebellion. When reports of unrest reached him, the Lion detached fifteen of his fastest capital ships and a force of veteran warriors, making for Tanagra. Its position at the junction of stable Warp corridors offered routes into Imperial space, while the Forge World of Diamat promised supplies and intelligence. Instead, the Dark Angels found that Diamat had already sworn allegiance to Horus. After pacifying the forge, the Lion discovered records confirming that the Warmaster was directing a rebellion long in preparation: within Diamat's vaults lay enormous Ordinatus engines of an unfamiliar design, incorporating technology forbidden by Mars and secretly commissioned by Horus some fifty years earlier.

Perturabo met the Lion in the ruins of Diamat, pretending that the Iron Warriors remained loyal. He informed his brother of the Isstvan massacres and Curze's offensive in the Eastern Fringe, but withheld the full extent of the betrayal. Acting on Horus' instructions, Perturabo steered the First Legion towards the Night Lords, ensuring that it remained occupied far from Terra. The Lion also entrusted the recovered weapons of Diamat to the Iron Warriors, through whom they would ultimately reach the Warmaster. Departing for Triplex, El'Jonson summoned the remaining elements of his Legion from beyond the Imperium's borders, intending to reach the Forge Worlds before Curze.

The black warships entered Triplex and destroyed the Mechanicum defence vessels and transports bearing the Eye of Horus with weapons of extraordinary range. A Night Lords squadron of heavy cruisers and frigates recognised the heraldry of the newcomers and engaged with unusual hesitation. The leading cruiser, Shadow of Justice, was crippled, and the rest of the squadron withdrew. Dreadwing warriors boarded the vessel and exterminated its defenders with phosphex and bio-phages, preserving the bridge for the Lion himself. El'Jonson overcame the cruiser’s praetor, who had sacrificed his own warriors in an unsuccessful attempt to strike the Primarch from behind. Rather than kill him, the Lion took him aboard the Invincible Reason for interrogation, seeking intelligence on the actions of Horus and Curze. His return marked the end of the First Legion's enforced absence from the civil war.

## The Fall of Triplex

The initial Dark Angels formation consisted of eighteen capital ships and no more than one hundred frigates and lesser vessels. Around Galatia, the Mechanicum assembled over one hundred gunships and defence barges, the remnants of the Night Lords squadron and vessels recently completed in its orbital shipyards. The defenders hesitated to attack while the Lion's interrogators extracted intelligence from their captive and additional First Legion formations arrived from the Warp. Nearly one hundred Dark Angels capital ships ultimately converged on the system. Despite being outnumbered approximately two to one, they shattered the assembled Traitor fleet with their superior relic weaponry. The Night Lords abandoned Galatia's defence and concentrated their efforts on crippling the battleship Undying Will, losing two ships in the process. Three surviving cruisers and their escorts broke away into the Warp, leaving the Mechanicum vessels to be destroyed. Within hours, the First Legion controlled Galatia's orbit.

The Magi of Galatia relied upon their surface fortifications, believing the valuable industrial infrastructure would deter indiscriminate orbital bombardment and that they could withstand a siege until reinforcements arrived from Phall. The Lion instead committed the Deathwing to a direct assault upon the principal forge-fane. Many landing craft and hundreds of veterans were lost to the volkite and photon defences, but the surviving Deathwing established a beachhead against the Galatian Thallax and tech-thrall custodian regiments. The second wave, led by the Dreadwing and Eskaton Marduk Sedras of the Order of the Shattered Sceptre, employed Interemptors and Terminator-armoured Naufragia to devastate the sacred forge halls and force the Mechanicum to commit its reserves. Galatia and Phall had long conducted secret research into forbidden technology and dark theorems. Faced with destruction, the Galatian Magi unleashed towering automata armed with reality-distorting weapons and raw Warp energy, alongside swarms of Harpax artificia whose collective intelligence increased with their numbers. The Mechanicum's cybertheurgic arts neutralised much of the Dark Angels' heavy armour and forced the Dreadwing onto the defensive.

## The Ikaros Contingency and the Excindio

The Mechanicum was indispensable to the Great Crusade, but its independent religious doctrine, technological resources and military power also made it a potentially existential threat to the Emperor's Imperium. To guard against rebellion, the Emperor established secret contingencies, among them the Ikaros Contingency, entrusted solely to Lion El'Jonson and the First Legion. Its first clause authorised the Dark Angels to retain prohibited relics from Old Night, including technologies capable of resisting the Mechanicum's arts. Its second permitted the Legion to undertake pre-emptive military action against Mechanicum elements judged to be acting against the interests of the Imperium. This second authority is recorded as having been invoked eight times in Imperial history; the encounter at Triplex was, at that point, the only openly acknowledged instance. Other details remained sealed in the Dark Angels' vaults, and the Lion would exercise his authority again before the Heresy ended.

On Galatia, El'Jonson invoked the contingency and ordered the release of twelve Excindio from the deepest stasis vaults of the Invincible Reason. Delivered to the surface within a detached section of the ship's hull, these immense humanoid constructs were surviving examples of the silica animus that had once ravaged humanity during the Golden Age. Mutilated and bound to the Lion's service, they carried weapons outlawed by the Emperor himself. Immune to the Mechanicum's cybertheurgy, the Excindio tore through the creations of the fallen Magi, while the Lion sought Galatia's commander. He confronted an immense multi-legged construct guarding the archmagi's sealed salvation-vaults. The precise details of the ensuing confrontation are not recorded, but when the Dark Angels returned to the battlefield at nightfall, El'Jonson stood alone amidst the wreckage and the dormant forms of seven surviving Excindio, their power reserves exhausted. The Dreadwing uncovered the vaults, and Sedras oversaw the destruction of the surviving Galatian leadership and forbidden research. Over the following weeks, the First Legion systematically eliminated the remaining hostile Tech-Priests and automata and demolished the forge's industrial infrastructure, preserving only the orbital shipyards for Imperial use.

Phall, the largest and last intact Forge World of Triplex, had gathered the surviving military assets of the system, but the fate of Galatia divided its Magi. Some fled, while the more orthodox elements overthrew their renegade brethren and offered the Lion unconditional surrender before his fleet arrived. Recognising the strategic value of Phall's industry, El'Jonson accepted their submission rather than destroy another forge. He seized the surviving forbidden research and installed Sedras with a Dreadwing garrison to administer Phall and supply the First Legion's coming campaign. Horus had thus lost the principal industrial prize of his eastern offensive, while the Dark Angels acquired a vital base of their own.

## A Bloody Equilibrium

The Lion now understood that Horus was leading a rebellion and that Curze had been tasked with conquering the Eastern Fringe. Although Terra's defence remained of paramount importance, El'Jonson concluded that it was too late for him to prevent an assault upon the Emperor's throne. If Horus destroyed the rest of the Imperium, Terra itself would become a meaningless prize. The Lion therefore resolved to preserve the Imperium through victory in the east. His assembled forces numbered approximately 70,000 Dark Angels, more than twice that number of support troops and three demi-Legio formations drawn from different Titan Legions. Although outnumbered by the Traitors, he could initially exploit the concentration of his army at Triplex while the enemy remained dispersed across two sectors.

The Lion's first objectives were Sheol III and Yaelis, vital links between the Aegis and Thramas sectors and the transit routes around the Crucible Exclusion Zone. The Night Lords committed their conscripts and slaves to delay the advancing First Legion, then resorted to guerrilla warfare. A few hundred VIII Legion warriors held tens of thousands of their opponents in place, and the conquest of both worlds occupied nearly a month. Yet the black banners of the First Legion inspired surviving Loyalist regiments to emerge from hiding and rally to the Lion. His army advanced cautiously through the surrounding worlds, gathering allies and avoiding overextension. After another month of planetary assaults and counter-insurgency operations, it reached Crucible, where Morhde and the remnants of the Nightwatch still resisted. By then the Dark Angels held a dozen worlds and could advance no further without endangering their gains. Thramas itself remained blockaded, while Gulgorahd continued to fight along its borders. A second front opened through Vestun in the Aegis sector, but the newly arrived First Legion detachments became embroiled with Traitor forces gathering from Memlock and the Heraclid nebula. The war now extended across four sectors, involving tens of millions of combatants in more than one hundred active warzones. Neither Legion could force a breakthrough or safely abandon the front.

Nakrid Thole, known after his humiliation on Thramas as the Faceless Prince, maintained a costly siege of that world with warbands loyal to him personally. His blockade and raids consumed supplies the Night Lords could no longer replace after losing Triplex, while his fixation on revenge prevented him from mounting a decisive conquest. Thole employed plague munitions against the population and increasingly relied upon captured blockade runners for ammunition and resources. Along the wider front, Curze led repeated attacks upon Dark Angels supplies and transports, while Sevatar and Anrek Barbatos struggled to restrain their Primarch's obsession with confronting the Lion. Their efforts redirected his fury into raids that supplied the VIII Legion and weakened its opponents, buying time to seek reinforcements from sources concealed during the Great Crusade.

## The Midnight Treaties and the Predator-Forge

The Midnight Treaties were secret agreements established by Curze with worlds on the fringes of Imperial space. Lacking the industrial patronage enjoyed by some other Legions, the Night Lords had accepted tribute in warriors and machines from populations whose genetic or technological practices placed them outside the Emperor's tolerance. In exchange, Curze protected them from annihilation. These arrangements had sustained the expansion of the VIII Legion during the final years of the Great Crusade, but the recruitment of warriors from such worlds had also contributed to its degradation. Now scout ships were dispatched to summon these hidden allies. Many were lost to Warp storms or intercepted by Loyalist vessels, but enough reached their destinations to bring the Night Lords new armies. Among the reinforcements were the brutish abhumans of Tohurk, cannibal legions from Glabro and battle-psykers from the lost moons of Thex.

The most dangerous of these allies was Ulan Hûda, a mobile predator-forge concealed within the Warp storm at the galaxy's edge. Its Magi had developed engines capable of moving the entire Forge World through the Warp and used their monstrous automata to dismantle and consume other worlds for industrial resources. Ulan Hûda had secretly supplied the Night Lords with Titans of the Legio Phasma in return for their silence. Reports of destroyed frontier colonies began reaching Thramas, but its defenders were too heavily committed at Crucible and their own world to intervene. The Faceless Prince eventually offered his Thramassi captives as thralls to the predator-forge in exchange for its direct intervention. By then Thramas had endured nearly two years of siege, and Thole controlled every other moon and asteroid in the system, but lacked the strength to capture the principal world. Answering his summons, Ulan Hûda entered the system, began consuming its outermost planetary bodies and turned its attention towards Thramas itself. Only a few Loyalist ships escaped to carry warning of the approaching catastrophe.

## The Brotherhood of the Shattered Shield

The final major addition to Curze's forces came from an unexpected quarter: the Brotherhood of the Shattered Shield, a White Scars detachment under Zhenjin Khan. Having been campaigning in distant regions when the Heresy began, the Brotherhood arrived at Nostramo seeking supplies and repairs, only to discover the ruined world and receive no response from nearby Imperial stations. The Night Lords received them as guests and claimed that Horus had ordered the pacification of the Eastern Fringe against rebellious forces. Knowing the First Legion largely through tales of its pride and secrecy, and remembering Horus as a patron and friend of the V Legion, Zhenjin Khan accepted their account. In gratitude for the supplies provided, he joined Curze with approximately 3,000 Great Crusade veterans and six capital warships. The VIII Legion thereby gained sufficient strength to challenge the Dark Angels in open war.

Meanwhile the Lion's attempts to contact the former Great Crusade bases at Tigrus and Honourum had failed, and the growing Ruinstorm obstructed communications with his loyal brothers. The First Legion could neither reach Thramas nor withdraw without abandoning the liberated worlds. Curze launched preliminary raids using expendable slave soldiers and abhuman auxiliaries to probe the Dark Angels' defences and deplete their resources. On Yrrdek and Chenro IX, conscripts were sacrificed to reveal defensive positions; on Sedricce, the Thunguard volunteers survived their failed attack only through the intervention of the White Scars, who also helped evacuate surviving Loyalists. The Night Lords mockingly named Zhenjin Khan the Saviour, an insult understood by the White Scars, who were increasingly disturbed by their allies' contempt for life and the honour shown by their supposed enemies.

## A Gathering of Malice

With preparations complete, Curze summoned the scattered formations of the VIII Legion. Approximately 100,000 Night Lords assembled at fifty staging points along the front, while their warlords gathered at Nergante. The Night Haunter cared principally for the prospect of confronting the Lion, leaving Sevatar and Barbatos to devise the campaign's strategy. Rivalries within the Legion were settled according to the violent traditions of Nostramo, further unsettling the White Scars. Thole alone refused the summons, retaining his personal followers at Thramas and pursuing his private revenge through Ulan Hûda.

In the closing days of 008.M31, the Night Lords launched coordinated attacks against six worlds. Three Chapters struck Chenro and overwhelmed the single defending Chapter of Dark Angels. At Sedricce, the battleship Tenebrous Will and its escorts drove off the Loyalist ships and devastated the surface. The Night Lords praetor killed the wounded Dark Angels commander in single combat, claiming his Calibanite warblade and the survivors of his force as trophies. Zhenjin Khan intervened, fighting a duel to secure the prisoners' lives and the fallen commander's sword, and brought them aboard his own ships. His conduct earned him no respect among the Night Lords, but his subsequent withdrawal from their fighting and increasing contact with his captives foreshadowed a reversal of allegiance. Four worlds fell, but the VIII Legion made no effort to consolidate them, pressing instead towards Crucible, the key to the First Legion's supply line from Triplex.

## The Battle of Crucible

The Dark Angels had concentrated substantial strength upon the devastated world of Crucible. Two Gloriana-class battleships, twenty-four cruisers and numerous frigates defended its orbit. On the surface, nine rebuilt Nightwatch regiments, Ordinatus weapons from Triplex and four full Chapters of the First Legion occupied prepared positions around the ruins of the fallen hive, supported by Ravenwing reserves. Against them came six Chapters of Night Lords, a sub-Legio deployment of the renegade Legio Victorum II and approximately 50,000 conscripts, abhumans and other auxiliaries, led by Curze himself. The White Scars initially remained apart from the fighting in orbit. Unable to destroy the defending fleet swiftly, the VIII Legion committed its ground forces while the space battle continued. The first wave of expendable troops absorbed much of the Loyalists' fire and ammunition, allowing the bulk of the Night Lords and their Titans to descend. Curze abandoned the coordinated plan devised at Nergante and ranged across the battlefield in search of the Lion, followed by Sevatar and his Atramentar. Though he broke through the defensive lines repeatedly, the Dark Angels' reserves sealed the breaches. The Titans of Legio Victorum II and Night Lords heavy assault formations pressed the defenders back, meeting the Deathwing's stubborn resistance, the disciplined withdrawals of the First Legion's line infantry and the fire of the Sagittar Ordinatus.

As the surviving Dark Angels fell back towards the central pillar of the ruined hive, the Night Lords commanders began quarrelling over their anticipated spoils. Their forces lost cohesion at the moment of apparent victory. The Lion then released his seven remaining Excindio, directing them specifically against Curze. They could not defeat the Night Haunter, but held him away from the main battle long enough for El'Jonson to emerge at the head of the bone-white Deathwing Companions and strike the disorganised VIII Legion leadership. The Dark Angels committed themselves to the counter-offensive, leaving the Nightwatch to hold the fortifications. Despite the Lion's intervention, the outcome remained uncertain until the White Scars finally joined the battle. Their ships opened fire upon their former allies in orbit, while the Brotherhood of the Shattered Shield descended alongside the Dark Angels survivors of Sedricce. Zhenjin Khan, wielding the fallen commander's Calibanite blade, led his warriors against the Night Lords. The combined offensive broke the Traitor advance. Sevatar and Barbatos dragged Curze away from the battlefield as he demanded that his brother face him, and the VIII Legion abandoned its auxiliaries and evacuated, preserving the greater part of its own strength at the cost of victory.

In the aftermath, Zhenjin Khan knelt before the Lion and offered both the recovered blade and his life in atonement for his mistaken allegiance. El'Jonson refused to treat the survivors solely according to their former loyalties, declaring that history would judge the dead and that the living must bear responsibility for their choices through the fighting still ahead. The First Legion had held Crucible, the Night Lords had been driven back, and the Brotherhood of the Shattered Shield had joined the Loyalist cause. The struggle for Thramas, however, was not yet concluded.

## When Dreams Become Nightmares

The Horus Heresy · The Thramas Crusade, conclusion

## The Slow March to Victory

The defeat at Crucible reversed the strategic balance of the Thramas Crusade. The Night Lords had previously relied on numerical superiority to offset the exceptional military skill of the First Legion, but the failed offensive deprived them of that advantage. With the initiative now passing to the Loyalists and Konrad Curze unable to intervene, the Dark Angels pursued the defeated Traitors with a severity that the chronicler presents as retribution for Isstvan V. Many Night Lords who failed to reach their evacuation craft scattered into the wilderness of Crucible, delaying the Loyalists by several days. Elsewhere along the front, the loss of reinforcements committed to Crucible left the VIII Legion exposed to Dark Angels counter-attacks. Its warlords increasingly abandoned engagements in which victory or plunder seemed unlikely. On Yrrdek, for example, the arrival of three additional Dark Angels companies prompted a withdrawal under cover of detonated Loyalist minefields. Such retreats, however dishonourable they might appear, preserved much of the remaining combat strength of the Legion and prevented the defeat from becoming an immediate collapse.

One account of the battle estimates that six reinforced Chapters of the Night Lords, comprising perhaps 12,000 Legionaries, fought on Crucible alongside 50,000 auxilia and 18 Titans identified there as belonging to Legio Victorum I, the Foe Slayers. Fewer than 5,000 of the conscripts and abhuman troops escaped the system alive, most of them reserves that had not yet disembarked. None of the deployed Traitor Titans escaped: some were destroyed by Loyalist ordinatus platforms and the rest by Legio Solaria, the Imperial Hunters, whose princeps sustained serious damage in their determination to eliminate their opponents. The Night Lords possessed more effective means of evacuation, including orbital teleportaria for elite formations, yet the Atramentar and Barbatos' retinue suffered particularly severe losses while protecting their Primarch. The defection of the White Scars, losses on other fronts and the arrival of reinforcements from Tigrus further reversed the numerical disparity. Surviving accounts give different figures for the proportion of Legionaries who escaped or were lost; these discrepancies are detailed below.

The Night Lords nevertheless retained sufficient assets to remain dangerous, and Curze escaped the First Legion fleet to his unfinished fortress on Tsagualsa, where he withdrew once more into isolation. The Dark Angels and their allies shifted from defensive operations to a broad offensive. Forces from the Forge World of Tigrus advanced through Verstun into the Aegis sector, compelling the rulers of Heraldor and Memlock to divert their armies and industrial production towards their own defence rather than the wider Traitor war effort. The main Loyalist host pressed deeper into Thramas, while the Night Lords fought only where local conditions favoured them. With Curze secluded, their individual commanders determined their own strategy, whereas the Loyalists remained united under the direction of Lion El'Jonson.

## The Witch of Thramas

The arrival of a badly damaged frigate brought news that Ulan Hûda, the wandering predator-forge summoned by Nakrid Thole, had reached Thramas. The capital remained behind the Traitor lines, defended against the surrounding Night Lords but still beyond the reach of the main Loyalist offensive. The Lion judged that a conventional campaign to relieve it would take too long and risk overstretching his forces. He therefore assembled a selected expedition centred on 1,000 Deathwing veterans drawn from seven companies shattered at Crucible, reinforced by the remaining Crimson Lion Chapter and Praetor Corswain. Zhenjin Khan volunteered the surviving warriors of the White Scars, who had sworn to redeem their Brotherhood of the Shattered Shield through service or death. The black-and-white fleet, led by the battleship Invincible Reason, took a perilous direct passage through the surrounding Warp storms. The Pale Horseman was destroyed by the storms and the Azure Bolt vanished without trace. The remaining ships, most of them damaged, reached Thramas in less than a week.

The situation was already catastrophic. The immense mass of Ulan Hûda was causing devastation on Thramas even before its machinery began to consume the planet. The surviving Thramassi warships and armed chartist vessels battled the ships of Nostramo and the scrap-barques of the predator-forge amid vast clouds of wreckage. The Lion directed the expedition against Ulan Hûda itself rather than allowing the fleet to become tied down in the orbital battle. The artificial moon was protected by kilometres of industrial structures and armoured forge halls, making an ordinary bombardment insufficient to stop it. Corswain assumed command of the fleet while the Lion led his Legionaries and the White Scars onto its surface. There, the Loyalists encountered hordes of flesh-drones and lacrymosan thralls, creatures whose bodies had been combined with forbidden technology. The Dark Angels dispersed into smaller formations to destroy the forge's installations, leaving Zhenjin Khan and the White Scars to protect their means of extraction. Despite the destruction of dozens of enormous cutting-beam emitters, hundreds remained operational and continued building the energy required to tear apart Thramas.

The ruler of the predator-forge, later remembered as the Witch of Thramas, was an ancient Dark Mechanicum magos whose surviving organic remains were contained within a vast mechanical shell. Her neural commands directed innumerable thralls and servant magi, whose losses were of no concern to her provided they exhausted the much smaller Loyalist force. She eventually committed her most powerful constructs, including Errak automata capable of tearing through power armour. The Lion and the Deathwing Companions halted their advance but became pinned in an extended struggle, unable to continue their campaign of sabotage. Meanwhile, Zhenjin Khan's scouts discovered unstable generators deep beneath the surface, guarded by powerful automata. The White Scars relinquished their extraction zones and made a final assault on the underground reactors. Their survivors detonated charges at the central generator cores, setting off a chain of explosions that disrupted the predator-forge's systems. A fragment of the Khan's final transmission survived, but not enough remained to establish his complete last words.

As Ulan Hûda began to disintegrate, failures in the noospheric network disrupted the Witch's control of her forces. The Dark Angels attempted to break through towards an extraction point, but the Witch's armour and immense physical strength made her almost impervious to their champions and specialist weapons. The Lion personally confronted the magos, his swordsmanship overcoming the strength of her war-throne. He severed its upper torso, which crawled away from the wreckage, leaving her ultimate fate unstated. The surviving Loyalists reached their shuttles and gunships before the White Scars' sacrifice culminated in a catastrophic eruption that tore a wound kilometres wide in the moon's surface. Ulan Hûda escaped into the Warp, grievously damaged, while the warriors of the Brotherhood who had remained below were consumed by the destruction. Thramas had been saved from immediate annihilation, but much of its population and infrastructure had already been destroyed by the predator-forge and the fighting. The few surviving hive spires offered limited shelter from the ruined world's climate and the burning debris that continued to fall for months. Among the survivors, some questioned whether the Lion's mercy differed greatly from the Night Haunter's vengeance.

## The Knight and the Warlord

While the Lion fought on Ulan Hûda, Corswain commanded the Invincible Reason against the Faceless Prince, Nakrid Thole. His small supporting squadron accounted for a dozen enemy ships at the cost of three of its own: the Ardent Knight was destroyed by the bombardment cruiser Eldritch Sign, the Pride of Gramarye was lost ramming the grand cruiser Endless Night, and the Warspite was crippled by debris from Ulan Hûda. Thole personally boarded the Invincible Reason and engaged Corswain, whose skill with a blade proved superior. The Night Lords commander resorted to treachery, releasing plasma from a conduit that killed several of his own men and burned Corswain's sword arm through his ornate armour. Corswain, trained to fight equally well with either hand, continued to resist until the defeat of Ulan Hûda forced Thole to retreat. The encounter further deepened the rivalry between the two commanders without ending the threat posed by the Faceless Prince.

## Savage Weapons: The Confrontation on Tsagualsa

The battle at Thramas marked the beginning of the second year of the crusade according to the account. The Dark Angels held the initiative, but the Traitors still controlled numerous worlds in Thramas and almost all of Aegis. Gulgorahd remained comparatively free of Traitor influence, its surviving Bastion worlds maintaining their resistance despite extensive devastation. Pressure from the Dark Angels and the forces of Tigrus drew Traitor armies away from Gulgorahd, allowing the latter's legions to capture Parthac and strip its resources to replenish their depleted forges. The Night Lords, meanwhile, retook Yaelis. Both sides were approaching exhaustion, and the civilian population increasingly regarded the Legiones Astartes as uncontrolled instruments of destruction rather than the liberators of the Great Crusade. Trade and food distribution broke down, refugees fled their homes, and worlds cut off from Imperial protection became vulnerable to famine and xenos attacks.

On Tsagualsa, Curze summoned Sevatar, Sheng, Barbatos and other favoured commanders and sent the Lion an invitation to meet him at the new Night Lords stronghold. The strategic objectives of Horus had become secondary to the Night Haunter's desire for a confrontation with his brother that might change the fate revealed by his visions. The possibility of ending a war that had already consumed thousands of his Legionaries and innumerable mortals compelled the Lion to accept, despite his expectation of a trap. Unwilling to weaken the wider Loyalist front, he took only a single Chapter and a squadron of fast ships, leaving the Invincible Reason behind. Tsagualsa lay near the southern edge of the Nostramo sector, within Traitor-controlled space. The increasingly turbulent Warp, disturbed by the Word Bearers' rituals at Calth, dispersed the squadron, though no ships were lost. The Lion arrived first aboard the strike cruiser Vehemence and waited for his forces to regroup. The Night Lords made no attempt to intercept them and instead invited the Primarch to descend to the surface.

The Lion teleported to Tsagualsa accompanied only by Alajos, Praetor of the Ninth, and Corswain, with his remaining warriors standing by aboard their ships. This was the first meeting of the two Primarchs since the beginning of the Heresy. Curze used what he knew of Horus' plans and his own prophetic visions to provoke his brother, attacking the pride that underpinned the Lion's sense of duty. His central accusation was that all Primarchs were savage instruments of war, including the supposedly honourable Lord of the First Legion. It was the Lion who struck first. The honour guards joined the confrontation and the battle spread as both Legions committed their forces. The encounter degenerated into a violent, disordered struggle in which neither side achieved a meaningful strategic objective. Eventually the Night Lords dragged their severely wounded Primarch away, while the Dark Angels were forced to withdraw as more Traitor ships arrived. The Chapter that had accompanied the Lion was greatly diminished. More damaging than the physical wounds was the accusation Curze had planted in his brother's mind: the conduct of both Legions on Tsagualsa had made the distinction between them seem far less clear.

## The Spite of Devils

Tsagualsa did not end the crusade. The Night Haunter's failed attempt to escape his predicted destiny accelerated the loss of discipline within the VIII Legion. Traitor rulers such as those of Heraldor and Memlock continued to fight for the Warmaster, but Curze and his more degraded followers increasingly abandoned territorial objectives in favour of destruction for its own sake. Conversely, the relief of Thramas released Nightwatch regiments for offensive action. Captain-general Morhde transferred the veteran troops to the front, leaving the Imperialis Militia to defend many Loyalist worlds. The forces of Tigrus, Gulgorahd and Thramas pressed the Traitor-held region from several directions, while the Dark Angels concentrated on its strongest positions, repeatedly seeking a decisive confrontation with their brother Legion.

One of the few such confrontations occurred at Memlock. A full Chapter of Night Lords commanded by Anrek Barbatos guarded the nine-tiered fortress and supervised the movement of materiel and conscripts to Tsagualsa. A veteran of the old Legion with Terran origins, Barbatos had resisted its degeneration and mourned the decline of Curze. He chose to stand rather than abandon the stronghold. For seven weeks his Legionaries and a dozen conscript regiments held back the Thramassi Nightwatch and three Chapters of the Dark Angels. They were defeated only when the Lion committed a further 1,000 warriors. A First Legion tradition attributes one of the scars on the Lion's armour to Barbatos' blade, commemorating the resistance of a warrior who had chosen to meet his end in battle.

Most Night Lords, however, refused to offer the First Legion another Memlock. Their scattered warbands turned to a scorched-earth campaign, destroying vulnerable settlements, stripping worlds of useful resources and withdrawing whenever superior Loyalist forces approached. This denied both Legions the infrastructure necessary to sustain operations, but it also prevented the Lion from forcing a decisive engagement. The Lion, still affected by Curze's words, responded with a policy of his own: worlds that could not be adequately defended were stripped or destroyed, their people forcibly relocated, while troops and munitions were concentrated at major strongholds. Militarily, this reduced opportunities for Traitor raids and prevented resources falling into enemy hands. For the inhabitants of the Eastern Fringe it meant further devastation inflicted by the same warriors who had once brought them into the Imperium.

## Regret and Desolation: The Death of Thramas

After more than two years of fighting, the communications and industrial networks that had sustained the Eastern Fringe were disintegrating. The worsening Ruinstorm, spreading from Ultramar, complicated Loyalist fleet movements, while the Night Lords appeared comparatively unaffected and continued to evade pursuit. Although the Dark Angels could reduce fortified Traitor positions and restore control over much of the Aegis sector, they could not end the war while the VIII Legion remained mobile. In the midst of this stalemate, Lion El'Jonson unexpectedly departed with 30,000 of his Legionaries, without disclosing his destination. The absence of so many Dark Angels and their commanders encouraged the Night Lords to resume offensive operations against the remaining garrisons. Most of their attacks failed to overcome entrenched defenders, but Thramas, stripped of its First Legion garrison and still damaged by Ulan Hûda, fell to Nakrid Thole and his 10,000 warriors within hours. The Regent's palace alone held out, defended by the Midnight Host, whose priority was the sanctum of the Astropathic choir rather than the throne room itself.

Thole refused to withdraw while the Regent, Mayvin Khelen, remained alive. After seven days of renewed atrocities, a much larger Dark Angels fleet arrived under Eskaton Marduk Sedras. Its objective was the destruction of the Night Lords gathered on Thramas, rather than the relief of the world. The Traitor fleet could not oppose the First Legion's superior warships and abandoned the orbital battle, but Thole remained committed to the palace assault. Sedras ordered a devastating bombardment, judging that little of value survived and that further risk to his warriors was unjustified. Three hours of phosphex and lance fire reduced the city to ruins, killing both Night Lords and surviving civilians. The Dreadwing descended to eliminate the remaining Traitors. Thole was found alone in the shielded palace and killed by Sedras; First Legion legend claims that the Faceless Prince bore the Regent's bleached skull upon his armour when he died. The Dark Angels declared victory, but Thramas, once a prosperous capital and bastion of the Great Crusade, had been destroyed by those who claimed to defend the Emperor's realm. Nor did the battle decisively alter the overall balance of the war.

## The Forbidden Battle: Perditus and Tuchulcha

The Lion's expedition had taken him to the mysterious system known as Perditus, meaning Forbidden. Of the 30,000 Dark Angels who departed with him, considerably fewer returned. The identity of his opponents and the precise purpose of the expedition remained secret; rumours suggested that another Legion had been present, but the account does not identify or confirm it. What is certain is that the Lion recovered an object that the First Legion called the Gatekeeper or the Key. It enabled the Dark Angels to circumvent the Warp storms that had isolated much of the Eastern Fringe and restricted their ability to pursue the Night Lords. The Lion regarded it as a necessary evil, a temporary instrument to be used as circumstances required and subsequently set aside. One record preserved in the guarded archives of Terra gives it the name Tuchulcha, associates it with the Deadly Seas and categorises it as a daemon. The precise nature of the object and the events of its recovery are not explained in the supplied account.

## Desperation Cuts Keenly: The Battle of Sheol IX

With the Key in his possession, the Lion was able to strike at the Night Lords with unprecedented speed. With Horus pressing towards Terra and alarming reports emerging from Ultramar, the First Legion could not afford several more years of fighting in the east. The Night Lords operated in dispersed raiding groups, making a single decisive battle difficult to arrange. Recovered data from ships destroyed over Thramas revealed their hidden anchorage near Sheol IX, in a system long abandoned by the Loyalists. The Lion first concentrated his fleet against isolated Traitor formations, scattering eight flotillas over the following months and forcing the Night Lords to withdraw from open combat. He then supplied the lords of Gulgorahd with the location of surviving elements of Legio Victorum, anticipating that their ancient hatred would draw them into an immediate offensive. Their deployment opened an apparent weakness in the Loyalist line, giving the Night Lords an incentive to gather for another attack. The Lion accepted the likely sacrifice of Gulgorahd's forces as the price of securing the confrontation he needed.

Using his new means of Warp travel, the Lion brought approximately 300 capital ships and twice as many smaller craft into the Sheol system, appearing less than one astronomical unit from its outer planets in formation around the Invincible Reason. The Night Lords possessed perhaps 200 capital ships and three times that number of lighter craft, but many remained at anchor over Sheol IX with the bulk of their Legionaries still on the surface. The relic warships of the First Legion enjoyed superior accuracy and effective range; their sudden arrival overwhelmed the VIII Legion's initial defence. Some Traitor ships fled while others attempted to organise resistance or evacuate their stranded brethren. Of the more than 100 capital ships that advanced against the Dark Angels, barely 50 broke through their lines, with many crippled vessels subsequently captured as prizes. The Lion declined to pursue the fugitives and instead concentrated against those that had remained to rescue the warriors on Sheol IX, including the Gloriana-class Nightfall and the heavy cruisers Shroud of Eventide and Feral Heart.

Curze himself was still on the surface, where the Night Lords attempted to protect their remaining embarkation points and destroy armour and supplies that could not be recovered. The battle became a desperate struggle both in orbit and on the planet. In two hours the VIII Legion suffered losses approaching a quarter of its remaining strength. The Lion confronted the Night Haunter once again, defeating him in a brutal clash. The surviving Night Lords committed their reserves to save their Primarch, and a full company sacrificed itself to cover the evacuation of the unconscious Curze to the Excoriator. The Nightfall, meanwhile, remained under attack from the surrounding First Legion ships and their Firewing boarding forces. Escape became possible only when the badly damaged Feral Heart destroyed itself by detonating its Warp core, tearing apart nearby ships and opening a breach in the Loyalist encirclement. The Nightfall forced its way through, followed by surviving vessels. The Absolute and Crown of Ashes remained behind to cover the retreat and were destroyed; three more ships were lost to catastrophic Warp-drive failures as the fleet escaped. The Dark Angels remained to destroy the Traitor forces left on Sheol IX. Amid the ruins, the Lion continued to brood on Curze's accusation that the Primarchs were all dangerous weapons whose deeds would be judged by history.

## A Legion Rent Asunder

After more than three years of war, Sheol IX marked the effective end of the Thramas Crusade and the collapse of the Night Lords as the Legion they had once been. Their fleet had been reduced to a shadow of its former size, and Curze's condition was so grave that some of his warriors feared he would never recover, while others hoped that he would not. With his authority absent, numerous warlords saw little reason to continue the Warmaster's rebellion instead of pursuing their own ambitions. To prevent the complete dissolution of the VIII Legion into independent bands of pirates and reavers, Sevatar and the Atramentar fought a brief internal war, established a new ruling council and gathered the remaining loyal elements at the star of Qetesh. Those unwilling to accept the new order were allowed to disappear into the Thramas sector.

The Night Lords gathered their surviving ships from Yrrkesh, Taur and other rally points, only to be surprised once again by a vastly superior Dark Angels fleet. As the Traitors attempted to escape, Curze awoke aboard the Excoriator and ordered a final, futile boarding assault against the Invincible Reason. The account places him alongside Sevatar and the Atramentar in battle aboard the Lion's flagship, after which he was counted among the missing. Of the more than 100,000 warriors of the VIII Legion who had entered the crusade, fewer than 60,000 are recorded as escaping the ambush at Sheol IX. These events marked the end of the old VIII Legion, an army that had helped win the Great Crusade before losing the constraints that once gave its methods purpose.

## A Resolve Tarnished

The Dark Angels had suffered far fewer casualties, with the account placing their combat dead at no more than 10,000, but the price of victory was measured in the destruction of the empire they had sought to preserve. Hundreds of worlds had been abandoned or sacrificed, and entire populations had been destroyed to prevent their exploitation by the Traitors. The Lion had long accepted the sacrifice of individual worlds for the survival of the Imperium, yet the scale of the Thramas Crusade made that principle increasingly difficult to distinguish from the methods of his fallen brother. Curze's accusations troubled him more deeply than physical injury or the judgement of others. The Lion believed that history would vindicate his loyalty and sacrifices, but he could not easily dismiss the possibility that the final stages of the war had been driven as much by hatred of Curze as by strategic necessity. The chronicler contrasts this willingness to sacrifice worlds with the compassion of Sanguinius and Guilliman's concern for productive civilisation.

With Thramas ruined and the Night Lords scattered, the Lion regarded further regret as futile. The First Legion had emerged battered but unbroken, and he resolved to continue the war in the Emperor's name. His ships did not turn towards distant Terra, still concealed by the Warp storms, but towards Ultramar, following disturbing reports received from Tigrus concerning the devastation wrought by the World Eaters and Word Bearers and the raising of a new banner amid the ruins of the Imperium. The Dark Angels departed in full battle order, prepared to confront another possible rebellion. Their destination was Imperium Secundus.

## Uncertainties in the historical records
The recorded geography of the eastern fringe around 010.M31 places Memlock, the Heraclid Nebula, Modrak and Mortain in the Aegis sector, with Yaelis, Makator and the Crucible Exclusion Zone on the approach to Thramas. One account states that only a quarter of the Night Lords sent into combat at Crucible returned to their dropships; another account states that one in three of the committed Night Lords failed to return. The Titan force at Crucible is variously identified as Legio Victorum I and Legio Victorum II. One account describes the Night Lords' rally at Qetesh but subsequently attaches the fewer-than-60,000 escape figure to Sheol IX. These discrepancies remain unresolved.

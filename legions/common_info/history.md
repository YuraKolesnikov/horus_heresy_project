## Dawn of Astartes

The first warriors of the Legiones Astartes went to war in grey armour, unadorned save for the Emperor's thunder strike symbol. The armour they wore was not new, but the same partially powered armour that had evolved o n Old Earth and was worn by the elite of both the Emperor's armies and the techno-barbarian tribes that had fought against him. 

Some of this 'Thunder Armour', first named for the Thunder Regiments that were the Legions forebears, was newly forged, but the Emperor's warsmiths also took or cannibalised many suits from the armouries and corpses of conquered foes. As if to mark a break from the wars of the past the armour was cast in storm cloud grey, and bore only the thunderbolt and lightning marks of Imperial unity.

Over time Legions gained their own marks of distinction and character, and names — Emperor-given in some cases — came to replace numbers, with many Space Marine companies seeking to single themselves out from their brother Legions. Battle honours were accumulated and the effect of each Legion’s character worked upon them, so that as the Legions expanded to conquer the galaxy, storm cloud grey became granite, silver, viridian, iron, sable, gold, ocean, ash or ice, and by the time of the Triumph at Ullanor, the grey Legions of unity were gone, lost to history.

## The Age of Darkness — Dispersed Legions and Blackshields

By 007.M31, Horus had largely neutralised the major Loyalist Legions. The Imperial Fists under Rogal Dorn were defending Terra, the Ultramarines had suffered catastrophic losses at Calth, and the Blood Angels, White Scars, and Dark Angels were scattered or isolated. Lorgar's Ruinstorm made communication and reinforcement between distant Legion forces almost impossible.

Many Space Marine detachments were consequently forced to fight independently, often without orders from their Primarchs. These small formations conducted raids, assassinations, and defensive campaigns that became known as the Shadow Wars.

At Nagathar, Imperial Fists and Dark Angels defended the population against Word Bearers, preventing the sacrifice of approximately one million civilians in an occult ritual.

Ultramarines Assault-Captain Roscius became a notable commander of these independent forces. His company captured a Sons of Horus strike cruiser at Plutol, subsequently using it against the Death Guard and Traitor Mechanicum. He eventually commanded over 3,000 Legionaries drawn from as many as four Legions, conducting raids against Traitor supply lines before his last recorded action at Duthovan.

The Knights-Errant were a secretive organisation established by Malcador the Sigillite. Their members included Space Marines descended from Traitor Primarchs who had rejected their fathers' rebellion. They abandoned their former Legion heraldry and undertook covert missions for Malcador. Some accounts suggest their activities conflicted with Rogal Dorn's objectives, although actual fighting between them and Loyalists remains unconfirmed.

The Shattered Legions emerged from the survivors of Isstvan V. Iron Hands, Salamanders, and Raven Guard remnants combined into irregular forces. Shadrak Meduson of the Iron Hands united elements of all three Legions and led a destructive campaign of vengeance against Horus' forces.

The Crusader Host was another unusual formation, comprising representatives of the Legions stationed on Terra since the beginning of the Great Crusade. Following Horus' betrayal, Rogal Dorn imprisoned its members because their loyalties could no longer be assumed. Some later joined the Knights-Errant.

One particularly unusual incident involved a joint force of Dark Angels, White Scars, and Iron Warriors that infiltrated the fortified palace of Magister-Militaris Karpath in the Viridis Sector and executed him. The Iron Warriors' participation remains unexplained.

The central revelation is that the Horus Heresy was not fought exclusively by armies commanded by Primarchs. Thousands of isolated Space Marines, including warriors from opposing Legions, formed independent forces whose actions often escaped the attention of both Horus and Terra.

The Shattered Legions' campaign of vengeance gradually evolved into a brutal shadow war fought far behind enemy lines. Initially targeting communications, supply routes, and strategic installations, these scattered Astartes formations increasingly engaged one another in bitter battles driven by personal hatred and revenge.

The Blackshields represented an even more radical departure from traditional Legion organisation. These were Space Marines who had renounced their original Legions, rejected their former allegiances, or become isolated and disillusioned by the Horus Heresy. Many painted their armour black, removed Legion insignia, or adopted entirely new heraldry to conceal their origins.

Blackshields fought for both Loyalist and Traitor causes, but often recognised neither the Emperor nor Horus as their master. Some sought vengeance or attempted to preserve the ideals of the Great Crusade, while others became pirates, warlords, and conquerors pursuing their own ambitions.

Their formations rarely exceeded a thousand warriors, generally operating from captured warships or small strike cruisers. Leadership depended heavily on powerful individuals capable of uniting warriors from different backgrounds, sometimes even from formerly hostile Legions.

Several Blackshield operations became notorious for their destructive consequences. At Gamma-Dvalin, unidentified Astartes stole forbidden dimensional weapons and subsequently used them against the Sons of Horus, devastating an entire continent. At Taracanis, another group sabotaged the Death Guard cruiser Morbid Revelation, turning its own nova cannon into a catastrophic liability.

During the Battle of the Ultinian Rings, a Blackshield force led by Raven Guard Strike-Centurion Morkan Sayle ambushed Sons of Horus troops under Tybalt Marr. The fighting destabilised a sky-hive containing approximately 100,000 colonists, sending it crashing into Ultinia 7. Morkan was presumed dead, although rumours of his survival persisted.

Other Blackshield groups were responsible for atrocities on an even greater scale. The Umbral Hundred devastated twelve systems in the Chonma Sector during 011.M31, while the Company of the Sundered established its own dominion at Boras Minor.

A particularly mysterious category consisted of Space Marines who actively rebelled against their own Primarchs, abandoning their Legion's identity to fight their former brothers. Their numbers and origins remain largely unknown.

The Blackshields demonstrate how completely the Horus Heresy shattered the unity of the Legiones Astartes. Beyond the armies of the Emperor and Warmaster, countless independent warbands fought their own wars, sometimes influencing the wider conflict without either side fully understanding their actions.

## Dispersed Legion Forces [007–010.M31]

Between 007.M31 and 010.M31, the Ruinstorm fragmented the Legiones Astartes across the galaxy. Although most Legions continued fighting under their Primarchs, countless detached formations lost contact with their commanders and were forced to operate independently, sometimes without knowing which side their Legion had joined.

The Dark Angels fought the Night Lords throughout the Thramas Crusade. Following Lion El'Jonson's victory over Konrad Curze at Tsagualsa, numerous Dark Angels strike forces pursued scattered Traitors across the eastern galaxy, often becoming isolated themselves.

The Emperor's Children underwent a profound transformation after Isstvan V. Fulgrim eventually regained control of his increasingly corrupted Legion, while many detached formations specialised in hunting and exterminating Loyalist survivors.

The Iron Warriors, under Perturabo, concentrated on conquering Loyalist fortress worlds protecting the approaches to Segmentum Solar. Detached siege forces subsequently occupied conquered territories and became adept at countering attacks by isolated Loyalist Astartes.

The White Scars were trapped in a prolonged war against the Orks at Chondax through Alpha Legion manipulation. With Jaghatai Khan's allegiance initially uncertain, distant formations had to determine their own loyalties. Some disgraced warriors were later dispatched as Sagyar Mazan, conducting near-suicidal infiltration and assassination missions.

The Imperial Fists remained concentrated around Terra under Rogal Dorn, preparing its defences. However, survivors scattered during the Phall campaign and isolated garrison detachments continued fighting independently throughout the Imperium.

The Night Lords became increasingly fragmented after their defeats against the Dark Angels. Sevatar attempted to restore central command, but numerous formations pursued independent campaigns of terror, even after their eventual reunion with Curze.

The Space Wolves suffered severe losses fighting the Thousand Sons at Prospero and subsequently the Alpha Legion. Nevertheless, they remained a formidable force, with much of their strength reserved for Terra's defence. Earlier detachments sent by Leman Russ to monitor other Legions sometimes operated independently.

The Blood Angels remained missing until 010.M31. Their deployment towards Signus had been disrupted by the Ruinstorm, scattering thousands of Legionaries. Some were serving as Lamentii honour guards, while others became stranded far from their brothers and believed themselves abandoned.

The Iron Hands were shattered by the death of Ferrus Manus at Isstvan V. Their Legion fragmented into independent warfleets, many joining Shadrak Meduson's Shattered Legions or fighting under Autek Mor. Others pursued campaigns of vengeance against the Traitors at Gagia, Sacrissan, and Agromis.

The World Eaters fought alongside the Word Bearers in the Shadow Crusade, while maintaining recruitment worlds such as Bodt. These centres rapidly produced new Legionaries known as Inductii. Loyalist Shattered Legion forces raided these facilities, provoking a prolonged shadow war against World Eaters hunter-killer formations.

The Ultramarines spent much of 007–008.M31 defending Ultramar against Angron and Lorgar. However, detached XIIIth Legion forces remained stationed on distant worlds such as Molech or accompanied Rogue Trader expeditionary fleets. Some operated independently until contact with Guilliman could be restored.

The Death Guard accompanied the main Traitor advance alongside the Sons of Horus. Their forces repeatedly clashed with Shadrak Meduson's raiders, who attacked Traitor positions and supply lines. Some Death Guard detachments deliberately committed atrocities against civilian worlds to lure isolated Loyalists into battle.

The Thousand Sons largely disappeared from Imperial knowledge during this period. Unconfirmed reports placed small groups at Prism, Chbal, Logan's World, and Yamnan, sometimes fighting for opposing sides or alongside Blackshields. Their actual loyalties and activities remain uncertain.

The Sons of Horus spearheaded Horus' advance towards Terra, while detached formations hunted survivors of Isstvan V alongside the Emperor's Children. Captain Tybalt Marr eventually recognised that repeated Shattered Legion attacks formed a coordinated strategy intended to gradually exhaust the Warmaster's forces.

The Word Bearers concentrated on the Shadow Crusade until their defeat in late 008.M31. Smaller detachments supported other Traitor Legions, frequently led by Chaplains or Diabolists who provided occult expertise and helped hunt surviving Loyalist formations.

The Salamanders suffered catastrophic losses at Isstvan V and believed Vulkan dead. Some survivors returned to Nocturne, while others fought alongside the Iron Hands and Raven Guard. The Legion would require generations to recover sufficiently for conventional campaigns.

The Raven Guard began rebuilding under Corax, who introduced accelerated recruitment into his experimental Raptor companies, although many recruits proved unstable. While the main Legion attacked worlds that had joined Horus, several dozen smaller groups apparently continued independent raiding campaigns.

The Alpha Legion remained one of the most mysterious forces of the Heresy. Following Chondax, elements of the XXth Legion specialised in hunting Loyalist Shattered Legions through espionage, infiltration, and disinformation, undermining isolated Loyalist forces without relying primarily on direct confrontation.

Together, these campaigns reveal a galaxy-wide war fought by fragmented Legions, where many Space Marines operated far beyond their Primarchs' immediate control, pursuing independent campaigns of survival, vengeance, and destruction.

## Chronology of the Great Betrayal and Age of Darkness

000–004.M31: The Ullanor Triumph and Fall of Horus. Following his victory at Ullanor, the Emperor returned to Terra and appointed Horus Warmaster. In 004.M31, Horus was mortally wounded on Davin and healed in the Serpent Lodge, where he fell under the influence of the Ruinous Powers. He subsequently began secretly preparing his rebellion.

004–005.M31: The Burning of Prospero. Magnus attempted to warn the Emperor of Horus' betrayal through forbidden psychic powers, inadvertently damaging his father's great work. Horus manipulated Leman Russ into believing Magnus must be killed. The Space Wolves devastated Prospero, reducing the Thousand Sons to approximately 1,000 surviving warriors, although Magnus escaped.

004–009.M31: The Signus Campaign. Horus dispatched Sanguinius and the Blood Angels to the Signus Cluster, deliberately trapping a Legion he knew would never willingly join his rebellion. There they encountered daemonic armies, devastating psychic attacks, and distortions of reality.

005–006.M31: The Betrayal at Isstvan III. Horus deployed four Legions to Isstvan III and attempted to eliminate tens of thousands of Legionaries whose loyalty he doubted. Some Loyalists escaped and carried news of the betrayal to Terra.

005–006.M31: The Schism of Mars. Fabricator-General Kelbor-Hal joined Horus, plunging Mars and the wider Mechanicum into civil war. Forge Worlds, Titan Legions, and Skitarii armies divided between Loyalists, Traitors, and independent factions.

006.M31: The Isstvan V Dropsite Massacre. Eight Legions were ordered to crush Horus' rebellion, but four were secretly loyal to the Warmaster. The second wave betrayed the first, nearly annihilating the Iron Hands, Salamanders, and Raven Guard. Ferrus Manus was killed, while the other two Loyalist Primarchs were lost amid the slaughter.

006.M31: The First Battle of Paramar. Alpha Legion forces, supported by Traitor Mechanicum and Titans, attacked the strategically important Paramar system. Loyalist Iron Warriors unexpectedly joined the defence alongside Legio Gryphonicus, but the system ultimately fell, disrupting northern Imperial transit routes.

007.M31: The Battle of Phall. Imperial Fists forces under Alexis Polux resisted an Iron Warriors attack despite being stranded by Warp storms. Their defence prevented the fleet's destruction, but Rogal Dorn's order to return to Terra forced them to abandon the engagement.

007–009.M31: The Manachean War. Horus expanded his Dark Compliances across the northern Imperium. The Sons of Horus and Death Guard conquered strategic territories, while the Warmaster secured Port Maw as a vital fleet anchorage. Loyalist Forge Worlds were isolated or besieged.

007.M31: The Wolf and the Khan. After learning of Horus' rebellion and the Space Wolves' destruction of Prospero, Jaghatai Khan encountered a weakened Space Wolves force threatened by the Alpha Legion. He chose to return to Terra rather than intervene, prioritising his higher duty.

007.M31: The Calth Conjunction. The Word Bearers betrayed the Ultramarines during their supposed joint muster against the Orks. Their true objective was a ritual involving the Veridia star, creating the Ruinstorm, which divided the galaxy and severely disrupted Warp travel and communication.

007–008.M31: The Shadow Crusade. Lorgar and Angron invaded Ultramar, devastating the Five Hundred Worlds and attempting to destroy Guilliman's power base while drawing Loyalist forces away from Terra.

007–009.M31: The Thramas Crusade. Horus dispatched Konrad Curze and the Night Lords to neutralise three important Loyalist Forge Worlds. They destroyed two and severely damaged the third, launching a campaign of terror across the region.

007–017.M31: The Underworld War. The surviving Ultramarines and Word Bearers continued fighting beneath Calth's devastated surface. The subterranean conflict lasted ten years, ending with the elimination of the remaining Traitors.

008.M31: Blood Among the Pale Stars. A mysterious Blackshield formation called the Dark Brotherhood established a corsair empire and fought an Alpha Legion hunter-killer force. Its activities exemplified the rise of independent Astartes warbands pursuing their own ambitions.

008.M31: The Destruction of Bodt. Autek Mor of the Iron Hands led an independent Loyalist force against the World Eaters' recruitment world. Following a brutal battle, he devastated its surface, permanently severing an important source of new World Eaters Legionaries.

## The Shattered Legions

The Shattered Legions emerged from the Isstvan V Dropsite Massacre, where the Iron Hands, Salamanders, and Raven Guard were nearly annihilated by the betrayal of the Word Bearers, Night Lords, Iron Warriors, and Alpha Legion. Survivors escaped aboard scattered warships, often forming mixed groups of warriors from all three Legions. Some attempted to return to their home worlds, while others remained together, united by their shared losses and determination to fight Horus.

After the remaining Iron Hands Clan-Council was destroyed at Oqueth Minor, Shadrak Meduson of Clan Sorrgol assumed command of the largest surviving formations. He united more than 20,000 Legionaries into independent, mixed-Legion strike cells and dispersed them throughout Traitor-controlled territory. For three years, these forces conducted thousands of raids, assassinations, and sabotage operations, gradually weakening Horus' armies and delaying their advance towards Terra. Their campaign was so carefully coordinated that even Horus initially failed to recognise that the seemingly unrelated attacks were directed by a single commander.

The Ruinstorm subsequently created countless other isolated Astartes formations across the galaxy. Some had no knowledge of their Primarchs' allegiances, while others deliberately rejected both sides and continued following their original orders. Unlike Meduson's coordinated forces, many pursued independent objectives, particularly vengeance, sometimes fighting throughout the Heresy and into the Great Scouring. Even without a unified strategy, their actions frequently disrupted major military operations and influenced the wider civil war.

The Shattered Legions depended heavily on strong commanders capable of uniting warriors from different Legions after losing contact with their Primarchs. Leaders such as Shadrak Meduson achieved this through charisma, tactical ability, or personal example, while others relied on intimidation and brutal discipline. Despite their mixed composition, these forces generally preserved the standardised rank structures established during the Great Crusade, allowing officers from one Legion to command warriors from another. A praetor might therefore lead hundreds of Legionaries from different Legions despite having only a handful of his own warriors under his command.

Most Shattered Legion formations numbered several hundred Astartes, although some reached approximately 2,000 warriors, commonly drawn from three different Legions. Their organisation varied considerably, and isolation from regular supply lines forced them to rely on whatever equipment and specialist units they possessed, including formations such as Iron Hands Medusan Immortals or Salamanders Pyroclasts. This adaptability made their attacks difficult to predict, while combining different Legions' fighting doctrines often produced unusually effective battlefield tactics. Similar formations also emerged among the Traitors, including isolated detachments continuing earlier orders and disgraced warriors sent on near-suicidal missions against Loyalist survivors. Thus, both sides fielded independent, mixed-Legion forces throughout the Horus Heresy.

## Strategic Situation after the Dropsite Massacre [006–007.M31]

The Isstvan V Dropsite Massacre shattered the Legiones Astartes and brought the Imperium to the brink of collapse. Horus secured the allegiance of half the Space Marine Legions, while the Loyalists were devastated or scattered across the galaxy.

Despite these victories, Horus failed to gain a decisive military advantage. The fierce resistance and sacrifices of Loyalist forces at Isstvan deprived him of the opportunity to launch a surprise assault on Terra.

With the Emperor now warned of his betrayal, Horus could no longer achieve a swift victory. Breaking the Imperial Palace's formidable defences would require overwhelming military superiority, forcing the Warmaster to prepare for a longer and more destructive war.

Horus retained the strategic initiative after Isstvan V, exploiting the Imperium's enormous size, fragmented government, and unreliable communications. Although billions of Imperial soldiers and the surviving Loyalist Legions could eventually oppose him, most worlds remained unaware of the betrayal or received only contradictory reports.

Warp storms spreading from the galactic north severely disrupted astropathic communication and interstellar travel. Loyalist survivors attempting to warn Terra were hunted by Traitor fleets, while countless supply convoys and military transports disappeared or were diverted. Horus exploited this confusion to isolate Imperial strongholds, spread misinformation, and force vulnerable worlds into submission.

The Sons of Horus and Death Guard spearheaded the conquest of territories bordering Isstvan, including the destruction of Manachea's defenders in 007.M31. These campaigns provided the Warmaster with industrial resources, manpower, and strategically important territories for his expanding armies.

The Night Lords were deployed against the Dark Angels in the Thramas Sector, preventing Lion El'Jonson's Legion from intervening elsewhere.

The Alpha Legion reportedly conducted simultaneous operations across widely separated regions, targeting both the Space Wolves and White Scars. Although these accounts remain unconfirmed, they suggest Alpharius commanded considerably more Legionaries and warships than previously estimated.

The Word Bearers and World Eaters were assigned one of Horus' most important objectives: the destruction of Ultramar. Their devastating campaigns across the eastern Imperium were intended to eliminate Roboute Guilliman's realm, one of the strongest remaining centres of Loyalist resistance.

Horus' overarching strategy was to defeat the Loyalist Legions separately, while Warp disturbances, misinformation, and collapsing supply networks prevented the Imperium from organising a united defence.

The detailed account of the Emperor's censure of Magnus, the Council of Nikaea, the Censure Host's campaign, the battle for Tizca, and the destruction of Prospero is preserved in [The Burning of Prospero](burning_of_prospero.md).

## The Imperium and the Warp

### Keeping the Darkness at Bay

Although the Warp existed beyond conventional reality, Daemons and other supernatural entities posed a tangible threat to humanity's existence. During the Horus Heresy, those who had dismissed these dangers were confronted with horrors capable of destroying both their bodies and minds. Yet throughout the Great Crusade, secret Imperial organisations had already been established to combat such threats, employing forbidden archaeotechnology, psychic sciences, and esoteric weapons capable of turning the Warp's own power against it. These instruments were extraordinarily dangerous, however, and the Imperium sometimes suffered as much from its protectors as from the entities they hunted.

During Old Night, humanity had endured thousands of years of technological tyranny, famine, warfare, uncontrolled psychic mutation, and daemonic incursions. Entire worlds were consumed by the Warp or enslaved by sorcerer-kings, while their populations suffered grotesque mutations and unimaginable cruelty. The Emperor's Imperial Truth was designed, at least in part, to prevent a return to this catastrophe. By dismantling traditional religions, cults, and competing belief systems, He sought to unite humanity under a single rational doctrine. An additional, potentially secret objective was to weaken the connection between mankind and the Ruinous Powers, since organised worship, superstition, and devotion to supernatural entities could attract the attention of the Warp. The deliberate suppression of knowledge about these entities, enforced through sometimes extreme measures, may therefore have been intended as a defensive strategy rather than merely an ideological one.

### The Actors of Illumination

The principal agents responsible for spreading the Imperial Truth were the Iterators, an Imperial order of rhetoricians, philosophers, and educators trained under the direction of Malcador the Sigillite. Recruited from Terra's most accomplished intellectuals, they accompanied the Great Crusade to persuade newly compliant populations to abandon their ancestral beliefs and embrace Imperial rationalism. Their mastery of debate, diplomacy, and propaganda enabled them to neutralise opposition without military intervention, while their teachings reinforced the Emperor's ideology on worlds already incorporated into the Imperium.

However, the Iterators were not universally welcomed. Many civilisations rejected their doctrines, particularly those whose religious traditions had survived for millennia. Some viewed Imperial enlightenment as another form of conquest, more insidious than military occupation because it sought to transform the very foundations of their cultures. Although countless worlds were pacified through persuasion, others resisted and ultimately required the intervention of the Legiones Astartes. The chronicler suggests that the Great Crusade's apparent ideological success concealed numerous populations whose resentment and suppressed beliefs would later provide fertile ground for rebellion and daemonic influence.

### Of Myths and Monsters

Malcador's historical scholars identified striking similarities between ancient Terran mythological creatures and genuine manifestations of Warp entities. Vampires, trolls, and other legendary monsters shared characteristics with beings encountered during the Great Crusade, including their predatory habits, cruelty, and unnatural physical traits. Whether these myths preserved distorted memories of actual daemonic encounters, or whether the Warp had somehow assumed forms inspired by humanity's collective imagination, remained uncertain.

### His Talons

Beyond the public teachings of the Iterators, the Emperor maintained specialised organisations dedicated to confronting supernatural threats directly. Foremost among them were the Talons of the Emperor — the Legio Custodes and the Sisters of Silence. The Custodes served not only as the Emperor's personal guardians but also as elite agents entrusted with hunting individuals possessing forbidden knowledge, recovering dangerous artefacts, and investigating ancient technologies and occult practices. In certain cases, those captured were executed immediately; in others, individuals possessing valuable expertise were brought before the Emperor and offered service instead of death.

The Sisters of Silence, renowned as witchseekers, specialised in locating and suppressing dangerous psykers. Alongside their duties supporting the Astra Telepathica, they investigated individuals whose abilities might have been corrupted through forbidden rituals, psychic contact, or the manipulation of supernatural entities. Their operations could involve Pursuer cadres, Erinyes jetbike formations, and specialised Divider stealth-craft, enabling them to conduct rapid and covert campaigns against psychic threats. When conventional containment failed, the Imperium could deploy assassins of the Officio Assassinorum or even the terrifying Psi-Titans of the Ordo Sinister.

These extreme measures were sometimes employed against entire worlds considered irretrievably corrupted by the Warp, resulting in their complete destruction. The paradox of the Emperor's secret war was that defending humanity from supernatural horrors often required the use of equally terrifying methods and weapons. While the Imperium publicly denied the existence of such threats, its hidden guardians fought a relentless war against them throughout the Great Crusade — a war whose true scale would only become apparent with the outbreak of the Horus Heresy.

### The Imperial Fists — Guardians of the Imperial Truth

Beyond their renowned role as defenders of the Imperium, the Imperial Fists may have been deliberately employed by the Emperor to combat Daemonology and the influence of the Warp. The VII Legion's genetically ingrained loyalty, discipline, and unwavering devotion to duty made its warriors particularly resistant to corruption. Unlike Magnus, whose pursuit of forbidden knowledge frequently exceeded the limits imposed upon him, Rogal Dorn possessed the restraint and caution necessary to study dangerous subjects without becoming consumed by them. The Emperor kept Dorn close throughout much of the Great Crusade, and some accounts suggest that He gradually entrusted the Primarch with secrets concerning the true nature of the Warp, the purpose of the Imperial Truth, and humanity's hidden enemies.

The Imperial Fists consequently became zealous defenders of the Imperial Truth, developing specialised methods for confronting sorcerers and Warp-corrupted adversaries. They pioneered the use of psyarkana devices, technologies designed to counter psychic phenomena and supernatural influences. Dorn also assembled an enormous collection of forbidden artefacts and esoteric knowledge within the vaults of the Phalanx, concealing these resources even from his brother Primarchs. Although he remained reluctant to employ such dangerous instruments, this secret arsenal proved invaluable during the Siege of Terra.

### The Death Guard — The Reapers of Corruption

Mortarion and the Death Guard also played a significant role in suppressing Warp-related threats, though their methods were considerably more ruthless. Driven by his hatred of sorcery and the supernatural, Mortarion actively sought worlds contaminated by psychic practices or forbidden rituals. Rather than approaching these populations as a liberator, he descended upon them as an executioner, accompanied by the XIV Legion's feared Destroyer formations.

The Death Guard were among the earliest Legions to employ bale flamers and psyk-out or psi-reactive munitions on a large scale. Mortarion regarded Warp corruption as an incurable disease that threatened to spread unless completely eradicated. His campaigns therefore resembled systematic purges, reflecting his conviction that sorcery was a contagion requiring extermination rather than understanding or containment.

### Independent Preparations

Other Imperial organisations independently developed methods of confronting supernatural threats. Within the Mechanicum, the Malagra functioned as an internal order of investigators and enforcers dedicated to identifying and eliminating technological heresy. Its agents hunted Heretek Magi and suppressed research involving forbidden artificial intelligence, the integration of xenos technology with Imperial machinery, and the direct extraction of energy from the Warp. Entire forge complexes could be declared dormant if evidence of sufficiently dangerous experimentation was discovered, illustrating the severity with which the Mechanicum treated violations of its most sacred technological prohibitions.

Meanwhile, Rogue Traders and Chartist Captains frequently encountered supernatural dangers while travelling beyond the Imperium's established borders. Their journeys through the Immaterium exposed them to entities and phenomena capable of driving ordinary humans insane. Operating far from Imperial supervision, many developed their own concealed defensive measures, including unconventional weapons and methods forbidden or discouraged by Imperial authorities. Although they rarely acknowledged such preparations publicly, their experience taught them that survival sometimes required confronting the very supernatural threats the Imperial Truth officially denied.

Together, these examples demonstrate that the Emperor's hidden war against the Warp extended far beyond the Custodes and Sisters of Silence. The Imperial Fists, Death Guard, Mechanicum, and independent voidfarers all contributed to containing supernatural dangers, whether through deliberate Imperial direction or their own experience. Their methods differed dramatically, but all reflected the same contradiction: the Imperium publicly denied the supernatural while secretly developing increasingly sophisticated means to fight it.

### The Ascendance of the Warp

During the Horus Heresy, the power of the Warp reached levels unseen since the Age of Strife, bringing Daemonic entities dangerously close to the boundary between the Immaterium and realspace. Across the Imperium, citizens discovered that the supernatural horrors denied by the Imperial Truth were real. Eyewitness accounts, rumours, and countless refugees spread stories of Daemonic manifestations, triggering widespread panic that frequently paralysed planetary defences before the Traitor Legions even arrived. Horus deliberately exploited this terror, sending emissaries ahead of his fleets to expose the Emperor's Great Lie and threaten populations with the supernatural powers now under his command.

Worlds conquered by the Warmaster were said to have fallen into darkness, both politically and literally. Trade and communication ceased, spaceports were blockaded, astropathic choirs silenced, and refugees prevented from escaping. A few vessels were deliberately permitted to flee, carrying terrifying stories and concealed Horus agents intended to destabilise neighbouring systems. Valuable industrial and Forge Worlds were enslaved under Dark Compliances, their populations forced to produce weapons and supplies for the Traitor armies. Others were stripped of resources, exterminated through atomic bombardment or Life-Eater weapons, or sacrificed entirely to sustain the Ruinstorm. Some heavily fortified Imperial worlds simply disappeared, their ultimate fates unknown.

The collapse of Imperial authority encouraged further catastrophes. Refugee fleets brought together followers of previously isolated religious cults, sometimes combining fragments of ancient rituals into dangerous new practices. The collective terror of entire populations resonated within the Warp, contributing to widespread Daemonic incursions. Cultists and rebels rose against Imperial governors, while long-suppressed xenos powers seized the opportunity to reclaim territory. Among the threats that resurfaced were the Unspeakable King and the horrors associated with Du'Maash. Quarantined alien civilisations discovered that their Imperial blockades had disappeared, while other hostile species expanded into abandoned Imperial territories. With surviving records fragmentary, later historians could not always distinguish genuine Daemonic attacks from the depredations of newly resurgent xenos.

### An Imperium Abandoned

The Imperium's defenders were overwhelmed by the scale of the catastrophe. Following Isstvan, Calth, Signus, and other devastating engagements, the Legiones Astartes had been divided and scattered across the galaxy. Surviving Loyalist formations conducted guerrilla campaigns against the Traitors but could rarely halt their advance. The Imperial Army was similarly fractured, its forces either fighting one another or responding to countless desperate requests for reinforcement. Planetary garrisons struggled to contain refugee movements, while mistrust between neighbouring systems increasingly replaced cooperation.

Amid this collapse, many questioned why the Emperor and His most trusted servants seemed to have abandoned humanity. Having returned to Terra following Ullanor, the Emperor was consumed by His secret Great Work, whose importance prevented Him from intervening directly in the wider war. To the trillions seeking His protection, His absence appeared to indicate indifference or ignorance. The Legio Custodes and Sisters of Silence largely withdrew to Terra, attending to the Emperor and carrying out His concealed instructions. Rogal Dorn and the Imperial Fists likewise concentrated on fortifying the Throneworld, although VII Legion detachments continued defending the Segmentum Solar and reassuring isolated planetary governors. These efforts were increasingly undermined by Horus' Solar War, a campaign of deception and strategic disruption designed to weaken Terra before the Traitor invasion.

#### Ancient Weapons

Early encounters between Space Marines and Daemons revealed a strange weakness in Warp entities. Newly manifested Daemons appeared highly resistant to advanced projectile and energy weapons, yet unusually vulnerable to blades and fire. Some scholars theorised that, because Daemons were shaped by emotion and symbolic meaning, their destruction depended partly upon humanity's primordial understanding of pain and violence rather than purely physical damage. This interpretation also offered a possible explanation for the Emperor's insistence that Space Marines retain swords and other close-combat weapons alongside their advanced firearms. However, the precise mechanism remained uncertain.

### The Fracturing of the Imperium's Hidden Defenders

The Mechanicum increasingly withdrew into isolation as its Forge Worlds struggled with internal division and the secession of Mars. The once-feared Malagra, responsible for suppressing technological heresy, became entangled in the emergence of the Dark Mechanicum and the conflicts dividing the priesthood of Mars. Many Forge Worlds prioritised their own survival and political interests over the defence of the wider Imperium.

Rogue Traders and Chartist Captains responded independently and unpredictably. Some appeared unexpectedly to aid Loyalist forces, while others, outraged by the civil war, attacked both sides indiscriminately. Notable incidents included the Golden Fleet's intervention above Tallarn and the Admiralty of Joss crippling the moon of Drussen IV. Rogue Trader fleets also reportedly participated on both sides of the Sea of Fire, the devastating void battles around Beta-Garmon. Despite these interventions, their scattered forces were too few and too distant from the Imperium's heartlands to decisively influence the war.

### The Fate of the Order Elucidatum

The secretive Order Elucidatum, established under Malcador the Sigillite, became a particular target of Horus' campaign against Imperial intelligence networks. During the Great Crusade, the Warmaster had noticed Imperial Army formations abandoning their assigned objectives to pursue mysterious operations, eventually tracing these activities to Malcador's agents. Recognising their potential danger, Horus ordered his equerry Maloghurst to exterminate the Elucidators, alongside his broader purge of Iterators, Remembrancers, and Imperial officials.

Outside the Traitor fleets, the Order struggled against an impossible expansion of its responsibilities. The emergence of the Ruinstorm created countless supernatural threats, while the Imperial Army units upon which the Elucidators depended were increasingly committed elsewhere. The organisation fragmented, its operatives becoming isolated and disappearing into the chaos of the Heresy. Evidence from the Unbalanced Scales further suggested that perhaps dozens of Elucidators had secretly been Alpha Legion double agents, recalled to unknown assignments when the rebellion began.

In 012.M31, Malcador formally dissolved the Order Elucidatum, erasing records of its existence. The surviving agents, known as Tallymen, who managed to return to Terra received neither public recognition nor punishment. Instead, they were quietly reassigned to other clandestine Imperial organisations, where their specialised experience remained valuable to the Sigillite.

The collapse of the Order Elucidatum reflected the wider failure of the Emperor's hidden defensive system: organisations created to combat the Warp through secrecy, specialised knowledge, and carefully controlled interventions were overwhelmed once the Horus Heresy transformed that concealed struggle into a galaxy-wide catastrophe.

### Arcana of the Legiones Astartes

During the Horus Heresy, virtually every Space Marine Legion encountered the supernatural forces of the Warp, whether deliberately seeking forbidden knowledge or attempting to destroy it. Many campaigns centred on recovering dangerous artefacts and technologies previously considered too hazardous for military use, while others were fought against Daemonic incursions caused by breaches between realspace and the Immaterium.

One notable confrontation was the Battle of Perditus in 009.M31, where the 98th Clan Company of the Iron Hands intercepted a Death Guard force led by its First Company near a forbidden Mechanicum research facility. The Death Guard sought an artefact believed capable of calming the Ruinstorm, whose power had engulfed the Ultima Segmentum less than two years earlier. The two Legions fought for more than a month before Lion El'Jonson intervened, ordering both forces to withdraw. According to Iron Hands accounts, the Dark Angels subsequently seized the artefact, considering it essential for navigating the Warp during the Ruinstorm.

Another incident occurred in 010.M31, when Ahzek Ahriman of the Thousand Sons infiltrated the psychic prison of Kamiti Sona, seeking an inmate whose identity and importance were known only to his Corvidae Occult. Several prominent Sisters of Silence were killed during the breach, disrupting the facility's psychic suppression systems. Hundreds of imprisoned psykers suddenly regained their abilities, allowing Warp entities to manifest through the chaos. Only the intervention of Space Wolves forces and a Knights Errant Librarian, both apparently pursuing Ahriman, prevented the catastrophe from spreading. The subsequent fate of the prison and its inmates was sealed.

Such incidents progressively weakened the boundaries between reality and the Warp. Repeated Daemonic manifestations and the activation of forbidden technoarcana created new breaches across the galaxy, allowing the Ruinstorm to expand and increasingly manifest under its own power. Each confrontation with the supernatural, whether intended to contain or exploit it, risked strengthening the very forces threatening the Imperium.

### Aetheric Ambitions

The Traitor Primarchs held sharply differing attitudes toward their new supernatural allies. Perturabo regarded the Warp with relative indifference, while Mortarion and Angron openly despised its entities. Fulgrim, by contrast, embraced the Warp's intoxicating and all-consuming power. Yet it was Lorgar Aurelian whose relationship with the Immaterium proved most significant to the spiritual foundations of Horus' rebellion.

Decades before the Heresy, Lorgar had already begun questioning the Emperor's denial of divinity. Following his humiliation at Monarchia, where the Emperor condemned the Word Bearers' religious devotion, Lorgar embarked upon a pilgrimage into the galaxy's darkest regions. Guided by his desire to prove that gods genuinely existed, he eventually encountered the powers that would become central to his doctrine of the Primordial Truth. His correspondence with Kor Phaeron and the teachings preserved in the Book of Lorgar suggest that this revelation was the culmination of a deliberate search. Unlike his brothers, who often encountered the Warp through circumstance or military necessity, Lorgar actively pursued its mysteries and ultimately embraced the entities the Emperor had forbidden humanity to worship.

### The Death Lord's Hypocrisy

Mortarion's alliance with Horus represented one of the greatest contradictions of the Heresy. Having spent much of his life opposing sorcery and psychic corruption, the Death Guard Primarch nevertheless joined a rebellion increasingly dependent upon Daemonic powers. His estranged sons condemned this apparent betrayal of his principles, although the extent to which Mortarion genuinely accepted the Warp remains uncertain.

Nathaniel Garro later suggested that Mortarion may have experienced considerable internal conflict and perhaps joined Horus while secretly intending to undermine the Ruinstorm from within. Under this interpretation, Mortarion's loyalty to the Warmaster might have concealed an effort to weaken the supernatural forces Horus intended to unleash upon the Imperium.

Captain Morturg offered another possibility: Mortarion may eventually have regretted his decision and longed for the earlier Great Crusade, when he had fought alongside Ferrus Manus against humanity's enemies. Nevertheless, the chronicler questions whether such regrets could excuse the atrocities already committed by the Death Guard.

The central contradiction remains unresolved: Mortarion despised the Warp yet allied himself with those who sought to unleash it. Whether this reflected hypocrisy, a concealed strategy, or a catastrophic misjudgement, his Legion had already become a devastating threat to the Imperium, even without the additional horrors of Daemonic corruption.

### Lorgar and the Primordial Truth

Following his discovery of the entities inhabiting the Warp, Lorgar Aurelian underwent a profound psychological and spiritual transformation. Later revisions of the Book of Lorgar, some written in blood rather than ink, adopted increasingly disturbing ritualistic language, while Kor Phaeron's correspondence began openly discussing supernatural powers in terms of religious worship. Rather than merely proving the Emperor's Imperial Truth false, Lorgar became a devoted prophet of the Chaos Gods. By the Horus Heresy, his ultimate ambition was the union of humanity and the Primordial Annihilator, creating a symbiotic relationship between mankind and the powers of the Warp. He pursued this vision methodically, spreading the religious traditions of Colchis throughout his Legion before establishing selective alliances with Warp entities sympathetic to his objectives.

Horus learned much of the Warp's deeper mysteries through Lorgar, but their ambitions differed fundamentally. Where Lorgar envisioned spiritual transformation and worship, Horus regarded Daemons as strategic resources to exploit in his campaign for galactic domination. He sought neither religious enlightenment nor a replacement for the Imperial Truth, but absolute political and military power. In his calculations, sacrificing warriors, worlds, and entire populations in exchange for Daemonic assistance appeared profitable. Horus believed that victory would allow him to renegotiate any obligations owed to the Warp, failing to understand the true cost of these bargains. Lorgar attempted to warn him against excessive arrogance and reckless exploitation of supernatural powers, but Horus increasingly dismissed his brother's advice, creating a growing philosophical rift between them.

#### The Warrior Lodges

The Warrior Lodges, secret fraternities established within numerous Space Marine Legions, were believed to have been promoted extensively by the Word Bearers during the Great Crusade. Although outwardly intended to strengthen brotherhood between Legionaries, they provided Horus with an effective mechanism for identifying, influencing, and recruiting warriors loyal to his cause. As the Heresy approached, these societies also became channels through which the Word Bearers introduced Traitor Legionaries to the mysteries of the Warp, preparing them for cooperation with the Ruinous Powers.

### The Confessions of the Hydra

Much of the available evidence concerning the Alpha Legion's relationship with the Warp originated from the testimony of an unidentified Legionary who surrendered to the VI Legion shortly after the Siege of Terra. Recorded under the designation LXX and subsequently executed for treason, the prisoner revealed that the Alpha Legion had secretly continued studying and employing psychic powers despite the restrictions established at Nikaea.

According to his testimony, Alpha Legion operatives infiltrated Warrior Lodges within several Traitor Legions, particularly those influenced by the Word Bearers and Sons of Horus. Their objective was to gather intelligence about Daemonic summoning, binding, possession, ritual symbols, and the extraction of supernatural power through sacrifice. Some agents experimented directly with these practices, although many died or disappeared. Unlike the Word Bearers, however, the Alpha Legion regarded the Warp primarily as a weapon and source of intelligence rather than an object of worship. Even after learning of the Chaos Gods, most apparently continued approaching supernatural forces through calculation and experimentation, accepting the risks as part of their clandestine operations.

The testimony also described dangerous experiments involving direct exposure to the Immaterium. In one incident, several Alpha Legionaries entered the Warp without protection; most perished, but two reportedly survived through a form of Daemonic symbiosis, emerging with unnatural abilities. Other operatives studied possession and summoning rituals before returning to share their discoveries with their Primarch. These accounts suggest that the Alpha Legion had developed considerable expertise in forbidden psychic practices while publicly maintaining a reputation for conventional covert warfare.

### The Ultima Taskforce

Following the catastrophic Battle of Calth, Roboute Guilliman recognised that the Ultramarines were dangerously unprepared for the supernatural weapons employed by the Word Bearers. Although he had previously obeyed the Emperor's restrictions on psychic warfare, the devastation demonstrated that conventional military doctrine alone could not counter the growing Daemonic threat.

Guilliman therefore secretly assembled thirteen senior Librarians, placing them within the secure Asteria Wing of the Fortress of Macragge. Alongside selected warriors, they formed the Ultima Taskforce, a clandestine organisation dedicated to studying the Warp and developing practical countermeasures against supernatural enemies.

Drawing upon captured Word Bearers, their knowledge of forbidden rituals, and the experience gained during Calth, the taskforce conducted systematic investigations into Daemonic phenomena. This represented a deliberate departure from the Edict of Nikaea, with Guilliman prioritising the survival of his Legion over continued obedience to restrictions that had left his warriors vulnerable.

Eventually, widespread supernatural disturbances forced the taskforce to relocate to a concealed laboratory on Prandium, where its members received greater resources and continued their research into weaponising captured Warp entities. The ultimate fate of the Ultima Taskforce remains uncertain, although later Imperial accounts suggest that its activities were eventually suppressed or concealed.

Together, these developments reveal three fundamentally different responses to the Warp during the Heresy: Lorgar sought spiritual union with its powers, Horus and the Alpha Legion attempted to exploit them as instruments of war, while Guilliman reluctantly studied them to defend humanity against their influence.

### The Death of Hope

The Horus Heresy shattered the foundations of the Imperium, leaving behind a civilisation consumed by uncertainty, fear, and mistrust. Although the Traitors had been driven from Terra and the Emperor's surviving loyal sons had scattered across the galaxy, the influence of Chaos, Daemons, and hostile xenos powers remained deeply embedded throughout Imperial territory. The Siege of Terra had eliminated the immediate threat to the Throneworld, but the corruption unleashed during the war could no longer be contained.

Looking back, the chronicler identifies numerous moments when humanity's fate might have been fundamentally altered. Had Guilliman learned of Isstvan before Calth, Sanguinius escaped the catastrophe of Signus, Magnus obeyed the Edict of Nikaea, or Jaghatai Khan received greater recognition from the Emperor, the Heresy might have unfolded differently. Similarly, the Alpha Legion might have used its forbidden knowledge to save the Imperium rather than follow Horus and the mysterious Outsider. The chronicler even raises the disturbing possibility that a swift victory for Horus might have spared humanity the endless suffering that followed. Yet these possibilities remain nothing more than the desperate speculation of survivors burdened by grief and guilt.

In the aftermath of the Siege, Terra was largely abandoned by its superhuman defenders, who departed to hunt the fleeing Traitors. Rogal Dorn remained consumed by grief and anger, struggling to rebuild the devastated Imperium while clinging to the Emperor's shattered dream. Roboute Guilliman, whose Legion had not fought on Terra during the Siege, instead concentrated on cultivating relationships with the emerging Imperial leadership and shaping the political narrative of the coming age. The chronicler viewed both Primarchs with suspicion, regarding their conduct as evidence of the weaknesses inherent in the Emperor's creations. The people of Terra had learned to fear their supposed protectors, yet continued placing their hopes in the Primarchs rather than the ordinary humans who had endured the Siege and sacrificed so much.

The survivors increasingly condemned the Emperor's deception and Horus' ambition as the twin causes of humanity's catastrophe. By concealing the truth about the Warp, the Emperor had inadvertently created conditions in which the very powers He sought to suppress could flourish. Horus, driven by pride and the desire for dominion, had opened a gateway to forces that could never again be fully excluded from reality. His death offered little consolation, for the Imperium now faced an apparently endless future of warfare, suspicion, and supernatural corruption.

Most troubling was the fear that the future rulers of Terra would suppress the lessons of the Heresy, concealing uncomfortable truths and perpetuating the ignorance that had contributed to the Imperium's downfall. Even the surviving chroniclers, armed with knowledge of the Emperor's mistakes and the true nature of Chaos, could no longer be certain that humanity possessed the means to escape its fate.

The Emperor's dream of an enlightened and united humanity had died, leaving an Imperium that might survive for millennia but would never truly recover what it had lost.

## A Gate Unsealed

### A Gate Unsealed

### Knives in the Dark

The campaigns at Signus and Chondax marked the opening moves of Horus' rebellion, transforming secret conspiracies into acts of deliberate betrayal. Rather than declaring war through conventional military conquest, Horus employed deception, isolation, and the exploitation of ancient grievances to weaken the Emperor's most powerful defenders. His plans separated the Loyalist Primarchs, disrupted Imperial communications, and exposed entire regions of the galaxy to forces that the Imperial Truth had long denied.

Although neither operation achieved everything Horus intended, their consequences could not be undone. The Blood Angels had been spiritually wounded at Signus, while the White Scars had been isolated and manipulated at Chondax. Both Legions survived, but their experiences destroyed much of the certainty and trust upon which the Great Crusade had been founded. Meanwhile, Daemonic incursions spread across the Imperium, and the Traitors exploited the confusion to claim new territories.

### A Future Written in Blood

### A Rising Storm

The chronicler argues that Horus' greatest crime was not simply his rebellion, but the deliberate destruction of the unity that had made the Imperium powerful. By setting brother against brother and sacrificing entire worlds to personal ambition, he created opportunities for enemies far beyond his own armies.

The catastrophic fighting weakened Imperial control across the galaxy, allowing xenos empires, independent warlords, and the mysterious Outsider to reclaim territories previously conquered during the Great Crusade. Meanwhile, the supernatural catastrophes unleashed at Signus demonstrated that the Warp's corruption could take root in entire planetary populations and remain long after the immediate battle had ended.

Signus and Chondax therefore represented twin wounds in the Imperium's foundations. Their significance lay not only in military losses, but in the destruction of trust, the survival of hidden corruption, and the transformation of once-united Legions into suspicious, isolated forces. The consequences of these campaigns would endure for centuries, perhaps threatening the entire galaxy long after Horus' death.

# Night Lords
For many Legions we are forced to wonder at how truest loyalty can be turned to treachery, purest devotion to hate, nobility to wickedness, but for the Night Lords we must ask if their hearts always belonged to darkness. Created with a higher purpose perhaps their end could have been different, but their story is one of poisoned ideology and atrocity. Even when they were counted loyal, their nature and actions were ever questioned. Some argued that they were simply a function or necessity, the monsters needed to drag a barbaric age into the light. Some say that they were a mistake, a misjudgement compounded by circumstance. A few wonder if they were damned from the moment they were born, that they were never destined to be part of the future they would help create. All such speculation is ultimately pointless; no matter the cause, they are creatures of horror and always were.  

## Legion Structure
The inheritance of Nostramo coiled throughout the structure of the Night Lords. Outwardly they followed a pattern close to many other Legions, but behind this basic skeleton: the courts of Nostramo, the gang traditions and aesthetics of terror infused every aspect of the Legion. The Night Lords were not without pride, and the trappings and titles of aristocracy and dominion formed a key part of their identity, and rivalry, often violent, was endemic among the Legion. There were few amongst their ranks who did not bear some form of title, and the craftsmanship with which they embellished their weapons and armour, was remarkable, if grotesque. Furthermore, they adorned their armour and vehicles with the brutalised and mutilated remains of those who had resisted them, and made an art of flaying and presenting the dead in order to sow fear in their foes.  
At the squad level Night Lords fielded a broad range of units, though taken as a whole the number of breachers and siege configured squads were proportionally rarer than in other Legions. The Night Lords also had a number of unique units: the infamous Terror squads, whose sole purpose was to create and embody the state of horror in their enemies, and the Night Raptors, who would soar above their enemies trailing the bloody remains of their kills while shrieking from modified vox casters. Almost all squads within the Legion had a name that they used in place of the simple designation. Squads within a company might be referred to as Claws, Talons or a number of other epithets often coupled with an indication of hierarchy or honorific: Stygian Talon, Tenth Claw, The Fifth Oathed. The Company was the basic strategic deployment within the Night Lords Legion which might number anywhere between 100 and 1000 warriors. Most companies had a title in addition to their numeric designation. The 27th Company were ‘The Shattered Skull’, the 104th ‘the Sable Brothers’, the 71st ‘The Crimson Judges’ and so on. Unlike many others, the Night Lords used battalions and chapters as semi-permanent grouping of companies, rather than universal structure favouring their own divisions. This seemingly byzantine complexity masked a surprisingly efficient and flexible approach to warfare which allowed the Legion to operate with high degree of fluidity and to be readily fractured into autonomous units or combined into ad hoc formations as their master dictated.  
Konrad Curze was the Dark King of his Legion, a figure of fear for his sons as much as object of loyalty. That many were genuinely loyal to him cannot be doubted, but some have been bound to the Primarch by fear rather than adoration, and some even hated their gene-sire. Curze appeared not to have cared so long as when he commanded, all obeyed.  
Around him the Dark King maintained a court of his most useful sons, called Kyroptera. The members were drawn from senior officers across the Legion. All had a quality that Curze found valuable, though in some cases that quality seems to have been more than distilled bitterness and cruelty. Members of Kyroptera were the ruling elite of Night Lords, and so few others would openly disobey a command from one of them. Alongside these served the Atramentar. A company-strength formation equipped with Terminator armor and armed with Legion’s finest weapons, they were a personal command of the First Captain of the Night Lords. Renowned for their cold brutality in battle and their unswerving loyalty to their Commander and their Primarch, they were enforcers of law and, as widely thought, served as Curze’s executioners, when the need arose. Beneath the Kyroptera were the many captains and companies. The few of these that had been graced with leading several companies under the banner of a battalion or chapter went by a variety of inconsistent ranks including commander, master and regent. Nevertheless, they were still subjected to the commands of different captain, if that captain were of Kyroptera, or exalted in some other way.  
Just as squads and companies bore names, so too did the commanders of the Legion, echoing the cursed nobility and gangs of Nostramo: Talonmaster, the Bloodless, the Sightless Revenant.
At the time of the Dropsite Massacre, the Night Lords Legion had been teetering on the edge of renegade status for years, fighting their own wars with little or no contact with the rest of the Great Crusade’s chain of command. Estimates for the strength of the Legion, therefore, vary wildly from 90 000 to 120 000. The Legion was known to have continued recruiting from subjugated worlds throughout the later part of the Great Crusade, in some cases stealing away the youth of entire systems as the base from which to winnow suitable aspirants. The use of rapid psycho-conditioning and accelerated gene-seed implantation further supported suggestions that their numbers were at least on par with many of the most numerous Legions.

### Night Lords pict-records

#### Headsman Nahmris

Operating as part of a deployment of three separate Chapters of the Night Lords, Headsman Nahmris and the Assault squads assigned to his command participated in the cleansing of three worlds, a campaign lasting nearly three months. In each case they faced only light opposition, most often in the form of Solar Auxilia detachments of the Thramassi Nightwatch, and were easily able to slaughter those that openly opposed them. The majority of the campaign would be spent in the systematic and brutal annihilation of the main planetary population centres, driving the inhabitants into the wilderness to starve. Such campaigns served as an object lesson to those who would oppose the Night Haunter and were key in forcing a number of surrenders by more heavily-forti1ied worlds. Headsman N ahmris himself was hailed for his zeal in such operations and would end the Thramas Crusade at the rank of Centurion thanks to his efforts.

#### Legionary Shek

One of many Night Lords warriors but recently raised to the Legion from the ruined world of Nostramo, Shek is typical of that malicious breed. Raised among the street gangs ofNostramo, he and his brethren paid more heed to the barbaric honour codes that had governed those fractious clans than to the dictates of the Principia Bellicosa, the doctrines of war that were the basis of the old Legiones Astartes. By the beginning of the Thramas Crusade, the 18th Chapter of the Night Lords had become little more than a warped mirror of N ostramo's dark side, more piratical marauders than soldiers of the Imperial Truth and little given to the discipline that had once characterised their breed.

During the Thramas Crusade, the 18th Chapter was one of many involved in the reduction of morale in the Thramas sector, striking at isolated and vulnerable targets on Loyalist worlds with the utmost ferocity. Such raiding forces left behind little but gruesome trophies of their conquests, seeking to break the foe's will to fight and spread fear throughout the Loyalist defenders of the sector. The 18th Chapter would see some early success but falter when confronted by elements of the Dark Angels during later battles, the First Legion's fury stoked by the tales of the atrocities committed by the Night Lords.

#### Huntmaster Kargas

Sporting the modified MkIV armour that was preferred by the Night Lords elite Raptors, Huntmaster Kargas is typical of the Night Lords as a Legion by the end of the Thramas Crusade. Of particular note are the numerous gruesome trophies that adorn his armour, a practise that had become ever more common amongst the Night Lords after the Night Haunter inherited his position as Primarch, and the Nostraman gang signs etched on parchment and affixed directly to the battleplate, each likely noting some ongoing feud within the Legion. Little remained of the old VIIIth Legion that had departed Terra in glory, for even the oldest Terran veterans were now subsumed into the vicious and bloodthirsty Nostraman cult that had infected the Night Lords - a curse that would only grow stronger as the Horus Heresy continued.

## Legion Chronicles

### Unification Wars
The VIIIth Legion were soaked in blood from their birth. The Legion’s first recruits came from the linked prison sinks of Ancient Terra. In vast caverns filled with the half crushed ruins of millennia there lived men and women who had transgressed against the laws of their masters. Condemned never to see the light again or breathe free air, they lived out their lives in fear and blind darkness. There was no law in these lightless lands, and survival existed only by a blade’s edge. Only the strongest and the most ruthless survived in the subterranean warrens, and those who did grew in cruelty and cunning. Fed by a constant influx from the hives above, the prison sinks were an ever hungering gate to madness and murder. But of the millions who lived and died in the sinks, not all had been banished from the world above. Amongst the bloodshed and fear, children were born. Cradled in the dark and raised amongst the dead, those who lived over a decade were pale, silent creatures who moved without a sound. ‘The night’s children’ the prisoners called them, and even the most savage of killers would not seek them out by choice.  
It was from these pale children that the Emperor would make the first warriors of the VIIIth Legion. Dour, with skin so pale it resembled ash or powdered bone, they were far from their brother Legionaries in manner and appearance. The gene-seed of the VIIIth Legion was well paired with the human stock of its first recruits, giving the sons of the underworld the ability to see through darkness to a degree that far exceeded that of other Legions. This gift though was also a curse, forcing them to see the light of suns and stars through filters and flare buffers.
The first use that the Emperor put his VIIIth Legion to was to bring to heel those who believed that the sins of the past could live on in the Imperium. Crimes against the new order took many faces: from the Saragoth Enclave whose gene atrocities continued in secret, to the psy-breeding of the Court of Antius, and the March of Ten Million, all showed that even in the face of the Emperor’s might, some would fall back into the ways of the Old Night.
Whether as a consequence of their genetic inheritance, or the combination of their origins and indoctrination, the warriors of VIIIth Legion seem to have tended towards moral absolutism and a drive to enact retribution. There were no shades of grey in the VIIIth Legion’s moral universe, no degrees of guilt or innocence. Truth and falsehood was as day is to the night. The dark was the realm of guilt, lies and monsters, and those who dwelt in the dark knew only the language of blood, the message of swift and merciless retribution for their actions. The warriors of the VIIIth were creatures made to live in the darkness, and to fight a war for a future of light. In their core, they were warriors for a future without creatures of their kind.

### The Vhnori Resurgence

During the opening years of the Great Crusade, Imperial rule over Terra was still precarious. Vhnori, a vast city enclave formerly belonging to the Panpacific Empire, had been among the last domains of Narthan Dume to fall. Its surface settlements extended for more than a hundred kilometres, while armoured buildings clung to the walls of a deep crevasse. Its inhabitants had accepted Imperial rule reluctantly, and their resentment provided fertile ground for the Crimson Walkers, a cabal of psykers, gene-splicers and surviving warlords connected to the tyrannies of Old Night. Having rebuilt their influence through clandestine alliances across Terra, the renegades restored their flesh-forges and other forbidden practices in Vhnori. Much of the population willingly rallied to them, while those who resisted became victims of their experiments. The prospect of a return to the horrors of the Age of Strife prompted the Emperor to order the VIII Legion to intervene.

Little more than five hundred Night Lords were initially available within the Sol System, far too few for a conventional conquest of a city inhabited by tens of millions. Instead, the Legion turned the enclave into an isolated theatre of psychological warfare. Six hours of orbital bombardment surrounded Vhnori with a kilometre-wide belt of devastation, while artificial darkness and chemically contaminated rain disoriented its inhabitants. Jump-pack troops descended into the crevasse and emerged from beneath the hanging city, broadcasting recordings of suffering as they advanced. The Crimson Walkers unleashed their gene-crafted monsters, including stitch-golems, mutilated thralls and enslaved psykers, but their creations proved insufficient against the Legion. Reinforcements arrived by drop pod and the assault spread through the city, driving the surviving renegades into flight.

The VIII Legion hunted down the dispersed Crimson Walkers, including those intercepted by Seeker squads at the city's perimeter. Their leaders were returned alive to the crevasse and cast into its depths. The Night Lords then destroyed the cliff-bound districts in which civilians had sought shelter, and departed the devastated enclave before natural daylight returned. The account claims that ten million civilians had sheltered in the doomed districts. Vhnori was not merely a suppression of rebellion but an act of exemplary punishment: the destruction of a centre of forbidden power and the terrorisation of an entire population. The campaign illustrates how the Emperor's early VIII Legion used fear as an instrument of Imperial retribution, long before its later reputation became inseparable from Konrad Curze.

### Rediscovery of Konrad Curze
It would be easy to say that Curze changed everything, that the fall of the Night Lords began at the moment the Emperor reunited with his eight son with his Legion. But it would be truer to that it was Nostramo that set both Curze and the Night Lords on the path of treachery. Curze was the father of the VIIIth Legion, but he had two fathers, two hands that shaped his nature and through him the fate of the Legion: the Emperor who spun the substance of Curze’s life, and the planet Nostramo which had raised him and taught him.
Nostramo was a sunless world of suffering, pain and corruption. At the heart of a string of planets which had kept the ability to cross the stars through the Age of Strife, it was a world of sprawling cities, of smoke, industry, and the sweat of millions. Nostramo’s wealth, for wealth there was, lay in the seams of adamantine ore beneath its surface. Worlds far from Nostramo fed on its output, and the mines had long wormed deep into its flesh. Its cities were warrens of stone and iron. Kilometre-tall smoke stacks pointed up at the perpetual night. Bridges of black metal criss-crossed the narrow ravines of alleys and streets. Manses, cathedrals and factories grew from the forest of slums, their faces and roofs crawling with gargoyles. Dust, smoke and the reek of chemicals filled the air, and worked into the flesh of every man, woman and child, trimming away their years so that the best that life could offer was a slow decline in grinding servitude, never glimpsing the brightness of hope or the warmth or the true happiness. The people of Nostramo were pale, and most were thin and gaunt, given by turns of distrust, dark humour and callousness.  
Nostramo and its existence was a horror to equal any xenos enslavement or nightmare of the Dark Age of Technology. Murder was a currency of life and strength came from violence. Every sin great and small had its home in Nostramo’s endless night. It has been said by those remembrancers who recorded its history after its reclamation that during this time weeping and pleading were the sounds carried eternally in the wind.
Corrupt and murderous gangs, whether or not they were named as such, ruled every part of Nostramo. Every inch of the planet was someone’s domain, someone’s territory or hunting ground. The gangs ruled by raw fear, killing and torturing as they pleased, fighting wars with the feral packs of outcasts who were closer to animals than men. The gangs went by names such as Iron Overseers, Hands of Coregado, Sons of Toil, Mirthless with their white-painted faces, the Blade Carrion with their cloaks of tattered velvet and the Chatterers, who cut away their lips to show sharpened teeth.  
Almost a century after the Great Crusade began, the Emperor came to Nostramo. His arrival brought the light of the sun to the night-shrouded world for the first time. Those who witnessed the reunion of the Primarch and his creator, say that Curze submitted to the Emperor’s will as if he had already seen it, as if he was playing out a part he had long feared would fall to him. From that moment on, the fate of the VIIIth Legion was set on a path to oblivion.  
Legions often changed after the discovery of their Primarch and their surrogate world. In the case of VIIIth, Nostramo and Curze doomed them, but at first they seemed the least changed of all of the Legions. Nostraman became the language of the VIIIth Legion, its curling runes and sibilant words spreading as Nostraman recruits began to outnumber the old Terran born. The character of the VIIIth Legion too began to include a dark and cruel sense of humour, and snide fatalism. New traditions, twisted reflections of Nostroman gang-rites and customs were adopted within the Legion, such as marking condemned Legionaries’ gauntlets red to show that a death sentence hung over them.  
These changes, though noticeable, did not touch the heart of the Night Lords Legion’s nature, if anything Curze’s return saw the righteous drive to punish intensify. Their ways and methods of war changed not at all, and the integration of Terran and Nostraman warriors was amongst the swiftest of any Legion. The old Legion and the new fitted together like two sides of a coin: both raised from darkness to create order in strife, both made of flesh born in shunned and lightless places.  
The truth, however, is that the reuniting of Primarch and Legion was the beginning of a spiral that would see the Night Lords descend further into horror and nihilism. After Curze’s departure Nostramo shook off his enforced peace, returning to lawlessness. From this point Nostramo fed the VIIIth not with the finest of its youth, but with gutter scum soaked in blood and cruelty. Some claim that this began to poison the Legion, twisting its purpose and making many Night Lords simple murderers gifted with the strength of demi-gods. Curze came to despise his sons, but he was still their Lord. Far from restraining the Legion he drove it on, bringing peace through atrocity to planet after planet. Sometimes, the only explanation for the decimation of populations, for the skinning pits and crucified cities, seems to be that Night Lords simply enjoyed it. Clearly, the Emperor had concerns about the actions of the VIIIth, but what is not clear is what was done to restrain Curze or his sons. There were words, demands, perhaps even threats, but no action, no hand of judgement to throttle the Night Lords’ crimes. It is a question that will never be answered. The chain of atrocities grew even longer in the decades before Curze finally turned against the Emperor. Of all the Legions, the Night Lords were the most sinister and the most suspect, having been censured for the enormities and massacre carried out in the Emperor’s service. They were creatures of the dark, harnessed to the will of a father wracked by righteousness and foreboding; what else could have been their fate but fall back into the night from which they came?  
When forces were assembled to strike against Horus and the four turncoat Legions, there were many who were surprised to learn that the Night Lords answered the call. Such was the desperate spirit of those times that few questioned Curze’s aid, and those who did perhaps remembered the Night Lords’ need to punish those who strayed from the light. However, their need for retribution had led them to become the traitors and criminals they had once so loathed.

### Velekshar

Velekshar was an otherwise insignificant, uninhabited system of gas giants and airless planets surrounding an aged red star. Its importance lay in its stable Warp routes and proximity to the Ork-infested Kaldrak Cluster, then the target of several Imperial fleets. Among those passing through the system was the 7521st Castigation Fleet, commanded by Balthasar Sul, Regent of the Night Lords' 19th Chapter. By order of Lord Commander Eidolon of the Emperor's Children, a smaller Iron Warriors fleet under Warsmith Vosh had been attached to Sul's command for passage into the war zone. Relations between the commanders were strained, with Sul openly disdainful of his allies and Vosh scarcely recognising his authority.

An immense Ork space hulk unexpectedly emerged from the Warp among the Imperial vessels, accompanied by swarms of smaller craft. The Night Lords scattered, while the slower-reacting Iron Warriors suffered immediate damage and were left to face the enemy. Sul refused to commit his force to a battle he considered unwinnable. Veterans of fighting the greenskins at Godrindan and Musatera, he and his warriors understood that the largest Orks possessed formidable strength and were largely indifferent to the fear upon which the VIII Legion normally relied. The Iron Warriors maintained a fierce defence against boarding attacks and the surrounding fleet, but were eventually overwhelmed despite inflicting enormous losses.

Sul had not abandoned the system itself. His fleet remained active among the outer planets and moons, harassing the Ork vessels in a prolonged campaign of attrition. He also sent a Terror cadre aboard the hulk, at the cost of three crippled ships. Rather than attempting to frighten an enemy resistant to terror, the Night Lords exploited the greenskins' tendency towards internal disorder and rivalry. Over five months, pressure from the fleet and the infiltrators prevented the Orks from consolidating their position or organising an effective campaign against the VIII Legion. Sul's Astropaths continued to request reinforcements throughout the fighting. When a much larger force of Ultramarines, Imperial Fists and Imperial Army formations finally arrived, the Night Lords recovered their surviving infiltrators and withdrew. Sul subsequently argued that his strategy had prevented the greenskins from establishing a fortified foothold on a critical supply route. Although the abandonment of Vosh was regarded by many as dishonourable, the historical account acknowledges that Sul's reasoning may have been genuine and that the system was reclaimed at a lower cost in time and resources.

### The Winnowing

The Winnowing took place in the later decades of the Great Crusade, when demands for the Emperor and War Council to restrain Curze and his Legion were growing. Rather than moderating their behaviour, the Night Lords appeared to become still more uncompromising. The Aeschylrai Systems, a chain of populous worlds near the galactic core, were ruled by warrior mystics and technomancers and were likely the remnants of a larger civilisation broken by the Age of Strife. Technologically sophisticated and resistant to Terran rule, they were a valuable target for Compliance. The campaign was entrusted to Nezurconia Kale, War Mistress of the Verezian and commander of the combined 856th Subjugation Fleet. Kale secured the sparsely populated Tenora system as a staging point before launching successive operations against Cordonus, Murinin and Jalth.

Kale initially expected to complete the conquest within a decade, but insurgency on the worlds already brought to Compliance slowed her advance. Iterators and Imperial officials were murdered, stores destroyed, supplies contaminated and factories sabotaged. An experienced commander, Kale considered the insurgency a manageable complication and informed the War Council without asking for reinforcements. Nevertheless, two Night Lords war fleets under Captain Var Jahan arrived without summons or warning. Ignoring Imperial protests, the VIII Legion subjected Tenora and the other compliant worlds to collective punishment, taking captives from households and publicly executing them. The Legion departed each world in silence, leaving the survivors to comprehend the consequences of continued resistance.

Jahan then extended the campaign to the remaining Aeschylrai worlds, disregarding Kale's authority and requests for restraint. The Legion's bonded tech-priests unleashed datawraiths and subversion djinn into local communications networks, broadcasting the suffering inflicted upon the previously conquered worlds. Rather than limiting their attacks to military objectives, the Night Lords deliberately targeted civilian population centres, using their destruction as a warning to the rest of each planet. After four worlds had undergone this treatment, the Aeschylrai surrendered. Even surrender did not end the reprisals: the Legion destroyed a city on each remaining world to demonstrate the consequences of renewed rebellion before withdrawing. The Winnowing epitomised the VIII Legion's increasingly extreme doctrine of Compliance through collective terror and the growing estrangement between the Night Lords and the wider Imperial war machine.

### The Dark King: The Cheraut Affair

The first recorded violent confrontation between Primarchs preceded both Isstvan V and Prospero. During the Cheraut Compliance, elements of the Emperor’s Children, Imperial Fists and Night Lords, accompanied by their respective Primarchs, fought to subjugate a particularly resistant human civilisation. Curze’s methods and his treatment of the defeated population brought him into increasingly bitter conflict with Dorn. Their dispute escalated into physical combat, leaving the Primarch of the Imperial Fists grievously injured. Witnesses were sworn to secrecy and Curze was placed under guard pending judgement by his brothers. He escaped confinement, killing those who stood in his way, and returned to Nostramo, destroying his adopted home world both as an act of defiance and an attempt to restore order to his domain. He then withdrew to the outer reaches of the Great Crusade.

The Imperial authorities concealed the incident rather than openly punish a Primarch and risk undermining the unity of the Crusade. No public condemnation or punitive expedition followed, and VIII Legion detachments serving with other Expeditionary fleets were not censured. Instead, supplies to the Night Lords’ territories were cut off and Curze was effectively removed from the forefront of the Imperial advance. In the early years of M31, the Court of Terra considered this punishment complete and recalled him, granting him a place in the force assembled to confront Horus’ betrayal. The concealment of the Cheraut incident thus preserved the outward unity of the Legions while leaving its underlying tensions unresolved.

##### The Path of Tears and the Opening Conquest

Following the atrocities at Isstvan, the Night Lords returned to their former domains: the ruins of Nostramo and the hive worlds of Cairn and Kruun. These populations had long endured the rule of the VIII Legion, under which the Night Haunter's harsh laws had gradually deteriorated into a system of honour killings and corruption. They surrendered their sons, daughters and resources to the Warmaster out of fear rather than loyalty. However, these systems had already been largely exhausted. Having replenished their ranks of thralls and their fleets' munition stores, the Night Lords advanced towards Thramas along a chain of inhabited worlds known as the Tithe Road, or Path of Tears. During the Great Crusade, the Legion had regularly exacted tribute from these worlds in exchange for protection, taking recruits from those who survived its brutal initiation trials.

The first world on this route was Tsagualsa, which had paid tribute to the VIII Legion for almost a century. In the closing months of 007.M31, the Night Lords returned under the terms of Horus' Dark Compliance, demanding every inhabitant capable of bearing arms or serving as a thrall, together with the world's meagre resources. Unwilling to sacrifice their homes and families for the Warmaster's distant war, the settlers refused. Their few militia regiments were powerless against the Legion, and Tsagualsa was devastated. A few hundred survivors were permitted to escape aboard decrepit ships so that they might spread news of the consequences of resistance. Upon the ruins of its cities, the Night Lords established the foundations of a new citadel to replace their former holdings, signalling their intention to become permanent overlords of the Eastern Fringe. Independent detachments, sometimes comprising little more than a cruiser and several companies, spread along the Path of Tears and imposed the same demands upon isolated worlds. Within months, refugee vessels crowded the borders of the Thramas sector and the Gulgorahd Protectorate.

##### The Madness of Konrad Curze and the Fracturing of the VIII Legion

By the outbreak of the Heresy, Konrad Curze had already destroyed Nostramo, suffered incarceration on Terra and assaulted his brother Rogal Dorn. The chronicle traces his tragedy to the flawed gift of foresight inherited from the Emperor. Whereas the Emperor could perceive multiple possible futures and navigate between them, Curze saw only a single course of events leading towards failure and death. Neither his achievements during the Great Crusade nor his escape from the horrors of his childhood could alter these visions. By the Crusade's final years he stood on the brink of insanity. His motives for joining Horus remain uncertain: he may have sought to escape the fate he foresaw, or to drown his visions in bloodshed. At the beginning of the Thramas campaign, he was a diminished version of his former self, increasingly neglecting his responsibilities and allowing individual warlords to consolidate their own power.

Originally, the terror employed by the VIII Legion had served a calculated military purpose, breaking the enemy's will before large-scale warfare became necessary. As recruits drawn from the criminal underclasses of Nostramo and neighbouring worlds came to dominate its ranks, however, terror increasingly became an end in itself. The Legion's strict hierarchy gave way to a loose association of warbands and gangs. Two informal factions emerged: warriors who remembered the Legion's former purpose and wished to preserve both it and its Primarch, and those who sought freedom to indulge their brutality without the Night Haunter's interference. The campaign thus became a struggle for the identity of the Legion as well as for control of the Eastern Fringe. Among the leading figures of the latter faction was Nakrid Thole, a charismatic Nostraman warlord and outspoken critic of Curze who commanded a substantial following among the newer recruits.

Thole's warriors led the devastation of the Thramas border worlds. These territories were defended by the Thramassi Nightwatch, regiments of the Solar Auxilia accustomed to guarding the Imperium's distant frontier. Fearing that resistance would bring destruction upon their populations, many garrisons abandoned their bases and withdrew to Thramas, leaving planetary militia and governors to fend for themselves. Some accepted the Warmaster's authority, while others concealed their equipment and disappeared among the civilian population. Within a few months, the Night Lords controlled almost half the sector, from Tsagualsa in the galactic north to the strategic crossroads world of Crucible. On Serk, the governor's refusal to deface monuments honouring loyal Legions provoked a massacre, during which Thole desecrated a statue of Dorn. Curze himself descended upon the hive world of Makator V to exact a bloody tithe from its wealthy merchants, in a grim repetition of his youth on Nostramo that failed to relieve the visions tormenting him.

##### Triplex: The Hidden War and the Traitor Victory

While Thole's followers ravaged the frontier, those who still remembered their duty advanced upon Triplex, the key to Horus' strategic ambitions. The system's military strength was sufficient to resist almost anything short of the entire VIII Legion, and an assault on that scale threatened the very industry the Warmaster wished to capture. A force of four full Chapters entered the system under Sevatar, lord of the Atramentar, and several veteran commanders, many of Terran origin. They discovered that Triplex had already been torn apart by its own civil war. Its outward unity concealed a longstanding rivalry between the three forges. Phall had prospered through the Great Crusade, employing its portion of Legio Victorum at Horus' behest; Galatia accepted a subordinate alliance with its wealthier sibling; and Thule, loyal to the Emperor, had attempted to undermine Phall through covert sabotage and by courting its neighbours. Horus' rebellion enabled Phall to abandon intrigue and unleash its military superiority. Thule's allies deserted it, Galatia ignored its destruction, and by the time the Night Lords arrived the conflict was almost over.

The last stronghold of the magi of Thule lay on Nehren, the outermost world of the system. Its underground fortress was protected by the surviving maniples of Legio Victorum III, the Foe Breakers, as well as orbital defence batteries, automata and thralls of the fallen forge. The Titan landers of the traitor Legio Victorum I and II could not safely deploy while the planetary defences remained operational. Sevatar led the Terminators of the Atramentar in a teleport assault into the fortress, disabling its principal defence cannon. The traitor Titans were then free to engage the remaining Foe Breakers, who fought against forces five times their own strength. Within the fortress, Sevatar personally overcame the automata guarding the Archmagos of Thule and captured him alive, declining any reward. With Triplex secured, the archmagi of Galatia and Phall formally allied themselves with the Warmaster and the Night Lords, offering their armies and the productive capacity of their manufactoria.

Curze arrived in Triplex after the victory, having entrusted the conquest of Thramas itself to Nakrid Thole. Like Sevatar, he rejected the archmagi's rewards and flattery, but claimed the captive Archmagos of Thule as a living trophy aboard his flagship, Nightfall. Although a new fortress was under construction on Tsagualsa, Triplex would serve as the base of the Primarch and the core of the VIII Legion for much of the campaign. Curze spent much of his time secluded in his private sanctum, leaving individual warlords to direct the Legion's operations. At this stage the traitors appeared to have gained an overwhelming advantage, encountering little organised resistance beyond scattered uprisings.

##### The Collapse of Command and the Sheol Incident

Nakrid Thole and his allies expected the remaining worlds, terrified by the fate of their neighbours and burdened by refugees, to surrender without significant resistance. A loose alliance of Chapters moved against the Gulgorahd Protectorate, while Thole claimed the right to receive the surrender of Thramas himself. With Curze increasingly absent from command, the Legion's warlords pursued personal territories and spoils rather than the common objectives of Horus. On the young hive world of Sheol III, two newly founded Chapters, the Dark Eyes and the Boneless, came into conflict. Both were composed almost entirely of the final intake of Nostraman recruits, were understrength and lacked heavy armour, making the world's population and industrial capacity attractive prizes. Their praetors attempted to settle their competing claims, but the dispute erupted into a savage internecine war. The Dark Eyes destroyed most of their rivals; the surviving Boneless were forced to adopt the victors' colours and join their ranks. The incident demonstrated how far the VIII Legion had deteriorated without a unifying leader or even a common enemy.

##### Total War and the Return of the Night Haunter

News of the uprising spread throughout the Eastern Fringe. Concealed Nightwatch soldiers emerged as guerrillas and saboteurs, attacking supply installations and isolated garrisons, while the armies of Thramas and Gulgorahd went on the offensive. The scattered Night Lords warbands were initially caught off guard, but retaliated with brutal reprisals and drew their enemies into urban warfare, where the superior numbers of the Nightwatch were offset by the skill of the Legiones Astartes. The conflict engulfed the sector, halting trade and bringing famine and disease even to worlds spared direct combat. The loyalists failed to capitalise on their initial advantage and became bogged down defending the few worlds they had secured. Qetesh Prime and Crucible became battlefields of relentless attrition; Thramas endured repeated raids ordered by the disfigured Nakrid Thole; and Gulgorahd, caught between the Night Lords and traitor worlds of the Aegis sector, struggled to hold its frontier. Even after the surface of Bastion-019 was scorched and irradiated, its Knights and automata continued fighting from subterranean fortifications.

For a time, the loyalists hoped that by holding their worlds they could deny Horus the resources of the Eastern Fringe and force the enemy into an exhausting stalemate. The return of Curze, accompanied by the armies of Triplex and the most loyal elements of his Legion, shattered this balance. Sheol III and Yaelis fell within a week. The defenders of Sheol destroyed their central hive spire rather than surrender. On Yaelis, survivors of a dozen decimated Auxilia regiments withdrew into the manufactoria of the lower hive, where Curze and his forces eliminated the remaining resistance. Bastions 011 and 009 fell in some of the largest Titan engagements of the campaign: the traitor Legio Victorum I and II and Legio Phasma, long bound to the Night Lords, opposed the bulk of Gulgorahd's Legio Adamantus and elements of the loyal Legios Atrox and Saevus. Nearly a hundred god-engines fell in a month of fighting. Legio Adamantus lived up to its name, the Unbroken, as many maniples fought to the last rather than retreat.

At this point in the chronicle, the loyalist cause stood on the brink of destruction. Curze's intervention had restored the traitors' decisive superiority, and defeat appeared to be a matter of time. Yet the introduction foreshadows the arrival of the Lion's army: if Thramas could endure long enough, the balance of the war might be reversed. The supplied excerpt ends before that intervention and does not describe the subsequent stages of the campaign.

#### A Legion Accursed

The Thramas Crusade was not merely a war between the First and Eighth Legions, but a decisive stage in the internal destruction of the Night Lords. The roots of their downfall lay in their origins on Terra, in the Emperor’s obscure designs and, above all, in the nature of their Primarch. During the Heresy, the VIII Legion turned upon itself in an attempt to escape the curse it associated with Konrad Curze. Its warriors blamed the corruption of their brotherhood on influences that seemed external to its original character, yet the source of the tragedy had always been at its heart: the Night Haunter himself. The destruction of the Legion as a unified force at Thramas was only a prelude to the darker future he had foreseen.

Curze had inherited a limited fragment of the Emperor’s foresight, which revealed to him a terrible possible future without granting him his father’s understanding of it. He had once tried to change that destiny through punishment, bloodshed and conquest, hoping that he could secure a place for himself and his sons in the Emperor’s future. Even Horus’ rebellion, which overturned the course of the Great Crusade, failed to alter the visions that tormented him. Convinced increasingly that his actions could make no difference, Curze entered the Thramas Crusade embittered by the apparent futility of his struggle, accepting the doom that he had once fought to avert.

#### The Outriders of Rebellion

At the outset of the Horus Heresy, the Night Lords appeared to be a powerful and dependable element of the Warmaster’s armies. They were experienced warriors with grievances against the Loyalists; Curze’s confinement following the Cheraut affair, his confrontation with Rogal Dorn and the destruction of Nostramo gave other Traitors reason to trust his opposition to the Emperor. After the Dropsite Massacre, Horus assigned the VIII Legion a role as the advance guard of his rebellion. They were to intimidate undecided worlds, demonstrate the consequences of resistance and secure their submission through Dark Compliance. Those who accepted the Warmaster’s rule would receive his protection from the very warriors who threatened them.

This strategy brought dozens of systems under Horus’ control and, within a few months, secured much of the northern Imperium as a base for the assault upon Terra. But a stable realm capable of supplying the rebellion could not coexist with the indiscriminate punishments Curze inflicted for every perceived betrayal or failure of production. The original VIII Legion had refined cruelty into an instrument of coercion; many of its formations now treated cruelty as an end in itself. Horus therefore redirected the Night Lords to the Eastern Fringe, where they were expected to expand his dominion and acquire additional manpower, industrial capacity and supplies for his growing armada.

#### An Empire Built on Fear

The Nostramo sector contained nearly a hundred inhabited systems, including prosperous colonies and populous hive worlds. Even after the destruction of its capital world, it represented an important source of recruits, labour and manufacturing capacity. With little attachment to any Loyalist commander, its population appeared well suited to Horus’ cause. For years the region had lived under Curze’s unforgiving legal code, which maintained order through the threat of severe punishment. During his absence in the latter part of the Great Crusade, many worlds overthrew the regimes imposed upon them by the Night Lords. Criminal syndicates and gangs regained control, reproducing the very corruption that the Primarch had sought to suppress.

These new rulers might have supplied the Warmaster as readily as legitimate governments, but Curze regarded them as a personal affront. The Syndarchs of the Blood Moon syndicate assembled on Kehdure IV to offer their allegiance, expecting to preserve their authority in exchange for tribute. Rather than accept their resources, the Night Haunter destroyed them and devastated their world. Even populations that had largely maintained his laws were subjected to further punishments. While other Traitor Legions fought for the routes towards Terra and contested worlds such as Paramar and Karadoc, the Night Lords pursued what amounted to a private war. Curze interpreted the collapse of his former realm as the first fulfilment of his visions of personal ruin and the damnation of his Legion. His campaign of retribution was an increasingly desperate attempt to prevent that future.

#### The Price of Infamy

The decay of the Nostramo sector had also undermined the quality of the Legion’s recruits. The VIII drew its new warriors from a comparatively narrow region surrounding Nostramo, and many of its later Companies consisted almost entirely of recruits raised under corrupt criminal regimes rather than the harsh but disciplined traditions of the old Legion. They remained formidable fighters, but lacked the restraint, cohesion and commitment to the Great Crusade associated with the Terran veterans and earlier Nostraman intakes. The original principle of retribution, in which wrongdoing incurred punishment, had given way to the belief that the strong could do as they pleased. The recruitment system brought the most ruthless elements of that society into the ranks of the Legion.

When Curze unleashed these warriors upon his former domain, they operated as scattered warbands and raiding hosts rather than a disciplined army. Their actions caused immense terror but left few populations capable of fulfilling the tithes demanded by Horus. The old veterans, by contrast, still employed a more measured and purposeful form of violence that yielded greater material benefits. Yet they lost influence over their Primarch as his visions drove him towards increasingly excessive retribution. Curze listened more readily to the younger and more vicious commanders whose methods appeared to answer his desperation. By the time the fleet assembled for the invasion of Thramas, the two groups regarded one another as threats to the future of the VIII Legion.

#### A Cross of Bone, A Hand of Blood

The Thramas campaign became the setting for a concealed struggle over the identity of the Night Lords and control of their Primarch. One faction sought to exploit the freedom afforded by Horus’ rebellion, abandoning the restraints of the Great Crusade and recreating the Legion in the image of Nostramo’s ancient gangs. Although united by that ambition, these warriors formed a loose collection of competing warlords rather than a disciplined political bloc. Their emblem was the Cross of Bone, whose appearance varied between warbands and whose origins lay in the traditions of worlds in the Nostramo sector. They intended to establish a kingdom of their own within Horus’ new empire, free to raid and rule as they pleased. Their principal figure was Nakrid Thole, a late Nostraman recruit shaped by hive-gang warfare and the punitive campaigns of the latter Great Crusade. Thole and his allies viewed Curze as a distant, increasingly unstable visionary whose withdrawal would allow them to pursue their ambitions unchecked.

The opposing faction included Terran veterans, older Nostraman warriors and some recent recruits who retained respect for their Primarch and the older traditions of the Legion. They were committed to Horus’ rebellion, not to the Emperor, but still regarded terror as an instrument of duty governed by a severe code rather than an excuse for random slaughter. They aspired to restore the VIII Legion’s military standing and secure an honoured position under the new regime, which required a strong and active Primarch. Sevatar was the foremost symbol of this faction, though he personally disdained its politics. His red gauntlet, traditionally a Nostraman death-mark, became the emblem of those who remained devoted to Curze. The irony was that a sign of dishonour now represented fidelity to the Primarch. Sevatar worked to further Curze’s objectives and strengthen his resolve, while Anrek Barbatos of the Primarch’s guard and sympathetic captains and praetors coordinated the faction’s efforts.

The conflict was initially covert: honour duels, suspicious battlefield incidents, accusations and political isolation gradually displaced the Legion’s former unity. During the advance along the Tithe Road and into the Thramas sector, rival commanders competed for prestige and access to Curze. Thole exploited the deaths of both his warriors and other members of the Legion to strengthen his influence. The Cross of Bone gained ascendancy through its battlefield successes and appeals to the appetites of the rank and file, leaving the older traditions of the VIII Legion increasingly marginalised.

#### A Decline Long Foreseen

While his sons fought over his allegiance, Curze withdrew into his own despair. Neither the order he had imposed upon Nostramo, the attempts to master his foresight under the Emperor and his brothers, nor the slaughter committed in Horus’ service had changed his visions. The fall of his domain into corruption and the outbreak of civil war seemed to confirm what he had long feared: an age of ten thousand years of violence that would continue beyond his own death. Following the failure of his efforts in Nostramo and the opening stages of the Thramas Crusade, he secluded himself in the unfinished fortress on Tsagualsa, surrendering much of the actual conduct of the war to Thole and his allies. Their campaigns placed greater value on the death toll than on strategic achievement. Sevatar and other loyal members of his inner circle remained bound to their Primarch, awaiting his orders and trying to persuade him to return to the battlefield.

The encounter with Lion El’Jonson at Tsagualsa briefly drew Curze out of his seclusion. His motives are not established in the account: he may have sought answers, attempted to warn his brother, or hoped to die at his hands and thus escape his foretold destiny. The confrontation achieved none of these possible ends. A similar pattern recurred at Sheol, where Curze confronted the Lion again and his most devoted warriors sacrificed themselves to recover their grievously wounded Primarch. Even incapacitated, he remained the central figure in the struggle between those who wished to free the Legion from his influence and those determined to restore him to his former greatness.

#### The Butcher’s Bill: The Kyroptera and the Legion’s Ruin

Curze’s decision to grant his most headstrong commanders freedom ultimately undermined their position. Their forces had become overextended in campaigns they could no longer sustain. During the closing months of the Thramas Crusade, Nakrid Thole fell at Thramas and Vaeduc the Maimed at Sheol, while Malithos and Cel Herec died at the hands of Sevatar and his Atramentar. With these figures removed, the faction loyal to the Primarch regained control. Sevatar assembled a new inner council, the Kyroptera, composed of warriors he trusted to preserve the Legion. With Curze near death, the council adopted a more pragmatic course than that which had dominated the earlier campaign.

The Kyroptera intended to abandon Thramas and the Nostramo sector, gather the surviving formations and return to Horus in the Segmentum Solar. Sevatar sought to restore the VIII Legion as a unified army fighting openly for the Warmaster, rather than as a collection of predatory wardens of a failed realm. In his conception, conventional warfare offered a means of escaping the burden of Nostramo’s destruction and the Night Haunter’s increasingly destructive obsession with his visions. He secured the adherence of the surviving fleet commanders through persuasion or force, but the Dark Angels intercepted the assembled ships before they could escape.

The First Legion inflicted further losses upon the Night Lords, yet the decisive blow to Sevatar’s plan came from Curze himself. Awakening from his incapacitation as the fleet approached its intended Warp translation point, he ordered another desperate assault upon the Dark Angels. Many Night Lords obeyed their gene-father rather than abandon him to the Loyalists. The attack failed to halt the pursuing fleet and shattered the cohesion Sevatar had struggled to restore. Those vessels that escaped scattered through the Warp without the means to reform the Legion, while those that turned back to fight were almost annihilated. Curze was left aboard the Dark Angels’ flagship, captive and confronted with the knowledge that his own actions had helped to realise the fate he had sought to escape. The surviving VIII Legion became a dispersed host of warbands, carrying its violence into the wider Imperium. The account closes by presenting this outcome as the eclipse of Konrad Curze by the Night Haunter: the next stage of his visions, not yet their final fulfilment.

### Scouring of Nostramo Sector [017.M31]

In 017.M31, following Horus' defeat, massive Loyalist retribution fleets launched the Scouring, pursuing the surviving Traitor Legions across the galaxy. Forces from Baal, Ultramar, and other Loyalist strongholds entered the Nostramo Sector, expecting fierce resistance from the Night Lords, who had spent nearly a decade fortifying their territory. The Imperial armada carried numerous Astartes battalions and vast stockpiles of previously forbidden weapons, including radiation and biological armaments, intending to eradicate the VIII Legion's remaining strongholds.

Instead, the Loyalists discovered an almost deserted sector. Only scattered Night Lords warbands remained, none larger than a reinforced company, alongside ruined fortresses and destroyed warships apparently devastated by an unknown enemy. Although intelligence suggested that approximately one-third of the VIII Legion's remaining strength was stationed there, six months of reconnaissance uncovered no trace of the missing warriors. The fleet eventually withdrew to Caith Laes before departing for other campaigns, leaving the fate of some 30,000 Night Lords unexplained. The mysterious destruction of their forces became one of the great unresolved questions of the Scouring.

The documents investigate a mystery from the Scouring: the disappearance of roughly 30,000 Night Lords from the Nostramo Sector. In 017.M31, Loyalist forces arrived expecting a major confrontation but discovered abandoned fortresses, destroyed warships, and massacred VIII Legion garrisons. At the fortress-moon Tenebor, Blood Angels investigators found hundreds of dead Night Lords, apparently killed by unidentified Astartes wearing black-and-red armour and using ancient Legion weaponry. Similar mysterious heraldry was later discovered among Astartes remains on the ruined hive world of Cairn.

The strongest explanation points towards the Ashen Claws, formerly the 18th Chapter of the Raven Guard. Led by Nerat Kirine, these predominantly Terran-born veterans had been exiled by Corvus Corax following the Battle of Gate Forty-Two because of their association with the old Legion's brutal traditions and loyalty to Horus. Sent into the distant Ghoul Stars, they disappeared from Imperial records and eventually developed into an independent force, owing allegiance to neither Horus nor the Emperor.

Their involvement is particularly evident during the Battle of Desperation, where mysterious Astartes warships attacked the Night Lords while their black-and-red armoured warriors simultaneously ambushed Ultramarines and looted military installations. The attackers then withdrew without identifying themselves. The documents strongly suggest that the Ashen Claws were responsible for devastating the Night Lords' Nostramo holdings, although the full extent of their campaign remains unconfirmed.

Essentially, the mystery is that a forgotten Raven Guard offshoot may have independently destroyed much of the Night Lords' remaining empire, while fighting Loyalists whenever it suited its own interests.

The collapse of the Nostramo Sector was partly caused by the Ashen Claws, a renegade Raven Guard fleet that had abandoned Imperial service in favour of raiding and acquiring resources. Originally dispatched in 002.M31 under Praetor Calvus, a veteran loyal to Corax, the expedition comprised approximately 4,000 Astartes, 13 capital warships, and 20 smaller vessels. By its return in 011.M31, Calvus had likely died, and nine years of isolation had apparently transformed the force into an independent, mercenary warband.

However, the Ashen Claws were not solely responsible for Nostramo's destruction. Increasing xenos incursions from the Ghoul Stars and bitter infighting among the leaderless Night Lords had already weakened the sector. The Ashen Claws exploited this instability, devastating Night Lords strongholds and plundering supplies. Following the sector's collapse, the High Lords of Terra declared Nostramo and neighbouring regions forbidden to most Imperial vessels. The ultimate fate of the Ashen Claws remains unknown, and they may still survive somewhere beyond Imperial control.

# Night Lords
For many Legions we are forced to wonder at how truest loyalty can be turned to treachery, purest devotion to hate, nobility to wickedness, but for the Night Lords we must ask if their hearts always belonged to darkness. Created with a higher purpose perhaps their end could have been different, but their story is one of poisoned ideology and atrocity. Even when they were counted loyal, their nature and actions were ever questioned. Some argued that they were simply a function or necessity, the monsters needed to drag a barbaric age into the light. Some say that they were a mistake, a misjudgement compounded by circumstance. A few wonder if they were damned from the moment they were born, that they were never destined to be part of the future they would help create. All such speculation is ultimately pointless; no matter the cause, they are creatures of horror and always were.  

## Legion Structure
The inheritance of Nostramo coiled throughout the structure of the Night Lords. Outwardly they followed a pattern close to many other Legions, but behind this basic skeleton: the courts of Nostramo, the gang traditions and aesthetics of terror infused every aspect of the Legion. The Night Lords were not without pride, and the trappings and titles of aristocracy and dominion formed a key part of their identity, and rivalry, often violent, was endemic among the Legion. There were few amongst their ranks who did not bear some form of title, and the craftsmanship with which they embellished their weapons and armour, was remarkable, if grotesque. Furthermore, they adorned their armour and vehicles with the brutalised and mutilated remains of those who had resisted them, and made an art of flaying and presenting the dead in order to sow fear in their foes.  
At the squad level Night Lords fielded a broad range of units, though taken as a whole the number of breachers and siege configured squads were proportionally rarer than in other Legions. The Night Lords also had a number of unique units: the infamous Terror squads, whose sole purpose was to create and embody the state of horror in their enemies, and the Night Raptors, who would soar above their enemies trailing the bloody remains of their kills while shrieking from modified vox casters. Almost all squads within the Legion had a name that they used in place of the simple designation. Squads within a company might be referred to as Claws, Talons or a number of other epithets often coupled with an indication of hierarchy or honorific: Stygian Talon, Tenth Claw, The Fifth Oathed. The Company was the basic strategic deployment within the Night Lords Legion which might number anywhere between 100 and 1000 warriors. Most companies had a title in addition to their numeric designation. The 27th Company were ‘The Shattered Skull’, the 104th ‘the Sable Brothers’, the 71st ‘The Crimson Judges’ and so on. Unlike many others, the Night Lords used battalions and chapters as semi-permanent grouping of companies, rather than universal structure favouring their own divisions. This seemingly byzantine complexity masked a surprisingly efficient and flexible approach to warfare which allowed the Legion to operate with high degree of fluidity and to be readily fractured into autonomous units or combined into ad hoc formations as their master dictated.  
Konrad Curze was the Dark King of his Legion, a figure of fear for his sons as much as object of loyalty. That many were genuinely loyal to him cannot be doubted, but some have been bound to the Primarch by fear rather than adoration, and some even hated their gene-sire. Curze appeared not to have cared so long as when he commanded, all obeyed.  
Around him the Dark King maintained a court of his most useful sons, called Kyroptera. The members were drawn from senior officers across the Legion. All had a quality that Curze found valuable, though in some cases that quality seems to have been more than distilled bitterness and cruelty. Members of Kyroptera were the ruling elite of Night Lords, and so few others would openly disobey a command from one of them. Alongside these served the Atramentar. A company-strength formation equipped with Terminator armor and armed with Legion’s finest weapons, they were a personal command of the First Captain of the Night Lords. Renowned for their cold brutality in battle and their unswerving loyalty to their Commander and their Primarch, they were enforcers of law and, as widely thought, served as Curze’s executioners, when the need arose. Beneath the Kyroptera were the many captains and companies. The few of these that had been graced with leading several companies under the banner of a battalion or chapter went by a variety of inconsistent ranks including commander, master and regent. Nevertheless, they were still subjected to the commands of different captain, if that captain were of Kyroptera, or exalted in some other way.  
Just as squads and companies bore names, so too did the commanders of the Legion, echoing the cursed nobility and gangs of Nostramo: Talonmaster, the Bloodless, the Sightless Revenant.   
At the time of the Dropsite Massacre, the Night Lords Legion had been teetering on the edge of renegade status for years, fighting their own wars with little or no contact with the rest of the Great Crusade’s chain of command. Estimates for the strength of the Legion, therefore, vary wildly from 90 000 to 120 000. The Legion was known to have continued recruiting from subjugated worlds throughout the later part of the Great Crusade, in some cases stealing away the youth of entire systems as the base from which to winnow suitable aspirants. The use of rapid psycho-conditioning and accelerated gene-seed implantation further supported suggestions that their numbers were at least on par with many of the most numerous Legions.

## Legion Chronicles
### Unification Wars
The VIIIth Legion were soaked in blood from their birth. The Legion’s first recruits came from the linked prison sinks of Ancient Terra. In vast caverns filled with the half crushed ruins of millennia there lived men and women who had transgressed against the laws of their masters. Condemned never to see the light again or breathe free air, they lived out their lives in fear and blind darkness. There was no law in these lightless lands, and survival existed only by a blade’s edge. Only the strongest and the most ruthless survived in the subterranean warrens, and those who did grew in cruelty and cunning. Fed by a constant influx from the hives above, the prison sinks were an ever hungering gate to madness and murder. But of the millions who lived and died in the sinks, not all had been banished from the world above. Amongst the bloodshed and fear, children were born. Cradled in the dark and raised amongst the dead, those who lived over a decade were pale, silent creatures who moved without a sound. ‘The night’s children’ the prisoners called them, and even the most savage of killers would not seek them out by choice.  
It was from these pale children that the Emperor would make the first warriors of the VIIIth Legion. Dour, with skin so pale it resembled ash or powdered bone, they were far from their brother Legionaries in manner and appearance. The gene-seed of the VIIIth Legion was well paired with the human stock of its first recruits, giving the sons of the underworld the ability to see through darkness to a degree that far exceeded that of other Legions. This gift though was also a curse, forcing them to see the light of suns and stars through filters and flare buffers.   
The first use that the Emperor put his VIIIth Legion to was to bring to heel those who believed that the sins of the past could live on in the Imperium. Crimes against the new order took many faces: from the Saragoth Enclave whose gene atrocities continued in secret, to the psy-breeding of the Court of Antius, and the March of Ten Million, all showed that even in the face of the Emperor’s might, some would fall back into the ways of the Old Night.   
Whether as a consequence of their genetic inheritance, or the combination of their origins and indoctrination, the warriors of VIIIth Legion seem to have tended towards moral absolutism and a drive to enact retribution. There were no shades of grey in the VIIIth Legion’s moral universe, no degrees of guilt or innocence. Truth and falsehood was as day is to the night. The dark was the realm of guilt, lies and monsters, and those who dwelt in the dark knew only the language of blood, the message of swift and merciless retribution for their actions. The warriors of the VIIIth were creatures made to live in the darkness, and to fight a war for a future of light. In their core, they were warriors for a future without creatures of their kind.  

### Rediscovery of Konrad Curze
It would be easy to say that Curze changed everything, that the fall of the Night Lords began at the moment the Emperor reunited with his eight son with his Legion. But it would be truer to that it was Nostramo that set both Curze and the Night Lords on the path of treachery. Curze was the father of the VIIIth Legion, but he had two fathers, two hands that shaped his nature and through him the fate of the Legion: the Emperor who spun the substance of Curze’s life, and the planet Nostramo which had raised him and taught him.   
Nostramo was a sunless world of suffering, pain and corruption. At the heart of a string of planets which had kept the ability to cross the stars through the Age of Strife, it was a world of sprawling cities, of smoke, industry, and the sweat of millions. Nostramo’s wealth, for wealth there was, lay in the seams of adamantine ore beneath its surface. Worlds far from Nostramo fed on its output, and the mines had long wormed deep into its flesh. Its cities were warrens of stone and iron. Kilometre-tall smoke stacks pointed up at the perpetual night. Bridges of black metal criss-crossed the narrow ravines of alleys and streets. Manses, cathedrals and factories grew from the forest of slums, their faces and roofs crawling with gargoyles. Dust, smoke and the reek of chemicals filled the air, and worked into the flesh of every man, woman and child, trimming away their years so that the best that life could offer was a slow decline in grinding servitude, never glimpsing the brightness of hope or the warmth or the true happiness. The people of Nostramo were pale, and most were thin and gaunt, given by turns of distrust, dark humour and callousness.  
Nostramo and its existence was a horror to equal any xenos enslavement or nightmare of the Dark Age of Technology. Murder was a currency of life and strength came from violence. Every sin great and small had its home in Nostramo’s endless night. It has been said by those remembrancers who recorded its history after its reclamation that during this time weeping and pleading were the sounds carried eternally in the wind.   
Corrupt and murderous gangs, whether or not they were named as such, ruled every part of Nostramo. Every inch of the planet was someone’s domain, someone’s territory or hunting ground. The gangs ruled by raw fear, killing and torturing as they pleased, fighting wars with the feral packs of outcasts who were closer to animals than men. The gangs went by names such as Iron Overseers, Hands of Coregado, Sons of Toil, Mirthless with their white-painted faces, the Blade Carrion with their cloaks of tattered velvet and the Chatterers, who cut away their lips to show sharpened teeth.  
Almost a century after the Great Crusade began, the Emperor came to Nostramo. His arrival brought the light of the sun to the night-shrouded world for the first time. Those who witnessed the reunion of the Primarch and his creator, say that Curze submitted to the Emperor’s will as if he had already seen it, as if he was playing out a part he had long feared would fall to him. From that moment on, the fate of the VIIIth Legion was set on a path to oblivion.  
Legions often changed after the discovery of their Primarch and their surrogate world. In the case of VIIIth, Nostramo and Curze doomed them, but at first they seemed the least changed of all of the Legions. Nostraman became the language of the VIIIth Legion, its curling runes and sibilant words spreading as Nostraman recruits began to outnumber the old Terran born. The character of the VIIIth Legion too began to include a dark and cruel sense of humour, and snide fatalism. New traditions, twisted reflections of Nostroman gang-rites and customs were adopted within the Legion, such as marking condemned Legionaries’ gauntlets red to show that a death sentence hung over them.  
These changes, though noticeable, did not touch the heart of the Night Lords Legion’s nature, if anything Curze’s return saw the righteous drive to punish intensify. Their ways and methods of war changed not at all, and the integration of Terran and Nostraman warriors was amongst the swiftest of any Legion. The old Legion and the new fitted together like two sides of a coin: both raised from darkness to create order in strife, both made of flesh born in shunned and lightless places.  
The truth, however, is that the reuniting of Primarch and Legion was the beginning of a spiral that would see the Night Lords descend further into horror and nihilism. After Curze’s departure Nostramo shook off his enforced peace, returning to lawlessness. From this point Nostramo fed the VIIIth not with the finest of its youth, but with gutter scum soaked in blood and cruelty. Some claim that this began to poison the Legion, twisting its purpose and making many Night Lords simple murderers gifted with the strength of demi-gods. Curze came to despise his sons, but he was still their Lord. Far from restraining the Legion he drove it on, bringing peace through atrocity to planet after planet. Sometimes, the only explanation for the decimation of populations, for the skinning pits and crucified cities, seems to be that Night Lords simply enjoyed it. Clearly, the Emperor had concerns about the actions of the VIIIth, but what is not clear is what was done to restrain Curze or his sons. There were words, demands, perhaps even threats, but no action, no hand of judgement to throttle the Night Lords’ crimes. It is a question that will never be answered. The chain of atrocities grew even longer in the decades before Curze finally turned against the Emperor. Of all the Legions, the Night Lords were the most sinister and the most suspect, having been censured for the enormities and massacre carried out in the Emperor’s service. They were creatures of the dark, harnessed to the will of a father wracked by righteousness and foreboding; what else could have been their fate but fall back into the night from which they came?  
When forces were assembled to strike against Horus and the four turncoat Legions, there were many who were surprised to learn that the Night Lords answered the call. Such was the desperate spirit of those times that few questioned Curze’s aid, and those who did perhaps remembered the Night Lords’ need to punish those who strayed from the light. However, their need for retribution had led them to become the traitors and criminals they had once so loathed.

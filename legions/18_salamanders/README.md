# Salamanders
In the great war of Horus Heresy, many deeds of infamy and valour were writ upon the bloody stars, and at the center of this maelstrom lay the Salamanders Legion; betrayed and struck down, laid waste and yet resolute against the foe. There are few Legions who have paid such a high price for their loyalty and honour as the Salamanders. There are fewer still who have so willingly, and so often shed their blood in Mankind’s defence beyond their measure and due. The Salamanders are such a Legion, and though driven to near destruction not once, but time and again, they have each time arisen from the ashes of war, stronger and tempered like the peerless steel blades fashioned in the legendary forges of their homeworld: unbroken, undefeated and true to their cause.  

## Legion Structure
When Vulkan took over his Legion, the XVIIIth had already developed a distinct character of its own - its scattered deployment and tendency towards asymmetrical engagements had created a considerable bias in its structures and tactics.  

Company level detachments, numbering a nominal hundred to around two hundred Legiones Astartes under a single captain were the principal unit of strategic action and logistic support, with larger battalion and chapter distinctions in practice seldom needed. The Legion’s senior command acted as a kind of heavy strategic reserve, weighing in with a concentration of force where it was most needed. At the battlefield level, circumstance as well as natural temperament focused the Legion’s tactics on relatively short range engagements. Here the individual physical power of the Space Marine, their ability to endure and relentless fury in battle, could be relied upon to inflict disproportionate damage on almost any foe. Such tactics naturally lent themselves to short-range but devastating arms such as flame, volkite-ray and melta weapons being preferred by the Legion. Supporting this tendency was the telling factor that even before the Nocturnian influence came to the fore, the technical aptitude demonstrated by the Legion allowed them to maintain and indeed field-manufacture such advanced weapons.  

One of Vulkan’s first actions was to unify his scattered Legion and do away with its ad-hoc strategic organisation, preserving, where possible, autonomy and self-sufficiency the units of XVIIIth had developed. At the strategic level, Vulkan ordered his Legion into the formation of seven ‘Realms’, each linked both in name and spirit with one of Nocturne’s seven great city-settlements. To each he assigned a Lord Commander, known also as a Protector, as it was their sworn duty, to protect the city-settlement in time of attack. Each city-settlement served as a focus of the Legion’s recruitment and their governance on the planet, anchoring the Legionaries to the humans for whom they fought, and each city-settlement went on to exhibit its own unique influences on the Legion’s evolving traditions. The strong links between the Salamanders brethren and the people of Nocturne came quickly to create bonds of kinship and loyalty within each company’s ranks. It is worthy of note that this grounded bias towards recruitment on Nocturne did have the adverse effect that slowed the pace at which the Legion’s numbers were increased. The counterpoint to this difficulty was, however, that Nocturne’s native population, although relatively small, were hardened survivors all, strong of constitution and psychologically resilient, therefore they made for excellent subjects for initiation into the Legion with a very low rate of implant rejection.  

Vulkan and his Legion also developed the asymmetrical warfare practiced early on by the XVIIIth to an even greater virtue, tempering the willingness to sacrifice with judgement as to the value of the long-term outcome, developing a mastery of close range fire-fights and Zone Mortalis operations. This superiority manifested most visibly in the Legion’s weapons, as under Vulkan’s mastery, the Salamanders became justly famed for its superior artifice in the creation of arms, from the bolters carried by its rank to the savage implements of destruction carried by the Legion’s Dreadnoughts. Each weapon was both a thing of lethal beauty and a masterwork of form and function. Accordingly, they maintained a high proportion of special weapon units, in particular flamer squads, including the uniquely armed Pyroclast elite who carried prototype and highly adaptable flame-projector weapons. Likewise, their Terminator armor-equipped forces were also noteworthy and regarded as amongst the most powerful in any Legion, with each armor suit being embellished and modified by its owner, with such innovation leading to introduction of the earliest strom shields in Legion service.  

The Salamanders Legion also maintained and manufactured a substantial armoury of heavy vehicles, biased towards tanks, armoured rams, gunships and mobile support weapons. The Salamanders Legion did not favour attritional warfare in its tactics, nor did it favour speed over survivability.  

### Command Hierarchy
The Legion was organised in seven ‘Realms’ each under the command of its Lord Protector, below which numerous line companies, each of around 120 Space Marines were formed. Aside from specialists, the Legion followed a simplified form transiting in descending order of rank from captain to lieutenant to master sergeant, sergeant and Legionary. Seniority was determined by length of service. Noted equality of speech and demeanour was practiced within the Legion outside of the press of battle, with little value given to the formality or rigid hierarchy practiced by Legions such as the Ultramarines or the Emperor’s Children.

Beyond this simple, but cohesive system, reinforced by self-reliance, self-discipline and loyalty promoted by the Promethean Cult a wider variety of titles of renown and spheres of influence also evolved. These developed as a record of deeds and achievements. The value of these titles such as ‘Forge Master’, ‘Storm Proven’ or ‘Wise Shield’, often recorded upon the bearer’s armour in stylised Nocturnian glyph-forms and branded into their flesh. Some like ‘Firedrake’ came to mark the Legion’s core of elite warriors and carried direct military authority as well as honorary significance.  

The Firedrakes, named for the greatest of the salamander saurids said to originate beneath the stones of Mount Deathfire, were the finest and most experienced warriors of the Legion. To be afforded this exalted status, mere skill-at-arms was not enough alone, and the warrior would have to have been proven of faultless bravery and just as importantly, faultless self-control. Amongst these, the greatest formed the Pyre Guard - the Primarch’s own loyal retainers. Each of the seven Realms had its own core of Firedrakes to serve as leaders and exemplars of the Legion, and in this they received a special duty to instruct by both word and deed. Firedrakes were particularly selected for their wisdom, learning and devotion to the spiritual aspects of the Promethean Cult.  

At the outset of the Horus Heresy, the bulk of the Salamanders Legion had been re-arming and re-equipping itself at Nocturne after a lengthy campaign of xenos eradication near the galactic core. As such it was able to answer the call to Istvaan V with the majority of its active strength. It is believed a force of around 83 000 Space Marines went with their Primarch to Istvaan V, the losses of which during the deadly action were almost total, rated by some sources as high as 98%.  

However the Legion did maintain other detachments that did not travel to the Istvaan system and so were spared the bloody cataclysm. Among these were the fully equipped garrison maintained at Geryon Deep and the Castellan of Prometheus’ forces at around 2000 to 3000 remaining, alongside the full intake of neophytes in training on Prometheus, combined with several line companies on detached deployment elsewhere. Therefore, the commonly held estimates of the Legion strength of approximately 89 000 Legiones Astartes, placing them among the smallest overall of the Legions in manpower. It remains a testimony to the undying resilience of the Legion and its spiritual and psychological strength that despite the losses on Istvaan V it was able to maintain coherence and recover its strength, dragging itself back from the precipice of oblivion and remaining a bloody thorn in the side of the Traitors for the rest of the War of the Heresy.

### The Promethean Cult

#### Salamanders — The Promethean Cult

Philosophy, ritual discipline and the culture of Vulkan’s Legion

#### Origins and Purpose

Established by Vulkan through written teachings and ritual practice, the Promethean Cult defined the culture he wished to instil in the Salamanders. It combined ancient Terran martial and philosophical traditions with the mythology and craftsmanship of Nocturne. Its central belief was that the Legiones Astartes existed for one immutable purpose: the protection and liberation of all humanity. Each Space Marine was a living weapon, physically and spiritually perfected through the hardships of war, much as a smith tempers a blade in the forge.

#### Virtues, Symbols and Rituals

The Cult prized self-reliance, loyalty, duty, patience, awareness and self-sacrifice, with particular emphasis on self-discipline, endurance and unwavering determination. Fire embodied both creation and destruction, while the hammer and anvil represented the Nocturnean traditions of craft and trial. Tests of physical strength and endurance became integral to Legion training and continuing spiritual discipline, often accompanied by ritual scarification or branding.

#### The Imperial Truth and Resistance to Corruption

The Cult’s mystical imagery and severe rituals attracted criticism from those who regarded them as barbaric or superstitious and potentially incompatible with the Imperial Truth. Nevertheless, it worshipped no gods and affirmed humanity’s primacy in the cosmos, largely shielding it from such objections. Later scholars suggested that its deeply rooted beliefs may also have helped protect the Salamanders from the subversion of the Word Bearers. Their emissaries met only polite indifference from Vulkan and his Legion, and no Warrior Lodge was established within its ranks to spread the Warmaster’s influence.

That escape did more than save him. In time the vessel was renamed Hesiod, after one of Nocturne’s great cities, and became part of Shadrak Meduson’s vengeance-driven Shattered Legions fleet.

Dha’lok continued to serve in a Tactical Support role. He is shown wearing a hybrid suit of battle plate later formalised as Mark V armour, while still retaining the Mark III helm and pauldrons he had worn during the massacre on Isstvan V.

Disciple of the Flames

After Cassian Dracos was recovered on Isstvan V, the Astartes aboard the Ebon Drake were visibly transformed in both belief and appearance. Many adopted the divinatory customs of Xiaphas Jurr’s home world, decorating their armour with charms and devotional imagery, and Legionary Shaan is presented as a typical example of this new identity. His once plain Salamanders plate had been heavily altered with black ash-and-fire patterns, images of Vulkan and the Dragon Revenant, chains bearing engraved bones and fragments taken from the dead of Isstvan V, and pages from Jurr’s Prophecies of the Flame, some sealed by Mezoa itself. Shaan thus embodies how the company evolved into the Disciples of the Flames, a force shaped as much by faith and omen as by war. Like many of them, his final fate is unknown, and even the Salamanders’ own records appear to have deliberately erased the details of his later deeds.

## Legion Chronicles

### Unification Wars
As with several of the proto-Legion groupings in the closing stages of the Unification Wars, much remains unknown as to the exact founding and intake of the XVIIIth Legion. These Legion-groups were formed and established largely in separation from the rest, and it is generally thought, created to very specific ends.  

The XVIIIth Legion, that would later become known as the Salamanders, along with the VIth and XXth comprised this group of proto-Legions. That this ‘trefoil’ of three proto-Legions was somehow veiled from the rest during their creation and establishment, was something known to their fellows, but by the edict of the Emperor and his agents, it remained unspoken of openly. This element of mystery surrounding them can be seen to establish the distance between the three and the others, which would later blossom into a level of distrust. This distrust would magnify for very different reasons in regards to what would later become the Space Wolves Legion and Alpha Legion, but would be ameliorated in the case of the Salamanders by their later conduct and the influence and person of their Primarch Vulkan. However, this was not so in their early days, and it is forgotten now by most that at the outset of the Great Crusade, the reputation being forged by the XVIIIth Legion was a far more bloody and bellicose one than they would later carry.  

The pattern of their early fate was prefigured by the first major battle for which they became publicly identified - the Assault of the Tempest Galleries in the dying days of the Unification Wars. In this, the Legion alone achieved a victory against impossible odds and survived, taking savage losses which reduced the newly invested Legion from around 20 000 to little more than 1000 warriors. The Legion’s strength was rapidly rebuilt with new waves of recruits, their victorious reputation seeing them favoured for the procurement of new wargear and supply, often over many of their peers but, regardless, the pause kept them from the initial explosion of expeditionary fleets departing Terra.

##### The Ethnarchy of the Caucasus Wastes

The assault on the Tempest Galleries was the first openly recorded battle honour of the then-unnamed XVIII Legion, fought long before its reunion with Vulkan. Earlier actions may have taken place, but their records were lost, deliberately erased or sealed. The campaign was among the final great struggles of the Unification Wars, whose conclusion established the Emperor as the undisputed master of Terra. The Caucasus Wastes were ruled by mutated eugenicist oligarchs, the Ethnarchy, whose strength lay in ancient relic-technologies and formidable weapons rather than sheer numbers. Its armies included armoured, gene-enhanced Ur-Khasis, comparable to the Thunder Warriors, and drug-enslaved psyker covens. Its mountain fortresses lay kilometres underground, protected by nearly impenetrable power-field networks. An earlier Imperial offensive had failed at the cost of almost 10,000 Thunder Warriors and over a million other casualties, leaving the region isolated but undefeated.

For the final campaign, the Emperor assembled six complete proto-Legions, the Custodian Guard and numerous allied armies, mechanised formations and warrior bands. Despite doubts within the Imperial command, he entrusted the operation on which the entire offensive depended to the untested XVIII. All 20,000 Legionaries were committed to destroying the subterranean geothermal installations powering the Ethnarchy’s defences. Wearing modified power armour in sulphur-yellow and black camouflage, they descended aboard newly built Termite boring machines incorporating Martian technology. Their destination was the Tempest Galleries, an artificial cavern complex extending roughly a hundred kilometres, filled with fire and electromagnetic disturbances generated by machinery of forgotten origin.

##### Into the Tempest Galleries

Contact with the Legion vanished almost immediately, and even Imperial psykers could not reach the force. Above ground, the Emperor’s armies waited for weeks. Below, nearly four-fifths of the XVIII had reached its destination; the remainder had succumbed to tectonic movements, magma and failing protective systems. The Legion discovered immense spindle-shaped machines suspended over molten material drawn from Terra’s core, their lightning-shrouded structures connected by silicate webs. No warriors of the Ethnarchy defended this domain: no human had entered it for millennia. Instead, thousands of ancient service-automata, still maintaining the installations after ages of isolation, responded to the intrusion as a threat to their system.

These tempest-automata exceeded contemporary Mechanicum battle-automata in strength and durability. Bolter, plasma and melta fire proved ineffective against their armour, and the Legion learned through heavy losses that their articulated joints could be disabled by powerful close-range impacts. The machines maintained relentless pressure, and enormous tunnelling spheres crushed many of the Termite transports, cutting off retreat. Thousands of Legionaries died within the opening twelve hours. Yet the XVIII retained its cohesion, with individual warriors willingly sacrificing themselves to save their brothers. Its survival ultimately depended as much on ingenuity as on endurance: the Legion repaired and augmented its equipment with salvaged machinery, turned damaged automata against their fellows and harnessed the installations’ own destructive energies. The warriors converted recovered components into improvised weapons, including power fists, thunder hammers, shock cannon and cutting saws.

##### The Battle for the Great Gallery

With fewer than a third of its original warriors remaining, the XVIII recognised that a prolonged struggle was impossible. The intelligence governing the complex could manufacture replacements, while the Legion could not. Its surviving commanders devised a coordinated offensive to destroy the central power-transmission node, interrupting the energy supply to the Ethnarchy’s defences even if the generators themselves survived. One division would draw the defenders away while the larger force struck the node. The central attack was considered effectively a death sentence; so many Legionaries volunteered that assignments had to be determined by lottery.

The offensive initially succeeded, as the governing intelligence divided its forces between the simultaneous threats. The main body reached the Great Gallery with its remaining Dreadnoughts and Rapier batteries, fighting across bridges suspended between molten depths and electrical storms. As the Legion approached the transmission node, it encountered the greatest of the maintenance engines: a vast kraken-like machine clad in synthetic black diamond, powerful enough to crush a battle Titan. Its attacks shattered the bridges, and the Legionaries were unable to harm it with their remaining weapons. Their final garbled report described a colossal tunnelling sphere crashing through the gallery and striking the node. It is believed that a diversionary unit may have commandeered the machine, although the exact cause remains uncertain. The ensuing explosion devastated the surrounding caverns, apparently destroyed the controlling machine intelligence and brought the entire complex to a halt.

##### In Fire and Darkness Tested

The destruction of the node produced earthquakes felt as far away as Gorodak and the Jade Citadel of Hangol. The power-fields protecting the Caucasus Wastes collapsed, allowing the Emperor’s assembled armies to crush the Ethnarchy. Its last ruler was taken in chains to the prison of Khangba Marwu, and its technological secrets passed into Imperial hands. The XVIII’s fate remained unknown until the closing days of the campaign, when a dormant volcano near Klostzatz ruptured and a repaired Termite emerged carrying just over a thousand survivors. The Legion had lost roughly 95% of its original strength. Its extraordinary account was accepted only with the corroboration of physical evidence and hololithic records.

The Emperor personally bestowed upon the survivors the Laurel of Victory for the campaign, a permanent distinction, and their first recorded battle honour, bearing the motto “In Fire and Darkness Tested”. The surviving Legionaries also recovered knowledge of metallurgy and machine craft predating the Age of Strife; this was shared with Imperial Command and retained within the Legion. The battle established a lasting precedent for the XVIII: technical resourcefulness, disciplined endurance, readiness to sacrifice oneself for one’s brothers and an expectation of victory against overwhelming odds. These qualities predated Vulkan’s leadership and would remain central to the identity of the Salamanders.

### Great Crusade
Instead, the XVIIIth Legion was deployed piecemeal as fresh chapters of the Legion were readied and pressing demands called for Space Marine involvement. For the first few decades of the Great Crusade, Legionaries of the XVIIIth were assigned across a considerable number of different reinforcement battle groups and specialist units such as Rogue Trader expeditions, and rarely fought together as a Legion. In many of these cases, the warriors of the Legion would be the only Space Marine contingent taking part in conflict, forcing them into close co-operation with other human forces and inevitably plunged them directly into the heaviest fighting and most hazardous theatres of battle.  

Seldom then did the XVIIIth fight on the battlefield of its own choosing. Its roll of battle honours was extensive, as was the diversity of its foes and the victories it secured. For the XVIIIth this created a paradoxical reputation both as savior but also a herald of bloody deeds, forlorn hopes and last ditch holding actions. Against the foes of humanity there were no odds the XVIIIth would not face, no drop of blood it would not shed, no sacrifice it would not make in terms of Legionaries in order to serve the Great Crusade and protect Mankind. This kind of asymmetrical battle, it was soon whispered by some of their contemporaries, had become an addiction of sorts to the Legion and their valour less glorious as almost suicidal in temper. Regardless of the truth of this assertion, it was plain that to the XVIIIth to triumph against the odds was the only victory worth the name. In the worst instances, such as the legendary Manticore Cataclysm, retreat for the XVIIIth became often unthinkable even when tactical expediency would dictate otherwise, and the Legion paid in blood for its own untenable standard of valour and service.  

In addition to the influence of the Legion’s interrupted development and evolution, attrition would ensure that in these early days, the XVIIIth Legion’s numbers never fully developed in the manner of others, battlelosses preventing them from ever reaching the nominal full strength accorded by the Imperial High Command, while its warriors and officers remained distanced from those in other Legions through a combination of circumstances and choice.  

### Rediscovery of Vulkan
When the Primarch Vulkan was restored to the Imperium, it was to be on the world of Nocturne. This world of his growth and maturity would shape much about him and, by extension, his Legion in later years. Nocturne is a death world, a turbulent volcanic orb riven with fire, radiation and subject to destructive tidal forces caused by its moon, Prometheus. Nocturne is vastly rich in mineral wealth, particularly in gemstones and precious metals, but sparse in the stability of climate or the abundance of organic flora which makes human life readily possible. Further to the perils of Nocturne are numerous forms of the highly evolved and adapted predator species and mega-fauna hardened themselves by the environment of this foreboding world and the struggle to survive there.  

The people of Nocturne had become, through design or natural evolution, a phenomenally hardly breed, markedly resistant both to extremes of climate, gravity and of ambient radiation far above the human form, yet were also possessed of considerable genetic stability. The Nocturnians had maintained a high degree of cultural sophistication and social cohesion, and the values of stoic resistance, perseverance in the face of adversity and, above all, courage had become the hallmarks of its tribal people. Deadly Nocturne was to its people ‘the anvil’.  

Among the hardly and stoic people of Nocturn, the infant Vulkan had fallen like a blazing comet, and into the home of a ‘Black Smiter’ - a metal worker from Hesiod, he had been taken as a foster child. Quickly, he has risen in strength and wisdom, embracing the culture which had taken him up, working in his adoptive father’s forge and hunting the great saurians and other beasts, becoming a legendary champion in his world’s defence against far more dangerous foes - ‘Dusk Wraiths’, as they were known on Nocturne. In reality, these creatures were of the most degenerate and vile class of Eldar Slavers, nightmarish creatures, for whom pain and slaughter were as meat and drink. Against this threat Vulkan became transformed, and his legend spread across his world. He was the ‘fire-born’ - an undefeated warrior whose superhuman strength had torn the slave-barges from the skies and whose granite-like flesh had scorned their poisoned blades unmoved, driving the Dusk Wraiths from Nocturne.  

When the Emperor came to Nocturne, he found his lost son as mighty protector who had refused a coronation he could have taken by force, and for who power for its own sake was a hollow trinket, nothing more. Having tested his lost son using the Nocturne’s own ritual trials, he judged him worthy and revealed his true nature to the Primarch. In answer, Vulkan’s only reservation on departing Nocturne was that he would not leave its people undefended, but in this the Emperor countered that Vulkan’s duty was not simply to one world but to many, worlds that knew terror of the darkness and the feasting of alien horrors uncounted.  

It is believed that Vulkan did not become unified with his Legion for some years after his rediscovery, but instead stayed alongside the Emperor under his direct tutelage, during which his presence was kept from the wider Imperium. During this period, Vulkan pursued with frightening speed and comprehension learning in the arts of war, history and science, displaying a ferocious intelligence, and also wisdom and compassion that were perhaps at odds with the role he has been destined to play as a general and breaker of the worlds. He fought at the side of the Emperor - a colossal, nameless warrior in emerald armour scaled like a dragon of ancient Terran myth - and studied closely in the weapon-forges of Mars with his brother Ferrus Manus.  

When Vulkan came to his Legion, it was in the hour of their need. The XVIIIth, led by their Lord Commander Cassian Vaughn had become embroiled in the rear-guard defence of a cluster of colony worlds near the Taras Division against a wave of Ork marauders. With the bulk of the Legiones Astartes either engaged with the expedition fleets breaching space towards the Eastern Fringe or committed as reserves against the horrors of the Rangda Incursion from the Halo Stars to the Galactic North, the XVIIIth was the only Space Marine Legion able to respond to the crisis. Fighting against vast and overwhelming odds, the Legion’s primary force, numbering some 19 000 Space Marines has marshalled the local defenders and held out for nearly a standard year in a series of running battles against well over a million Ork raiders scattered across hundreds of ramshackle ships, ‘rok’ asteroid vessels and dozens of hulks. The actions of the Legion had allowed the evacuations of three entire planetary populations to the nominal safety of the Taras system, but at terrible cost. As the conflict progressed, they suffered the grievous wounding of their commander, while the remainder of the XVIIIth became all but trapped on the dead world of Antaem - a lightning-rod drawing the Orks to them for battle.  

Their Primarch, learning of their plight refused to stand by and brought his plans to join them to rapid fruition. When Vulkan arrived he brought with him 3000 new initiates - the first of the Legion to be raised from Nocturne - along with a host of new warships, war machines and arms, all fabricated to the Primarch’s own specifications. They fell upon the Orks like a thunderbolt, and shattered the largest of the hulks orbiting Antaem. Spurred on by this unexpected aid, the rest of the XVIIIth hurled themselves in renewed fury at the Orks besieging them, slaughtering and scattering the xenos before them, heedless of their depleted munitions and manpower, leaving nothing for a reserve should they fail. The Ork horde was broken and put to flight, and the survivors were relentlessly pursued and consumed with fire.  

In the aftermath, the two halves of the XVIIIth Legion met and were unified. As their saviours removed their helms and the Terran Legionaries looked upon the faces of their brothers, they could not help but know that they were one and their Primarch had come to claim them. The survivors of the Terran XVIIIth knelt immediately before their Primarch, but Vulkan bid them rise, saying that all his sons were equals and he was no petty king needing shows of obedience. Instead, it was he who knelt in honour of the lives they had saved and the price they had paid. Then, seeking out the mortally wounded Lord Commander, he conferred the formal transfer of the Legion’s mastery by presenting the fallen warrior with the broken power klaw of the Ork Warlord to seal the pact - they would fight for him, but he would also fight for them in turn.  

After the battle at Antaem, Vulkan set about remarking and re-forging his Legion, keen to keep and honour the spirit of self-sacrifice for the Imperium and bravery he found, but also determined that it should stand together and become more disciplined both in its temper and wiser in its pursuit of battle. With its forces brought together, Vulkan returned the legion on the Nocturne to where a powerful stronghold was being constructed on Nocturne’s moon of Prometheus to serve as its headquarters and armoury, just as Nocturne would now be the source of the majority of the Legion’s fresh recruits. Here the Legion was reordered and re-armed and, most importantly, Vulkan set about giving it common purpose, and common belief. In order to do this, he drew on the culture and deeply-ingrained warrior and mystical traditions of Nocturne in which Vulkan had been raised. In this Vulkan was wise enough to retain and value the experience of the Terran veterans, and show them to respect and hold in high esteem what they had accomplished.  

For the fallen first master of the XVIIIth, Cassian Vaughn, Vulkan fashioned with his own hands a unique Dreadnought sarcophagus, the Iron Dragon, so that Vaughn could serve as Castellan and Protector of Prometheus, and the future of the Legion.  

This remade Legion would now take its name from the greatest of Nocturne’s saurian predators; ancient and deadly creatures whose blood was fire and whose hides were as hard as emerald steel; the Salamanders of Nocturne. In this Vulkan’s choice carried a layered meaning, for not only were Nocturnean Salamanders monsters of savage power with a great totemic significance to the native people, but as creatures they showed unflinching loyalty to their own blood and offspring, and were never more ferocious than in their defence. It was from the bleached-white skull of one such great beast - Kasare - that adorned the shoulder-guard of their Primarch’s armour, the Legion would now take its new emblem.  

A fresh Legion quickly took up its place at the forefront of the Great Crusade, smashing alien empires and bringing lost worlds into compliance. Despite the numbers, its power and battle-prowess was undeniable, and its conduct in war was regarded as exemplary. It had been tempered and proven, retaining the fearlessness and savage spirit for which Vulkan had been renowned, but those traits were now governed and kept in check by stoicism and lack of hubris. Once they decided to unleash their wrath, it was as unstoppable and terrifying as the volcanic fury of the dark world they now called home.

### Istvaan V
The Loyalist warships hurled onwards, towards the planet’s upper atmosphere, the ornate and savage aspect of the bronze-chased and emerald reptilian-armoured hulls of the Salamanders vessels, contrasting with sable-black of the Raven Guard’s own ships, darker than the void through which they flew, and at their head, the pitiless iron barbed spike of the Ferrum, descending like a spear hurled from the hand of wrathful god.  

Attacking northwards towards the fortress-line, the Loyalists’s left flank was taken by the massed forces of the Salamanders Legion. Here the battleground offered the most even approach to the fortress, but here the highest concentration of zig-zagging slip-trenches and redoubts had been constructed to deny such approach.  

With their Primarch Vulkan at their head, the Salamanders turned trench after trench into a river of fire before them, only to have the Death Guard answer with tainted flames of their own.  

..More enemy heavy support squads appeared to ring the outer hills..blasted from their cover by annihilating rays of volkite carronades as Salamanders Legion Glaive super-heavy tanks took up guarding positions on the flanks of the advancing Loyalist forces.  

After 3 hours of battle, Salamanders and Raven Guard began to fall back to regroup and rearm, having expended their supplies.  

..Vulkan of the Salamanders was also in favour of consolidation, his Legion having seen some of the most relentless and savage fighting against the Death Guard..Almost all of Vulkan’s warriors were counted wounded..

### The Ebon Drake and Siege of Mezoa (after Istvaan V)

Almost a year after Vulkan and the main Salamanders Legion departed for Isstvan V, Nocturne remained unaware of their fate. Lord Chaplain Nomus Rhy'tan, appointed Keeper of the Legion fortress of Prometheus, had received only fragmentary rumours of a growing Traitor empire and fallen Primarchs. With merely a few thousand veteran instructors and largely untested neophyte battalions defending the home world, he could neither launch a major expedition nor abandon his duty to protect Nocturne. Instead, he resolved to dispatch a single vessel to discover what had happened to Vulkan and his Legion.

The chosen ship was the Ebon Drake, a prototype assault cruiser designed and constructed by the Salamanders themselves. Unlike conventional warships adapted from older designs, it had been developed specifically around the XVIII Legion's preferred methods of warfare, combining formidable combat capabilities with the speed and flexibility needed to escape superior enemies. Rhy'tan entrusted its command to Chaplain-Lieutenant Xiaphas Jurr, a promising officer recruited from Proximal, a world whose inhabitants had once called the Salamanders the Devils from the Outer Dark. Jurr's profound devotion to Vulkan made the mission both a military responsibility and a personal obligation. His force comprised 30 veteran Firedrakes and a full company of newly promoted Legionaries, deliberately selected from Nocturne's various domains and the Legion's off-world territories to represent the XVIII Legion as a whole.

Jurr departed Nocturne immediately and imposed relentless combat training upon his inexperienced crew. However, worsening Warp disturbances made a direct journey to Isstvan impossible, forcing the Ebon Drake to follow a circuitous route with frequent returns to realspace to verify its position and gather intelligence. These necessary stops exposed the small expedition to potentially hostile forces, turning its search for Vulkan into an increasingly dangerous mission through a galaxy already descending into civil war.

The Ebon Drake's journey through the Imperium remains largely undocumented, although surviving navigation records reveal that its first known destination was the Sulis system. Its rulers had declared independence from both the Emperor and Horus, sealing their territory behind heavily fortified orbital defences. Unaware of the full extent of the Horus Heresy, Chaplain-Lieutenant Xiaphas Jurr regarded this rejection of Imperial authority as an intolerable betrayal of the Great Crusade's ideals. When his messages of support from Nocturne were answered with insults and gunfire, he launched an immediate assault.

Over three days, the Ebon Drake systematically destroyed Sulis' orbital defence platforms and intercepted its heavily armed patrol vessels, exploiting the prototype cruiser's superior speed and manoeuvrability. Jurr personally led boarding actions against the defenders, culminating in an assault on the system's primary orbital bastion. His Salamanders overwhelmed its Solar Auxilia garrison and sabotaged the station's gravity engines, sending the immense fortress crashing into the golden palace-city of Sulis' rulers. Having eliminated the system's ability to resist Imperial forces, Jurr withdrew to continue his search for Vulkan.

The victory ultimately served Horus rather than the Emperor. By destroying Sulis' defences without remaining to secure the system, the Salamanders inadvertently left it vulnerable to the Warmaster's advancing armies, which later conquered the world without resistance.

Following the destruction of Sulis' defences, the Ebon Drake continued its search for Vulkan and the missing Salamanders, encountering increasingly mysterious and dangerous threats. Fragmentary records mention the disappearance of three Legionaries at the Oasis of Nightmares and an encounter with an unidentified crystalline xenos vessel that left the cruiser powerless for several days. A possible sighting at Valthrudnir described a ship bearing a black dragon emblem and crewed by void-devils. More significantly, Iron Warriors records describe the grand cruiser Grim Paragon, stationed above Olympia, suffering severe damage after confronting an unidentified Salamanders vessel seeking information about Isstvan. Although the identification remains uncertain, this suggests that Xiaphas Jurr's expedition may have fought the Iron Warriors above Perturabo's home world.

At Anvilus, one of the Mechanicum's greatest centres of arms production, the Ebon Drake encountered a devastating civil war instead of the repairs and supplies it desperately needed. With both Mechanicum factions appearing virtually identical and their loyalties impossible to determine, the Salamanders found themselves attacked by both sides. Hostile machine-code transmissions attempted to infiltrate and disable the cruiser's systems, but the prototype vessel's unusual engineering prevented a complete takeover, limiting the damage to failures in several secondary systems. Jurr diverted power to the oversized engines and ordered an immediate withdrawal, evading plasma bombardments from Mechanicum warships while his Legionaries fought murderous servitors that had boarded during the temporary disruption.

The Ebon Drake escaped Anvilus but suffered serious damage to its hull and internal systems, alongside mounting Astartes casualties. After successive engagements across the galaxy, Jurr's force was dangerously depleted. Without substantial repairs and reinforcements, the expedition had little chance of completing its original mission to discover Vulkan's fate.

The Ebon Drake next reached Baal, the home world of the Blood Angels, where Xiaphas Jurr hoped to repair his damaged cruiser and discover the fate of Vulkan. Most of the IX Legion had departed for Signus Prime under Horus' orders years earlier, leaving a small garrison commanded by Warden Arkhad, Seneschal of Baal, while Sanguinius himself remained missing. Baal had become a refuge for battered Imperial forces retreating from the northern war zones, including survivors of Isstvan V. Although Jurr finally learned something of the Dropsite Massacre, the survivors could offer only fragmented accounts, and none knew the fate of the missing Primarchs. Many already mourned their gene-fathers as dead, constructing symbolic tombs in their honour.

Among the Salamanders survivors was Praetor-Captain Kar'Tor, heir-designate to the Protectorate of Themis, once a highly respected warrior of Nocturne. Traumatised by Isstvan V, Kar'Tor refused Jurr's request for reinforcements, insisting that the surviving Salamanders remain to defend Baal. Nevertheless, 38 Salamanders who had escaped aboard the Thunderhawk Ohidoran joined the Ebon Drake, accompanied by 17 Raven Guard under Legionary Kirhane and three Iron Hands Cataphractii Terminators. All were determined to discover the fate of their missing Primarchs, even at the cost of their lives.

Warden Arkhad also opposed the expedition, considering it reckless to remove a fully armed Astartes cruiser from Baal's already weakened defences. He eventually permitted the Ebon Drake to depart only after Jurr threatened his orbital fortifications with the ship's bombardment cannon. Arkhad condemned the departing warriors as little better than Traitors, accusing them of abandoning living Imperial defenders for the sake of those who might already be dead. His words left the crew troubled, but Jurr continued towards Isstvan, now reinforced by survivors of three shattered Loyalist Legions and determined to uncover the truth about Vulkan.

After leaving Baal, the Ebon Drake continued directly towards Isstvan V, abandoning a planned stop at Port Maw. Xiaphas Jurr's decision to avoid further contact with Imperial defenders unknowingly saved the ship from encountering Horus' fleet, which had recently occupied the great naval anchorage. Approaching Isstvan, the Salamanders encountered a violent Warp vortex that appeared to have been deliberately created to imprison something within the northern systems. Despite the inability of their Navigator and Astropath to penetrate its veil, Jurr insisted on proceeding, guided by his oaths and omens interpreted through the flames of his votive forge. The Ebon Drake forced its way through the storm, severely straining its Gellar field, and emerged at the edge of the Isstvan System prepared for battle. Instead of Horus' armies, however, the crew discovered only abandoned warship wrecks and the devastated remains of Isstvan V. The Traitors had departed long before, leaving mass graves stripped of useful equipment and no indication of what had happened to Vulkan, Ferrus Manus, or Corvus Corax.

For three days, the Salamanders, Raven Guard, and Iron Hands searched the ruined surface of Isstvan V, while Jurr continuously scanned the planet from orbit, increasingly exhausted and desperate. On the third day, the Ebon Drake detected an extremely faint signal beneath a vast plain of glass created by the Traitors' atomic bombardment. Its unusual transmission wavelength was recognisable only to certain members of the Salamanders' command hierarchy. Lacking suitable excavation equipment, the Legionaries spent another day breaking through the fused ground with their hands and combat blades. The three Iron Hands Cataphractii eventually recovered the ruined sarcophagus of a Mk IV Dreadnought, its limbs almost entirely destroyed and its hull blackened by atomic fire. Despite having remained buried for more than a year, its damaged systems confirmed that its occupant was still alive.

The Dreadnought was Cassian Dracos, the last mortal commander to hold the title of Master of the XVIII Legion before Vulkan's arrival. Recognising his distinctive heraldry, Jurr connected the shattered sarcophagus to an external vox unit. On the dead surface of Isstvan V, surrounded by the remnants of three shattered Legions, the ancient commander spoke once more, offering the expedition its first living witness to the catastrophe that had consumed the Salamanders.

You come seeking our father, but he is not here. I searched for him on the field of battle until they struck me down with a sword of flame that scorched the very heavens, and yet I did not die. I searched for him in the endless vaults of the dead, where the crushing blackness tore at my very soul, and yet I endured. I was tested once more in fire and in darkness, and passed again through the crucible, and I tell you this: Vulkan does not walk among the dead!

While Cassian Dracos' survival brought renewed hope to the Salamanders, Horus was assembling an enormous invasion armada at Port Maw, which had become the principal staging ground for his advance towards Terra. There, the Traitor Legions repaired and refitted their fleets using resources originally intended for the Great Crusade. As preparations intensified, Horus ordered the destruction of the Loyalist Forge World Mezoa, whose defenders had already repelled two invasions. Previously considered easier to isolate than conquer, Mezoa now represented an unacceptable threat to the Warmaster's rear lines. However, its conquest promised heavy casualties, and rival Traitor commanders manoeuvred against one another to avoid responsibility for the campaign. Ultimately, the assignment fell to two formations weakened by their failure at Fortress Stranivar: the Iron Warriors' 114th Grand Battalion under Nârik Dreygur and the Alpha Legion's 78th Chapter under Autilon Skorr.

Consul-Delegatus Autilon Skorr was appointed overall commander of the Mezoan expedition, using his seniority to seize most available supplies and reinforcements for the Alpha Legion. This left Dreygur's already depleted Iron Warriors struggling to recover from their losses at Stranivar, with little hope of support from the distant Perturabo or other Traitor commanders. Despite these internal divisions, the invasion force comprised 9,000 Legionaries, twelve capital warships, including two Mangonel-class bombardment cruisers, three Taghmata from M'Pandex, and 20,000 auxiliary troops. It was among the largest independent Traitor battlegroups entrusted to a commander other than a Primarch during the early Heresy. Victory promised Skorr considerable favour from Horus, making the expedition both a military opportunity and a means of advancing his personal ambitions. With Davinite priests assuring the Navigators of a safe passage, the armada departed Port Maw, poised to attack Mezoa within days of the Ebon Drake's arrival.

The recovery of Cassian Dracos restored hope and unity among the Salamanders, Raven Guard, and Iron Hands aboard the Ebon Drake. Although Dracos could offer no assurance about the fate of Ferrus Manus or Corvus Corax, his survival and conviction that Vulkan still lived inspired the entire company. Once installed aboard the cruiser, his ancient Dreadnought chassis, forged by Vulkan himself, began repairing its damaged systems without assistance. Despite more than a year of conscious entombment beneath Isstvan V, Dracos refused to enter the restorative sleep customary for ancient Dreadnoughts. Instead, he became an increasingly influential figure, delivering cryptic prophecies and recounting memories of centuries of warfare. Many Legionaries adopted new devotional heraldry in his honour, and those who gathered around him became known as the Disciples of the Flames.

Dracos' influence extended beyond the Astartes. Servitors and automata aboard the Ebon Drake began exhibiting inexplicable behaviour, with some abandoning their duties to attend him without receiving orders. These disturbances were apparently never investigated. Within days, the ancient warrior, now known as the Dragon Revenant, had effectively assumed command of the expedition. Although its original mission was complete and many expected to return to Nocturne, Dracos insisted that the cruiser instead travel to the Forge World of Mezoa. Few questioned his authority, and the Ebon Drake departed Isstvan V under his direction, leaving the ruined system behind forever.

The Ebon Drake arrived at Mezoa to find the Loyalist Forge World already under siege by a powerful Traitor armada. Iron Warriors and Alpha Legion warships were battling a heavily outnumbered defensive fleet composed of scattered Legion vessels, Imperial Navy ships, and Mezoan warcraft. Emerging unexpectedly into the middle of the engagement, the Salamanders cruiser briefly confused the Traitors, giving its crew enough time to identify friendly vessels and open fire. The Ebon Drake then joined the Loyalist defenders, attempting to prevent Horus' forces from securing control of the planet's orbital approaches.

The battle continued for nine hours, with the Traitors sacrificing warships to gradually overwhelm the defenders. The Loyalists suffered from divided command, as their various contingents answered to separate leaders and lacked effective coordination. Eventually, they were forced to abandon Mezoa's orbit and retreat towards the system's outer reaches. Several Loyalist vessels attempted desperate final approaches to deploy reinforcements onto the planet, but only three of the six ships involved survived. With the defensive fleet driven away, the Iron Warriors and Alpha Legion were free to position their troop transports and heavy cruisers above Mezoa, systematically destroying its orbital fortifications and defence satellites. The final invasion of the Forge World had begun.

As the Iron Warriors and Alpha Legion prepared their invasion of Mezoa, Cassian Dracos, whose Dreadnought body had partially regenerated through Vulkan's extraordinary craftsmanship, entered the planet's central forge alongside his Disciples of the Flames. There he met the Norn-regents, three ancient magos-sisters whose interconnected minds governed the Forge World as a single intelligence. Even their formidable Domitar automata withdrew at his approach. Dracos recounted his suffering beneath Isstvan V and his visions concerning Vulkan, finding unexpected common ground with Mezoa's religious beliefs. The Norn-regents proclaimed him a living avatar of the Machine God, granted him a high position within their defensive forces, and interpreted his arrival as a sign of approaching victory. The remarkable influence Dracos exerted over both machines and people later led scholars to speculate that his prolonged entombment within Isstvan's Warp disturbances had altered him in ways not yet understood.

While Mezoa's finest artificers took Dracos into the planet's hidden forges to restore his damaged Dreadnought chassis, Xiaphas Jurr assumed command of the Disciples of the Flames in the coming defence. The Loyalist garrison included the battle-hardened Taghmata Mezoa, the 891st Lethe Cohort of the Solar Auxilia, Knights of House Hermetika, and 48 Imperial Fists stranded after the fighting at Manachea. Together with combat automata and adsecularis troops, approximately 12,000 defenders prepared to resist the overwhelming Traitor invasion as enemy transports filled the skies above Mezoa.

The third invasion of Mezoa began with Autilon Skorr sacrificing thousands of Moabite conscripts and renegade Imperial Army troops in an expendable first wave. The defenders responded by using immense subduction furnaces to launch magma and bedrock into orbit, destroying most of the approaching transports. Those who reached the surface were slaughtered by Vorax battle-automata. Anticipating that Mezoa could not sustain such bombardment without destabilising its own crust, Skorr committed the Iron Warriors' 114th Grand Battalion under Nârik Dreygur as the second wave. Once they landed, Skorr threatened to destroy any vessel attempting evacuation, effectively forcing the Iron Warriors to fight to the death. Despite fierce Loyalist resistance, Dreygur's warriors established a beachhead using their transports as improvised fortifications. During the first day's fighting, Dreygur personally killed Imperial Fists champion Valtus Moran, whose body was subsequently torn apart by battle-automata under his command.

With the landing zone secured, the Traitors deployed their main army beneath a protective network of void shields generated by Sokar-class Stormbirds. The Iron Warriors bore the brunt of attacks against Mezoa's strongest defences, suffering mounting casualties while battling Mechanicum automata. Meanwhile, the Alpha Legion conducted precision raids against vulnerable command centres and isolated officers, exploiting the distractions created by their allies. The Loyalists responded with aggressive counterattacks: Xiaphas Jurr's Disciples of the Flames operated in demi-companies of fifty, supported by the surviving Imperial Fists, while Solar Auxilia and siege-automata defended the forge-fanes. Mezoan tech-adepts deliberately collapsed sections of the planetary crust, burying Traitor formations beneath magma and isolating enemy detachments for destruction. Suicide units of automata and adsecularis also lured attackers into areas targeted by devastating volcanic bombardments, regardless of friendly casualties. Despite these successes, the defenders' meticulous casualty calculations revealed that their smaller army was losing the war of attrition, and Mezoa's eventual defeat appeared increasingly likely.

For nine days, Autilon Skorr directed an unrelenting siege against Mezoa, combining orbital bombardment with devastating ground assaults. The defenders retaliated by launching molten rock from the planet's mantle, turning entire battlefields into wastelands of ash, steam, and magma. Although Xiaphas Jurr and the Disciples of the Flames watched Mezoa's fortifications gradually collapse, the Norn-regents refused to surrender, ordering their forces to fight until the last automaton was destroyed. Recognising that a prolonged siege would delay his ambitions of joining Horus' advance on Terra, Skorr devised a decisive assault intended to destroy Mezoa's leadership. He ordered Nârik Dreygur and the depleted Iron Warriors 114th Grand Battalion to attack the heavily fortified Tertial-05 bastion, promising Alpha Legion support. The Iron Warriors advanced through murderous fire and drew substantial Loyalist reinforcements into the battle, but eventually became trapped in a devastating crossfire. Dreygur fortified his captured positions and repeatedly requested the promised reinforcements, receiving no response.

Skorr had deliberately used the Iron Warriors as a diversion while his relatively untouched Alpha Legion headhunters infiltrated Mezoa's central forge-spires. With most defenders committed to Tertial-05, the infiltrators overwhelmed the remaining automata and advanced towards the chambers of the Norn-regents. Approximately fifty Disciples of the Flames were guarding Cassian Dracos, who remained sealed within the fabrication vaults, and had been ordered not to abandon their posts. Nevertheless, Raven Guard Legionary Kirhane led a small contingent to defend the Norn-regents alongside the surviving Domitar automata. Despite their resistance, Skorr and twenty of his personal guards penetrated the chamber and killed one of the three interconnected magos-sisters using lascutters and meltaguns. The destruction of a single Norn-regent crippled Mezoa's command network, causing battle-automata to collapse or attack one another while Skitarii and Thallax formations lost central direction. Traitor forces surged forward across the planet, and only at Tertial-05 did the Solar Auxilia and Disciples of the Flames continue holding the Iron Warriors at bay. With its leadership shattered and defences disintegrating, Mezoa appeared on the verge of defeat.

Cassian Dracos emerged from Mezoa's fabrication vaults with his Dreadnought chassis fully restored, but instead of confronting Autilon Skorr and the Alpha Legion attacking the central forge-spires, he led the remaining Disciples of the Flames towards Tertial-05. As he advanced, the malfunctioning battle-automata encountered along his path inexplicably recovered from their madness and submitted to his command. By the time he reached the Iron Warriors' fortified positions, Dracos had gathered nearly 1,000 automata under his control. Rather than attacking, he approached the enemy alone, passing unharmed through Iron Warriors automata that refused orders to engage him, and demanded an audience with Nârik Dreygur.

The conversation between Dracos and Dreygur remains largely unknown, although the Prophecies of the Flames suggests that Dracos possessed an extraordinary influence over technology, including enemy war machines and warriors altered by mechanical augmentation. Following their brief exchange, Dreygur addressed his Iron Warriors and apparently underwent a profound change in allegiance or purpose. His private journals ended that day, and he subsequently avoided all company except that of Dracos and his automata. The precise words that persuaded him remain unknown.

'Brothers, once more an oath to us stands broken. The Alpha Legion has abandoned us once again. Now we shall show that we do not renege on our vows. The dead of Stranivar and Mezoa both call for vengeance, and we shall grant it to them?’

Autilon Skorr's apparent victory at Mezoa collapsed when Cassian Dracos returned to battle alongside nearly 1,000 automata and the remnants of Nârik Dreygur's Iron Warriors, who had unexpectedly turned against the Alpha Legion. Trapped between the reactivated Mezoan defenders and advancing Loyalist forces, Skorr found his army surrounded while Solar Auxilia armoured columns attacked the Traitor landing zones. Meanwhile, Loyalist warships emerged from hiding among the outer moons and ambushed the dispersed Traitor fleet. Within hours, most of the invasion force was destroyed, with fewer than 3,000 Traitors escaping the planet. Skorr himself barely survived, suffering a shattered arm during an encounter with Xiaphas Jurr. Despite initially retaining orbital superiority, the remaining Traitor vessels were eventually driven from the system by Loyalist raids and Mezoa's volcanic bombardments.

Cassian Dracos emerged as the saviour of Mezoa, although his mysterious influence over automata and Mechanicum priests inspired suspicion as well as devotion. He subsequently led devastating raids against neighbouring Traitor-held systems, spreading the growing cult of the Disciples of the Flames. Nârik Dreygur became his devoted lieutenant, remaining at the Iron Dragon's side throughout the Horus Heresy. Despite its victory, Mezoa had suffered catastrophic damage to its industrial infrastructure and lost one of its three Norn-regents, severely weakening its ability to support the wider Loyalist war effort. Nevertheless, the Forge World remained independent and became a refuge for isolated Astartes formations, many operating under Dracos' authority. Their continued campaigns helped preserve scattered Loyalist strongholds and disrupt Horus' advance towards Terra, demonstrating the strategic importance of independent Space Marine forces during the Heresy.

### Scouring of the Cyclops Cluster [006–007.M31]

- About twenty surviving Salamanders held the Guildmasters' underground fortress on Dominica Minor against Mortarion's Death Guard. Consul-Centurion Shar'rac Grenn, in a Contemptor Dreadnought, struck and staggered Mortarion before the primarch killed him and dismantled the war machine.

### Loyalist Counter-Invasion of Numinal

- Nar'Shak later fought at Numinal as part of Battlegroup Revenant, displaying the battlegroup emblem alongside traditional Nocturnean honour markings. Revenant conducted attacks on Dark Mechanicum installations.

### Fragmented Legion Operations [007–010.M31]

The Salamanders suffered catastrophic losses at Isstvan V and believed Vulkan dead. Some survivors returned to Nocturne, while others fought alongside the Iron Hands and Raven Guard. The Legion would require generations to recover sufficiently for conventional campaigns.

The Salamanders' losses scattered them among Shattered Legions detachments and improvised defensive formations. Their Ebon Drake expedition and its recovery of Cassian Dracos are recorded separately in the Heresy-era chronology.
